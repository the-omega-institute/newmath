\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\OrderedSemiringUp$}

Following Bourbaki on ordered algebraic structures and Golan on semirings, the packet records a semiring carrier whose additive and multiplicative rows are compatible with a stable order classifier; it consumes \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-semiring-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-order-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-rat-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet gives order-sensitive L10 arithmetic handoffs one named BEDC surface for monotone addition and positive-row multiplication, while keeping ordered rings, field order, quotient order, classical trichotomy, and any standard bridge outside the carrier.

\begin{definition}[Ordered semiring finite-history carrier]
\label{def:ordered-semiring-bhist-carrier}
An $\OrderedSemiringUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
O=(S,Q,R,D,E,A,M,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $S$ is the $\SemiringUp$ source row naming the shared carrier, addition, multiplication, distributivity, zero-annihilation ledger, transport, replay, provenance, and local $\NameCert$ surface.
\item $Q$ is the $\OrderUp$ comparison row naming the stable order classifier attached to the same displayed carrier entries read by $S$.
\item $R$ is the $\RatUp$ arithmetic readback row used by rational order-facing consumers without importing field order.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ handoff row used by dyadic interval and sign-ledger consumers.
\item $E$ is the $\RealUp$ terminal read row reached only after the semiring and order rows remain visible.
\item $A$ records addition monotonicity rows: whenever two displayed input comparisons are accepted by $Q$, the corresponding sum comparison is replayed through the addition route of $S$.
\item $M$ records multiplication monotonicity by positive rows: whenever $Q$ displays a nonnegative or positive multiplier row admitted by the rational or dyadic handoff, the product comparison is replayed through the multiplication route of $S$.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the semiring row, order row, rational readback, dyadic handoff, Real read, monotonicity ledgers, replay, provenance, and local name.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from each order-aware arithmetic request through $S,Q,R,D,E,A,M,H,P,N$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\OrderedSemiringUp$, $\SemiringUp$, $\OrderUp$, $\RatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\OrderedSemiringUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier predicate asserts exactly that these rows form one finite order-compatible semiring packet. It has no coordinate for subtraction, additive inverses, field order, quotient order, trichotomy, ambient Real completeness, or a standard bridge.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Ordered semiring classifier]
\label{def:ordered-semiring-classifier}
Two accepted $\OrderedSemiringUp$ carriers
$$
O=(S,Q,R,D,E,A,M,H,C,P,N)
\quad\text{and}\quad
O'=(S',Q',R',D',E',A',M',H',C',P',N')
$$
are classified together when the semiring rows classify through the displayed $\SemiringUp$ classifier, the order rows classify through the displayed $\OrderUp$ endpoint transport, the rational, dyadic, and Real handoff rows transport componentwise by $\hsame$, and the addition and multiplication monotonicity ledgers replay the same $\Cont$ routes after that transport. The classifier compares no host ordered algebra and no quotient relation.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Ordered semiring NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:ordered-semiring-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\OrderedSemiringUp$ carrier $O=(S,Q,R,D,E,A,M,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\OrderedSemiringUp}$ surface consists exactly of five finite rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item carrier habitation: the displayed $\BHist$ packet of \autoref{def:ordered-semiring-bhist-carrier} is inhabited by a semiring row, an order row, rational and dyadic readback rows, a Real read row, monotonicity ledgers, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming;
\item classifier transport: the classifier of \autoref{def:ordered-semiring-classifier} transports semiring, order, rational, dyadic, Real, monotonicity, replay, provenance, and local name rows componentwise;
\item addition monotonicity: the ledger $A$ records only the comparison of sums obtained by replaying the addition route of $S$ after input comparisons supplied by $Q$;
\item multiplication monotonicity by positive rows: the ledger $M$ records only product comparisons whose multiplier positivity is already displayed by $Q$ together with the $R$ or $D$ handoff row;
\item ledger nonescape: the rows $H,C,P,N$ preserve and replay only the displayed finite inventory and export no ordered ring, field order, classical trichotomy, quotient order, or standard bridge.
\end{enumerate}
No ordered semiring obligation is stored outside these five rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:ordered-semiring-bhist-carrier}. It exposes the $\SemiringUp$ operation row $S$, the $\OrderUp$ comparison row $Q$, the rational, dyadic, and Real handoff rows $R,D,E$, and the two monotonicity ledgers $A,M$.

The classifier of \autoref{def:ordered-semiring-classifier} then fixes the only allowed transport. It moves semiring, order, rational, dyadic, Real, monotonicity, replay, provenance, and local naming rows componentwise, so an accepted comparison after transport is still a comparison of the same displayed packet.

The addition and multiplication clauses are read from $A$ and $M$. The addition row allows only monotone sum replay through $S$ after input order rows from $Q$; the multiplication row allows only product replay when the multiplier's positivity has already been displayed by $Q$ and by the rational or dyadic handoff.

Finally $H,C,P,N$ exhaust the ledger. Since the packet has no row for additive inverses, ordered rings, field order, quotient order, classical trichotomy, or a standard bridge, the five listed rows are the whole local $\NameCert_{\OrderedSemiringUp}$ surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Ordered semiring addition and multiplication order compatibility]
\label{thm:ordered-semiring-add-mul-order-compatibility}
Every order-aware arithmetic consumer of an accepted $\OrderedSemiringUp$ carrier reads addition monotonicity and positive-row multiplication monotonicity only through
$$
S,Q,R,D \longmapsto A,M,H,C,P,N .
$$
In particular, a $\RatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, or $\RealUp$ handoff may use the displayed semiring operations and stable order classifier, but it cannot extract an ordered ring, a field order, a quotient order, classical trichotomy, or any standard bridge from the packet.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with an order-aware arithmetic request. By \autoref{def:ordered-semiring-bhist-carrier}, the available operation row is $S$ and the available comparison row is $Q$; the rational and dyadic handoff rows $R,D$ only record the finite readback needed by their own consumers.

Addition monotonicity is therefore forced through $A$. The ledger reads the two input comparisons from $Q$, applies the addition route of $S$, and records the sum comparison as a finite replay row.

Positive-row multiplication monotonicity is forced through $M$. The multiplier condition is not a host positivity predicate; it is the displayed positivity row already visible through $Q$ and the $R$ or $D$ handoff. Only then may the multiplication route of $S$ be replayed to produce the product comparison.

The Real-facing row $E$ is downstream of these checks. Since the structural rows $H,C,P,N$ preserve only the displayed route, no consumer can replace the route by ordered-ring structure, field order, quotient order, trichotomy, or a standard bridge.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Ordered semiring kernel scope]
\label{thm:ordered-semiring-kernel-scope}
The $\OrderedSemiringUp$ kernel scope is exactly the finite BEDC packet consisting of the semiring carrier row, stable order classifier, rational readback, dyadic handoff, Real read boundary, addition monotonicity, positive-row multiplication monotonicity, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local $\NameCert$ row. Every L10 arithmetic handoff that claims this scope factors through those rows and through no stronger ordered-algebraic structure.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The carrier definition lists the rows that form the packet, and the classifier definition states the only transport relation between accepted packets. Hence a kernel consumer receives exactly those named components.

The NameCert obligation theorem identifies the five finite rows that make the packet usable: habitation, classifier transport, addition monotonicity, multiplication monotonicity by positive rows, and ledger nonescape.

The compatibility theorem then identifies the allowed handoff route for $\RatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, and $\RealUp$ consumers. Combining these three readings leaves no remaining coordinate for ordered rings, field order, quotient order, trichotomy, or a standard bridge, so the displayed finite packet is exactly the kernel scope.
\end{proof}
