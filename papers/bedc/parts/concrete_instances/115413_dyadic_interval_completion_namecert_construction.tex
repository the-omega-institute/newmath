\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\DyadicIntervalCompletionUp$}

Following Bishop and Bridges on interval methods and regular real approximation, the $\DyadicIntervalCompletionUp$ packet realizes the L10 interval-completion route of \autoref{ch:visions-real-completeness} as finite NameCert data. It consumes the dyadic endpoint surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the finite observation windows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names the finite route from shrinking dyadic intervals to a Real-facing seal; it does not add a quotient stream, selected limit, countable choice, host equality, trichotomy, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness principle.

\begin{definition}[Dyadic interval completion carrier]
\label{def:dyadic-interval-completion-carrier}
A $\DyadicIntervalCompletionUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
K=(I,T,S,R,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $I$ is the shrinking dyadic interval row, recording left and right endpoint data over $\DyadicRatCoreUp$.
\item $T$ is the endpoint tolerance ledger, recording the finite radius and endpoint comparison obligations attached to the same interval row.
\item $S$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ finite-window row exposing exactly the inspected interval stages.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row obtained from those windows and tolerance ledgers.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal row, available only after $I,T,S,R$ have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the interval, tolerance, stream-window, readback, seal, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from an interval-completion request through $I,T,S,R,E$ and back to the structural rows.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\DyadicIntervalCompletionUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\DyadicIntervalCompletionUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier exports only this finite interval-completion packet. It has no coordinate for quotient streams, selected limits, countable-choice schedules, host equality, trichotomy, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Dyadic interval completion NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:dyadic-interval-completion-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\DyadicIntervalCompletionUp$ carrier $K=(I,T,S,R,E,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\DyadicIntervalCompletionUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission through the displayed packet, interval shrinking through $I$, endpoint tolerance ledger exposure through $T$, finite-window readback through $S$ and $R$, Real-seal nonescape through $E$, componentwise transport through $H$, displayed replay through $C$, provenance exhaustion through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:dyadic-interval-completion-carrier}. The rows $I,T,S,R,E,H,C,P,N$ are the complete visible packet, so carrier admission can only admit data already displayed in those rows.

The interval route first reads $I$ and $T$. Thus the shrinking dyadic endpoints and their endpoint tolerance ledger are exposed before any stream window, regular readback, or real seal can be consumed.

Next read $S$ and $R$. The stream row names the finite inspected stages, while the regular rational row supplies readback from the same finite data. The seal row $E$ is downstream of that finite route and cannot introduce an independent completeness principle.

Finally use $H,C,P,N$. These rows transport, replay, source, and name the same packet, so the provenance ledger is exhausted by the displayed carrier surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Dyadic interval completion Real-seal route]
\label{thm:dyadic-interval-completion-real-seal-route}
Every completion-facing read of an accepted $\DyadicIntervalCompletionUp$ packet factors through
$$
\DyadicRatCoreUp \longmapsto \StreamNameUp \longmapsto \RegSeqRatUp \longmapsto \RealUp
$$
using the finite rows $I,T,S,R,E,H,C,P,N$. The route may transport and replay the displayed interval-completion packet, but it cannot export a quotient stream, selected limit, countable choice, host equality, trichotomy, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply the obligation theorem. It forces every completion-facing consumer to expose the dyadic interval row, endpoint tolerance ledger, finite stream window, and regular rational readback before the terminal Real seal can be inspected.

The cited sibling chapters supply exactly those surfaces. DyadicRatCore supplies the endpoint and tolerance rows, StreamName supplies finite windows, RegSeqRat supplies regular rational readback, and the Real chapter supplies the terminal seal row.

The structural rows then preserve the same route. Componentwise $\hsame$ transport and displayed $\Cont$ replay cannot add a new completion coordinate, while $P$ and $N$ source and name the finite packet. No row remains from which a consumer could recover quotient streams, selected limits, countable choice, host equality, trichotomy, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{proof}
