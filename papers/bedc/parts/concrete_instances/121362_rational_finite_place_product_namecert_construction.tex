\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\RationalFinitePlaceProductUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-rational-finite-place-product-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following the classical product formula for the rationals, this chapter opens the $\RationalFinitePlaceProductUp$ NameCert entry over the existing $\RationalUp$ rational valuation interface for a BEDC-native finite-place product surface. The target is to connect the finite prime-power counts exposed by \texttt{PrimeFactorizationProduct} to the existing zero-axiom rational valuation interface, including \texttt{ratVal}, \texttt{ratValIndex}, \texttt{ratVal\_mul}, and the archimedean absolute-value surface \texttt{ratAbsInfty}. The intended terminal equality is the rational global product formula, with the finite prime-indexed factor and the archimedean factor assembled inside BEDC rather than imported from mathlib. The complete acceptance law family, anti-degeneracy constraints, and bridge authority are the contract at \texttt{papers/bedc\_mathlib\_bridge/gap\_contracts/RATIONAL\_PRODUCT\_FORMULA\_FINITE\_PRODUCT.yaml}; this chapter records only the main-pipeline entrance for that contract.

The finite support is read from the prime-factorization rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-prime-namecert}, while the local factors are routed toward the restricted-product consumers of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-adele-namecert} and the valued-field surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-localfield-namecert}. Thus the chapter sits in the concrete-instance dependency lattice as a rational product bridge: prime-power rows determine the finite index support, the rational valuation rows determine each local factor, and the downstream Adele/local-field consumers receive only those displayed finite rows rather than a mathlib place object.

\begin{definition}[Rational finite-place support target]
\label{def:rational-finite-place-support-target}
For a nonzero rational name $q$, the intended finite-place support is the finite list of prime places whose \texttt{ratVal} index for $q$ is nonzero, derived from the \texttt{PrimeFactorizationProduct} data of the numerator and denominator. It is not an arbitrary natural-index surface and it carries no mathlib place object.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Rational finite-place product target]
\label{def:rational-finite-place-product-target}
The intended finite-place product is a BEDC-native finite product over the support of \autoref{def:rational-finite-place-support-target}. Each local factor is read from the existing rational valuation surface, while the infinite factor is read from \texttt{ratAbsInfty}; the terminal target is the cancellation statement
$$
\begin{aligned}
\texttt{ratAbsInfty}(q)\cdot\prod_{p \in \operatorname{supp}(q)} |q|_{p}=1 .
\end{aligned}
$$
The displayed equation is a target signature for the seed entrance only, not a claimed theorem of this chapter.
\end{definition}
