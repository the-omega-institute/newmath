\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\DenseGDeltaUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-dense-g-delta-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Baire and Oxtoby, the $\DenseGDeltaUp$ packet records the standard dense $G_\delta$ surface as finite BEDC NameCert data. It sits between the countable open-row schedule of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-g-delta-set-namecert}, the residual evidence carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-residual-set-namecert}, the dense-open Baire route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-bairecategory-namecert}, and the complete-metric inspection surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-complete-metric-baire-surface-namecert}. The packet names the positive dense-open face of residual evidence; it does not choose a generic point, assert a host intersection, import countable choice, or identify quotient subsets.

\begin{definition}[DenseGDelta carrier]
\label{def:dense-gdelta-carrier}
A $\DenseGDeltaUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
D=(T,G,S,O,B,R,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $T$ is the ambient topology or complete-metric source row over which density is read.
\item $G$ is the $\GDeltaSetUp$ countable open-row schedule, displayed as finite prefix data.
\item $S$ is the dense-open schedule row, read through the Baire-category surface before any residual consumer receives the packet.
\item $O$ records the open-row readback and local density witnesses for the schedule positions visible through $G$ and $S$.
\item $B$ is the Baire route row linking the dense-open schedule to the inspected complete-metric Baire surface.
\item $R$ is the $\ResidualSetUp$ handoff row exposed only after the positive dense $G_\delta$ side has been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $T,G,S,O,B,R,C,P,N$.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay of the schedule, density, Baire, residual, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\DenseGDeltaUp$, $\GDeltaSetUp$, $\ResidualSetUp$, $\BaireCategoryUp$, $\CompleteMetricBaireSurfaceUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\DenseGDeltaUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for a selected generic point, a host-level countable intersection, unrestricted set equality, quotient subset equality, or countable-choice selector outside the displayed BEDC rows.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[DenseGDelta NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:dense-gdelta-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\DenseGDeltaUp$ carrier $D=(T,G,S,O,B,R,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\DenseGDeltaUp}$ surface consists exactly of ambient source admission through $T$, countable open-schedule exposure through $G$, dense-open schedule exposure through $S$, open-row and density readback through $O$, Baire route handoff through $B$, residual handoff through $R$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport through $H$, displayed $\Cont$ replay through $C$, $\Pkg$ provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:dense-gdelta-carrier}. The accepted packet displays exactly the ambient source row, countable open-row schedule, dense-open schedule, open-row readback, Baire route, residual handoff, transport, replay, provenance, and local name.

The positive topological content is exhausted by $G,S,O,B$. The $G$ row supplies the $G_\delta$ schedule, the $S$ row supplies the dense-open requests, $O$ records the local readback and density witnesses, and $B$ routes the read through the Baire surface.

The residual side is only a downstream handoff. The row $R$ may be consumed after the positive dense $G_\delta$ evidence has been displayed, while $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same finite route.

No coordinate stores a chosen generic point, a host intersection object, countable-choice data, or quotient subset equality. The listed rows therefore exhaust the local NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[DenseGDelta residual handoff]
\label{thm:dense-gdelta-residual-handoff}
Every residual consumer of an accepted $\DenseGDeltaUp$ packet factors through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-g-delta-set-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-bairecategory-namecert}, and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-complete-metric-baire-surface-namecert} before it reads the $\ResidualSetUp$ row. Thus the residual route receives a positive dense-open $G_\delta$ certificate, not a hidden host intersection or selected generic point.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project $D=(T,G,S,O,B,R,H,C,P,N)$. A residual consumer cannot read $R$ until the preceding rows expose the countable open schedule, dense-open schedule, local density readback, and Baire route.

The $G$ row routes through the $\GDeltaSetUp$ certificate, while $S$ and $O$ display the dense-open and local readback data. The Baire row $B$ then passes through the complete-metric Baire surface before the residual handoff is available.

Since the carrier contains no host intersection coordinate, no selected generic point, no countable-choice selector, and no quotient subset equality, the residual consumer receives only the displayed positive dense $G_\delta$ route.
\end{proof}
