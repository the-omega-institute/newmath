\begin{definition}[BMO finite-window carrier]
\label{def:bmo-finite-window-carrier}
A $\BMOFiniteWindowUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
B=(S,W,A,O,D,M,Q,Z,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $S$ is the finite source row naming the displayed function or scalar sample family whose oscillation is inspected.
\item $W$ is the finite window ledger of balls, intervals, or dyadic cubes on which averages are allowed to be read.
\item $A$ records the $\RealUp$ average readback on each displayed window.
\item $O$ records the mean-oscillation bound row comparing the local deviation from $A$ with the displayed tolerance.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ scale and tolerance ledger for window inclusion, subdivision, and oscillation comparisons.
\item $M$ is the $\HardyLittlewoodMaximalUp$ consumer row allowed to read the displayed average-deviation data.
\item $Q$ is the $\MuckenhouptWeightUp$ compatibility row for weighted finite-window consumers.
\item $Z$ is the $\CalderonZygmundOperatorUp$ handoff row, recording which truncated singular-integral request may consume the oscillation packet.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for source, window, average, oscillation, dyadic, consumer, handoff, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from window selection to average readback, oscillation comparison, and consumer handoff.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\BMOFiniteWindowUp$, $\RealUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\HardyLittlewoodMaximalUp$, $\MuckenhouptWeightUp$, $\CalderonZygmundOperatorUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\BMOFiniteWindowUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for an equivalence class modulo constants, a completed $\mathrm{BMO}$ normed space, a John--Nirenberg exponential-integrability theorem, selected representatives, ambient measure completion, quotient function equality, or a host singular-integral theorem.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[BMO finite-window NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:bmo-finite-window-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\BMOFiniteWindowUp$ carrier
$$
B=(S,W,A,O,D,M,Q,Z,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\BMOFiniteWindowUp}$ surface consists exactly of source admission through $S$, finite-window exposure through $W$, $\RealUp$ average readback through $A$, mean-oscillation comparison through $O$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ scale accounting through $D$, maximal and weighted consumer exposure through $M,Q$, Calderon--Zygmund handoff through $Z$, componentwise transport through $H$, displayed replay through $C$, package provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:bmo-finite-window-carrier}. The tuple displays the source, inspected windows, averages, oscillation comparison, dyadic ledger, maximal consumer, weighted compatibility, Calderon--Zygmund handoff, transport, replay, provenance, and local name.

The scalar rows are read first. The average row $A$ is sealed through $\RealUp$, while the window and tolerance comparisons in $D$ are read through $\DyadicRatCoreUp$; no host completion or quotient representative is introduced by those reads.

The consumer rows then expose $M,Q,Z$. These rows name the allowed Hardy--Littlewood maximal, Muckenhoupt-weight, and Calderon--Zygmund consumers, so the local certificate is exhausted by the displayed finite-window route before $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, package, and name it.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[BMO finite-window mean-oscillation induction]
\label{thm:bmo-finite-window-mean-oscillation-induction}
Any induction over accepted $\BMOFiniteWindowUp$ mean-oscillation reads proceeds only by extending the finite window ledger $W$ with a displayed window whose $\RealUp$ average row $A$, oscillation comparison row $O$, and $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ scale row $D$ are already present. The induction step may transport the ledger by $H$ and replay it by $C$, but it cannot infer an ambient $\mathrm{BMO}$ quotient class, an unrestricted supremum over all windows, or a completed norm from the finite packet.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The base case is the empty or singleton displayed-window ledger, where the only readable data are the rows named in \autoref{def:bmo-finite-window-carrier}. The local NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:bmo-finite-window-namecert-obligations} require average, oscillation, and dyadic rows before a window contributes to the packet.

For the extension step, append one displayed window to $W$. Its average is read through $A$, its deviation comparison through $O$, and its scale and tolerance budget through $D$; the rows $H$ and $C$ only preserve this already displayed data under transport and replay.

Thus the induction remains finite-window induction. Since neither the carrier nor the obligation surface contains a coordinate for quotient representatives, unrestricted window suprema, or completed norms, no such object is exposed by the induction.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[BMO finite-window Calderon-Zygmund handoff]
\label{thm:bmo-finite-window-calderon-zygmund-handoff}

\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start from the obligation theorem. The handoff row $Z$ appears only after $S,W,A,O,D,M,Q$ have been displayed in the same packet.

The maximal row $M$ records the $\HardyLittlewoodMaximalUp$ route for local average control, and the weighted row $Q$ records the $\MuckenhouptWeightUp$ compatibility route for weighted finite-window consumers. Therefore a Calderon--Zygmund consumer can inspect only the finite oscillation certificate carried by $Z$ together with the preceding rows. The packet exposes no principal value, quotient function equality, completed measure space, or global weak-type estimate.
