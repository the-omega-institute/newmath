Following Sobczyk and the constructive Banach-space reading of separable extension theory, the $\SobczykTheoremUp$ packet records only the finite projection data visible when a displayed separable Banach row is read through a named $c_0$ subspace boundary. It sits over the Banach carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-banach-namecert}, the norm and linear-map rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-norm-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-linearmap-namecert}, the finite observation discipline of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, and the Hahn--Banach boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-hahn-banach-namecert}. The packet names a finite-modulus projection corridor; it does not assert full Hahn--Banach, unrestricted choice of extensions, arbitrary complementability, or a host-level projection operator on every Banach space.

\begin{definition}[Sobczyk theorem carrier]
\label{def:sobczyk-theorem-carrier}
A $\SobczykTheoremUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
S=(X,C,E,M,P_0,B,H,R,Q,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X$ is the separable $\BanachUp$ source row whose dense finite inspection schedule is read through $\StreamNameUp$.
\item $C$ is the named $c_0$ subspace row, equipped with its inherited $\NormUp$ and inclusion as a displayed $\LinearMapUp$ row.
\item $E$ is the finite extension-window row recording the bounded coordinate functionals inspected at the current schedule depth.
\item $M$ is the projection-modulus row, giving a rational bound for the finite coordinate projection read from $E$.
\item $P_0$ is the displayed partial projection row from the inspected window of $X$ back to the $c_0$ row.
\item $B$ is the boundedness ledger comparing the norm of $P_0$ against $M$ inside the same finite window.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the source, subspace, window, modulus, projection, and bound rows.
\item $R$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from separable Banach source through the finite extension window to the projection row.
\item $Q$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance row over $\SobczykTheoremUp$, $\BanachUp$, $\NormUp$, $\LinearMapUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\SobczykTheoremUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for a global Hahn--Banach oracle, a choice-selected extension family, a quotient Banach-space equality, an unbounded projection, or a complementability assertion outside the displayed separable $c_0$ corridor.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Sobczyk theorem NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:sobczyk-theorem-namecert-obligations}
For an accepted $\SobczykTheoremUp$ carrier $S=(X,C,E,M,P_0,B,H,R,Q,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\SobczykTheoremUp}$ surface consists exactly of separable Banach-source visibility, $c_0$ subspace visibility, finite extension-window visibility, projection-modulus visibility, displayed partial-projection visibility, boundedness ledgering, componentwise transport, displayed replay, package provenance, and local naming for the finite packet of \autoref{def:sobczyk-theorem-carrier}.

In particular, every public Sobczyk read is a constructive finite-modulus projection read: it presents a bounded projection witness over the inspected window and its replay ledger before any consumer may cite complementability. It refuses full Hahn--Banach, unrestricted extension choice, ambient quotient equality, or an unbounded host projection as part of the certificate.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier. The analytic rows are $X,C,E,M,P_0,B$, and the support rows are $H,R,Q,N$.

Read $X$ and $C$ first. The source row supplies the separable Banach inspection schedule, while the $c_0$ row supplies the named subspace together with its norm and inclusion rows.

Read $E,M,P_0,B$ next. The extension-window row lists the finitely inspected coordinate data, the modulus row bounds the projection read on that window, the partial projection row displays the map back to $c_0$, and the boundedness ledger checks the same modulus against the displayed norm rows.

Finally use transport, replay, provenance, and local naming. These rows preserve the finite projection corridor under NameCert reading, but they do not add a global Hahn--Banach principle, a choice-selected extension family, quotient equality, or an unrestricted complementability theorem.
\end{proof}
