\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\LocatedCauchySubnetUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-located-cauchy-subnet-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Kelley and Moore--Smith subnet terminology together with Bishop--Bridges located Cauchy completion, the $\LocatedCauchySubnetUp$ packet records the finite route by which a cofinal subnet of a Cauchy source remains located before the Real seal is inspected. It consumes the Moore--Smith subnet surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-moore-smith-subnet-namecert}, the Cauchy restriction surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cauchy-subnet-namecert}, the located-distance request of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-locatedspace-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal Real boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names a finite completion-neighbourhood route; it is not a selected limit, quotient net, countable-choice schedule, host equality statement, or ambient Real completeness theorem.

\begin{definition}[Located Cauchy subnet carrier]
\label{def:located-cauchy-subnet-carrier}
A $\LocatedCauchySubnetUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
L=(M,S,Q,D,R,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $M$ is the $\MooreSmithSubnetUp$ cofinal-window row, recording the source index window, subnet index window, and displayed cofinal tail route.
\item $S$ is the $\CauchySubnetUp$ row, requiring the source Cauchy request to pass through the same cofinal subnet ledger before any completion-facing handoff is read.
\item $Q$ is the located-distance request row, read through $\LocatedSpaceUp$ and carrying the finite source, target, and tolerance request.
\item $D$ is the dyadic tolerance ledger attached to the located request and the Cauchy comparison.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row attached to the subnet windows and located-distance comparison.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal, exposed only after $M,S,Q,D,R$ have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the subnet window, Cauchy request, located request, tolerance ledger, regular readback, Real seal, continuation, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay of every accepted read through
$$
M,S,Q,D,R \longmapsto E .
$$
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\LocatedCauchySubnetUp$, $\MooreSmithSubnetUp$, $\CauchySubnetUp$, $\LocatedSpaceUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\LocatedCauchySubnetUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier exports no selected subnet representative, quotient net, countable-choice selector, host equality row, selected limit, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness object.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Located Cauchy subnet NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:located-cauchy-subnet-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\LocatedCauchySubnetUp$ carrier $L=(M,S,Q,D,R,E,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\LocatedCauchySubnetUp}$ surface consists exactly of the subnet cofinal window, Cauchy request, located-distance request, RegSeqRat readback, Real seal, $\hsame$ transport, $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local NameCert rows displayed in \autoref{def:located-cauchy-subnet-carrier}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier. The cofinal-window row $M$ is the only subnet route, and the Cauchy row $S$ is read through that same route before any completion-facing consumer can inspect the packet.

The located-distance request is then exposed by $Q$ together with the tolerance ledger $D$. The regular rational readback $R$ is the only row translating the displayed subnet windows and located comparison into finite rational data.

Only after those rows have been replayed may the packet reach the Real seal $E$. Componentwise transport, continuation replay, provenance, and local naming are confined to $H,C,P,N$, so the listed rows exhaust the NameCert surface and leave no coordinate for selected limits, quotient nets, choice, host equality, or ambient completeness.
\end{proof}
