\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\BishopGraphCompletionUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-bishop-graph-completion-namecert}

Following Bishop and Bridges on constructive completions, the $\BishopGraphCompletionUp$ packet records the finite graph-read surface by which a metric or real-facing completion consumer may inspect vertices, approximation edges, and regular rational labels before any completed point is exposed. It sits after the finite stream-window interface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, the metric carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}, and the continuous-map handoff of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-continuousmap-namecert}. The packet is a finite NameCert carrier for graph-shaped completion reads; it is not a host graph-completion oracle, quotient stream equality, selected limit, countable choice principle, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness theorem.

\begin{definition}[Bishop graph-completion carrier]
\label{def:bishop-graph-completion-carrier}
A $\BishopGraphCompletionUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
G=(V,E,W,Q,R,M,F,L,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $V$ is the finite vertex row, listing the named approximation states available to the completion consumer.
\item $E$ is the directed edge row, recording the admissible refinement arrows between vertices.
\item $W$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ window row through which vertex and edge reads are requested.
\item $Q$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ rational-readback row attached to the same finite windows.
\item $R$ is the $\RealUp$ seal row exposed only after $V,E,W,Q$ have been consumed.
\item $M$ is the $\MetricSpaceUp$ source row whose displayed distance comparisons govern accepted refinement edges.
\item $F$ is the $\ContinuousMapUp$ handoff row, recording graph-compatible maps that preserve displayed refinement and metric reads.
\item $L$ is the local graph-completion ledger, pairing each accepted consumer request with a finite path in $E$ and a terminal Real-facing seal in $R$.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for vertices, edges, windows, rational readbacks, metric comparisons, continuous-map handoffs, ledger rows, replay, provenance, and local naming.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a completion request through the finite vertex-edge path, StreamName windows, RegSeqRat readback, metric source, continuous-map handoff, Real seal, and local ledger.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\BishopGraphCompletionUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\MetricSpaceUp$, $\ContinuousMapUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\BishopGraphCompletionUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted carriers only through $V,E,W,Q,R,M,F,L,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for host graph-completion oracles, quotient streams, selected limits, countable choice, ambient $\RealUp$ completeness, or bridge checking.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Bishop graph-completion NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:bishop-graph-completion-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\BishopGraphCompletionUp$ carrier
$$
G=(V,E,W,Q,R,M,F,L,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\BishopGraphCompletionUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the displayed vertex row, directed edge row, finite StreamName windows, RegSeqRat readback, Real seal, MetricSpace source, ContinuousMap handoff, graph-completion ledger, componentwise transport, displayed replay, package provenance, and local naming. Edge stability is read from $E$ together with $M$; readback stability is read from $W,Q,R$; map stability is read from $F$ under $H,C$; and ledger nonescape keeps host graph-completion oracles, quotient streams, selected limits, countable choice, ambient $\RealUp$ completeness, and bridge checking outside the packet.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:bishop-graph-completion-carrier}. Carrier admission exposes exactly the rows $V,E,W,Q,R,M,F,L,H,C,P,N$ and no further completion coordinate.

The graph read is finite before it is completion-facing. A consumer request first names windows in $W$, then reads the vertex and edge rows $V,E$, and then obtains rational labels through $Q$ over the same finite requests.

Metric and map stability are displayed rows, not ambient facts. The metric source $M$ governs which refinement arrows are admissible, while the continuous-map row $F$ records preservation of those displayed arrows under the same transport and replay rows $H,C$.

Finally the local ledger $L$ pairs each accepted request with the finite path and Real seal already displayed by the packet. Since the carrier contains no host oracle, quotient-stream coordinate, selected-limit coordinate, countable-choice scheduler, ambient-completeness row, or bridge-checking row, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Bishop graph-completion Real handoff]
\label{thm:bishop-graph-completion-real-handoff}
Every $\RealUp$, $\MetricSpaceUp$, or $\ContinuousMapUp$ consumer of an accepted $\BishopGraphCompletionUp$ carrier receives completion-facing data only through the finite route
$$
\StreamNameUp,\ \RegSeqRatUp,\ \MetricSpaceUp,\ \ContinuousMapUp \longmapsto \RealUp .
$$
Consequently the consumer may inspect the displayed vertex-edge path, rational readback, metric comparison, map handoff, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows, but it cannot read a host graph-completion oracle, quotient stream, selected limit, countable choice witness, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness proof from the packet.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Open an accepted carrier $G=(V,E,W,Q,R,M,F,L,H,C,P,N)$. The completion-facing seal $R$ is downstream of the finite windows $W$, the graph rows $V,E$, the rational readback $Q$, the metric source $M$, and the continuous-map handoff $F$.

A Real-facing consumer must therefore consume the same displayed route before $R$ is available. The ledger $L$ records the finite edge path used by the request, while $H$ and $C$ preserve the route under componentwise transport and replay.

The packet has no other completion field. In particular, it stores neither quotient-stream equality nor a selected host limit, and it supplies no countable-choice scheduler, host graph-completion oracle, or ambient completeness proof. The available handoff is exactly the finite route displayed in the statement.
\end{proof}
