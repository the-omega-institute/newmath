Following Riemann, Henstock, and Kurzweil, the $\TaggedPartitionUp$ packet records a finite interval subdivision whose cells carry displayed tags before any finite sum, gauge integral, or completion-facing read is exported. It sits after the dyadic cell discipline of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadic-partition-namecert}, the interval-subdivision carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-riemann-partition-namecert}, and the gauge-window route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-henstock-kurzweil-namecert}. The packet gives constructive integration consumers a finite tagged-subinterval interface without importing host finite sets, quotient partition equality, selected mesh limits, countable choice, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[Tagged partition carrier]
\label{def:tagged-partition-carrier}
A $\TaggedPartitionUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
T=(I,B,S,E,L,D,G,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following visible rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $I$ is the $\RiemannPartitionUp$ interval-subdivision row. It displays the ordered subinterval spine, adjacency ledger, endpoint coverage, and mesh-refinement data that every tagged read must preserve.
\item $B$ is the boundary row for the inspected interval, with left and right endpoint names read through $\RatUp$ or $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ witnesses.
\item $S$ is the finite subinterval row. Each entry stores a left endpoint, a right endpoint, and the local inclusion route tying the cell back to $I$.
\item $E$ is the endpoint exactness row. It records that consecutive subintervals meet at the displayed boundary and that the first and last endpoints match $B$.
\item $L$ is the tag ledger. For each displayed subinterval it names one tag and the finite proof row that the tag lies in that same subinterval.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ comparison row used to read endpoint order, cell width, and mesh-window bounds through dyadic rational data.
\item $G$ is the gauge-window row. It records the local radius request attached to each tag and the finite test that the tagged cell is contained in the displayed gauge window.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for interval source, boundary, subintervals, endpoint exactness, tags, dyadic comparisons, gauge windows, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a tagged-partition request through $I,B,S,E,L,D,G,H,P,N$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\TaggedPartitionUp$, $\RiemannPartitionUp$, $\HenstockKurzweilUp$, $\RatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\TaggedPartitionUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $I,B,S,E,L,D,G,H,C,P,N$. There is no coordinate for host finite-set membership, quotient partition equality, an untagged mesh limit, countable choice, a selected refinement family, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Tagged partition NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:tagged-partition-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\TaggedPartitionUp$ carrier $T=(I,B,S,E,L,D,G,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\TaggedPartitionUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission, interval-subdivision exposure through $I$, boundary exposure through $B$, finite subinterval exposure through $S$, endpoint exactness through $E$, tag-in-cell exactness through $L$, dyadic endpoint and mesh comparison through $D$, gauge-window compatibility through $G$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport through $H$, displayed $\Cont$ replay through $C$, $\Pkg$ provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.

No obligation may be discharged by a host finite set of cells, quotient equality of partitions, selected mesh limits, countable choice, a detached gauge refinement theorem, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:tagged-partition-carrier}. The displayed rows are exactly $I,B,S,E,L,D,G,H,C,P,N$, so a local certificate can read only the interval subdivision, boundary, subinterval, endpoint, tag, dyadic, gauge-window, transport, replay, provenance, and naming data.

The subdivision side starts with $I,B,S,E$. The Riemann partition row supplies the ordered cell spine, while the boundary and endpoint rows force the displayed subintervals to remain attached to the same interval.

Next read $L,D,G$. The tag ledger attaches one visible tag to each displayed cell, the dyadic comparison row provides the endpoint and mesh comparisons, and the gauge-window row tests the same tagged cell against its local radius request.

Finally replay $H,C,P,N$. These structural rows preserve the same finite packet, and the carrier has no coordinate for the excluded host set, quotient, selected-limit, choice, detached-refinement, or ambient-completeness data. The listed rows therefore exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Tagged partition gauge-window handoff]
\label{thm:tagged-partition-gauge-window-handoff}
Every $\HenstockKurzweilUp$, Riemann-sum, or completion-facing consumer of an accepted $\TaggedPartitionUp$ packet receives the finite route
$$
\RiemannPartitionUp \longmapsto \TaggedPartitionUp \longmapsto \HenstockKurzweilUp
$$
only after reading the interval source $I$, boundary row $B$, subinterval row $S$, endpoint exactness row $E$, tag ledger $L$, dyadic comparison row $D$, gauge-window row $G$, transports $H$, replay $C$, provenance $P$, and local name $N$. The handoff exports a tagged gauge window for the displayed partition, not integrability, Darboux equality, selected refinements, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with an accepted tagged-partition read. By \autoref{thm:tagged-partition-namecert-obligations}, the consumer first receives the interval-subdivision rows $I,B,S,E$ and cannot replace them by a host list or quotient partition.

Read the tag and gauge side through $L,D,G$. The tag ledger binds each tag to the same displayed subinterval, the dyadic row supplies the endpoint and mesh comparisons, and the gauge-window row records the local radius containment test before the handoff is public.

The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ then transport, replay, source, and name the same packet. Since the carrier has no integrability, Darboux-equality, selected-refinement, or ambient-completeness coordinate, the exported route is exactly a local tagged gauge-window handoff.
\end{proof}
