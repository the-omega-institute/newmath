Following Bishop and Bridges, the $\WeakCounterexampleUp$ packet records the constructive boundary at which a Real or Completion-facing consumer may cite weak-counterexample data without importing a classical decision principle. It sits beside the lawless-sequence boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-lawlesssequence-namecert}, the Specker sequence boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-specker-sequence-boundary-namecert}, the limited-principle and LLPO boundaries of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-limited-principle-omniscience-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-llpo-boundary-namecert}, and the Markov principle boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-markov-principle-boundary-namecert}. The packet names finite refusal data only; it is not LPO, LLPO, Markov's principle, a selected limit, quotient real equality, countable choice, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{theorem}[Weak-counterexample NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:weak-counterexample-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\WeakCounterexampleUp$ carrier
$$
W=(L,S,O,M,A,R,D,E,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\WeakCounterexampleUp}$ surface consists exactly of lawless finite-prefix exposure through $L$, Specker obstruction exposure through $S$, limited-principle refusal through $O$, Markov-boundary exposure through $M$, LLPO-boundary exposure through $A$, regular rational readback through $R$, dyadic tolerance accounting through $D$, Real-seal handoff through $E$, componentwise transport through $H$, displayed replay through $C$, package provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier. The rows $L,S,O,M,A$ are the only constructive-boundary rows, and they are displayed as finite observation, obstruction, and refusal data.

Read the Real-facing rows next. The rational readback $R$, dyadic tolerance ledger $D$, and terminal seal $E$ keep the packet inside inspected approximation data rather than a selected completed real.

Finally inspect $H,C,P,N$. Transport, replay, provenance, and naming preserve the same finite route and add no decision-principle coordinate. The listed rows therefore exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Weak-counterexample choice-principle boundary]
\label{thm:weak-counterexample-choice-principle-boundary}
Every Real or Completion consumer of an accepted $\WeakCounterexampleUp$ packet must first read
$$
L,S,O,M,A,R,D,E,H,C,P,N
$$
before citing weak-counterexample data. The consumer may use the displayed finite lawless observation, Specker obstruction, LPO-shaped refusal row, LLPO boundary, Markov boundary, rational readback, dyadic tolerance, and Real seal as boundary evidence, but it receives no LPO, no LLPO, no Markov's principle as a global rule, no selected limit, no quotient real equality, no countable choice, and no ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:weak-counterexample-namecert-obligations}. They place the constructive boundary rows and Real-facing readback rows on one finite carrier.

The lawless and Specker rows provide only finite observation and obstruction data. The LPO-shaped, LLPO, and Markov rows are refusal boundaries, so they cannot be projected as available decision principles.

The rational and dyadic rows then pass through the Real seal. Because the seal is downstream of the refusal rows and no carrier coordinate names a selector or quotient equality, the consumer receives weak-counterexample boundary data only.
\end{proof}
