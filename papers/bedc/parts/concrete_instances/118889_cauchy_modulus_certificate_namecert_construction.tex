Following Bishop and Bridges on regular Cauchy real names with explicit moduli, the $\CauchyModulusCertificateUp$ packet records the finite threshold data by which a convergence modulus is displayed before a regular rational readback or terminal real-name seal is consumed. It sits after the finite observation windows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the dyadic tolerance ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the terminal $\RealUp$ boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, and the convergence-modulus ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-modulus-of-convergence-namecert}. The packet names a finite certificate route for L10 Real and Completion consumers; it is not a selected limit, quotient Cauchy name, countable-choice schedule, host real equality, or ambient completeness theorem for $\RealUp$.

\begin{definition}[Cauchy modulus certificate carrier]
\label{def:cauchy-modulus-certificate-carrier}
A $\CauchyModulusCertificateUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
K=(D,W,Q,M,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance request row.
\item $W$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ finite tail-window row selected for the tolerance request.
\item $Q$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row attached to the displayed window.
\item $M$ is the convergence-modulus certificate row, recording that every later window accepted by the packet satisfies the request named by $D$.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal, available only after $D,W,Q,M$ have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for tolerance requests, finite windows, regular readback, modulus certificate rows, Real seals, replay, provenance, and local naming.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a tolerance request through the window, readback, modulus certificate, and Real seal route.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\CauchyModulusCertificateUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealModulusOfConvergenceUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\CauchyModulusCertificateUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted carriers only through $D,W,Q,M,E,H,C,P,N$. No coordinate stores quotient stream equality, selected representatives, selected limits, countable choice, host real equality, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Cauchy modulus certificate NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:cauchy-modulus-certificate-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\CauchyModulusCertificateUp$ carrier $K=(D,W,Q,M,E,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\CauchyModulusCertificateUp}$ surface consists exactly of dyadic tolerance admission, finite window admission, regular rational readback, convergence-modulus certificate exposure, Real seal handoff, componentwise stability transport, displayed replay, provenance, local naming, and ledger refusal for quotient streams, selected representatives, selected limits, countable choice, host real equality, and ambient Real completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:cauchy-modulus-certificate-carrier}. The displayed route begins with the dyadic tolerance request $D$, then exposes the finite window $W$, the regular readback $Q$, and the convergence-modulus certificate row $M$ before the terminal seal $E$ can be consumed.

Componentwise transport through $H$ and replay through $C$ preserve that order. The provenance and naming rows $P,N$ source and name the same finite route, rather than adding analytic data outside the carrier.

The refusal ledger is the complement of the displayed coordinates. Since the carrier has no row for quotient stream equality, selected representatives, selected limits, countable choice, host real equality, or ambient completeness, no local NameCert obligation can export those objects.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Cauchy modulus certificate regular-Cauchy and Real route]
\label{thm:cauchy-modulus-certificate-regular-cauchy-route}
Every $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealModulusOfConvergenceUp$, or $\RealUp$ consumer of an accepted $\CauchyModulusCertificateUp$ packet must read the finite route
$$
\DyadicRatCoreUp,\StreamNameUp \longmapsto \RegSeqRatUp \longmapsto \RealModulusOfConvergenceUp \longmapsto \RealUp
$$
through the displayed rows $D,W,Q,M,E,H,C,P,N$. Thus the certificate supplies a named regular-Cauchy and Real handoff without asserting Real completeness, quotient Cauchy-name equality, selected limits, or countable-choice schedules.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start from an accepted carrier $K=(D,W,Q,M,E,H,C,P,N)$. The tolerance row $D$ and finite window row $W$ must be displayed before any regular rational readback is available.

The readback row $Q$ is then the only route into $\RegSeqRatUp$. The modulus row $M$ records the convergence certificate consumed by \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-modulus-of-convergence-namecert}, and the terminal seal $E$ is downstream of that certificate row.

Finally inspect $H,C,P,N$. They transport, replay, source, and name the same finite route, so any Real or Completion consumer receives only the displayed regular-Cauchy handoff. The carrier inventory contains no completeness, quotient, selected-limit, choice, or host-equality coordinate, and therefore cannot export such a principle.
\end{proof}
