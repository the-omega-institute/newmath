\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\NormedGroupUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-normed-group-namecert}

Following Bourbaki, \emph{General Topology}, the $\NormedGroupUp$ packet records the traditional normed-group interface as finite BEDC NameCert data. It consumes the algebraic operation rows of $\GroupUp$ from \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-group-namecert}, the distance row of $\MetricUp$ from \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}, and the value row of $\NormUp$ from \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-norm-namecert}. The carrier binds these three siblings as one displayed packet; it does not import quotient groups, host equality, selected representatives, or choice of completions.

\begin{definition}[Normed group carrier]
\label{def:normed-group-carrier}
A $\NormedGroupUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
G=(A,M,I,E,N,D,T,H,C,P,L)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $A$ is the consumed $\GroupUp$ row, including carrier admission, multiplication, unit, inverse, and group-law evidence.
\item $M$ is the consumed $\MetricUp$ row on the same displayed carrier.
\item $I$ is the displayed identity row inherited from the group packet.
\item $E$ is the displayed inverse row inherited from the group packet.
\item $N$ is the consumed $\NormUp$ value row, read only on displayed group endpoints.
\item $D$ is the norm-distance row recording $d(x,y)=N(x^{-1}y)$ inside the packet.
\item $T$ is the translation-stability row recording that left and right multiplication preserve the displayed distance readback.
\item $H$ is the componentwise $\hsame$ transport row for $A,M,I,E,N,D,T$.
\item $C$ is the displayed $\Cont$ replay row for the same fields.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\NormedGroupUp$, $\GroupUp$, $\MetricUp$, $\NormUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $L$ is the local $\NameCert_{\NormedGroupUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $A,M,I,E,N,D,T,H,C,P,L$. It has no coordinate for host group equality, quotient completion, an ambient topological group, a chosen dense subgroup, or an external real-valued norm object outside the displayed $\NormUp$ row.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Normed group NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:normed-group-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\NormedGroupUp$ carrier $G=(A,M,I,E,N,D,T,H,C,P,L)$, the local $\NameCert_{\NormedGroupUp}$ surface consists exactly of group-source admission, metric-source admission, norm-source admission, the identity and inverse readback rows, the norm-distance equation row $D$, translation stability $T$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport $H$, displayed $\Cont$ replay $C$, package provenance $P$, and local naming $L$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:normed-group-carrier}. The algebraic source is the consumed $\GroupUp$ row $A$, and the metric and norm sources are the consumed $\MetricUp$ and $\NormUp$ rows $M$ and $N$.

The identity and inverse rows $I$ and $E$ are inherited from the same group packet. The distance row $D$ is not an additional metric carrier; it is the displayed equation reading the metric value through the norm of $x^{-1}y$.

Translation stability is the row $T$. Transport, continuation, provenance, and local naming preserve exactly the displayed rows, so the obligation surface is exhausted by the listed finite packet.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Normed group metric translation stability]
\label{thm:normed-group-metric-translation-stability}
Every accepted $\NormedGroupUp$ consumer that reads a translated pair must expose the same $\GroupUp$ multiplication row, inverse row, $\MetricUp$ distance row, and $\NormUp$ value row before reading the translated distance. Thus left and right translation are certificate-preserving routes through $T$, not host-level isometries imported from outside the packet.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Read the translated pair through the group row $A$. Multiplication and inverse endpoints are visible only as rows of the consumed $\GroupUp$ packet.

Next read $M$ and $N$. The metric value and norm value are available only through those two source rows, and $D$ ties the distance comparison to the norm of the displayed group difference.

The row $T$ records the translation-stability replay. Since $H,C,P,L$ preserve the same packet and no host isometry coordinate appears in the carrier, translated reads remain inside the accepted NameCert route.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Normed group norm-distance compatibility]
\label{thm:normed-group-norm-distance-compatibility}
For an accepted $\NormedGroupUp$ carrier, a metric read $d(x,y)$ and a norm read $N(x^{-1}y)$ are compatible only through the displayed row $D$. A consumer cannot replace $D$ by host equality of group elements, quotient equality of representatives, or an unrecorded real-valued norm while retaining the local $\NameCert_{\NormedGroupUp}$ read.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the distance and norm rows from \autoref{def:normed-group-carrier}. The metric row $M$ supplies the displayed distance read, and the norm row $N$ supplies the displayed value read.

The group row $A$ supplies the inverse and multiplication endpoints used to form the displayed difference. The compatibility assertion is exactly row $D$, so the route has no independent equality or quotient coordinate.

Transport and continuation replay the same row data. Hence any replacement by host equality, quotient equality, or an unrecorded norm leaves the accepted packet.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Normed group ledger nonescape]
\label{thm:normed-group-ledger-nonescape}
Every downstream completion, topological-group, or Banach-facing consumer citing $\NormedGroupUp$ must first expose $A,M,I,E,N,D,T,H,C,P,L$. Dropping the group row, the metric row, the norm row, the norm-distance row, or the translation-stability row exits the accepted normed-group ledger and cannot be repaired by provenance or local naming alone.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply the obligation surface of \autoref{thm:normed-group-namecert-obligations}. The group, metric, and norm rows are independent consumed siblings, while $D$ and $T$ are the local rows that bind them into one normed-group packet.

Completion and topological consumers may inspect the metric-facing and translation-facing rows only after those local rows are displayed. Banach-facing consumers may inspect norm values only through $N$ and the compatibility row $D$.

The support rows $H,C,P,L$ preserve an admitted packet. They do not synthesize a missing algebraic source, metric source, norm source, compatibility row, or translation-stability row, so dropping any of those rows leaves the accepted ledger.
\end{proof}
