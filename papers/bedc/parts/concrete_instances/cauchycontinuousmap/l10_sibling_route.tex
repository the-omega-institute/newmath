\begin{theorem}[CauchyContinuousMap L10 sibling route]
\label{thm:cauchy-continuous-map-l10-sibling-route}
Every $\CauchyContinuousMapUp$ route accepted by an L10 real-completion client first reads the $\StreamNameUp$ source windows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, and the $\RealUp$ seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The client receives the displayed carrier rows $W,R,D,E,H,C,P,N$ and the continuity replay route attached to them; it receives no compactness principle, selected limit, quotient stream equality, or general completeness theorem.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.CauchyContinuousMapUp.CauchyContinuousMap\_l10\_sibling\_route}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier of \autoref{def:cauchy-continuous-map-carrier}. Its source-facing rows are the finite window surface $W$, the regular rational image-readback row $R$, the dyadic tolerance ledger $D$, and the Real-seal handoff $E$.

Follow the displayed continuation route $C$. A completion-facing client must replay the same windows and tolerance checks before it can inspect the seal, and the componentwise transport row $H$ preserves that order.

The remaining rows $P$ and $N$ package and name this finite route. Since the carrier has no coordinate for compactness, selected limits, quotient stream equality, or general completeness, the L10 client can only consume the displayed sibling route through $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, and $\RealUp$.
\end{proof}
