\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\TotallyBoundedCompletionCriterionUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-totally-bounded-completion-criterion-namecert}

Following Bishop and Bridges constructive analysis and Kelley metric completion, the $\TotallyBoundedCompletionCriterionUp$ packet records the finite-metric criterion that a displayed metric source reaches its completion-facing compact metric form by combining finite $\varepsilon$-net data with complete-metric limit data. It consumes the total-boundedness surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-totallybounded-namecert}, the complete-metric limit surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-completemetric-namecert}, the metric-completion handoff of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metriccompletion-namecert}, and the compact-metric certificate surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}. The packet is a BEDC criterion row, not an open-cover compactness theorem, a selected-limit principle, a quotient-completion equality, or a host compactness claim.

\begin{definition}[Totally bounded completion criterion carrier]
\label{def:totally-bounded-completion-criterion-carrier}
A $\TotallyBoundedCompletionCriterionUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
T=(X,E,L,Q,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X$ is the underlying $\MetricUp$ source row, including its point classifier and $\mathsf{MetricDistanceWitness}$ surface.
\item $E$ is the $\TotallyBoundedUp$ finite-net ledger, recording for each visible positive $\RatUp$ tolerance a closed $\mathsf{ProbeBundle}$ of centers, membership rows, coverage rows, and rational bound comparisons.
\item $L$ is the $\CompleteMetricUp$ limit ledger, recording regular $\StreamNameUp$ Cauchy names, explicit moduli, displayed limit histories, and convergence-distance rows.
\item $Q$ is the $\MetricCompletionUp$ handoff row, recording whether a completion-facing consumer reads the source through the Cauchy-filter branch or the Cauchy-net branch before any separated-limit comparison.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $X,E,L,Q$, the replay row, the provenance row, and the local naming row.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay for finite-net coverage, complete-limit witnesses, completion handoff, separated comparison, and compact-metric consumption.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\MetricUp$, $\TotallyBoundedUp$, $\CompleteMetricUp$, $\MetricCompletionUp$, $\CompactMetricUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\TotallyBoundedCompletionCriterionUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for open-cover compactness, selected limits, countable choice, quotient completion equality, host compactness, or a detached ambient complete metric space.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Totally bounded completion criterion NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:totally-bounded-completion-criterion-namecert-obligations}
For an accepted $\TotallyBoundedCompletionCriterionUp$ packet $T=(X,E,L,Q,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\TotallyBoundedCompletionCriterionUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the displayed metric source $X$, finite-net coverage through $E$, complete-limit witnesses through $L$, completion handoff through $Q$, componentwise transport through $H$, replay through $C$, package provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.

Every compact-metric consumer of this criterion must read the finite-net row before invoking total boundedness, read the limit row before invoking completeness, and pass through the completion handoff row before comparing displayed completion endpoints. No consumer can project open-cover compactness, selected representatives, countable-choice schedules, quotient equality of completion names, or host compactness from the packet.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted packet through \autoref{def:totally-bounded-completion-criterion-carrier}. The source metric row is $X$, and every finite-net request is forced through $E$ because no other row carries a $\mathsf{ProbeBundle}$ center ledger or rational coverage comparison.

The complete-metric part is forced through $L$. It carries the regular stream name, modulus, displayed limit, and convergence-distance rows, while the completion-facing branch is forced through $Q$ before separated comparison can be replayed.

The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, package, and name exactly the same finite tuple. Since the tuple has no coordinate for open covers, selected limits, countable choice, quotient completion equality, or host compactness, the listed obligations exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Totally bounded completion finite-net route]
\label{thm:totally-bounded-completion-criterion-finite-net-route}
Let $T=(X,E,L,Q,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\TotallyBoundedCompletionCriterionUp$ packet. Every finite-net compactness use of $T$ factors first through the $\TotallyBoundedUp$ row $E$, then through the $\CompleteMetricUp$ limit row $L$, and then through the $\MetricCompletionUp$ handoff row $Q$ when the consumer asks how a Cauchy-filter or Cauchy-net reading reaches a displayed completion endpoint.

The resulting route is the finite BEDC chain
$$
X\longmapsto E\longmapsto L\longmapsto Q\longmapsto H,C,P,N ,
$$
not an open-cover compactness proof, not a selected-limit principle, not a quotient of completion names, and not a host compact metric object.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with a compact-metric consumer. The metric carrier and distance rows are projected from $X$, while total boundedness is available only through the finite $\mathsf{ProbeBundle}$ data in $E$.

Completeness is read next through $L$. This row supplies the displayed regular Cauchy stream, modulus, limit, and convergence-distance witnesses; it does not select a host representative or assert ambient completeness outside the packet.

When the consumer asks for the completion-facing route, $Q$ records whether the visible evidence is filter-based or net-based before any separated-limit comparison is used. The transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows then keep the same finite chain stable, so no open-cover, choice, quotient-equality, or host-compactness principle is exported.
\end{proof}
