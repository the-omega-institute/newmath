\begin{theorem}[InnerProduct finite orthogonality derivation]
\label{thm:innerproduct-finite-orthogonality-derivation}
For every accepted $\InnerProductUp$ packet, the zero-left orthogonality row is derived by finite inspection of the $\VecSpaceUp$ carrier, the scalar endpoint classifier, the sesquilinear rows, the conjugate-symmetry row, the positivity row, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and the local $\NameCert$ row. The derivation is the BEDC route through \autoref{def:innerproduct-bhist-carrier}, \autoref{def:innerproduct-bhist-source}, \autoref{thm:innerproduct-symmetry-positivity-row}, \autoref{thm:innerproduct-vecspace-linearity-row}, \autoref{def:innerproduct-orthogonality-row}, and \autoref{thm:innerproduct-zero-left-orthogonal-row}; it is not Hilbert completeness, Banach completion, spectral theory, or a host inner-product-space theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start from the displayed carrier and source rows. The $\VecSpaceUp$ carrier and scalar endpoint classifier are already visible in \autoref{def:innerproduct-bhist-carrier} and \autoref{def:innerproduct-bhist-source}, so the zero-left claim is read inside the accepted BEDC packet rather than in a host vector space.

Next inspect the form laws. The sesquilinear source row of \autoref{thm:innerproduct-vecspace-linearity-row} and the conjugate-symmetry and positivity rows of \autoref{thm:innerproduct-symmetry-positivity-row} expose exactly the scalar-form data used by the orthogonality classifier.

Finally apply the orthogonality rows. The definition \autoref{def:innerproduct-orthogonality-row} names the finite classifier surface, and \autoref{thm:innerproduct-zero-left-orthogonal-row} derives the zero-left row from that surface. Transport, replay, provenance, and local naming keep the same rows attached to the accepted packet, so the result is a finite BEDC route and exports no Hilbert, Banach, spectral, or host-completeness principle.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[InnerProduct finite orthogonal projection derivation]
\label{thm:innerproduct-finite-orthogonal-projection-derivation}
For every accepted $\InnerProductUp$ packet and every displayed carried pair
$x,y:C_V$ whose two-vector Gram source has a diagonal scalar row
$\langle y,y\rangle_{\InnerProductUp}$ separated from
$0_{\mathsf{scal}}$, the finite projection component of $x$ along $y$ is read
from
\autoref{def:innerproduct-two-vector-gram-source}, the Gram symmetry and
diagonal rows of \autoref{thm:innerproduct-gram-symmetry-zero-exactness}, the
parallelogram and Pythagorean norm seed rows of
\autoref{def:innerproduct-parallelogram-norm-seed-source} and
\autoref{thm:innerproduct-pythagorean-row}, the orthogonality closure rows of
\autoref{thm:innerproduct-orthogonality-right-scalar-action-closure}, and the
scalar-action and additive rows retained by the embedded $\VecSpaceUp$ source.
The row states only that the BEDC packet can inspect the displayed scalar
coefficient, carried scalar multiple, residual vector, and scalar-zero
orthogonality classifier for that finite pair; it does not assert Hilbert
projection existence, closed-subspace projection, least-distance attainment,
Banach completeness, or a host projection theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with \autoref{def:innerproduct-two-vector-gram-source}. It displays the
four scalar endpoints for $x$ and $y$, in particular
$\langle x,y\rangle_{\InnerProductUp}$ and
$\langle y,y\rangle_{\InnerProductUp}$, together with the transport, symmetry,
positivity, orthogonality, and ledger rows needed to keep those endpoints in
one finite packet.

The separated diagonal row lets the retained scalar source form the displayed
coefficient
$$
\alpha=
\langle x,y\rangle_{\InnerProductUp}
\cdot_{\mathsf{scal}}
\langle y,y\rangle_{\InnerProductUp}^{-1}.
$$
This is a scalar-source row, not an analytic minimizer. The embedded
$\VecSpaceUp$ source then carries $\alpha\cdot_V y$ and the displayed residual
$x-_V(\alpha\cdot_V y)$ by its scalar-action and additive rows.

Use \autoref{thm:innerproduct-gram-symmetry-zero-exactness} to keep the
off-diagonal Gram row symmetric and to identify the diagonal zero rows with
vector-zero classification. Use
\autoref{thm:innerproduct-orthogonality-right-scalar-action-closure} and the
linearity row of \autoref{thm:innerproduct-vecspace-linearity-row} to inspect
the scalar endpoint
$$
\langle x-_V(\alpha\cdot_V y),y\rangle_{\InnerProductUp}.
$$
The scalar inverse row for the displayed nonzero diagonal cancels the
$\langle y,y\rangle_{\InnerProductUp}$ factor, leaving the retained
scalar-zero classifier for the residual against $y$.

Finally read the norm side only as a finite consistency check. The
parallelogram seed source and \autoref{thm:innerproduct-pythagorean-row} say
that once the residual is orthogonal to the carried multiple of $y$, the
diagonal norm-squared rows split through the displayed scalar ledger. This
records the projection row as finite BEDC data over $x,y,\alpha,\alpha\cdot_V
y$ and the residual, with no coordinate for Hilbert completeness,
closed-subspace projection, least-distance existence, or host-space
projection.
\end{proof}
