\begin{theorem}[Regular Cauchy gluing sibling forward link]
\label{thm:regular-cauchy-gluing-sibling-forward-link}
Every accepted $\RegularCauchyGluingUp$ packet that is read as a gluing consumer first passes through the L10 sibling route of \autoref{thm:regular-cauchy-gluing-l10-sibling-route}. In particular, the consumer must expose the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert} and the finite $\StreamNameUp$ windows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert} before the local gluing $\NameCert$ row is available.

The forward link is therefore a concrete sibling dependency route rather than a detached hub edge. It also preserves the exclusions already named by the L10 route: no quotient stream equality, selected representative, countable-choice schedule, host real equality, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness is exported by the gluing read.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the carrier and obligation rows of \autoref{def:regular-cauchy-gluing-carrier} and \autoref{thm:regular-cauchy-gluing-namecert-obligations}. They display the paired local windows, common dyadic ledger, finite observations, glued regular readback, terminal Real seal, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.

Apply the L10 sibling route of \autoref{thm:regular-cauchy-gluing-l10-sibling-route}. That route orders the same gluing read through $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\CauchyReindexingUp$, and $\RealUp$ before any gluing certificate can be exported.

Thus the hub has a visible child route into the RegSeqRat and StreamName sibling chapters. The refused objects remain absent because neither the local carrier nor the L10 sibling route contains coordinates for quotient stream equality, selected representatives, choice, host equality, or ambient completeness.
\end{proof}
