\begin{theorem}[Complete metric limit uniqueness separated reflection route]
\label{thm:complete-metric-limit-uniqueness-separated-reflection-route}
Every $\CompleteMetricLimitUniquenessUp$ read that exports uniqueness for two displayed complete-metric limit rows must consume the separated-reflection classifier route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-separatedmetricreflection-namecert} after the $\MetricUp$ distance witness and the $\RealUp$ zero-distance row have been displayed. The route therefore passes through the packet rows
$$
A,L_{0},L_{1},M,Z,S,H,C,P,N
$$
before any uniqueness handoff is exported. It does not identify quotient representatives, choose a limit, invoke ambient completeness, or export host equality of metric points.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:complete-metric-limit-uniqueness-carrier}. The two limit rows $L_{0}$ and $L_{1}$ are tied to the same Cauchy-source row $A$ before any comparison is available.

Use the metric distance witness $M$ and the Real zero-distance row $Z$. These rows display the finite zero-distance comparison, but they do not by themselves export uniqueness or host point equality.

Now route the comparison through the separated-reflection classifier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-separatedmetricreflection-namecert}. The separated row $S$ is therefore consumed only after $M$ and $Z$ are visible, and before the uniqueness handoff is read by any downstream consumer.

Finally carry the route through $H,C,P,N$. These rows transport, replay, source, and name the same finite packet, so the handoff remains a separated-reflection dependency rather than a quotient, selected-limit, ambient-completeness, or host-equality principle.
\end{proof}
