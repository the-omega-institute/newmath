\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\DyadicMeshLocatorUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-dyadic-mesh-locator-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges on constructive real approximation and finite dyadic partitions, the $\DyadicMeshLocatorUp$ packet records the finite cell-location read used when a regular real window is compared with a displayed dyadic mesh. It sits over the mesh rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finite-dyadic-mesh-namecert}, the rounded locator route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-rounded-dyadic-locator-namecert}, the dyadic tolerance ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the finite stream window of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal real seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names a finite locator primitive; it does not decide host trichotomy, select a nearest cell, quotient real names, import countable choice, or assert ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[Dyadic mesh locator carrier]
\label{def:dyadic-mesh-locator-carrier}
A $\DyadicMeshLocatorUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
L=(M,Q,S,E,T,W,R,A,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $M$ is the $\FiniteDyadicMeshUp$ row naming the finite mesh and its ordered cell rows.
\item $Q$ is the locator request row containing the requested precision and the inspected real-window address.
\item $S$ is the selected mesh-cell row returned by the finite locator.
\item $E$ is the endpoint-tolerance row proving that the selected cell endpoints were read at the requested dyadic precision.
\item $T$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance row used to compare the endpoints and the window.
\item $W$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ finite-window row inspected by the locator.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row attached to $W$.
\item $A$ is the $\RealUp$ seal row reached only after $M,Q,S,E,T,W,R$ have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the mesh, request, selected cell, endpoint tolerances, stream window, regular readback, real seal, and structural rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a locator request through the mesh, selected cell, endpoint tolerances, stream window, regular readback, and real seal.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\DyadicMeshLocatorUp$, $\FiniteDyadicMeshUp$, $\RoundedDyadicLocatorUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\DyadicMeshLocatorUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $M,Q,S,E,T,W,R,A,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for host trichotomy, a nearest-cell selector, quotient real equality, countable choice, or ambient real completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Dyadic mesh locator NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:dyadic-mesh-locator-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\DyadicMeshLocatorUp$ carrier $L=(M,Q,S,E,T,W,R,A,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\DyadicMeshLocatorUp}$ surface consists exactly of finite mesh admission, locator-request admission, selected-cell row exposure, endpoint-tolerance accounting, dyadic tolerance comparison, StreamName window read, RegSeqRat readback, Real seal handoff, componentwise transport, displayed replay, provenance, and local naming.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:dyadic-mesh-locator-carrier}. The visible inventory is $M,Q,S,E,T,W,R,A,H,C,P,N$, and every locator obligation is assigned to one of those rows.

The finite mesh and request rows are read first. Row $M$ supplies the inspected dyadic mesh, while $Q$ records the precision and window address that the locator is allowed to use. The selected cell row $S$ is therefore a finite row of the same mesh rather than a host-side nearest-cell choice.

The endpoint and tolerance data are then displayed. Row $E$ records the endpoint tolerances, and row $T$ supplies the dyadic rational comparison budget used by the locator. Rows $W$ and $R$ expose the stream window and regular rational readback that the real-facing request actually inspects.

Finally $A,H,C,P,N$ seal, transport, replay, source, and name the same finite route. Since the carrier contains no trichotomy decision, nearest-cell selector, quotient real equality, countable-choice row, or ambient-completeness witness, those objects are not local obligations of the packet.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Dyadic mesh locator finite cell handoff]
\label{thm:dyadic-mesh-locator-finite-cell-handoff}
Every $\RealUp$ consumer of an accepted $\DyadicMeshLocatorUp$ carrier receives the finite route
$$
M \longrightarrow Q \longrightarrow S \longrightarrow E \longrightarrow T \longrightarrow W \longrightarrow R \longrightarrow A \longrightarrow H,C,P,N .
$$
Thus the consumer reads a displayed finite dyadic mesh, locator request, selected cell, endpoint tolerance ledger, dyadic rational tolerance row, StreamName window, and RegSeqRat readback before it may consume the terminal real seal.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start from the carrier definition. The terminal real seal is $A$, and it appears only after the finite mesh, request, selected cell, endpoint tolerance, dyadic tolerance, stream window, and regular readback rows have been displayed.

Apply \autoref{thm:dyadic-mesh-locator-namecert-obligations}. It makes the route through $M,Q,S,E,T,W,R$ part of the local NameCert surface, so a consumer cannot replace the route by trichotomy, a nearest-cell selector, quotient equality of real names, or a choice-based search over mesh cells.

The transport and replay rows preserve the same finite order for downstream consumers, while $P$ and $N$ record provenance and local naming. The handoff is therefore the displayed mesh-cell route rather than an ambient completeness or choice principle.
\end{proof}
