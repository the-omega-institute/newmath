Following Bishop and Bridges and Kelley, the $\FiniteMetricCompactnessUp$ packet records the elementary compactness certificate for a finite metric carrier. It sits between the distance rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}, the finite-net compactness surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}, the total-boundedness ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-totallybounded-namecert}, the dyadic tolerance reads of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, and the finite-net Heine--Cantor route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-heine-cantor-finite-net-namecert}. The packet names only finite point rows, explicit distance comparisons, dyadic tolerance rows, finite-net coverage, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming; it does not import open-cover compactness, sequential compactness, choice-selected subsequences, quotient equality, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[Finite metric compactness carrier]
\label{def:finite-metric-compactness-carrier}
A $\FiniteMetricCompactnessUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
F_{\mathrm{mc}}=(X,D,E,R,T,K,L,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $X$ is the finite point-row bundle, listing every admitted point of the carrier before any compactness read is exported.
  \item $D$ is the $\MetricUp$ distance row over $X$, displaying symmetry, diagonal, and triangle-comparison reads only for the finite point inventory.
  \item $E$ is the finite equality and $\hsame$-stability row for point names, distance cells, tolerance cells, coverage rows, and local naming rows.
  \item $R$ is the $\RealUp$ dyadic tolerance row, exposing positive dyadic requests and comparison readback for the metric inequalities used by the net ledger.
  \item $T$ is the $\TotallyBoundedUp$ finite-net row, assigning to each displayed tolerance in $R$ a finite center subrow drawn from $X$.
  \item $K$ is the $\CompactMetricUp$ ledger row that records the same finite-net coverage as a compact-metric certificate and nothing stronger.
  \item $L$ is the local coverage ledger: for each point row of $X$ and each tolerance row of $R$, it displays a center from the finite center subrow of $T$ and the corresponding $D$-comparison.
  \item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for point rows, metric rows, dyadic tolerance rows, finite-net coverage, compact-metric ledger rows, replay rows, provenance rows, and local naming rows.
  \item $C$ records $\Cont$ replay from a tolerance request through $X,D,E,R,T,K,L,H,P,N$.
  \item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\FiniteMetricCompactnessUp$, $\MetricUp$, $\RealUp$, $\TotallyBoundedUp$, $\CompactMetricUp$, $\HeineCantorFiniteNetUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
  \item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\FiniteMetricCompactnessUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through these rows. There is no coordinate for open covers, infinite subsequences, countable choice, quotient metric equality, or a completeness argument over ambient real names.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Finite metric compactness NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:finite-metric-compactness-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\FiniteMetricCompactnessUp$ carrier $F_{\mathrm{mc}}=(X,D,E,R,T,K,L,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\FiniteMetricCompactnessUp}$ surface is exhausted by six finite obligation rows:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item finite point-row admission for $X$;
  \item $\MetricUp$ distance-row exactness for $D$ over the listed points;
  \item $\RealUp$ dyadic tolerance admission and comparison readback for $R$;
  \item $\TotallyBoundedUp$ finite-net coverage and $\CompactMetricUp$ ledger agreement through $T,K,L$;
  \item componentwise $\hsame$ transport and $\Cont$ replay through $E,H,C$;
  \item provenance and local naming through $P,N$.
\end{enumerate}
No obligation is discharged by open-cover compactness, selected subsequences, countable choice, quotient equality of metric presentations, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:finite-metric-compactness-carrier}. The point inventory $X$ is finite, and every distance comparison consumed by the certificate is displayed by $D$ over that same inventory.

Read the tolerance and coverage rows. The row $R$ supplies only dyadic tolerance requests and comparisons, while $T$ and $L$ answer each such request with finitely many centers drawn from $X$ and displayed distance inequalities. The compact-metric ledger $K$ records this finite-net evidence as its compactness surface.

Finally replay the structural rows. The rows $E,H,C,P,N$ transport, continue, package, and name the displayed packet, so the excluded open-cover, subsequence, choice, quotient, and completeness principles have no carrier coordinate from which to enter the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite metric compactness finite-net sequential bridge]
\label{thm:finite-metric-compactness-net-sequential-bridge}
Every consumer that reads an accepted $\FiniteMetricCompactnessUp$ packet as a sequential compactness witness must factor that read through the explicit finite-net coverage rows
$$
X,D,R,T,L \longmapsto K \longmapsto H,C,P,N .
$$
The bridge therefore exports a finite-center repetition route: an infinite sequence over the finite point-row inventory yields a recurrent center only because the carrier exposes the finite inventory and the finite-net coverage ledger. It does not export a Bolzano--Weierstrass theorem, a choice-selected subsequence operator, an open-cover compactness principle, or an ambient completeness theorem for $\RealUp$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The carrier contains no sequential object as primitive data. A sequential consumer must first read the finite point-row inventory $X$ and the metric comparisons $D$.

The finite-net rows then supply the only compactness route. For each dyadic tolerance request in $R$, the rows $T$ and $L$ display finitely many centers and the distance comparisons that cover all listed points. The ledger $K$ repackages precisely that evidence as a $\CompactMetricUp$ row.

Any sequential reading is therefore downstream of a finite-center repetition argument over the displayed inventory. Since the structural rows $H,C,P,N$ preserve only the same packet, the bridge cannot introduce open covers, choice-selected subsequences, Bolzano--Weierstrass data, or ambient completeness.
\end{proof}
