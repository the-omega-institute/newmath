\begin{definition}[Hyperbolic convex disk carrier]
\label{def:hyperbolic-convex-disk-carrier}
A $\HyperbolicConvexDiskUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$

with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $U$ is the finite Poincare-disk window row naming the disk points under inspection;
\item $M$ is the $\PoincareDiskMetricUp$ metric-read row on $U$;
\item $G$ is the finite geodesic-window row, listing the displayed geodesic segments whose endpoints lie in $U$;
\item $B$ is the $\HyperbolicGeodesicBoundaryUp$ endpoint and boundary-transport row for the same windows;
\item $W$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ sampling row used to enumerate the inspected endpoints and interior witnesses;
\item $R$ is the $\RealUp$ seal row for the finite metric and parameter reads;
\item $H$ is the componentwise $\hsame$ transport row for replacing the displayed disk window by an extensionally same one;

The carrier has no coordinate for an arbitrary host subset of the disk, an infinite convex hull, quotient boundary equality, hidden geodesic existence, or an ambient completeness principle.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Hyperbolic convex disk NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:hyperbolic-convex-disk-namecert-obligations}
For the carrier of \autoref{def:hyperbolic-convex-disk-carrier}, the local $\NameCert_{\HyperbolicConvexDiskUp}$ surface consists of carrier admission for $D=(U,M,G,B,W,R,H,C,P,N)$, disk-window exposure through $U$, metric-read exposure through $M$, finite geodesic-window exposure through $G$, boundary endpoint exposure through $B$, stream sampling through $W$, Real sealing through $R$, componentwise transport through $H$, replay through $C$, provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:hyperbolic-convex-disk-carrier}. The admitted object is exactly the ten-row finite packet $D=(U,M,G,B,W,R,H,C,P,N)$.

The geometric rows expose the convexity test. The disk window $U$, metric row $M$, geodesic-window row $G$, and boundary row $B$ are the only rows from which a consumer can read hyperbolic convexity.

The sampling and seal rows make the finite measurement explicit. The stream row $W$ enumerates the inspected endpoints and witnesses, while $R$ records the Real-facing metric and parameter seal.

Finally $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the displayed rows. Since no host subset, infinite hull, hidden geodesic, quotient boundary equality, or ambient completeness coordinate is admitted, these rows exhaust the local NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Hyperbolic convex disk geodesic stability]
\label{thm:hyperbolic-convex-disk-geodesic-stability}
Let $D=(U,M,G,B,W,R,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\HyperbolicConvexDiskUp$ packet. If a displayed geodesic segment has endpoints in the finite disk window and its sampled interior witnesses are carried by the same stream and Real-seal rows, then replacing the disk window by an extensionally same window preserves the accepted convex disk packet through the same boundary, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the geodesic-window row $G$. Its endpoints are recorded inside the finite disk window $U$, and its metric read is governed by $M$.

The interior witness read is finite. The stream row $W$ lists the sampled witnesses, while the Real seal $R$ records the metric and parameter values needed by the displayed segment check.

Now apply the componentwise transport row $H$ to replace $U$ by an extensionally same disk window. The boundary row $B$ transports the endpoint read, the replay row $C$ repeats the same finite route, and the provenance and NameCert rows keep the packet identity fixed. Therefore the convex disk certificate is stable under displayed geodesic-window transport.
\end{proof}
