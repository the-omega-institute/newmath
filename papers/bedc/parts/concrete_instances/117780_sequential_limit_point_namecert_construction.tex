\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\SequentialLimitPointUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-sequential-limit-point-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Kelley and Bourbaki on limit points and filters, together with Bishop--Bridges constructive sequence practice, the $\SequentialLimitPointUp$ packet names the sequence-specific route by which a displayed sequence supplies a limit-point candidate through finite tail evidence. It consumes the tail filter of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-frechetfilter-namecert}, the cluster handoff of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cluster-filter-namecert}, the finite source windows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet is a finite NameCert interface for sequence-limit arguments; it does not import a selected host limit, quotient filter equality, ambient compactness, countable choice, or completeness of $\RealUp$.

\begin{definition}[Sequential limit-point carrier]
\label{def:sequential-limit-point-carrier}
A $\SequentialLimitPointUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
L=(S,F,Q,R,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $S$ is the source $\StreamNameUp$ sequence row, exposing only finite observation windows of the named sequence;
\item $F$ is the $\FrechetFilterUp$ tail row, recording the eventual-tail requests through which subsequence or neighbourhood reads are inspected;
\item $Q$ is the $\ClusterFilterUp$ handoff row, used only after the Frechet tail row has been displayed;
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row for the finite source and tail windows;
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal row reached after $S,F,Q,R$ have been exposed;
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport of $S,F,Q,R,E$;
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a sequence-window request through the tail and cluster rows to the terminal seal;
\item $P$ records $\Pkg$ provenance over $\SequentialLimitPointUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\FrechetFilterUp$, $\ClusterFilterUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$;
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\SequentialLimitPointUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for a selected host limit, quotient filter equality, arbitrary subnet selection, open-cover compactness, countable choice, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Sequential limit-point NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:sequential-limit-point-namecert-obligations}
For the carrier of \autoref{def:sequential-limit-point-carrier}, the local $\NameCert_{\SequentialLimitPointUp}$ surface consists exactly of source StreamName admission through $S$, Frechet tail admission through $F$, ClusterFilter handoff through $Q$, RegSeqRat readback through $R$, Real seal handoff through $E$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport through $H$, displayed $\Cont$ replay through $C$, $\Pkg$ provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier. The source sequence row $S$ and Frechet tail row $F$ are the only rows that expose sequence observations and eventual-tail requests.

Read the cluster handoff and regular readback next. The row $Q$ is downstream of the tail filter, and $R$ reads only the displayed finite windows supplied by $S$ and $F$ before any real-facing seal is available.

Finally the seal and structural rows $E,H,C,P,N$ replay, transport, source, and name the same finite route. Since the carrier contains no host-limit, quotient-filter, subnet-choice, compactness, countable-choice, or completeness coordinate, none of those principles can be exported by the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Sequential limit-point Frechet cluster route]
\label{thm:sequential-limit-point-frechet-cluster-route}
Every accepted $\SequentialLimitPointUp$ packet reaches a cluster-facing or real-facing consumer only through
$$
\StreamNameUp \longmapsto \FrechetFilterUp \longmapsto \ClusterFilterUp \longmapsto \RegSeqRatUp \longmapsto \RealUp .
$$
Equivalently, a consumer must first inspect the source sequence windows, then the Frechet tail row, then the cluster handoff, then the regular rational readback, and only then the terminal real seal.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the carrier row $S$. It supplies finite sequence windows and no infinite representative.

The Frechet tail row $F$ then fixes the eventual-tail request, and the cluster row $Q$ may be consumed only after that tail request is displayed. Thus cluster use remains sequence-specific rather than an arbitrary filter import.

The readback row $R$ and seal row $E$ finish the route through regular rational data and the terminal $\RealUp$ boundary. The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ preserve that order, so the route cannot bypass the Frechet tail or cluster handoff by selecting a host limit or using quotient filter equality.
\end{proof}
