\begin{theorem}[DyadicIntervalNet interval-arithmetic sibling route]
\label{thm:dyadic-interval-net-interval-arithmetic-sibling-route}
Every $\DyadicIntervalNetUp$ consumer that uses interval arithmetic must expose the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\DyadicIntervalArithmeticUp$, $\FiniteCoverUp$, and $\RealUp$ seal rows before treating the net as a cover witness. The accepted read is the displayed net route together with those sibling rows; it is not a host interval algorithm, a detached finite-cover proof, or an ambient real-completeness claim.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the displayed $\DyadicIntervalNetUp$ carrier. Its mesh and endpoint rows provide finite dyadic interval data, but they do not by themselves certify arithmetic closure or real-cover consumption.

Next read the sibling arithmetic route. The $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ row supplies the rational tolerance ledger, and the $\DyadicIntervalArithmeticUp$ row supplies the endpoint-operation surface needed before interval calculations can be consumed.

Finally attach the cover and real-seal rows. The $\FiniteCoverUp$ row records the finite cover witness, while the $\RealUp$ seals keep the completed-real boundary visible. Thus an interval-arithmetic consumer receives exactly the displayed net plus its arithmetic, cover, and real-seal siblings.
\end{proof}
