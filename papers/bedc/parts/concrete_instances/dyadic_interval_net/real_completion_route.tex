\begin{theorem}[DyadicIntervalNet Real-completion route]
\label{thm:dyadic-interval-net-real-completion-route}
Every $\DyadicIntervalNetUp$ packet consumed by a Real or regular-Cauchy completion route must expose the finite dyadic interval cells, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ endpoint arithmetic, $\StreamNameUp$ endpoint windows, $\RegSeqRatUp$ regular-rational readback, and the terminal $\RealUp$ seal before the consumer reads completion data. Thus the dyadic interval net supplies the finite mesh route consumed by \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, with the same finite-cell discipline used by \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compact-interval-uniform-modulus-namecert}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted $\DyadicIntervalNetUp$ carrier. Its finite cells and endpoint incidences are displayed first, and the endpoint arithmetic is read through $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ rather than through host real equality.

The displayed cells open finite $\StreamNameUp$ endpoint windows. Those windows are then read as $\RegSeqRatUp$ data, so a completion-facing consumer receives rational tolerance information before any terminal real seal is visible.

Only after this route has exposed cells, dyadic arithmetic, stream windows, regular-rational readback, and the $\RealUp$ seal may the consumer read completion data. The route exports no selected limit, quotient stream equality, countable-choice schedule, host real endpoint, or ambient completeness theorem.
\end{proof}
