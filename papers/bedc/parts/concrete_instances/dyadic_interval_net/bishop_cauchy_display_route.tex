\begin{theorem}[DyadicIntervalNet BishopCauchyDisplayCertificate route]
\label{thm:dyadic-interval-net-bishop-cauchy-display-route}
Every $\DyadicIntervalNetUp$ consumer feeding a $\BishopCauchyDisplayCertificateUp$ route must expose the finite dyadic interval-net rows before the display certificate may read $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, or $\RealUp$ seal rows. In carrier notation, the interval-net rows $D,J,E$ are displayed first, the endpoint windows and regular readback pass through $S,R$, and only then may the Bishop Cauchy display certificate consume the Real-facing seal $Q$ together with the structural rows $H,C,P,N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier of \autoref{def:dyadic-interval-net-carrier}. The rows $D,J,E$ are the finite interval-net data: dyadic endpoint arithmetic, the listed cells, and endpoint incidence.

Route those rows into the display-certificate surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-bishop-cauchy-display-certificate-namecert}. Its Cauchy display reads finite windows, regular rational readback, dyadic tolerances, and a terminal Real seal, so the interval-net source must first make the finite cells and endpoint bounds visible.

The downstream read therefore passes through $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, and $\RealUp$ only after the finite dyadic interval net has been displayed. The route exports no selected limit, quotient stream equality, countable choice, host endpoint equality, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{proof}
