\begin{theorem}[Dyadic interval net compact interval forward route]
\label{thm:dyadic-interval-net-compact-interval-forward-route}
Every compact-interval or closed-bounded-interval consumer of an accepted $\DyadicIntervalNetUp$ packet must first read the finite cell row, endpoint incidence, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ endpoint arithmetic, $\StreamNameUp$ window row, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback, and $\RealUp$ seal before compact interval or uniform-modulus rows are public. In particular, consumers routed to \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compact-interval-uniform-modulus-namecert} or \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-closedboundedintervalnet-namecert} receive the interval net only as the displayed finite handoff
$$
D,J,E \longmapsto S,R \longmapsto Q .
$$
The route exports no open-cover compactness theorem, selected finite subcover, quotient stream equality, host real equality, countable choice, trichotomy, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier of \autoref{def:dyadic-interval-net-carrier}. The finite cell row and endpoint incidence are the rows $J,E$, while the endpoint arithmetic is read from the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ row $D$.

Use the cover refinement theorem \autoref{thm:dyadic-interval-net-cover-refinement}. It places every cover-facing read through the finite cell and endpoint rows before any Real or Completion consumer can inspect the terminal seal.

Next use the StreamName and RegSeqRat handoff of \autoref{thm:dyadic-interval-net-streamname-regseqrat-handoff}. The window row $S$ and regular rational row $R$ are forced between the finite interval-net rows and any compact-interval consumer.

Finally replay the Real-seal nonescape theorem \autoref{thm:dyadic-interval-net-real-seal-nonescape}. The seal $Q$ remains downstream of $D,J,E,S,R$, so the compact interval and uniform-modulus routes receive only the finite dyadic handoff and not a host compactness or choice principle.
\end{proof}
