\begin{theorem}[DyadicIntervalNet forward dependency route]
\label{thm:dyadic-interval-net-forward-dependency-route}
Every $\DyadicIntervalNetUp$ finite-cell route consumed by compact interval, finite subcover modulus, or regular-real completion consumers first exposes $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ endpoint arithmetic, $\StreamNameUp$ endpoint windows, $\RegSeqRatUp$ tolerance readback, and the terminal $\RealUp$ seal. The forward dependency route therefore factors through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert} before any compactness, finite-subcover, or completion-facing consumer can treat the finite interval net as public.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier of \autoref{def:dyadic-interval-net-carrier}. Its finite-cell data is not public on its own; the endpoint arithmetic is read through the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ row, while endpoint windows and tolerance readback are routed through $\StreamNameUp$ and $\RegSeqRatUp$.

Use the existing sibling dependency and compact interval routes for $\DyadicIntervalNetUp$. They place compact interval and finite-subcover consumers downstream of the displayed finite cells, endpoint arithmetic, stream windows, and regular-rational readback.

Finally apply the terminal Real-seal discipline of the same packet. Since the $\RealUp$ seal remains downstream of those rows, regular-real completion consumers receive only the displayed forward dependency route and cannot replace it with an ambient compactness theorem, selected finite subcover, quotient stream equality, host real endpoint, or unrestricted completion principle.
\end{proof}
