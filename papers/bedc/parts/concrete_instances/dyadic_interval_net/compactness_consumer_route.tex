\begin{theorem}[Dyadic interval net compactness forward reference]
\label{thm:dyadic-interval-net-forward-reference}
The $\DyadicIntervalNetUp$ packet is consumed by the finite interval-net compactness route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadic-interval-net-compactness-namecert} through dyadic endpoint rows, finite interval cells, $\StreamNameUp$ windows, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback, and the terminal $\RealUp$ seal. In carrier notation, every such compactness-facing read first exposes the dyadic endpoint arithmetic $D$, the finite cell row $J$, and endpoint incidence $E$, then passes through the window and readback rows $S,R$, and only then reaches the Real seal $Q$ and the structural rows $H,C,P,N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:dyadic-interval-net-carrier}. The dyadic endpoint row $D$, finite interval-cell row $J$, and endpoint-incidence row $E$ are the only rows that can feed the compactness consumer.

The compactness chapter \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadic-interval-net-compactness-namecert} reads those rows as finite interval-net data. Its consumer route therefore receives cell coverage from the displayed dyadic rows, not from an ambient open-cover compactness principle.

The analytic readback remains downstream. Endpoint observations are exposed through $S$, regular rational tolerances are read through $R$, and the Real-facing boundary is sealed only at $Q$.

Finally the structural rows $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same packet. Thus the forward reference records a substantive child route from $\DyadicIntervalNetUp$ to its compactness consumer without adding selected subcovers, countable choice, quotient streams, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{proof}
