\begin{theorem}[DyadicIntervalNet finite-mesh extraction]
\label{thm:dyadic-interval-net-finite-mesh-extraction}
Every $\DyadicIntervalNetUp$ finite mesh consumed by a $\RegularCauchySequenceUp$ or $\RealUp$ completion route factors through the displayed chain
$$
\DyadicRatCoreUp,\ \StreamNameUp,\ \RegSeqRatUp,\ \RealUp .
$$
The extraction first reads dyadic endpoint and containment arithmetic, then opens the finite StreamName windows induced by the mesh, then reads those windows as regular rational data, and finally exposes the terminal Real seal through the existing finite-mesh Real-completion route of \autoref{thm:dyadic-interval-net-finite-mesh-real-completion-route}. It exports no selected limit, quotient stream equality, host real equality, open-cover compactness, countable-choice selector, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the finite mesh Real-completion route \autoref{thm:dyadic-interval-net-finite-mesh-real-completion-route}. It already places the finite dyadic mesh row, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ arithmetic row, $\StreamNameUp$ windows, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback, and terminal $\RealUp$ seal in the required order.

Now read the regular-Cauchy forward route of \autoref{thm:dyadic-interval-net-regular-cauchy-real-forward-route}. A $\RegularCauchySequenceUp$ consumer may inspect interval windows only after they have been scheduled as StreamName windows and read back as regular rational data.

Combining the two routes makes finite mesh extraction a displayed dyadic, stream, regular-rational, and Real-seal handoff. Since neither route contains selected limits, quotient stream equality, host real equality, open-cover compactness, countable choice, or ambient completeness as carrier rows, the extraction cannot export those coordinates.
\end{proof}
