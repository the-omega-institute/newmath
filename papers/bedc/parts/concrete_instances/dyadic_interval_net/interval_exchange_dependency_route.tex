\begin{theorem}[DyadicIntervalNet interval-exchange dependency route]
\label{thm:dyadic-interval-net-interval-exchange-dependency-route}
Every future $\IntervalExchangeUp$ consumer of an accepted $\DyadicIntervalNetUp$ packet must first read the dyadic endpoint arithmetic of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the finite dyadic interval cells and endpoint-incidence rows of this chapter, the $\StreamNameUp$ endpoint windows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the $\RegSeqRatUp$ tolerance and readback rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal $\RealUp$ seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. Only after that route is displayed may the consumer interpret the finite partition as an interval exchange surface. The dependency exports no invariant measure, minimality claim, unique-ergodicity claim, host interval function, quotient real equality, countable-choice schedule, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness principle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier $I=(D,J,E,S,R,Q,H,C,P,N)$ of \autoref{def:dyadic-interval-net-carrier}. The endpoint arithmetic row $D$ and the interval-cell rows $J,E$ are the only finite partition data available before any Real-facing read.

Apply the cover refinement theorem \autoref{thm:dyadic-interval-net-cover-refinement}. It forces every partition consumer to expose listed dyadic cells, endpoint incidence, and dyadic comparisons before opening StreamName, RegSeqRat, or Real rows.

The interval-exchange consumer then reads the same L10 route used by Real and completion consumers. Endpoint observations pass through $S$, tolerance and rational readback pass through $R$, and the terminal real-name boundary is available only at $Q$.

Finally replay $H,C,P,N$. Transport, continuation replay, provenance, and local naming preserve that finite dependency route, so an interval-exchange surface cannot import invariant-measure, minimality, host-function, quotient-equality, choice, or ambient-completeness data from the dyadic net.
\end{proof}
