\begin{theorem}[DyadicIntervalNet sibling anchor]\label{thm:dyadic-interval-net-sibling-anchor}
Every accepted $\DyadicIntervalNetUp$ consumer that supplies finite interval-net data must first expose the finite epsilon-net rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finite-epsilon-net-namecert} and the located endpoint rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-locatedinterval-namecert}. The dyadic interval-net packet therefore consumes finite center-radius coverage data together with located interval endpoint evidence before any L10 finite-net consumer may read it as interval-net coverage.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Read the finite epsilon-net sibling first. Its rows supply the displayed finite family, radius ledger, and coverage request that a dyadic interval net later specializes to interval cells.

Next read the located interval sibling. Its endpoint rows provide the located lower and upper boundary data needed before a dyadic interval cell can be treated as an interval row rather than a bare dyadic token.

Finally combine the two routes through the local $\DyadicIntervalNetUp$ NameCert rows. The resulting consumer receives finite epsilon-net coverage and located interval endpoint evidence as explicit inputs, and it receives no host compactness theorem, selected subcover, quotient real equality, countable choice, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness coordinate.
\end{proof}
