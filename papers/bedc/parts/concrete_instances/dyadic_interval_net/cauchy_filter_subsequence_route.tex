\begin{theorem}[DyadicIntervalNet CauchyFilterSubsequence route]
\label{thm:dyadic-interval-net-cauchy-filter-subsequence-route}
Every $\DyadicIntervalNetUp$ consumer that exports finite dyadic interval windows toward $\CauchyFilterSubsequenceUp$ must expose the dyadic endpoint and radius cells before the route reaches \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cauchy-filter-subsequence-namecert}. The visible handoff factors through the existing $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance arithmetic, the $\StreamNameUp$ finite-window schedule, and the $\RegSeqRatUp$ regular rational readback before the Cauchy-filter subsequence packet may consume those windows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier of \autoref{def:dyadic-interval-net-carrier}. The finite net gives only displayed dyadic cells, endpoint comparisons, and radius budgets; it does not by itself select a subsequence or a filter limit.

Route those cells through the dyadic tolerance arithmetic, then through the StreamName schedule and RegSeqRat readback already used by the regular-Cauchy Real forward route. These rows are exactly the visible windows required by \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cauchy-filter-subsequence-namecert}.

Thus the Cauchy-filter subsequence consumer receives finite dyadic net windows as displayed BEDC rows. The route exports no hidden subsequence selector, quotient stream equality, countable choice, host interval endpoint, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{proof}
