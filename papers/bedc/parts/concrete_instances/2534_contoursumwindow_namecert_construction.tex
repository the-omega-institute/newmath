\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\ContourSumWindowUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-contoursumwindow-namecert}
\origin{ai}

The $\ContourSumWindowUp$ interface gives the contour-integral operation a finite window packet that downstream analytic chapters can inspect without importing a host integration operator. It records one piecewise-linear contour row, one holomorphic input row, a finite subdivision and sample ledger, the resulting Riemann-sum value, the output complex-history row, transport and continuation rows, package provenance, and a local naming row.

The window packet is the finite Riemann-sum face of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-contourintegraloperation-namecert}. It consumes the holomorphic input surface before exposing the $\ComplexUp$ output row, so the analytic dependency is the displayed contour-window route rather than an ambient contour-integral operator.

\begin{definition}[ContourSumWindow carrier]
\label{def:contour-sum-window-carrier}
A $\ContourSumWindowUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
W=(\gamma,f,S,R,I,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\gamma$ is the $\mathsf{PLContour}$ row naming the finite endpoint list and segment history used by the contour read;
\item $f$ is the $\HolomorphicUp$ input row on the displayed neighbourhood of $\gamma$;
\item $S$ is the finite subdivision and sample row, listing the segment subdivision, sample point, and local mesh witness for every segment;
\item $R$ is the Riemann-sum ledger, recording the finite summands, the mesh comparison, and the value computed from $f$ on the samples of $S$;
\item $I$ is the output $\ComplexUp$ history row classified as the window value associated to the displayed ledger $R$;
\item $H$ is the componentwise $\hsame$ transport row for $\gamma$, $f$, $S$, $R$, and $I$;
\item $C$ is the finite $\Cont$ route row carrying subdivision refinement, contour endpoint transport, holomorphic-input transport, and output readback;
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\mathsf{PLContour}$, $\HolomorphicUp$, finite subdivision rows, Riemann-sum rows, $\ComplexUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$;
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\ContourSumWindowUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[ContourSumWindow NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:contour-sum-window-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\ContourSumWindowUp$ carrier $W=(\gamma,f,S,R,I,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\ContourSumWindowUp}$ surface consists exactly of finite carrier admission, subdivision ledger exactness, contour route stability, output readback through $I$, package provenance through $P$, and non-escape from host integration, open-cover compactness, selected limits, quotient values, or unlisted analytic function-space objects.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.ContourSumWindowUp.ContourSumWindowCarrier\_namecert\_obligations}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:contour-sum-window-carrier}. Carrier admission is the display of $\gamma,f,S,R,I,H,C,P,N$ as rows of one finite $\BHist$ packet, so no consumer can read a contour, integrand, subdivision, sum, or output row outside that list.

Read the subdivision and Riemann-sum coordinates next. The row $S$ supplies the finite segment subdivision and sample data, while $R$ supplies the summand ledger and mesh comparison attached to those samples. Exactness is therefore ledger exactness for the listed rows, not convergence in an ambient integration theory.

Finally apply the displayed transport and continuation rows. The $\hsame$ row transports the contour, input, ledger, and output componentwise, and the $\Cont$ row records only the finite routes named in the packet. Since no coordinate names host integration, compactness, selected limits, or quotient values, the local $\NameCert$ exports only the finite window surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[ContourSumWindow Cauchy boundary]
\label{thm:contour-sum-window-cauchy-boundary}
Every Cauchy-facing read of an accepted $\ContourSumWindowUp$ carrier factors through the same $\mathsf{PLContour}$ row $\gamma$, the same $\HolomorphicUp$ row $f$, the displayed subdivision row $S$, the Riemann-sum ledger $R$, the $\ComplexUp$ output row $I$, and the visible $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and $\NameCert$ rows. The boundary exports only finite contour-window data and does not assert a host Cauchy integral theorem, a host contour integral, open-cover compactness, selected limiting values, quotient complex histories, or an ambient completeness principle.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.ContourSumWindowUp.ContourSumWindowTasteGate\_single\_carrier\_alignment}
\begin{proof}
Start with the finite window packet. The only route from the contour and holomorphic input to an output row passes through the subdivision row $S$ and the Riemann-sum ledger $R$ before reaching the $\ComplexUp$ row $I$.

The Cauchy-facing consumer can therefore inspect the displayed ledger and its transports, but it cannot replace the finite ledger by a host integral or by a selected limiting process. Those objects are not coordinates of the carrier.

The non-escape clauses of \autoref{thm:contour-sum-window-namecert-obligations} close the boundary. They keep Cauchy-style cancellation and boundary reads inside the displayed contour, subdivision, Riemann-sum, output, transport, continuation, package, and naming rows, with no open-cover compactness or quotient-value surface added.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[ContourSumWindow subdivision exactness]
\label{thm:contour-sum-window-subdivision-exactness}
For every accepted $\ContourSumWindowUp$ carrier $W=(\gamma,f,S,R,I,H,C,P,N)$, each summand exposed by the window is read only through the displayed subdivision and sample row $S$ together with the Riemann-sum ledger $R$. No summand may be supplied by a host contour integral, a selected limit, an unlisted mesh, or an ambient analytic function-space object.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.ContourSumWindowUp.ContourSumWindowCarrier\_subdivision\_exactness}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:contour-sum-window-carrier}. The row $S$ names the finite segment subdivision, sample point, and mesh witness for every segment, while $R$ records the finite summands and their mesh comparison.

Use \autoref{thm:contour-sum-window-namecert-obligations}. Its subdivision ledger exactness clause makes $S$ and $R$ the only admissible readback for summand data, and its non-escape clause removes host integration, selected limits, quotient values, and unlisted analytic objects from the packet.

The Cauchy-facing boundary theorem \autoref{thm:contour-sum-window-cauchy-boundary} preserves the same rows for every boundary read. Thus the subdivision and Riemann-sum ledger are the complete summand surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[ContourSumWindow refinement stability]
\label{thm:contour-sum-window-refinement-stability}
Let $W=(\gamma,f,S,R,I,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\ContourSumWindowUp$ carrier. Any subdivision refinement accepted by the window must replay through the displayed continuation row $C$ after preserving $\gamma$, $f$, $S$, $R$, and $I$ componentwise through $H$. The refinement route cannot introduce a host contour integral, a new holomorphic input, a quotient complex value, or a selected limiting process.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.ContourSumWindowUp.ContourSumWindowCarrier\_refinement\_stability}
\begin{proof}
Read the transport coordinates in \autoref{def:contour-sum-window-carrier}. The row $H$ transports the contour, input, subdivision, ledger, and output componentwise, and the row $C$ records subdivision refinement as one of the finite continuation routes of the packet.

Apply \autoref{thm:contour-sum-window-namecert-obligations}. Contour route stability and output readback require refinement to keep the same displayed rows before any consumer can inspect the refined window.

Finally use \autoref{thm:contour-sum-window-cauchy-boundary}. Since every Cauchy-facing read factors through $\gamma,f,S,R,I,H,C,P,N$, refinement has no coordinate for a host contour integral, a fresh analytic input, a quotient value, or a selected limit.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[ContourSumWindow residue boundary]
\label{thm:contour-sum-window-residue-boundary}
Any residue-facing consumer of an accepted $\ContourSumWindowUp$ carrier must first expose the $\mathsf{PLContour}$ row $\gamma$, the $\HolomorphicUp$ row $f$, the subdivision and sample row $S$, the Riemann-sum ledger $R$, the $\ComplexUp$ output row $I$, and the structural rows $H,C,P,N$ before reading a residue handoff. The boundary exports no host contour integral, no residue theorem, no quotient complex value, no selected limiting process, and no ambient analytic function-space object.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:contour-sum-window-carrier}. The rows available to a residue-facing consumer are exactly $\gamma,f,S,R,I,H,C,P,N$, with $I$ read only after the finite Riemann-sum ledger $R$ has been displayed.

Apply subdivision exactness, \autoref{thm:contour-sum-window-subdivision-exactness}. It forces each summand to be read from the subdivision and sample row $S$ together with the ledger $R$, so a residue handoff cannot replace the window by an unlisted contour-integral value.

Apply refinement stability, \autoref{thm:contour-sum-window-refinement-stability}, and then the Cauchy boundary theorem \autoref{thm:contour-sum-window-cauchy-boundary}. The same contour, holomorphic input, subdivision, ledger, output, transport, replay, provenance, and naming rows remain visible at the boundary, while host contour integrals, residue theorems, quotient values, selected limits, and ambient analytic function spaces remain outside the packet.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[ContourSumWindow output seal nonescape]
\label{thm:contour-sum-window-output-seal-nonescape}
For every accepted $\ContourSumWindowUp$ carrier $W=(\gamma,f,S,R,I,H,C,P,N)$, the $\ComplexUp$ output row $I$ is sealed only after the $\mathsf{PLContour}$ row $\gamma$, the $\HolomorphicUp$ row $f$, the subdivision row $S$, and the Riemann-sum ledger $R$ have been displayed. The sealed output exports no quotient complex value, no selected limiting value, and no host contour integral.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.ContourSumWindowUp.ContourSumWindowCarrier\_output\_seal\_nonescape}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier rows. The output row $I$ is classified as the window value associated to the displayed ledger $R$, and $R$ itself is computed from the samples listed in $S$ against the contour and input rows $\gamma$ and $f$.

Use \autoref{thm:contour-sum-window-namecert-obligations} to read output readback through $I$ and package provenance through $P$. These clauses make the output seal depend on the visible contour, holomorphic-input, subdivision, and Riemann-sum rows.

The boundary theorem \autoref{thm:contour-sum-window-cauchy-boundary} keeps the same seal for Cauchy-facing consumers. It therefore exports only the finite contour-window output row and refuses quotient complex values, selected limits, and host contour integration.
\end{proof}
