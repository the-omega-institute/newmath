Following Bishop--Bridges constructive compactness and Kelley product-space compactness, the $\FiniteProductCompactnessUp$ packet records finite products of compact metric NameCert data through explicit product finite-net witnesses. It consumes compact components from \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}, the product topology carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-product-topology-namecert}, the metric source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}, the terminal real-distance seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, and the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}. Its role is finite: the product net is displayed as data before any compact-metric product consumer reads compactness.

\begin{definition}[FiniteProductCompactness packet]
\label{def:finite-product-compactness-packet}
A $\FiniteProductCompactnessUp$ packet is a finite $\BHist$ carrier
$$
F=(I,C,P,N,D,R,H,K,L)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $I$ is the finite index spine listing the component coordinates and their product order.
\item $C$ is the finite family of component $\CompactMetricUp$ packets, one for each row of $I$.
\item $P$ is the $\ProductTopologyUp$ packet that reads exactly the component topology rows named by $I$ and $C$.
\item $N$ is the finite product-net witness: for each dyadic radius request, it lists component net choices and the induced finite product mesh.
\item $D$ is the dyadic radius ledger controlling the component radii and the product-radius comparison.
\item $R$ is the $\RealUp$ and $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback surface for component distances, product distances, and radius bounds.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for index, compact component, product topology, product-net, dyadic, readback, replay, provenance, and naming rows.
\item $K$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a product compactness request through $I,C,P,N,D,R,H,L$.
\item $L$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance and local $\NameCert_{\FiniteProductCompactnessUp}$ row over $\CompactMetricUp$, $\ProductTopologyUp$, $\MetricSpaceUp$, $\RealUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and $\NameCert$.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier of accepted packets compares only these displayed rows. It has no coordinate for countable choice, open-cover compactness, host product spaces, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[FiniteProductCompactness NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:finite-product-compactness-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\FiniteProductCompactnessUp$ packet $F=(I,C,P,N,D,R,H,K,L)$, the local $\NameCert_{\FiniteProductCompactnessUp}$ surface consists exactly of the following obligation rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item carrier admission for the finite index spine, compact component packets, product topology packet, product finite-net witness, dyadic radius ledger, readback surface, transport row, replay row, provenance row, and local name;
\item component compactness, requiring each coordinate named by $I$ to expose its $\CompactMetricUp$ packet before the product mesh is assembled;
\item finite product-net construction, requiring $N$ to be built from the component finite nets and the displayed finite index spine;
\item projection stability, requiring product-net membership and radius comparison to project back to the component packets named by $C$;
\item metric readback, requiring all product-radius and distance comparisons to pass through $D$ and $R$ before a $\RealUp$ seal is read;
\item transport and replay stability, requiring $H$ and $K$ to preserve the same finite product-net route under componentwise $\hsame$ transport and displayed $\Cont$ replay;
\item provenance exactness, requiring $L$ to name exactly the displayed dependencies and the local certificate row;
\item non-escape, excluding countable choice, open-cover compactness, host product spaces, and ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{enumerate}
No finite-product compactness obligation is stored outside these rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Carrier admission is projection from \autoref{def:finite-product-compactness-packet}. The tuple displays the index spine, component compact packets, product topology packet, finite product-net witness, dyadic ledger, readback surface, transport, replay, provenance, and local name.

Component compactness and product-net construction follow the order $I,C,P,N$. Each component compact packet is read before the product topology and before the finite product mesh is assembled, so the net witness is finite data over the displayed index spine.

Projection stability is part of the same construction. A product mesh element records one component net entry per coordinate in $I$, and every radius comparison returns to the component packet named by $C$.

Metric readback is forced through $D$ and $R$. The dyadic radius ledger supplies the requested tolerances, while the $\RegSeqRatUp$ and $\RealUp$ rows record the distance readback and final seal.

The rows $H,K,L$ preserve, replay, source, and name this route. Since the carrier has no coordinate for the excluded choice, open-cover, host-product, or ambient-completeness objects, no accepted consumer can recover one from the packet.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[FiniteProductCompactness projection-net handoff]
\label{thm:finite-product-compactness-projection-net-handoff}
Every product compactness consumer of an accepted $\FiniteProductCompactnessUp$ packet first reads each component compact packet in $C$ and the finite product-net ledger $N$ before any product compact-metric handoff. The handoff therefore exposes the finite index spine $I$, the product topology row $P$, the dyadic radius ledger $D$, the $\RealUp$ and $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row $R$, and the structural rows $H,K,L$ before it can be consumed as product compactness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start from the displayed carrier $F=(I,C,P,N,D,R,H,K,L)$. The component packets $C$ are indexed by $I$, and the product topology row $P$ reads those component topology rows before any product compactness request is answered.

The finite product-net ledger $N$ is the next required row. It records the finite mesh assembled from the component nets and the product-radius request, so a compact-metric product handoff cannot bypass the component compact packets.

The dyadic and metric readback rows then control the handoff. The ledger $D$ supplies the radius comparisons, and $R$ records the $\RegSeqRatUp$ and $\RealUp$ readback used by the consumer.

Finally, $H,K,L$ transport, replay, source, and name the same displayed route. Since no row stores countable choice, an open-cover compactness principle, a host product space, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness, the handoff is exactly the finite product-net route.
\end{proof}
