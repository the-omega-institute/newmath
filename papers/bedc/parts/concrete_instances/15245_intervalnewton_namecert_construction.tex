\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\IntervalNewtonUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-intervalnewton-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Moore--Kearfott--Cloud interval analysis, the $\IntervalNewtonUp$ packet records interval Newton and Krawczyk enclosure certificates as finite BEDC rows. It names a displayed box, function enclosure, slope or derivative enclosure, correction row, narrowing ledger, validated readback route, real-name readback, transport witnesses, replay data, provenance, and local naming row. It is not a solver oracle, a host zero-existence theorem, a global root-isolation method, a completeness assertion, or a floating-point semantics package.

The carrier reads interval operations through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-intervalarithmetic-namecert}, finite validated enclosures through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-validatednumerics-namecert}, displayed correction provenance through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-newtoniteration-namecert}, and finite Taylor or slope remainders through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-taylormodel-namecert}. These sibling rows are the entire dependency surface for the enclosure certificates below.

\begin{definition}[Interval Newton carrier]
\label{def:interval-newton-carrier}
An $\IntervalNewtonUp$ carrier is a finite $\mathsf{BHist}$ packet
$$
I=(B,F,D,N,K,V,R,H,C,P,L)
$$
with the following displayed coordinates:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $B$ is the interval box row, read through $\IntervalArithmeticUp$ endpoint and enclosure ledgers;
\item $F$ is the function-enclosure row over $B$, with every enclosure read routed through finite interval operations;
\item $D$ is the derivative or slope enclosure row, supplied either directly as an interval row or through a finite $\TaylorModelUp$ remainder packet;
\item $N$ is the finite Newton or Krawczyk correction row, including the displayed center, inverse or preconditioner row, and correction enclosure;
\item $K$ is the containment and narrowing ledger recording that the correction row stays inside, or narrows, the displayed box $B$;
\item $V$ is the $\ValidatedNumericsUp$ enclosure row read by downstream consumers;
\item $R$ is the $\RealUp$ or $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback route for the center, endpoint, and correction observations;
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transports for the box, function, derivative, correction, containment, readback, and validated rows;
\item $C$ records the $\Cont$ routes by which the displayed rows are replayed inside their source certificates;
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\IntervalArithmeticUp$, $\ValidatedNumericsUp$, $\NewtonIterationUp$, $\TaylorModelUp$, $\RealUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, and the displayed kernel rows;
\item $L$ is the local $\NameCert_{\IntervalNewtonUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
Two accepted carriers are classified together only by transporting these displayed coordinates through the recorded $\hsame$ and $\Cont$ rows. The classifier has no coordinate for a hidden solver state, a selected zero, host floating-point behavior, quotient real equality, or completeness of the ambient real line.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Interval Newton NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:interval-newton-namecert-obligations}
The local $\NameCert_{\IntervalNewtonUp}$ surface for the carrier of \autoref{def:interval-newton-carrier} consists of six rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item carrier admission for the finite packet $I=(B,F,D,N,K,V,R,H,C,P,L)$;
\item correction containment saying that the displayed Newton or Krawczyk row $N$ is admitted only with its containment and narrowing ledger $K$;
\item interval-arithmetic soundness for the box, function, derivative or slope, and correction enclosures;
\item validated-consumer handoff through the displayed $\ValidatedNumericsUp$ row $V$ and the readback row $R$;
\item transport under the componentwise $\hsame$ rows of $H$ and replay under the $\Cont$ routes of $C$;
\item ledger non-escape, meaning every consumer-visible claim is projected from $B,F,D,N,K,V,R,H,C,P,L$ and from no other numerical object.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.IntervalNewtonUp.IntervalNewtonNameCertObligations}
\begin{proof}
Carrier admission is projection from \autoref{def:interval-newton-carrier}. The box, function enclosure, derivative or slope enclosure, correction, containment ledger, validated readback, real-name route, transport, replay, provenance, and local name are visible fields of one finite packet.

Correction containment is read from $N$ together with $K$. The packet may expose a Krawczyk or interval Newton row only when the same displayed ledger records how that row is contained in, or narrows, the box $B$.

Interval-arithmetic soundness is inherited by routing all endpoint and enclosure operations through the $\IntervalArithmeticUp$ rows named in $P$. When a derivative or slope enclosure is supplied by a Taylor model, the packet reads only the finite $\TaylorModelUp$ remainder and interval-operation rows.

The consumer handoff is then $V$ together with $R$. Transport and replay use only the componentwise rows $H$ and $C$, so every exported obligation is exhausted by the carrier coordinates and no solver oracle or external real-semantics datum remains.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Interval Newton Krawczyk enclosure]
\label{thm:interval-newton-krawczyk-enclosure}
Let $I=(B,F,D,N,K,V,R,H,C,P,L)$ be an accepted $\IntervalNewtonUp$ carrier. Every Krawczyk or interval Newton enclosure exported by $I$ narrows the displayed box $B$ only through the $\IntervalArithmeticUp$ operation rows named by $P$ and through the displayed $\NewtonIterationUp$ or $\TaylorModelUp$ rows used to form $N$ and $D$. The exported enclosure is exactly the row recorded by $K$ and validated by $V$.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.IntervalNewtonUp.IntervalNewtonKrawczykEnclosure}
\begin{proof}
Start with a consumer-visible narrowing claim. By the carrier definition, the only box available to the packet is $B$, and the only correction row available is $N$.

Read the arithmetic used by the correction through the $\IntervalArithmeticUp$ dependency rows named in $P$. The center, inverse or preconditioner, function enclosure, derivative or slope enclosure, and final interval operation are all displayed rows of the same finite ledger.

If the correction row is presented as a Newton step, its provenance is the finite $\NewtonIterationUp$ row named in $P$. If the slope or derivative enclosure is presented by finite Taylor data, it is read through the $\TaylorModelUp$ row named in $P$. In either case, the packet keeps the operation inside visible BEDC rows.

Finally project $K$ and $V$. The containment ledger $K$ records the narrowed box, and the validated row $V$ is the only downstream enclosure exported by the packet. No separate analytic root theorem, host solver state, or floating-point rounding semantics is consulted.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Interval Newton Krawczyk radius lock]
\label{thm:interval-newton-krawczyk-radius-lock}
Let $I=(B,F,D,N,K,V,R,H,C,P,L)$ be an accepted $\IntervalNewtonUp$ carrier whose interval-arithmetic row supplies a Krawczyk enclosure. Then the correction radius, the Taylor or slope remainder row, the $\RealUp$ seal, and the $\ValidatedNumericsUp$ consumer boundary remain in the same finite packet $I$ before any zero-enclosure read is exported.

In particular, the exported zero-enclosure read factors through the visible rows $B,F,D,N,K,V,R,H,C,P,L$: $N$ supplies the correction radius, $D$ supplies the slope or $\TaylorModelUp$ remainder row, $R$ supplies the real-name seal route, and $V$ supplies the validated-consumer boundary.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.IntervalNewtonUp.IntervalNewtonKrawczykRadiusLock}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier of \autoref{def:interval-newton-carrier}. The correction radius is not a separate analytic datum; it is part of the displayed correction row $N$, and its containment is read with the ledger $K$.

Apply \autoref{thm:interval-newton-krawczyk-enclosure}. A Krawczyk enclosure can narrow the displayed box only through the interval-arithmetic operation rows named in $P$ and through the displayed $\NewtonIterationUp$ or $\TaylorModelUp$ rows used to form $N$ and $D$.

Use the interval-arithmetic soundness obligation of \autoref{thm:interval-newton-namecert-obligations}. It keeps the box, function, slope or Taylor remainder, and correction enclosures inside the same finite interval-arithmetic replay before $K$ and $V$ are visible.

Finally apply the validated-consumer handoff obligation of \autoref{thm:interval-newton-namecert-obligations} together with \autoref{thm:interval-newton-validated-consumer-boundary}. The validated row $V$ and readback row $R$ are downstream of $B,F,D,N,K$, so no zero-enclosure read can leave the packet before the correction radius, remainder row, real-name route, and validated boundary have all been bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Interval Newton correction transport scope]
\label{thm:interval-newton-correction-transport-scope}
Let $I=(B,F,D,N,K,V,R,H,C,P,L)$ be an accepted $\IntervalNewtonUp$ carrier. Every transported correction row exported by $I$ factors through the visible interval box $B$, the $\IntervalArithmeticUp$ operation rows named in $P$, the finite $\TaylorModelUp$ row or derivative and slope row $D$, the $\NewtonIterationUp$ correction provenance for $N$, and the $\ValidatedNumericsUp$ row $V$ before the validated enclosure is exported.

Consequently the transported correction cannot read a solver oracle, a host zero, floating-point semantics, quotient real equality, or ambient completeness outside the displayed carrier rows.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.IntervalNewtonUp.IntervalNewtonCorrectionTransportScope}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:interval-newton-carrier}. The correction surface is $B,F,D,N,K,V$, while $R,H,C,P,L$ supply readback, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming.

Invoke \autoref{thm:interval-newton-krawczyk-enclosure}. It forces every Krawczyk or interval Newton enclosure to narrow the displayed box $B$ only through $\IntervalArithmeticUp$ operations and through the displayed $\NewtonIterationUp$ or $\TaylorModelUp$ rows used to form $N$ and $D$.

The transported row then passes through $H$ and $C$ without changing its dependency route. The validated export is $V$, so a downstream validated-numerics consumer reads the correction only after the interval, derivative or Taylor-model, Newton-iteration, containment, and validation rows have appeared.

No excluded datum is a carrier coordinate or a provenance row in $P$. Thus the correction transport remains inside the visible interval, Taylor-model or derivative, Newton-iteration, validated-numerics, transport, replay, and local naming rows.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Interval Newton validated consumer boundary]
\label{thm:interval-newton-validated-consumer-boundary}
Every $\ValidatedNumericsUp$ or root-isolation consumer of an accepted $\IntervalNewtonUp$ packet reads the correction only through the displayed carrier
$$
I=(B,F,D,N,K,V,R,H,C,P,L).
$$
The consumer receives the interval box, function enclosure, derivative or slope enclosure, correction row, containment ledger, validated enclosure row, real-name readback route, transport rows, replay routes, provenance, and local naming row. It receives no solver oracle, no host zero, no floating-point semantics, no quotient real equality, and no completeness datum.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.IntervalNewtonUp.IntervalNewtonValidatedConsumerBoundary}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier. A validated or root-isolation consumer can inspect only the fields $B,F,D,N,K,V,R,H,C,P,L$.

Use \autoref{thm:interval-newton-namecert-obligations} to identify the exported obligation rows. The consumer handoff is $V$ and $R$, while transport and replay are restricted to $H$ and $C$.

Use \autoref{thm:interval-newton-krawczyk-enclosure} for the correction read. The Krawczyk or interval Newton row is visible only through $\IntervalArithmeticUp$ operations and through the displayed $\NewtonIterationUp$ or $\TaylorModelUp$ provenance named in $P$.

Thus every consumer-facing row is a projection of the finite packet. Since no carrier coordinate names a solver oracle, selected zero, hardware arithmetic semantics, quotient-real equality witness, or completeness theorem, those data cannot be exported by the boundary.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Interval Newton containment obligation]
\label{thm:interval-newton-containment-obligation}
For every accepted $\IntervalNewtonUp$ carrier $I=(B,F,D,N,K,V,R,H,C,P,L)$, the containment obligation is exactly the visible pair $(N,K)$. The correction row $N$ may be consumed only with the containment and narrowing ledger $K$, and every $\hsame$ transport or $\Cont$ replay of the correction must preserve the same box row $B$, the same correction row $N$, and the same containment ledger $K$ before the local $\NameCert_{\IntervalNewtonUp}$ row $L$ is read.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier. The only correction coordinate is $N$, and the only containment coordinate attached to it is $K$.

Use \autoref{thm:interval-newton-namecert-obligations}. It requires correction containment as a named row of the local NameCert surface, so a consumer cannot detach $N$ from the ledger $K$.

Transport and replay then use $H$ and $C$. Since those rows are componentwise over the carrier coordinates, they preserve $B,N,K$ before the local naming row $L$ is exposed.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Interval Newton interval-arithmetic soundness obligation]
\label{thm:interval-newton-interval-arithmetic-soundness-obligation}
For every accepted $\IntervalNewtonUp$ carrier $I=(B,F,D,N,K,V,R,H,C,P,L)$, interval-arithmetic soundness is the obligation that the box row $B$, the function-enclosure row $F$, the derivative or slope row $D$, and the correction row $N$ are all replayed through the $\IntervalArithmeticUp$ rows named by $P$. When $D$ is supplied by a finite $\TaylorModelUp$ remainder packet, the same obligation routes the Taylor remainder through interval endpoint operations before $K$ and $V$ are visible to consumers.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Read the arithmetic provenance from $P$. It names the interval-arithmetic surface through which endpoint, enclosure, derivative or slope, and correction operations are replayed.

Apply \autoref{thm:interval-newton-krawczyk-enclosure}. The narrowing claim is not a free analytic assertion; it is assembled from $B,F,D,N$ through the displayed interval operations.

If the derivative or slope row is supplied by $\TaylorModelUp$, the finite Taylor remainder is still routed through the same interval endpoint operations before containment is recorded in $K$. The validated row $V$ is therefore downstream of the interval-arithmetic replay, not a replacement for it.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Interval Newton validated handoff obligation]
\label{thm:interval-newton-validated-handoff-obligation}
For every accepted $\IntervalNewtonUp$ carrier $I=(B,F,D,N,K,V,R,H,C,P,L)$, the validated handoff obligation is the route from the validated enclosure row $V$ and readback row $R$ through the transport row $H$, replay row $C$, provenance row $P$, and local naming row $L$. A downstream $\ValidatedNumericsUp$ consumer may read the correction only after $V,R,H,C,P,L$ expose the same interval box, correction containment, and provenance rows already present in $B,F,D,N,K$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start from the validated consumer boundary. It says the consumer receives the displayed carrier rows and no solver oracle, host zero, floating-point semantics, quotient real equality, or completeness datum.

The handoff itself is $V$ together with $R$. These rows expose the validated enclosure and readback route, while $H$ and $C$ preserve them under transport and replay.

Finally $P$ and $L$ provide provenance and local naming. Since the route still points back to $B,F,D,N,K$, the validated consumer can read only the correction already certified by containment and interval-arithmetic soundness.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Interval Newton Real readback scope]
\label{thm:interval-newton-real-readback-scope}
Let $I=(B,F,D,N,K,V,R,H,C,P,L)$ be an accepted $\IntervalNewtonUp$ carrier. Any $\RealUp$-facing or $\RegSeqRatUp$-facing validated enclosure read first exposes the interval box $B$, function-enclosure row $F$, derivative or slope row $D$, correction row $N$, and containment ledger $K$. It then exposes the validated row $V$ and only then the readback row $R$.

Transport through $H$, replay through $C$, provenance through $P$, and local naming through $L$ preserve that same route. They cannot add a solver oracle, selected zero, quotient real equality, floating-point semantics, or ambient completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:interval-newton-carrier}. The only Real-facing or RegSeqRat-facing readback coordinate is $R$, and it is stored with $B,F,D,N,K,V,H,C,P,L$ in the same finite packet.

Apply \autoref{thm:interval-newton-krawczyk-enclosure}. The Krawczyk or interval Newton enclosure is assembled from the displayed box, function, derivative or slope, correction, and containment rows before it can be named as an enclosure.

Use \autoref{thm:interval-newton-interval-arithmetic-soundness-obligation}. This keeps $B,F,D,N$ inside the interval-arithmetic replay named by $P$ before the containment ledger $K$ and validated row $V$ become consumer-visible.

Finally apply \autoref{thm:interval-newton-validated-handoff-obligation}. The handoff places $V$ before $R$, and $H,C,P,L$ only transport, replay, name, and package the same route. Since no excluded datum is a carrier coordinate, the Real or RegSeqRat readback cannot export one.
\end{proof}
