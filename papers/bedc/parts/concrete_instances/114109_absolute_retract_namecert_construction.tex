\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\AbsoluteRetractUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-absolute-retract-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Borsuk and Dugundji, the $\AbsoluteRetractUp$ packet records a constructive retract-extension neighbour for compact metric and located metric analysis. It sits after the compact-source surfaces of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compact-namecert}, the closed extension route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-tietze-extension-namecert}, the separating-function route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-urysohn-lemma-namecert}, the convex target rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-convexset-namecert}, and the finite distance comparison route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-locatedmetric-namecert}. The packet names only displayed extension and retraction rows; it does not assert an ambient absolute retract theorem, selected neighbourhood retractions, quotient compacta, open-cover compactness, or choice of extension operators.

\begin{definition}[Absolute retract carrier]\label{def:absolute-retract-carrier}
An $\AbsoluteRetractUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
A=(X,K,L,T,U,V,E,R,S,H,C,P,N)
$$
where:
\begin{itemize}
\item $X$ is the displayed source carrier row;
\item $K$ is the compact or compact-metric source row read through $\CompactUp$ and $\CompactMetricUp$;
\item $L$ is the $\LocatedMetricUp$ distance-comparison row;
\item $T$ is the $\TietzeExtensionUp$ extension row for closed real-valued data;
\item $U$ is the $\UrysohnLemmaUp$ separation row used by closed-neighbourhood reads;
\item $V$ is the $\ConvexSetUp$ target row recording the convex retract codomain;
\item $E$ records the finite extension request from a displayed closed subpacket;
\item $R$ records the candidate retraction row;
\item $S$ records stability of the retraction after the extension route is displayed;
\item $H,C,P,N$ are componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and the local $\NameCert_{\AbsoluteRetractUp}$ row.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Absolute retract NameCert obligations]\label{thm:absolute-retract-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\AbsoluteRetractUp$ carrier $A=(X,K,L,T,U,V,E,R,S,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\AbsoluteRetractUp}$ surface consists exactly of source admission, compact-source readback, located-metric comparison, Tietze extension exposure, Urysohn separation exposure, convex-target routing, finite extension request, retraction row, retraction-stability row, componentwise transport, displayed replay, provenance, and local naming. Any consumer that omits one of $K,L,T,U,V,E,R,S,H,C,P,N$ is outside the packet.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:absolute-retract-carrier}. Each row is displayed as finite $\BHist$ data, and the local naming row $N$ records the accepted packet only over those displayed coordinates.

The compact and metric rows give the source scope through $\CompactUp$, $\CompactMetricUp$, and $\LocatedMetricUp$. The extension and separation rows then enter through $\TietzeExtensionUp$ and $\UrysohnLemmaUp$, while the target constraint is read through $\ConvexSetUp$.

Finally, $E,R,S,H,C,P,N$ package the extension request, retraction, stability, transport, replay, provenance, and name. Since no row contains an ambient absolute retract principle or selected global operator, the listed surface is the entire local obligation set.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Absolute retract retraction stability]\label{thm:absolute-retract-retraction-stability}
Every accepted $\AbsoluteRetractUp$ packet whose retraction row $R$ is consumed must first expose the compact-source row $K$, located-metric row $L$, Tietze extension row $T$, Urysohn separation row $U$, convex-target row $V$, and finite extension request $E$. The stability row $S$ then transports the retraction through $H,C,P,N$ without replacing the packet by an ambient neighbourhood-retract theorem or a selected extension operator.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:absolute-retract-namecert-obligations}. They make $K,L,T,U,V,E,R,S,H,C,P,N$ the visible surface for any accepted packet.

The retraction read cannot precede the extension route. The compact and located-metric rows bound the source request, the Tietze and Urysohn rows expose the extension and separation data, and the convex row records the target shape before $R$ is read.

The stability row $S$ is therefore only a transport of the displayed retraction along the same finite route. It does not create a host absolute retract theorem, quotient compactum, chosen operator, or open-cover compactness argument.
\end{proof}
