\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\BusemannConvexMetricUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-busemann-convex-metric-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Busemann and Bridson--Haefliger on convexity in geodesic metric spaces, the $\BusemannConvexMetricUp$ packet records the finite midpoint-distance convexity surface that sits between displayed geodesic metric data and stronger CAT(0) or Hadamard consumers. It sits over the metric carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}, the segment-window route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-geodesic-metric-namecert}, the convex-set rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-convexset-namecert}, the CAT(0) comparison surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cat-zero-metric-namecert}, the Hadamard-space carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-hadamard-space-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names midpoint convexity for displayed geodesic windows; it is not a full CAT(0) comparison theorem, not a selected geodesic family, not quotient path equality, and not ambient completeness.

\begin{definition}[Busemann convex metric carrier]
\label{def:busemann-convex-metric-carrier}
A $\BusemannConvexMetricUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
B=(M,G,X,Y,S,T,D,R,V,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $M$ is the $\MetricSpaceUp$ source row, exposing only point classifiers, distance readbacks, and the metric-law ledger used by the convexity request.
\item $G$ is the $\GeodesicMetricUp$ row naming the finite geodesic segment windows inspected by the packet.
\item $X$ and $Y$ are endpoint-pair rows for two displayed geodesic windows, with every endpoint admitted through $M$.
\item $S$ and $T$ are the finite segment-parameter rows for the two windows, including the midpoint or equal-parameter addresses used by the convexity comparison.
\item $D$ is the distance-comparison row, recording the finite Busemann convexity inequality that the distance between equal-parameter points on the two displayed windows is bounded by the corresponding convex combination of endpoint distances.
\item $R$ is the $\RealUp$ seal row for the distance values read by $D$, reached only after the metric, geodesic, endpoint, and parameter rows have been displayed.
\item $V$ is the $\ConvexSetUp$ handoff row for consumers that read the same midpoint or equal-parameter windows as convex-domain data.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the metric, geodesic, endpoint, parameter, distance-comparison, Real-seal, convex-handoff, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a finite Busemann-convexity request through
$$
M,G,X,Y,S,T,D,R,V \longmapsto H,P,N .
$$
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\BusemannConvexMetricUp$, $\MetricSpaceUp$, $\GeodesicMetricUp$, $\ConvexSetUp$, $\CatZeroMetricUp$, $\HadamardSpaceUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\BusemannConvexMetricUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through these displayed rows. It has no coordinate for a global CAT(0) triangle comparison, a Hadamard-space theorem, selected geodesics, quotient path equality, nearest-point projection, or ambient completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Busemann convex metric NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:busemann-convex-metric-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\BusemannConvexMetricUp$ carrier
$$
B=(M,G,X,Y,S,T,D,R,V,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\BusemannConvexMetricUp}$ surface consists exactly of metric-source admission, geodesic segment-window admission, endpoint-pair admission, finite parameter admission, Busemann distance-convexity ledger exactness, terminal $\RealUp$ distance sealing, convex-window handoff, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming over the rows of \autoref{def:busemann-convex-metric-carrier}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Carrier admission is projection from \autoref{def:busemann-convex-metric-carrier}. The accepted packet displays the metric source, geodesic row, two endpoint-pair rows, two finite parameter rows, the distance-convexity ledger, Real seal, convex handoff, transport, replay, provenance, and local name.

The convexity obligation is localized in $D$. That row reads only the equal-parameter or midpoint observations supplied by $S$ and $T$, compares their distance through $M$, and seals the resulting distance data through $R$.

Rows $V,H,C,P,N$ do not enlarge the geometric object. They route the same displayed convexity window to convex-domain consumers, preserve the rows componentwise, replay the same finite request, record provenance, and name the local certificate. Thus no obligation row can be read as CAT(0) comparison, Hadamard completeness, selected geodesics, quotient path equality, projection selection, or ambient completeness.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Busemann convex midpoint non-escape]
\label{thm:busemann-convex-metric-midpoint-nonescape}
Every midpoint-facing or equal-parameter consumer of an accepted $\BusemannConvexMetricUp$ packet factors through the finite route
$$
\MetricSpaceUp,\ \GeodesicMetricUp,\ \ConvexSetUp,\ \RealUp,\ \BHist,\ \hsame,\ \Cont,\ \Pkg,\ \NameCert .
$$
It may consume the displayed Busemann convexity ledger for the named segment windows, but it cannot infer a CAT(0) comparison triangle, a Hadamard-space theorem, selected geodesics, quotient path equality, nearest-point projection, or ambient completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use \autoref{thm:busemann-convex-metric-namecert-obligations}. The consumer receives midpoint or equal-parameter information only after the metric source, geodesic windows, endpoint pairs, finite parameter rows, distance-convexity ledger, and Real seal have been displayed.

The convex-domain handoff then passes through $V$. This lets a later $\ConvexSetUp$, $\CatZeroMetricUp$, or $\HadamardSpaceUp$ consumer read the finite Busemann convexity row as local certificate data, without converting it into a stronger global comparison theorem.

Inspecting the carrier gives the exclusion. There is no row for a full CAT(0) triangle ledger, global Hadamard completeness, selected geodesic family, path quotient, projection selector, or ambient completeness proof, so any route requiring one of those objects lies outside the accepted packet.
\end{proof}
