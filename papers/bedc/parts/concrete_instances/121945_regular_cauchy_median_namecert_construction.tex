\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\RegularCauchyMedianUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-regular-cauchy-median-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges constructive analysis, the $\RegularCauchyMedianUp$ packet records the median of three regular Cauchy real-facing names by finite comparison windows. It reads the regular rational rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the finite schedules of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the dyadic comparison budgets of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the sibling minimum and maximum routes of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regularcauchymin-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regularcauchymax-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names the middle window by displayed finite data; it does not import trichotomy, quotient real equality, selected representatives, countable choice, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[Regular Cauchy median carrier]
\label{def:regular-cauchy-median-carrier}
A $\RegularCauchyMedianUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
M=(A,B,C,W_A,W_B,W_C,D,m_{AB},j_{AB},u,v,R,E,H,K,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $A,B,C$ are the three source $\RegSeqRatUp$ regular rational Cauchy name rows.
\item $W_A,W_B,W_C$ are the $\StreamNameUp$ schedules used to inspect the three source names on the same finite precision request.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ comparison-budget row, recording the endpoint tolerances, overlap slack, and dyadic cells consumed by the displayed pairwise comparisons.
\item $m_{AB}$ is the $\RegularCauchyMinUp$ sibling route for the lower row of $A$ and $B$ over the inspected windows.
\item $j_{AB}$ is the $\RegularCauchyMaxUp$ sibling route for the upper row of $A$ and $B$ over the same inspected windows.
\item $u$ is the $\RegularCauchyMaxUp$ row for the displayed maximum of $m_{AB}$ and $C$.
\item $v$ is the $\RegularCauchyMinUp$ row for the displayed minimum of $u$ and $j_{AB}$; it is the median row.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row exported by $v$.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal reached only after the readback row $R$ has been exposed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the three source rows, schedules, dyadic budget, sibling min and max rows, median row, readback, seal, replay, provenance, and local naming.
\item $K$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a median request through $W_A,W_B,W_C,D,m_{AB},j_{AB},u,v,R,E,H,P,N$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\RegularCauchyMedianUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\RegularCauchyMinUp$, $\RegularCauchyMaxUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\RegularCauchyMedianUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through these rows. It has no coordinate for host trichotomy, quotient stream equality, selected limits, countable-choice schedules, ambient $\RealUp$ completeness, or an unbounded comparison procedure.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Regular Cauchy median NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:regular-cauchy-median-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\RegularCauchyMedianUp$ carrier
$$
M=(A,B,C,W_A,W_B,W_C,D,m_{AB},j_{AB},u,v,R,E,H,K,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\RegularCauchyMedianUp}$ surface consists exactly of three-source $\RegSeqRatUp$ admission, synchronized $\StreamNameUp$ window exposure, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ comparison-budget accounting, the displayed $\RegularCauchyMinUp$ and $\RegularCauchyMaxUp$ sibling routes, median readback, terminal $\RealUp$ sealing, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, local naming, and non-escape from those rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Carrier admission is projection from \autoref{def:regular-cauchy-median-carrier}. The source rows are $A,B,C$, the finite observation schedules are $W_A,W_B,W_C$, and the common comparison budget is $D$.

The operation rows are the sibling routes already named in the carrier. The rows $m_{AB}$ and $j_{AB}$ expose the lower and upper pairwise bounds for $A$ and $B$; the row $u$ then places $C$ against the lower bound, and the row $v$ places the result against the upper bound. Thus the median read is assembled from min and max rows over the same finite windows.

The readback and seal rows add no comparison principle. The row $R$ is downstream of $v$, and the terminal seal $E$ is downstream of $R$.

Finally $H,K,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name this displayed route. Since the carrier contains no other coordinate, the local NameCert exports no host trichotomy, quotient stream equality, selected representative, countable-choice schedule, ambient completeness, or unbounded comparison datum.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Regular Cauchy median finite-window route]
\label{thm:regular-cauchy-median-finite-window-route}
Every $\RegSeqRatUp$ or $\RealUp$ consumer of an accepted $\RegularCauchyMedianUp$ carrier reaches the median row only through the finite route
$$
(A,B,C,W_A,W_B,W_C,D)
\longrightarrow
(m_{AB},j_{AB})
\longrightarrow
(u,v)
\longrightarrow
R
\longrightarrow
E .
$$
The route is a displayed finite-window median operation over regular Cauchy names. It is not a trichotomy on completed reals, not a quotient-real equality principle, not a selected limit, and not an ambient completeness argument.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with a consumer of the accepted carrier. By \autoref{thm:regular-cauchy-median-namecert-obligations}, it must first expose the three regular rational sources, the three finite stream schedules, and the common dyadic comparison budget.

Next read the sibling operation rows. The first pair of rows, $m_{AB}$ and $j_{AB}$, is supplied by the finite $\RegularCauchyMinUp$ and $\RegularCauchyMaxUp$ chapters over $A$ and $B$. The second pair, $u$ and $v$, uses the same sibling routes to compare $C$ with the displayed lower and upper rows.

The median row is therefore $v$, and the readback row $R$ is downstream of that displayed operation packet. The terminal $\RealUp$ seal $E$ is reached only after $R$ has been exposed.

Transport through $H$ and replay through $K$ preserve exactly the same finite route. The provenance and local naming rows source the route but do not add a host comparison, quotient stream, selected representative, countable choice, or completeness coordinate.
\end{proof}
