\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\DyadicIntervalSubdivisionUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-dyadic-interval-subdivision-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges, the $\DyadicIntervalSubdivisionUp$ packet records the finite dyadic cell subdivision used by compact interval arguments before any finite-cover or uniform-modulus consumer can read a mesh. It refines the interval-net discipline of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-closedboundedintervalnet-namecert}, the cover-refinement carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadic-cover-refinement-namecert}, the compact metric source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}, and the tolerance-only handoff of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-uniformmodulus-namecert}. The packet is a concrete dyadic subdivision surface for compact-uniform-continuity arguments, not an open-cover compactness theorem, a selected subcover, quotient real equality, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[Dyadic interval subdivision carrier]
\label{def:dyadic-interval-subdivision-carrier}
A $\DyadicIntervalSubdivisionUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
D=(I,E,Q,G,V,W,M,U,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $I$ is the $\ClosedBoundedIntervalNetUp$ source row exposing the closed interval endpoints and the finite interval-net boundary.
\item $E$ is the endpoint-exactness row, tying every dyadic cell endpoint back to the same lower and upper interval endpoints.
\item $Q$ is the dyadic scale row, sourced through $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, at which the interval is subdivided.
\item $G$ is the ordered dyadic grid row listing the cell endpoints at scale $Q$.
\item $V$ is the cell-coverage row witnessing that every interval read is routed through one displayed dyadic cell or a shared endpoint boundary.
\item $W$ is the common-refinement row comparing the displayed grid with a later $\DyadicCoverRefinementUp$ cover row.
\item $M$ is the mesh-bound row recording the dyadic diameter bound exported to compact finite-cover consumers.
\item $U$ is the $\UniformModulusUp$ handoff row, available only after the grid, coverage, common-refinement, and mesh rows have been consumed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for interval-net source, endpoint exactness, dyadic scale, grid, coverage, common-refinement, mesh, uniform-modulus handoff, replay, provenance, and local naming.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from interval endpoints to dyadic grid rows, from grid rows to coverage rows, from coverage to common refinement, and from mesh bounds to the $\UniformModulusUp$ handoff.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\DyadicIntervalSubdivisionUp$, $\DyadicCoverRefinementUp$, $\ClosedBoundedIntervalNetUp$, $\CompactMetricUp$, $\UniformModulusUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\DyadicIntervalSubdivisionUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $I,E,Q,G,V,W,M,U,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for an open-cover subcover selector, quotient real equality, a hidden grid chooser, arbitrary compactness, or ambient metric completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Dyadic interval subdivision NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:dyadic-interval-subdivision-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\DyadicIntervalSubdivisionUp$ carrier $D=(I,E,Q,G,V,W,M,U,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\DyadicIntervalSubdivisionUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission, interval-net source exposure, endpoint exactness, dyadic scale classification, ordered grid coverage, cell-coverage exactness, common-refinement compatibility with $\DyadicCoverRefinementUp$, mesh-bound export, $\UniformModulusUp$ handoff, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming. The finite route is
$$
\ClosedBoundedIntervalNetUp
\longmapsto
\DyadicIntervalSubdivisionUp
\longmapsto
\DyadicCoverRefinementUp
\longmapsto
\UniformModulusUp ,
$$
with the $\CompactMetricUp$ source row read only through the displayed finite interval-net and mesh data.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Carrier admission is projection from \autoref{def:dyadic-interval-subdivision-carrier}. The source interval row $I$, endpoint exactness row $E$, dyadic scale row $Q$, ordered grid row $G$, coverage row $V$, common-refinement row $W$, mesh row $M$, and handoff row $U$ are the only mathematical rows available.

Read $I,E,Q,G,V$ first. These rows display the closed interval endpoints, fix the dyadic scale, list the finite grid, and route every interval containment request through a displayed cell or shared boundary.

Next consume $W,M,U$. The common-refinement row compares the dyadic grid with the $\DyadicCoverRefinementUp$ cover route, the mesh row exports the dyadic diameter bound to compact finite-cover consumers, and the $\UniformModulusUp$ row is visible only after those finite checks.

Finally replay $H,C,P,N$. Transport, continuation replay, provenance, and local naming preserve the same subdivision packet, so no open-cover selector, hidden grid choice, quotient real equality, arbitrary compactness principle, or ambient completeness row can enter the certificate.
\end{proof}
