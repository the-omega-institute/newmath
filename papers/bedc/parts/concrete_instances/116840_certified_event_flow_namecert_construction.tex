\begin{definition}[CertifiedEventFlow carrier]
\label{def:certified-event-flow-carrier}
A $\CertifiedEventFlowUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
F=(S,B,T,E,L,A,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $S$ is the source-event row naming the finite event before channel encoding.
\item $B$ is the legal channel body row, recording the finite encoded body and its body-safety classifier.
\item $T$ is the terminator row, displaying the local channel endpoint token.
\item $E$ is the endpoint row, recording the first legal endpoint read and the decoded source event.
\item $L$ is the local termination row, separating channel-level endpoint detection from certificate-level halting.
\item $A$ is the audit handoff row, exposing the event-flow packet to GroundCompiler audit and hendeca event-flow consumers only through displayed rows.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for source event, channel body, terminator, endpoint, local termination, audit handoff, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay for endpoint-facing and audit-facing consumers.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\CertifiedEventFlowUp$, $\ChannelTerminationHaltingBoundaryUp$, $\ChannelTerminationBudgetCertificateUp$, $\GroundCompilerEventFlowAuditUp$, $\BHistHendecaEventFlowNameCertUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and local naming rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\CertifiedEventFlowUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for hidden source text, an unrestricted evaluator, a global halting oracle, compiler correctness, carried metadata, or a host channel parser.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[CertifiedEventFlow NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:certified-event-flow-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\CertifiedEventFlowUp$ carrier
$$
F=(S,B,T,E,L,A,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\CertifiedEventFlowUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for $F$, source-event exposure through $S$, legal channel-body exposure through $B$, terminator exposure through $T$, endpoint exposure through $E$, local termination separation through $L$, audit handoff through $A$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport through $H$, displayed $\Cont$ replay through $C$, $\Pkg$ provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier. The data rows $S,B,T,E,L,A$ are the complete source/channel endpoint inventory, and the structural rows $H,C,P,N$ only transport, replay, source, and name those rows.

Read the source and channel side first. The source event $S$ is separated from the legal channel body $B$, the terminator $T$, and the endpoint read $E$ before a local termination row can be exported.

Next apply the channel-termination boundary and budget siblings. They keep the endpoint row local to the channel and prevent it from becoming a certificate-level halting classifier.

Finally read the audit handoff through the GroundCompiler event-flow audit and hendeca event-flow neighbours. The handoff row $A$ exposes the packet as finite event-flow data, while no coordinate supplies hidden source text, unrestricted evaluation, global halting, compiler correctness, carried metadata, or a host parser.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[CertifiedEventFlow channel-termination handoff]
\label{thm:certified-event-flow-channel-termination-handoff}
Every endpoint-facing consumer of an accepted $\CertifiedEventFlowUp$ carrier must read
$$
S,B,T,E \longmapsto L,A,H,C,P,N
$$
before it treats the event as locally terminated. The row $E$ is certified only as the endpoint of the legal channel body $B$ at terminator $T$, and the row $L$ records that this local endpoint is not a global halting classifier for arbitrary certificate continuations.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the source/channel separation. The source event $S$ and legal body $B$ are distinct rows, so a consumer cannot identify channel bytes with source identity before the endpoint has been decoded.

Open the terminator and endpoint rows. The terminator $T$ marks the local channel endpoint, and $E$ records the first legal endpoint read together with the decoded source event.

Now attach the local termination row $L$. It reads the endpoint as local channel termination while preserving the refusal boundary against certificate-level halting.

The audit, transport, replay, provenance, and naming rows $A,H,C,P,N$ carry that same finite route to downstream consumers. Since the packet contains no global halting oracle or host parser, the handoff exports source/channel endpoint separation and nothing stronger.
\end{proof}
