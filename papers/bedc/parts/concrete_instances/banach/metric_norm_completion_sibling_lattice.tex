\begin{theorem}[Banach metric-norm completion sibling lattice]
\label{thm:banach-metric-norm-completion-sibling-lattice}
Every public $\BanachUp$ read factors through the displayed $\NormUp$,
$\MetricUp$, $\CompleteMetricUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, and
$\RealUp$ rows before a bounded-operator, fixed-point, or
functional-analysis consumer inspects it. The available route is the
norm-induced metric over the Banach carrier, the regular Cauchy stream and
modulus surface, the complete-metric limit witness, and the RealUp distance
readback recorded by \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-norm-namecert},
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert},
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-completemetric-namecert},
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert},
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, and
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Read the Banach carrier through the norm-completion certificate. The files
\texttt{norm\_completion\_certificate} and
\texttt{complete\_metric\_namecert\_interface} identify the metric coordinate
as the $\NormUp$-induced RealUp distance and identify accepted regular Cauchy
inputs as $\StreamNameUp$ observations equipped with displayed modulus and
late-distance ledgers.

Next read the limit surface certificate. It exposes the same Banach carrier,
stream observations, modulus history, carried limit row, norm-distance ledger,
and consumed $\CompleteMetricUp$ witness before any downstream consumer receives
a limit or bounded-operator row.

Thus a public Banach read has no independent metric island. Its metric,
norm, completion, RegSeqRat, StreamName, and RealUp coordinates are the
visible sibling lattice already displayed by the three child files, and all
bounded-operator or fixed-point consumers can inspect only that finite route.
\end{proof}
