It consumes the matrix carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-matrix-namecert}, the inner-product rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-innerproduct-namecert}, the Hermitian surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-hermitian-matrix-namecert}, the normal-operator routing of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-normal-operator-namecert}, and the finite gap window of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finite-dimensional-spectral-gap-namecert}.

\begin{definition}[CourantFischerMinimax carrier]
\label{def:courant-fischer-minimax-carrier}
A $\CourantFischerMinimaxUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
K=(M,O,I,W,R,L,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $M$ is the finite $\MatrixUp$ row, recording the coordinate table used by the spectral read.
\item $O$ is the $\NormalOperatorUp$ or $\HermitianMatrixUp$ operator row attached to the same matrix table.
\item $I$ is the $\InnerProductUp$ row through which vector lengths and orthogonality tests are read.
\item $W$ is the finite subspace-window row, listing the inspected dimensions, inclusions, and complement windows.
\item $R$ is the Rayleigh quotient ledger, recording numerator and denominator reads through $M,O,I,W$.
\item $L$ is the min-max comparison row, displaying the finite lower-window and upper-window comparisons for the same dimension index.
\item $E$ is the extremal ledger, recording which displayed subspace windows witness the lower and upper sides of the comparison.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the matrix, operator, inner-product, subspace-window, Rayleigh, comparison, extremal, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a spectral min-max request through $M,O,I,W,R,L,E,H,P,N$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\CourantFischerMinimaxUp$, $\MatrixUp$, $\InnerProductUp$, $\HermitianMatrixUp$, $\NormalOperatorUp$, $\FiniteDimensionalSpectralGapUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\CourantFischerMinimaxUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through these rows. It has no coordinate for a host eigenvector choice, a spectral-completeness theorem, an ambient quotient of subspaces, selected extrema outside $E$, or a hidden diagonalization procedure.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[CourantFischerMinimax NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:courant-fischer-minimax-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\CourantFischerMinimaxUp$ carrier
$$
K=(M,O,I,W,R,L,E,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\CourantFischerMinimaxUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission, Rayleigh quotient readback, finite subspace-window stability, min-max ledger exactness, sibling transport through $\MatrixUp$, $\InnerProductUp$, $\HermitianMatrixUp$, $\NormalOperatorUp$, and $\FiniteDimensionalSpectralGapUp$, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming. A spectral min-max read is admitted only after the same matrix, operator, inner-product, subspace-window, Rayleigh, comparison, and extremal rows are visible in the packet.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:courant-fischer-minimax-carrier}. The matrix row $M$, operator row $O$, and inner-product row $I$ supply the finite algebraic surface on which Rayleigh reads can be formed.

The subspace-window row $W$ fixes the finite inspection domain. The Rayleigh ledger $R$ is read only through $M,O,I,W$, so every quotient claim remains tied to the displayed matrix and inner-product coordinates.

The min-max comparison row $L$ records the lower-window and upper-window comparisons for the same finite dimension index. The extremal ledger $E$ names the displayed windows that witness the two sides, without selecting host eigenvectors.

Finally, componentwise transport by $H$ preserves the sibling rows, while $C,P,N$ replay, source, and name the same packet. Since the carrier has no field for spectral completeness, diagonalization, quotient subspace equality, or external extrema, these rows exhaust the local NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}
