\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\ClosedIntervalFiniteNetUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-closed-interval-finite-net-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges on finite epsilon-net data for compact intervals, the $\ClosedIntervalFiniteNetUp$ packet records the finite dyadic mesh surface by which a displayed closed real interval can be consumed by total-bounded and compact-metric routes. It sits after the dyadic arithmetic source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the dyadic cell surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicinterval-namecert}, the located-real comparison row of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-locatedreal-namecert}, the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, the finite epsilon-net interface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finite-epsilon-net-namecert}, and the total-bounded consumer surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-totallybounded-namecert}. The packet names explicit endpoint, mesh, coverage, transport, replay, provenance, and local NameCert data; it does not assert open-cover compactness or an ambient Heine--Borel theorem.

\begin{definition}[Closed interval finite-net carrier]
\label{def:closed-interval-finite-net-carrier}
A $\ClosedIntervalFiniteNetUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
K=(E,M,L,F,W,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $E$ is the endpoint row, carrying lower and upper closed-interval endpoint histories together with the finite comparison ledger that places the lower endpoint before the upper endpoint.
\item $M$ is the dyadic mesh row, a finite $\DyadicIntervalUp$ subdivision schedule read through $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ arithmetic and a positive tolerance request.
\item $L$ is the located $\RealUp$ seal row, displaying the located-real endpoint reads and the terminal real-name seals used by interval consumers.
\item $F$ is the finite-net list row, a closed finite list of mesh centers admitted between the endpoint rows and tagged by the mesh scale.
\item $W$ is the coverage witness row, recording for each displayed interval point the inspected center row and the dyadic bound showing that the point is covered at the requested tolerance.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for endpoint rows, dyadic mesh rows, located real seals, finite net lists, coverage witnesses, replay rows, provenance rows, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from an interval finite-net request through $E,M,L,F,W,H$ before any total-bounded or compact-metric consumer reads the packet.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\ClosedIntervalFiniteNetUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\DyadicIntervalUp$, $\LocatedRealUp$, $\RealUp$, $\FiniteEpsilonNetUp$, $\TotallyBoundedUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\ClosedIntervalFiniteNetUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $E,M,L,F,W,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for arbitrary open covers, selected finite subcovers, a host Heine--Borel principle, quotient interval equality, countable choice, ambient $\RealUp$ completeness, Lean verification, or bridge checking.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Closed interval finite-net obligation surface]
\label{thm:closed-interval-finite-net-obligation-surface}
For every accepted $\ClosedIntervalFiniteNetUp$ carrier $K=(E,M,L,F,W,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\ClosedIntervalFiniteNetUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission, endpoint enclosure, finite-net coverage, mesh-refinement stability, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and ledger non-escape. A consumer may treat the packet as closed-interval finite-net data only after those rows have been displayed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:closed-interval-finite-net-carrier}. Carrier admission is the displayed packet $K$, endpoint enclosure is the row $E$, and the dyadic mesh is the row $M$.

The finite-net rows are $F,W$. The list row fixes the admitted mesh centers, while the coverage row records the inspected center and dyadic bound for each displayed interval point. Mesh-refinement stability is read through the same endpoint and mesh rows, so a refined request keeps the coverage ledger inside $E,M,F,W$.

The remaining rows $H,C,P,N$ supply transport, replay, provenance, and local naming for that same finite packet. Since the classifier has no coordinate outside $E,M,L,F,W,H,C,P,N$, no ledger read can escape to arbitrary open covers, selected subcovers, host interval equality, countable choice, or ambient real completeness.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Closed interval finite-net total-bounded handoff]
\label{thm:closed-interval-finite-net-total-bounded-handoff}
Every $\TotallyBoundedUp$ or compact-metric consumer that uses interval compactness from an accepted $\ClosedIntervalFiniteNetUp$ packet must first read the finite dyadic net row $F$ and the located endpoint rows $E,L$. The consumer may then inspect coverage witnesses, mesh refinement, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming, but it receives no open-cover compactness theorem, selected cover, countable-choice schedule, host Heine--Borel principle, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness witness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $K=(E,M,L,F,W,H,C,P,N)$ be accepted. The interval boundary is visible only through the endpoint row $E$ and the located $\RealUp$ seal row $L$, while the finite dyadic net is visible only through $M,F$.

Apply \autoref{thm:closed-interval-finite-net-obligation-surface}. It forces any total-bounded or compact-metric consumer to inspect carrier admission, endpoint enclosure, finite-net coverage, mesh-refinement stability, transport, replay, provenance, and ledger non-escape before treating the packet as interval finite-net data.

Thus the handoff to $\TotallyBoundedUp$ and compact-metric consumers is the displayed finite route $E,M,L,F,W,H,C,P,N$. The excluded open-cover, selected-cover, choice, host Heine--Borel, quotient-interval, and ambient-completeness objects are not carrier fields, so none can be exported by the handoff.
\end{proof}
