$$

\begin{enumerate}

\item $J$ is the mirror-involution row recording the displayed map $s\mapsto 1-\overline{s}$ and its finite replay data.
\item $Z$ is the orbit-zero row naming the two possible zero reads $Z_{\zeta}(s)$ and $Z_{\zeta}(J(s))$.
\item $F$ is the fixedness boundary row, separating fixed mirror rows from non-fixed mirror rows without deciding the equality globally.
\item $A$ is the apart non-fixed witness row when a positive rational separation is displayed.

\begin{theorem}[RH mirror-orbit target NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:rh-mirror-orbit-target-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\RHMirrorOrbitTargetUp$ carrier
$$

$$
the local $\NameCert_{\RHMirrorOrbitTargetUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission, critical-strip candidate exposure, mirror-involution replay, orbit-zero exposure, fixedness-boundary retention, apart non-fixed witness exposure when present, weak-exclusion obstruction retention, componentwise transport, displayed replay, provenance, and local naming.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Carrier admission projects $S,J,Z,F,A,W,H,C,P,N$. The critical-strip candidate row $S$ and mirror row $J$ are the only rows from which an orbit target can be read.

The orbit-zero row $Z$ keeps the two zero reads visible as a finite disjoint target surface. The fixedness boundary row $F$ records whether the consumer is reading a fixed row, a non-fixed row, or only a refutational boundary.

The apart row $A$ is available only when a positive rational separation is displayed, and the obstruction row $W$ records the gap between weak exclusion and fixed-section evidence. Transport, replay, provenance, and local naming preserve these rows without adding a hidden RH conclusion.

Since the carrier has no coordinate for an RH proof, global zero-free strip, classical decision of fixedness, quotient candidate equality, host complex-analysis oracle, or unrecorded functional-equation transport, the displayed rows exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[RH mirror-orbit fixedness boundary]
\label{thm:rh-mirror-orbit-target-fixedness-boundary}
Every accepted $\RHMirrorOrbitTargetUp$ consumer that reads an orbit-zero row must keep the finite route
$$
S \longmapsto J \longmapsto Z \longmapsto F,W
$$
visible before any apartness or fixed-section handoff is consumed. If the apart witness row $A$ is absent, the packet records a weak refutation boundary only; it does not inhabit the fixed-section target.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier. The critical-strip source is $S$, the mirror involution is $J$, and orbit-zero exposure is $Z$.

The fixedness boundary $F$ and obstruction row $W$ are downstream of $Z$. They record whether the consumer has a fixed-row route, a non-fixed refutation route, or only a double-negation boundary.

The apartness row $A$ is a separate positive witness row. Without it, the route has no coordinate for located equality transport, so it cannot be read as fixed-section evidence. The structural rows preserve this separation.
\end{proof}
