\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\LocatedRationalIntervalUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-located-rational-interval-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges' constructive treatment of located intervals and regular rational approximation, the $\LocatedRationalIntervalUp$ packet records the finite interval-location object used on the L10 real-completion path. It sits between the dyadic tolerance core of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the rational interval carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-rationalinterval-namecert}, the located endpoint carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-locatedinterval-namecert}, the stream-window interface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the Real seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, and the interval-domain consumer of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-intervaldomain-namecert}. The chapter names only a finite location certificate for rational intervals; it is not a quotient-cut construction, endpoint selector, trichotomy principle, host real equality, or ambient completeness theorem.

\begin{definition}[LocatedRationalInterval carrier]
\label{def:located-rational-interval-carrier}
A $\LocatedRationalIntervalUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
I=(R,L,D,W,Q,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following visible rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $R$ is the $\RationalIntervalUp$ row carrying rational endpoint order, containment, and rational width data.
\item $L$ is the located-endpoint row inherited from $\LocatedIntervalUp$, binding each endpoint read to a displayed location witness.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance row used to compare rational widths and endpoint windows.
\item $W$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ window row through which finite endpoint requests are read.
\item $Q$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row serving the same endpoint windows with regular rational observations.
\item $E$ is the $\RealUp$ seal row reached only after $R,L,D,W,Q$ have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the rational interval, located endpoints, dyadic tolerance, stream window, regular readback, and Real seal rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay for the same row order.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\LocatedRationalIntervalUp$, $\RationalIntervalUp$, $\LocatedIntervalUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\LocatedRationalIntervalUp}$ evidence row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares packets only by these displayed finite rows and their transports. No carrier coordinate supplies quotient cuts, selected endpoints, trichotomy, host real equality, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[LocatedRationalInterval NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:located-rational-interval-namecert-obligations}
For an accepted $\LocatedRationalIntervalUp$ carrier $I=(R,L,D,W,Q,E,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\LocatedRationalIntervalUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission, located-endpoint stability, dyadic tolerance stability, stream-window readback, Real-seal non-escape, componentwise transport, displayed replay, provenance, and local naming. Every interval-location read must display the rational interval row, located endpoints, dyadic tolerance, $\StreamNameUp$ window, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback, $\RealUp$ seal, $\hsame$ transport, $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local $\NameCert$ row before a completion-facing consumer can inspect the packet.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Carrier admission is projection from \autoref{def:located-rational-interval-carrier}. The tuple lists $R,L,D,W,Q,E,H,C,P,N$ as the complete visible surface of the packet.

Located-endpoint stability reads $R,L,H,C$. The rational interval row supplies endpoint order and containment, while the located endpoint row supplies the displayed location witnesses transported with the same interval read.

Dyadic tolerance stability and stream-window readback read $D,W,Q$. The dyadic row supplies the finite tolerance budget for endpoint windows, the stream row names the finite requests, and the regular rational row supplies the readbacks for those same requests.

The Real-seal clause follows because $E$ is downstream of $R,L,D,W,Q$ and the structural rows. Since the carrier has no quotient-cut, endpoint-selector, trichotomy, host-equality, or completeness coordinate, none of those principles is exported by the local NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[LocatedRationalInterval Real seal handoff]
\label{thm:located-rational-interval-real-seal-handoff}
Let $I=(R,L,D,W,Q,E,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\LocatedRationalIntervalUp$ carrier. Any $\IntervalDomainUp$ or $\RealUp$ completion-facing consumer of the seal row $E$ must first read the finite route
$$
\RationalIntervalUp,\LocatedIntervalUp,\DyadicRatCoreUp,\StreamNameUp,\RegSeqRatUp,\RealUp,\BHist,\hsame,\Cont,\Pkg,\NameCert.
$$
The handoff is a displayed interval-location route over rational endpoints and regular readbacks; it exports no quotient cuts, selected endpoints, trichotomy, host real equality, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:located-rational-interval-namecert-obligations}. They force a completion-facing read to expose $R,L,D,W,Q,E,H,C,P,N$ before the seal can be consumed.

Read $R$ and $L$ through the sibling rational and located interval routes. The rational interval row supplies endpoint order and width data, while the located interval row supplies the finite endpoint-location witnesses.

Read $D,W,Q$ next. The dyadic tolerance row supports the displayed endpoint windows, the $\StreamNameUp$ row names those requests, and the $\RegSeqRatUp$ row gives the regular rational readback used by the downstream completion route.

Only then can the consumer inspect $E$. The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same finite route, so the handoff remains available to $\IntervalDomainUp$ and $\RealUp$ without adding quotient cuts, selected endpoints, trichotomy, host real equality, or ambient completeness.
\end{proof}
