Following Bishop and Bridges, Constructive Analysis, the $\BishopCauchyCompletionEquivalenceUp$ packet names the finite equivalence surface between Bishop regular Cauchy completion data and the sealed $\RealUp$ completion route. It sits over the dyadic tolerance ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the finite stream windows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational handoff of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the admission criterion of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cauchycompletioncriterion-namecert}, and the terminal real seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet records an equivalence of displayed NameCert routes, not a quotient real construction, a selected limit, countable choice, or completeness as a primitive.

\begin{definition}[Bishop Cauchy completion equivalence carrier]
\label{def:bishop-cauchy-completion-equivalence-carrier}
A $\BishopCauchyCompletionEquivalenceUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
E=(D,S,R,K,L,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance ledger used by both directions of the comparison.
\item $S$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ finite-window schedule through which Cauchy observations are read.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ regular rational row exposed by those windows.
\item $K$ is the $\CauchyCompletionCriterionUp$ admission row that certifies the displayed Cauchy route before any real seal is read.
\item $L$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal row reached from the same $D,S,R,K$ surface.
\item $H$ stores componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the dyadic ledger, stream schedule, regular rational row, criterion row, real seal, replay, provenance, and local naming.
\item $C$ stores displayed $\Cont$ replay from Bishop regular Cauchy data to the criterion row and from the criterion row back to the same finite real-seal read.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\BishopCauchyCompletionEquivalenceUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\CauchyCompletionCriterionUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\BishopCauchyCompletionEquivalenceUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted carriers only through these nine rows. It contains no coordinate for quotient streams, selected host limits, countable choice, host real equality, or ambient completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Bishop Cauchy completion equivalence NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:bishop-cauchy-completion-equivalence-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\BishopCauchyCompletionEquivalenceUp$ carrier $E=(D,S,R,K,L,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\BishopCauchyCompletionEquivalenceUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission, dyadic tolerance admission, finite-window readback, regular rational handoff, completion-criterion admission, real-seal handoff, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:bishop-cauchy-completion-equivalence-carrier}. The rows $D,S,R,K,L,H,C,P,N$ are the only classifier-visible coordinates.

The Bishop regular Cauchy side is read through $D,S,R$. The dyadic ledger supplies the tolerance scale, the stream schedule supplies finite windows, and the regular rational row supplies the Cauchy handoff available to the criterion row.

The completion side is read through $K,L$. The criterion row admits the displayed Cauchy route, and the real seal row is exposed only after that admission has been consumed.

The remaining rows preserve and name the same route. Since no quotient stream, selected limit, countable-choice row, host equality, or completeness coordinate appears in the carrier, the listed obligations exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Bishop Cauchy completion equivalence route]
\label{thm:bishop-cauchy-completion-equivalence-route}
Every consumer of an accepted $\BishopCauchyCompletionEquivalenceUp$ carrier factors through the displayed route
$$
\DyadicRatCoreUp,\ \StreamNameUp,\ \RegSeqRatUp \longmapsto \CauchyCompletionCriterionUp \longmapsto \RealUp
$$
together with the stored $\hsame$ transport, $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local $\NameCert$ row. The route may be read from the Bishop regular Cauchy side toward the real seal or from the real seal back to the displayed criterion surface, but both reads use the same finite rows $D,S,R,K,L,H,C,P,N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with an accepted carrier $E=(D,S,R,K,L,H,C,P,N)$. The source side contains exactly the dyadic ledger $D$, stream schedule $S$, and regular rational row $R$.

Apply the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:bishop-cauchy-completion-equivalence-namecert-obligations}. They require the criterion row $K$ to be the admission point between the displayed regular Cauchy data and the real seal row $L$.

The replay row $C$ records both readings of the same finite route: from $D,S,R$ to $K,L$, and from a real-seal read back to the criterion surface that licensed it. The transport row $H$ and provenance row $P$ keep the route componentwise stable, while $N$ names the local certificate.

Thus a consumer cannot substitute a host quotient, a selected limit, or a second completion principle for the displayed rows. Both directions of the equivalence are reads of the same finite NameCert route.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Bishop Cauchy completion equivalence non-escape]
\label{thm:bishop-cauchy-completion-equivalence-non-escape}

The packet exports exactly the rows
$$

$$
It exports no quotient stream, selected host limit, countable-choice schedule, host real equality, ambient completeness, or primitive completion operator.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The carrier definition lists the full observation surface. The NameCert obligations bind each row to its displayed role: dyadic tolerance, finite windows, regular rational handoff, criterion admission, real seal, transport, replay, provenance, and naming.

The route theorem shows that both readings of the equivalence factor through the same finite surface. A Bishop regular Cauchy read reaches the seal only through $D,S,R,K,L$, and a real-seal read returns only to the same criterion route.

No further row remains from which to read quotient streams, selected limits, countable choice, host equality, ambient completeness, or a primitive completion operator. The displayed rows therefore exhaust the $\BishopCauchyCompletionEquivalenceUp$ packet.
\end{proof}
