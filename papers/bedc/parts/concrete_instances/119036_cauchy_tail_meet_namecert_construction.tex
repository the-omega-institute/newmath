Following Bishop and Bridges constructive analysis, the $\CauchyTailMeetUp$ packet records the finite common-tail witness used when two displayed Cauchy requests must be synchronized before a regular rational or real-name consumer reads them. It sits over the one-tail estimate route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regularcauchytailestimate-namecert}, the finite window discipline of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the dyadic tolerance meet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names a finite common threshold and its inspected windows; it is not a selected limit, quotient stream equality, countable-choice schedule, host real equality, or ambient completeness theorem for $\RealUp$.

\begin{definition}[Cauchy tail meet carrier]
\label{def:cauchy-tail-meet-carrier}
A $\CauchyTailMeetUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
T=(A_0,A_1,\tau,W_0,W_1,D,R_0,R_1,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $A_0$ and $A_1$ are the two admitted tail requests, each naming a finite request into the sibling $\RegularCauchyTailEstimateUp$ surface.
\item $\tau$ is the common threshold row at which both requests are simultaneously inspected.
\item $W_0$ and $W_1$ are the finite $\StreamNameUp$ windows read at and beyond $\tau$.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance meet, recording the common dyadic budget used by both tail windows.
\item $R_0$ and $R_1$ are the two $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback rows reached from $W_0,D$ and $W_1,D$.
\item $E$ is the $\RealUp$ seal boundary available only after the two requests, common threshold, finite windows, tolerance meet, and regular readbacks have been replayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the two requests, common threshold, windows, tolerance meet, readbacks, seal, continuation, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a paired tail request through $\tau,W_0,W_1,D,R_0,R_1$ and then $E$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\CauchyTailMeetUp$, $\RegularCauchyTailEstimateUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\CauchyTailMeetUp}$ row naming exactly this finite common-tail packet.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted carriers only through these rows. It has no coordinate for a selected infinite tail, quotient stream equality, countable choice, host real equality, trichotomy, or ambient completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Cauchy tail meet NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:cauchy-tail-meet-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\CauchyTailMeetUp$ carrier
$$
T=(A_0,A_1,\tau,W_0,W_1,D,R_0,R_1,E,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\CauchyTailMeetUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission, two tail-request reads, common-threshold visibility, finite $\StreamNameUp$ window admission, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance meet, two $\RegSeqRatUp$ readbacks, $\RealUp$ seal handoff, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming. Every common-tail consumer must pass through
$$
A_0,A_1,\tau,W_0,W_1,D,R_0,R_1,E,H,C,P,N
$$
and through no selected limit, quotient stream equality, countable-choice schedule, host real equality, trichotomy, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness row.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:cauchy-tail-meet-carrier}. The rows $A_0,A_1$ expose the two finite tail requests, and the row $\tau$ is the single common threshold at which both requests are inspected.

Read the analytic rows after the threshold. The windows $W_0,W_1$ give the finite $\StreamNameUp$ observations, $D$ is the shared $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance meet, and $R_0,R_1$ are the only regular rational readbacks downstream of those windows.

Finally read the seal and structural rows. The row $E$ is available only after the displayed request, threshold, window, tolerance, and readback rows have been replayed, while $H,C,P,N$ preserve, replay, source, and name the same finite route.

Since no carrier coordinate stores a selected infinite tail, quotient stream equality, countable-choice schedule, host real equality, trichotomy, or ambient completeness witness, none of those objects can appear as a local NameCert obligation.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Cauchy tail meet finite induction]
\label{thm:cauchy-tail-meet-finite-induction}
Every predicate $Q$ on accepted $\CauchyTailMeetUp$ packets is established by finite induction when the following displayed cases are stable under $\hsame$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the two tail requests $A_0,A_1$ have been admitted;
\item the common threshold $\tau$ has been read;
\item the two finite windows $W_0,W_1$ have been inspected;
\item the shared dyadic meet $D$ and regular readbacks $R_0,R_1$ have been replayed;
\item the real seal $E$, continuation row $C$, provenance row $P$, and local name $N$ have been consumed.
\end{enumerate}
Thus common-tail arguments over $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, and $\RealUp$ use only finite rows of $T$ and do not appeal to a choice-selected threshold family.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induct over the displayed rows of the carrier. The base stage admits $A_0,A_1$ and exposes no threshold-dependent data.

The threshold stage adds the single row $\tau$. Since both requests read the same threshold row, the induction context has one common finite index rather than two independently chosen tails.

The window and readback stages add $W_0,W_1,D,R_0,R_1$ in the displayed order. Stability under the componentwise $\hsame$ rows transports each finite observation and dyadic meet without changing the route.

The final stage reads $E,C,P,N$. The seal, continuation, provenance, and local name consume the accumulated finite route and introduce no further threshold selector, so the predicate $Q$ is obtained by the stated finite induction.
\end{proof}
