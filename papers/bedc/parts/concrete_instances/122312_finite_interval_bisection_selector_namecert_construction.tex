\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\FiniteIntervalBisectionSelectorUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-finite-interval-bisection-selector-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges on interval bisection and regular Cauchy construction, the $\FiniteIntervalBisectionSelectorUp$ packet records the finite selector used when an L10 Real or Completion route must choose a displayed half of a finite interval cell. It sits after the endpoint-order lattice of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finite-interval-lattice-namecert}, the dyadic interval cells of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicinterval-namecert}, the stream schedules of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The selector supplies a finite bisection handoff; it is not an open-cover compactness principle, a quotient real equality row, an ambient completeness theorem, or a choice function over all intervals.

\begin{definition}[Finite interval bisection selector carrier]
\label{def:finite-interval-bisection-selector}
A $\FiniteIntervalBisectionSelectorUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
F=(D,I,B,S,Q,R,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ endpoint-order row naming the lower endpoint, midpoint, and upper endpoint comparisons used by the bisection.
\item $I$ is the $\DyadicIntervalUp$ cell row naming the displayed parent interval and its two finite half-cells.
\item $B$ is the selected-half row, recording whether the accepted continuation consumes the left half, the right half, or a refusal row when the half predicate is not displayed.
\item $S$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ schedule row for the finite endpoint and midpoint observations used by the selector.
\item $Q$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row for the rational observations attached to the selected half.
\item $R$ is the $\RealUp$ seal row exposed only after $D,I,B,S,Q$ have remained visible.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for endpoint order, interval cells, selected-half data, stream windows, readback, Real sealing, replay, provenance, and local naming.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a parent interval request to the selected half and then to the regular-rational and Real-facing rows.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\FiniteIntervalBisectionSelectorUp$, $\FiniteIntervalLatticeUp$, $\DyadicIntervalUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\FiniteIntervalBisectionSelectorUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for a global interval choice function, a selected limit point, quotient equality of real names, open-cover compactness, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Finite interval selected-half exactness]
\label{thm:finite-interval-bisection-selected-half-exactness}
Let $F=(D,I,B,S,Q,R,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\FiniteIntervalBisectionSelectorUp$ carrier. Every selected-half read from $B$ must expose the parent interval, midpoint split, and chosen half through the dyadic endpoint-order row $D$ and the displayed cell row $I$ before it can be consumed by a StreamName, RegSeqRat, or Real-facing continuation. The packet therefore selects only one of the two listed half-cells of $I$ and exports no hidden representative, quotient real equality, open-cover compactness, or ambient completeness principle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:finite-interval-bisection-selector}. The rows $D$ and $I$ display the lower endpoint, midpoint, upper endpoint, parent interval, and the two finite half-cells before the selector row is read.

Use the endpoint-order and Real-completion route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finite-interval-lattice-namecert}. That route already forces interval consumers to keep dyadic endpoint evidence and finite cell membership visible before analytic handoff.

The selected-half row $B$ is then interpreted only relative to those displayed endpoint and cell rows. If the requested half is absent, the refusal row remains inside $B$; if it is present, the chosen half is one of the two cells listed by $I$.

Finally $S,Q,R,H,C,P,N$ preserve the same displayed route by stream scheduling, regular rational readback, Real sealing, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming. None of those rows introduces a hidden representative, quotient real equality, open-cover compactness, or ambient completeness coordinate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite interval bisection refinement induction]
\label{thm:finite-interval-bisection-refinement-induction}
Let $F=(D,I,B,S,Q,R,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\FiniteIntervalBisectionSelectorUp$ carrier. For every displayed finite bisection depth, iterating the selector rebuilds a finite chain of dyadic half-cells whose endpoint comparisons remain in $D$, whose interval cells remain in $I$, whose selected-half rows remain in $B$, and whose analytic handoff remains the route
$$
S,Q,R,H,C,P,N .
$$
Thus refinement induction supplies only a finite chain of displayed bisections and cannot introduce an infinite nested interval object, selected limit point, countable choice schedule, quotient real equality, or ambient completion theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induct on the displayed bisection depth. At depth zero the chain is the parent cell named by $I$, with endpoint order and analytic handoff already recorded by the carrier.

For the successor step, apply selected-half exactness, \autoref{thm:finite-interval-bisection-selected-half-exactness}. The next half-cell is one of the two displayed halves of the current cell, and its lower endpoint, midpoint, and upper endpoint comparisons are still read through $D$.

The finite interval lattice refinement induction of \autoref{thm:finite-interval-lattice-refinement-induction} supplies the table-level refinement discipline for the surrounding cell lattice. The selector adds only the row recording which displayed half is consumed at the current step.

Finally the stream schedule, regular rational readback, Real seal, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows remain $S,Q,R,H,C,P,N$ at every finite stage. The induction therefore builds a finite displayed chain and never supplies an infinite nested object, selected limit point, countable choice schedule, quotient real equality, or ambient completion theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite interval bisection RegSeqRat handoff]
\label{thm:finite-interval-bisection-regseqrat-handoff}
Every RegSeqRat-facing consumer of an accepted $\FiniteIntervalBisectionSelectorUp$ carrier factors through the displayed route
$$
\FiniteIntervalLatticeUp,\ \DyadicIntervalUp,\ \DyadicRatCoreUp,\ \StreamNameUp
\longrightarrow
\RegSeqRatUp .
$$
The selected-half row $B$ may narrow the visible interval cell, but it does not create a rational sequence outside the displayed stream schedule or bypass the endpoint-order evidence.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the carrier of \autoref{def:finite-interval-bisection-selector}. The dyadic endpoint row $D$, interval-cell row $I$, selected-half row $B$, and stream schedule row $S$ are upstream of the regular rational readback row $Q$.

Apply selected-half exactness. It forces $B$ to choose only a displayed half-cell whose endpoint order is already visible through $D$ and $I$.

Apply refinement induction. Every finite tail of repeated bisection remains a finite chain of displayed cells, so a RegSeqRat-facing readback sees the same endpoint and stream-window route at each finite stage.

Thus $Q$ is reached only through the displayed finite interval lattice, dyadic interval, dyadic endpoint, and stream schedule rows. No quotient stream, selected representative, countable-choice subsequence, or ambient Real-completion route is imported by the handoff.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite interval bisection Real-seal nonescape]
\label{thm:finite-interval-bisection-real-seal-nonescape}
Let $F=(D,I,B,S,Q,R,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\FiniteIntervalBisectionSelectorUp$ carrier. A $\RealUp$ or $\CompletionUp$ consumer reaches the terminal seal $R$ only after the dyadic endpoint row $D$, displayed interval-cell row $I$, selected-half row $B$, stream schedule $S$, and RegSeqRat readback $Q$ have been exposed. The Real-facing seal therefore records the same finite bisection route and not a chosen limit, quotient real equality, open-cover compactness principle, or ambient completeness witness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the RegSeqRat handoff of \autoref{thm:finite-interval-bisection-regseqrat-handoff}. It places every regular rational readback downstream of the finite interval lattice, dyadic interval, endpoint-order, selected-half, and stream schedule rows.

The Real-completion route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finite-interval-lattice-namecert} keeps analytic consumers inside the displayed finite lattice route before a Real-facing seal is read.

Finally replay the structural rows $H,C,P,N$. Componentwise transport, continuation replay, provenance, and local naming preserve the same packet, so the terminal row $R$ cannot add a chosen limit, quotient real equality, open-cover compactness principle, or ambient completeness witness.
\end{proof}
