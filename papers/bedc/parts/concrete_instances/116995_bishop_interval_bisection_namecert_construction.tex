\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\BishopIntervalBisectionUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-bishop-interval-bisection-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges, the $\BishopIntervalBisectionUp$ packet names the finite constructive bisection route by which a located rational interval is repeatedly halved, inspected through rational locator rows, repacked as a regular rational readback, and sealed only at the terminal real-name boundary. It is the Bishop-style neighbor of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-locatedinterval-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-located-interval-bisection-namecert}, and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-rational-interval-locator-namecert}: the packet consumes located endpoint rows, rational endpoint bracketing, dyadic midpoint cells, finite window schedules, and regular rational readbacks before any $\RealUp$ consumer may inspect the result.

\begin{definition}[Bishop interval bisection carrier]
\label{def:bishop-interval-bisection-carrier}
A $\BishopIntervalBisectionUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
B=(L,Q,D,S,R,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $L$ is the $\LocatedIntervalUp$ source row. It supplies the located endpoint surface and the endpoint-window discipline consumed from \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-locatedinterval-namecert}.
\item $Q$ is the $\RationalIntervalLocatorUp$ bracketing row. It records the rational endpoint cells and comparison boundary consumed from \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-rational-interval-locator-namecert}.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ midpoint and radius ledger for the requested finite bisection depth.
\item $S$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ window schedule that records which endpoint and midpoint observations are inspected at each finite branch.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row obtained from the retained rational cells and stream windows.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal row. It is read only after $L,Q,D,S,R$ have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the located interval, rational locator, dyadic midpoint ledger, stream windows, readback, and seal rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from the bisection request through rational bracketing, midpoint halving, retained-window readback, and the terminal seal.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\BishopIntervalBisectionUp$, $\LocatedIntervalUp$, $\RationalIntervalLocatorUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\BishopIntervalBisectionUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier predicate asserts that these rows form one finite Bishop interval bisection packet. It has no coordinate for a selected completed endpoint, quotient real equality, host interval equality, open-cover compactness, countable choice, or an ambient completeness theorem.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Bishop interval bisection halving stability]
\label{thm:bishop-interval-bisection-halving-stability}
Let $B=(L,Q,D,S,R,E,H,C,P,N)$ be an accepted $\BishopIntervalBisectionUp$ carrier. Every finite halving step requested by the packet is stable under the displayed $\hsame$ transport and $\Cont$ replay rows: the located interval row $L$, rational locator row $Q$, dyadic midpoint ledger $D$, stream-window schedule $S$, regular readback $R$, and terminal seal $E$ move as one packet, and the retained child interval remains attached to the same rational bracketing route.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the packet through \autoref{def:bishop-interval-bisection-carrier}. The halving step has no independent host interval coordinate; it can read only the located endpoint row $L$, the rational locator row $Q$, and the dyadic midpoint ledger $D$.

Transport is componentwise. The row $H$ moves $L,Q,D,S,R,E$ together, while the row $C$ replays the same bisection route after transport. Thus the retained child interval is still the child selected by the displayed rational bracketing and midpoint rows.

The stream and readback rows are downstream of that retained child. Since $S$ and $R$ are transported with the same history and provenance, the finite halving route remains stable before the terminal $\RealUp$ seal is inspected.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Bishop interval bisection Real-seal nonescape]
\label{thm:bishop-interval-bisection-real-seal-nonescape}
Every $\RealUp$-facing read of an accepted $\BishopIntervalBisectionUp$ carrier factors through the finite route
$$
L,Q,D,S,R,E,H,C,P,N .
$$
The consumer first reads the located interval surface, then the rational interval locator, then the dyadic halving ledger, then the finite stream windows and $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback, and only then the terminal $\RealUp$ seal. The packet exports no selected limit, no quotient equality of real names, no countable-choice sequence, no open-cover compactness witness, and no ambient real-completeness coordinate.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the displayed carrier. The only Real-facing coordinate is $E$, and the definition places $E$ after the located interval, rational locator, dyadic midpoint, stream-window, and regular-readback rows.

The rational locator row $Q$ supplies endpoint bracketing before any midpoint branch is retained. The dyadic ledger $D$ records the finite halving data, and the stream schedule $S$ records exactly which finite observations are inspected.

The regular readback $R$ is therefore the last non-Real row on the route. Componentwise transport, replay, provenance, and local naming are exactly $H,C,P,N$, so no host analytic object can be inserted between $R$ and the terminal seal $E$.
\end{proof}
