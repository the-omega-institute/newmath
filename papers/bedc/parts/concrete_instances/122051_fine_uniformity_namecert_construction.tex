\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\FineUniformityUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-fine-uniformity-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bourbaki and Kelley, the $\FineUniformityUp$ packet records the fine uniformity carried by a completely regular topology as finite BEDC naming data. It sits between the topology source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert}, the real-valued separation surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-completely-regular-space-namecert}, the uniform-space carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-uniformspace-namecert}, the relative-open route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-subspace-topology-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names the displayed entourage base generated by real-valued continuous probes; it is not a selected filter, a host powerset, a choice principle for entourages, a metrization theorem, or a completion claim.

\begin{definition}[Fine uniformity carrier]
\label{def:fine-uniformity-carrier}
A $\FineUniformityUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
F=(T,C,U,S,R,E,H,K,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $T$ is the ambient $\TopologyUp$ row, exposing the open-index classifier, neighbourhood reads, finite-intersection rows, and arbitrary-union rows used by the packet.
\item $C$ is the $\CompletelyRegularSpaceUp$ row, supplying the real-valued point-versus-closed-set separation route over the same topology source.
\item $U$ is the $\UniformSpaceUp$ row whose entourages are read only through the displayed fine-uniformity base.
\item $S$ is the $\SubspaceTopologyUp$ compatibility row, recording that relative opens admitted by a displayed subspace read the same topology source before any restricted uniformity request.
\item $R$ is the $\RealUp$-valued continuous-probe row. Each accepted probe is a displayed $\ContinuousMapUp$ read into $\RealUp$ whose inverse uniform window contributes an entourage-base entry.
\item $E$ is the finite entourage-base ledger generated from the displayed real-valued probes and their finite intersection reads.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $T,C,U,S,R,E,K,P,N$ and for the displayed probe-window and entourage-base rows.
\item $K$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a fine-uniformity request through topology, complete-regularity, uniform-space, subspace, real-probe, entourage-base, transport, provenance, and naming rows.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\FineUniformityUp$, $\TopologyUp$, $\CompletelyRegularSpaceUp$, $\UniformSpaceUp$, $\SubspaceTopologyUp$, $\RealUp$, $\ContinuousMapUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\FineUniformityUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $T,C,U,S,R,E,H,K,P,N$. It has no coordinate for a host powerset of $X \times X$, a selected entourage filter, a choice of all real-valued functions, a metric generator, a completion object, or ambient real completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Fine uniformity NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:fine-uniformity-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\FineUniformityUp$ carrier
$$
F=(T,C,U,S,R,E,H,K,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\FineUniformityUp}$ surface consists exactly of topology-source admission, complete-regularity source admission, uniform-space handoff, subspace-topology compatibility, $\RealUp$-valued continuous-probe exposure, finite entourage-base exposure, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, provenance, and local naming. Every fine-uniformity consumer must read the route
$$
T,C,U,S,R,E,H,K,P,N
$$
before using an entourage as public.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:fine-uniformity-carrier}. The accepted packet displays the topology source, completely regular source, uniform-space row, subspace compatibility row, real-valued probe row, finite entourage-base ledger, and structural rows.

The real-valued probes are read before the entourage base. The row $C$ supplies the point-versus-closed-set separation route, while $R$ records only displayed $\RealUp$-valued $\ContinuousMapUp$ probes over the same topology source.

The entourage-base row $E$ is finite and generated from the displayed probe windows. A consumer may inspect an entourage entry or a finite-intersection read already stored in $E$, but it cannot replace that row by a host powerset, a selected filter, or a choice of all probes.

Finally $H,K,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and locally name the same finite route. Hence the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert surface, and no metric generator, completion object, selected filter, host powerset, or ambient real-completeness principle can enter the packet.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Fine uniformity compatible-topology handoff]
\label{thm:fine-uniformity-compatible-topology-handoff}
Let $F=(T,C,U,S,R,E,H,K,P,N)$ be an accepted $\FineUniformityUp$ carrier. Any $\TopologyUp$, $\SubspaceTopologyUp$, or $\UniformSpaceUp$ consumer that reads the topology induced by the fine uniformity must first expose the displayed topology source $T$, complete-regularity route $C$, real-valued probe row $R$, finite entourage-base ledger $E$, and structural rows $H,K,P,N$.

Consequently the topology handoff is the finite route
$$
\TopologyUp,\CompletelyRegularSpaceUp,\RealUp,\ContinuousMapUp
\longmapsto
\UniformSpaceUp,\SubspaceTopologyUp
\longmapsto
H,K,P,N ,
$$
not a host powerset topology, a selected entourage filter, a metric presentation, a completion theorem, or a choice principle for probes.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the carrier definition. The topology source $T$ and complete-regularity row $C$ are the only entries that expose the open and real-valued separation vocabulary used by the packet.

Read the real-valued probe row $R$ and the finite entourage-base ledger $E$. These rows display how the fine-uniformity windows are generated before the $\UniformSpaceUp$ handoff is consumed, so the induced topology cannot be read from an untracked filter or host powerset.

For subspaces, the row $S$ keeps the same topology source visible before restriction. Thus a $\SubspaceTopologyUp$ consumer receives a relative-open route tied to $T$ and $E$, not an independent restricted uniformity object.

Finally $H,K,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name only the displayed rows. The handoff therefore exports compatible topology data through the finite certificate route while refusing selected filters, host powersets, metric presentations, completion claims, and choice over probes.
\end{proof}
