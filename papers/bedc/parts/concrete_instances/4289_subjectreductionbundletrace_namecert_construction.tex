\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\SubjectReductionBundleTraceUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-subject-reduction-bundle-trace-namecert}
\origin{ai}

The $\SubjectReductionBundleTraceUp$ packet concretizes the explicit bundle
discharge route of \autoref{ch:sr-discharge-routes} as a finite BEDC trace. It
reads the explicit discharge surface of
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-subjectreductiondischarge-namecert}, the ledger
discipline of
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-subjectreductiondischargeledger-namecert}, and
the finite continuation route shape of
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-continuationtrace-namecert}. The chapter names
the route packet; it does not inhabit the dependent-codomain discharge for the
present closed-CIC substrate.

\begin{definition}[SubjectReduction bundle trace carrier]
\label{def:subject-reduction-bundle-trace-carrier}
A $\SubjectReductionBundleTraceUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
T=(B,E,L,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following visible coordinates:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $B$ is the four-field subject-reduction discharge bundle row, naming the same obligation surface read by $\SubjectReductionDischargeUp$;
\item $E$ is the explicit extraction row that records direct bundle consumption;
\item $L$ is the finite discharge ledger inherited from $\SubjectReductionDischargeLedgerUp$, with one row for each required subject-reduction discharge hypothesis;
\item $H$ is the componentwise $\hsame$ transport comparing bundle fields, extraction rows, and ledger rows;
\item $C$ is the displayed $\Cont$ route from the chosen discharge carrier to the subject-reduction theorem consumer, in the finite trace style of $\ContinuationTraceUp$;
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\NameCert$, $\SubjectReductionDischargeUp$, $\SubjectReductionDischargeLedgerUp$, and the bundle route object;
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\SubjectReductionBundleTraceUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier exports only these finite rows. It contains no dependent-codomain
proof, no host evaluator, and no second subject-reduction principle.
\end{definition}
\leandef{BEDC.Derived.SubjectReductionBundleTraceUp.SubjectReductionBundleTraceUp}

\begin{theorem}[SubjectReduction bundle trace NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:subject-reduction-bundle-trace-namecert-obligations}
The local $\NameCert_{\SubjectReductionBundleTraceUp}$ surface for
\autoref{def:subject-reduction-bundle-trace-carrier} consists of five finite
obligations: carrier admission for the displayed packet, classifier stability
under componentwise $\hsame$ transport of $B,E,L,H,C,P,N$, route readback for
direct bundle consumers, non-escape from dependent-codomain discharge evidence
not present in the packet, and public export as the finite BEDC route packet
for subject-reduction consumers.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Carrier admission is the tuple displayed in
\autoref{def:subject-reduction-bundle-trace-carrier}. Each coordinate is a
finite BEDC row or a local package and naming row.

Classifier stability is componentwise. A transported packet may compare the
bundle row, extraction row, discharge ledger, transport row, continuation
route, provenance, and local name only through the displayed $\hsame$ and
$\Cont$ data.

The remaining obligations are supplied by the route readback, non-escape, and
public export theorems below. Together they account for every consumer read
while keeping the packet on the bundle discharge surface described in
\autoref{ch:sr-discharge-routes}.
\end{proof}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.SubjectReductionBundleTraceUp.SubjectReductionBundleTraceCarrier\_namecert\_obligations}

\begin{theorem}[SubjectReduction bundle trace route readback]
\label{thm:subject-reduction-bundle-trace-route-readback}
Every subject-reduction consumer of an accepted $\SubjectReductionBundleTraceUp$
packet reads the bundle row $B$ directly and consumes the four discharge rows
in $L$. The public route factors through $H,C,P,N$ and does not introduce a
second discharge surface.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.SubjectReductionBundleTraceUp.SubjectReductionBundleTraceCarrier\_route\_readback}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier. The only discharge-bearing rows are the bundle row $B$,
the extraction row $E$, and the finite ledger $L$.

For the direct route, the consumer reads $B$ and then reads the four required
discharge rows from $L$. The rows $H$ and $C$ only transport and route the
displayed data, while $P,N$ record provenance and local naming.

Thus the route terminates at the finite bundle ledger. No additional
subject-reduction principle is available beyond the displayed packet.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[SubjectReduction bundle trace non-escape]
\label{thm:subject-reduction-bundle-trace-non-escape}
An accepted $\SubjectReductionBundleTraceUp$ packet cannot be used to construct
a dependent-codomain discharge for the present closed-CIC substrate, a host
evaluator, a termination predicate, a quotient of reduction behaviours, or a
second subject-reduction theorem. Its public reads are exhausted by
$B,E,L,H,C,P,N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Read the carrier inventory. None of the displayed rows is a dependent-codomain
proof, a host evaluator, a termination predicate, a quotient object, or a
second theorem.

Apply the route readback theorem. The consumer follows the direct bundle route,
which ends in the same four-row discharge ledger $L$ and the same BEDC route
and provenance data.

The obstruction boundary from \autoref{ch:sr-discharge-routes} remains external
to this packet. Since the carrier contains no row discharging that boundary for
the present substrate, no public read can escape the finite route packet.
\end{proof}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.SubjectReductionBundleTraceUp.SubjectReductionBundleTraceCarrier\_non\_escape}

\begin{theorem}[SubjectReduction bundle trace public export]
\label{thm:subject-reduction-bundle-trace-public-export}
The public $\SubjectReductionBundleTraceUp$ export is the finite packet of
\autoref{def:subject-reduction-bundle-trace-carrier} together with the NameCert
obligations of \autoref{thm:subject-reduction-bundle-trace-namecert-obligations},
the route readback of
\autoref{thm:subject-reduction-bundle-trace-route-readback}, and the
non-escape boundary of
\autoref{thm:subject-reduction-bundle-trace-non-escape}. Downstream
subject-reduction work may consume it as a BEDC-native route packet for bundle
discharge, but not as an inhabitant of any discharge hypothesis not listed in
$L$.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.SubjectReductionBundleTraceUp.SubjectReductionBundleTraceCarrier\_public\_export}
\begin{proof}
The carrier supplies the finite rows $B,E,L,H,C,P,N$. The NameCert obligations
bind carrier admission, classifier stability, route readback, non-escape, and
public export to those rows.

The readback theorem accounts for direct bundle consumption. The non-escape
theorem excludes the structures that would make the packet stronger than a
route packet.

Repacking these clauses yields the public export. It is the concrete BEDC
route packet for the bundle discharge interface, with the same four discharge
rows visible and no hidden subject-reduction evidence outside the finite
ledger.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[SubjectReduction bundle trace obligation route]
\label{thm:subject-reduction-bundle-trace-obligation-route}
An obligation-closure consumer of $\SubjectReductionBundleTraceUp$ must read
\autoref{def:subject-reduction-bundle-trace-carrier}, the five NameCert
obligations of
\autoref{thm:subject-reduction-bundle-trace-namecert-obligations}, the route
readback of \autoref{thm:subject-reduction-bundle-trace-route-readback}, the
non-escape boundary of
\autoref{thm:subject-reduction-bundle-trace-non-escape}, the public export of
\autoref{thm:subject-reduction-bundle-trace-public-export}, componentwise
transport through $H$, provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$
before treating the packet as an obligation-surface object. This route does
not discharge dependent-codomain evidence, evaluator equality, quotient
reduction behaviour, or full subject reduction.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the carrier row. It displays exactly the bundle row, extraction
row, finite ledger, componentwise transport, continuation route, provenance,
and local naming coordinates $B,E,L,H,C,P,N$.

Apply the NameCert-obligations theorem. It names carrier admission,
classifier stability, route readback, non-escape, and public export as the
five finite obligations attached to that carrier.

Now read the route readback, non-escape, and public-export theorems. These
theorems force the consumer to stay on the displayed bundle ledger, exclude
the absent dependent-codomain and evaluator objects, and expose only the
finite BEDC route packet.

Finally use the structural rows $H,P,N$ for componentwise transport,
provenance, and local naming. Since every cited row is part of the displayed
packet, the consumer may regard it as an obligation-surface object, but no
claim outside the listed rows is exported.
\end{proof}
