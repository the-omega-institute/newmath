\begin{definition}[Regular Cauchy limit comparison carrier]
\label{def:regular-cauchy-limit-comparison-carrier}
A $\RegularCauchyLimitComparisonUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
C=(A,B,W,D,R,E,H,Ct,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $A$ is the first displayed regular-Cauchy limit packet, including its source, modulus, diagonal witness, and Real-facing seal request.
\item $B$ is the second displayed regular-Cauchy limit packet over the same comparison request.
\item $W$ is the shared finite $\StreamNameUp$ window in which both packets are inspected.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance comparison row, recording the requested precision, slack, endpoint comparison, and retained dyadic budget.
\item $R$ is the paired $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row for the two windows selected by $W$.
\item $E$ is the paired $\RealUp$ seal row reached only after $W,D,R$ have been displayed.
\item $H$ stores componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $A,B,W,D,R,E$ and the structural rows.
\item $Ct$ stores displayed $\Cont$ replay from the two limit packets through the shared window, dyadic comparison, readback, and Real seal.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\RegularCauchyLimitReadbackUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\RegularCauchyLimitComparisonUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for quotient stream equality, a selected limiting real, trichotomy, countable choice, or an ambient completeness theorem for $\RealUp$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Regular Cauchy limit comparison NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:regular-cauchy-limit-comparison-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\RegularCauchyLimitComparisonUp$ carrier
$$
C=(A,B,W,D,R,E,H,Ct,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\RegularCauchyLimitComparisonUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for $C$, shared-window exposure through $W$, dyadic tolerance comparison through $D$, paired rational readback through $R$, paired Real seal nonescape through $E$, componentwise transport through $H$, displayed replay through $Ct$, provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier to the tuple of \autoref{def:regular-cauchy-limit-comparison-carrier}. The two limit presentations are the rows $A$ and $B$, and the comparison data available to public consumers is exactly $W,D,R,E,H,Ct,P,N$.

The shared finite window $W$ fixes the StreamName read for both packets. The dyadic row $D$ records the tolerance comparison for that same window, and the paired readback row $R$ exposes only the rational observations displayed by the window.

The Real seal row $E$ is downstream of those rows. It can report the paired seal as a finite read of the two presentations, but the carrier contains no coordinate for quotienting streams, selecting a host limit, invoking trichotomy, or importing ambient completeness.

Finally $H,Ct,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same finite surface. These structural rows do not add analytic content, so the listed obligations exhaust the local $\NameCert$ packet.
\end{proof}

\label{thm:regular-cauchy-limit-comparison-l10-route}

$$

\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the accepted carrier $C=(A,B,W,D,R,E,H,Ct,P,N)$. The only window row is $W$, so any comparison consumer must first read the common $\StreamNameUp$ exposure for the two displayed limit packets.

Apply \autoref{thm:regular-cauchy-limit-comparison-namecert-obligations}. It places the dyadic comparison row $D$ after the shared window and before the paired $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row $R$.

The Real-facing seal $E$ is then available only after $W,D,R$ have been displayed. The structural rows $H,Ct,P,N$ preserve this route under transport, replay, provenance, and local naming.

Since the carrier of \autoref{def:regular-cauchy-limit-comparison-carrier} has no quotient, selector, trichotomy, choice, or completeness coordinate, no accepted comparison read can export any of those stronger endpoints.
\end{proof}
