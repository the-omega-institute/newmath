Following Jordan and von Neumann, the polarization identity is read as a finite reconstruction route from inner-product rows to norm/parallelogram rows, not as host Hilbert-space equality. The $\PolarizationIdentityUp$ packet sits between the scalar field of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-field-namecert}, the vector-space source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-vecspace-namecert}, the inner-product surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-innerproduct-namecert}, the norm-square boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-norm-namecert}, and the Hilbert consumer boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-hilbert-namecert}.

\begin{definition}[PolarizationIdentity carrier]
\label{def:polarization-identity-carrier}
A $\PolarizationIdentityUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
P=(F,V,I,N,Q,H,C,R,L)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $F$ is the $\FieldUp$ scalar row that supplies the additive, multiplicative, sign, and division data used by the scalar reconstruction coefficients.
\item $V$ is the $\VecSpaceUp$ row whose vector addition, inverse, zero, and scalar-action rows are the only vector endpoints admitted by the packet.
\item $I$ is the $\InnerProductUp$ scalar-form row naming the accepted inner-product observations on the displayed vector endpoints.
\item $N$ is the $\NormUp$ norm-square row read from the diagonal inner-product observations and the parallelogram ledger.
\item $Q$ is the $\HilbertUp$ consumer boundary row; it receives only the reconstructed norm/parallelogram route and not a host Hilbert-space equality.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for scalar data, vector data, inner-product observations, norm-square rows, the Hilbert boundary, continuation, provenance, and local naming.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from the inner-product rows through the norm-square and parallelogram rows to the Hilbert-facing boundary.
\item $R$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\FieldUp$, $\VecSpaceUp$, $\InnerProductUp$, $\NormUp$, $\HilbertUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $L$ is the local $\NameCert_{\PolarizationIdentityUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $F,V,I,N,Q,H,C,R,L$. It has no coordinate for a completed Hilbert space, quotient vector equality, external bilinear map, selected orthonormal basis, spectral theorem, or host parallelogram proof.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[PolarizationIdentity NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:polarization-identity-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\PolarizationIdentityUp$ carrier $P=(F,V,I,N,Q,H,C,R,L)$, the local $\NameCert_{\PolarizationIdentityUp}$ surface consists of carrier admission for scalar field data, vector-space endpoints, inner-product scalar-form observations, norm-square and parallelogram rows, Hilbert consumer boundary, componentwise transport, displayed continuation replay, package provenance, and local naming. Every public read of the polarization route is generated by these rows, and no read exports host Hilbert equality, quotient vector equality, a selected basis, an external bilinear map, or ambient completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Read the carrier rows in the displayed order. The scalar row $F$ and vector-space row $V$ provide the only scalar coefficients and vector endpoints available to the packet.

The inner-product row $I$ supplies the scalar-form observations, and the norm-square row $N$ records the diagonal and parallelogram-facing reads obtained from those observations. Thus the reconstruction route is finite data over the displayed rows rather than an equality inside a host Hilbert space.

Finally $Q,H,C,R,L$ carry the Hilbert-facing boundary, componentwise transport, replay, provenance, and local naming. Since no coordinate stores a completed Hilbert space, quotient vector equality, selected basis, external bilinear map, or ambient completeness, those objects cannot be exported by the local certificate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[PolarizationIdentity inner-product norm handoff]
\label{thm:polarization-identity-innerproduct-norm-handoff}
Every $\NormUp$ or $\HilbertUp$ consumer of an accepted $\PolarizationIdentityUp$ packet reads the inner-product-to-norm reconstruction only through
$$
\FieldUp,\quad \VecSpaceUp,\quad \InnerProductUp,\quad \NormUp,\quad \HilbertUp,\quad \BHist,\quad \hsame,\quad \Cont,\quad \Pkg,\quad \NameCert .
$$
The handoff sends diagonal scalar-form observations and parallelogram rows to the norm-square boundary, then presents the resulting finite route to Hilbert-facing consumers. It does not supply a host Hilbert equality, quotient vector equality, selected orthonormal basis, spectral theorem, or external completeness principle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with \autoref{def:polarization-identity-carrier}. The only route from inner-product observations to norm-square data is the visible $I,N$ pair over the scalar and vector rows $F,V$.

Apply \autoref{thm:polarization-identity-namecert-obligations}. It requires the Hilbert-facing read to pass through carrier admission, inner-product observations, norm-square and parallelogram rows, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming.

Therefore a downstream $\NormUp$ or $\HilbertUp$ consumer receives exactly the finite handoff $F,V,I,N,Q,H,C,R,L$. The refused host equality, quotient, basis, spectral, and completeness objects are absent from the carrier and cannot be reconstructed by replay.
\end{proof}
