\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\FortissimoSpaceUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-fortissimo-space-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Steen--Seebach and Kelley on classical point-set examples, the $\FortissimoSpaceUp$ packet records the fortissimo topology by finite observable-open rows rather than by an unrestricted host topology. It sits after the topology carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert}, the Sierpinski observable truth surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-sierpinski-space-namecert}, the finite schedule discipline of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, and the set-like membership boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-setlike-namecert}. The certificate names a distinguished point, isolated-point rows, and countable-exception neighbourhood schedules; it does not assert a host separation theorem, a completed power set of opens, a selected enumeration of every exception set, or an ambient real line.

\begin{definition}[FortissimoSpace carrier]
\label{def:fortissimo-space-carrier}
A $\FortissimoSpaceUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
F=(X,\omega,I,Q,O,T,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X$ is the accepted point carrier row, read as a $\SetLikeUp$ membership surface for the displayed finite public points.
\item $\omega$ is the distinguished-point row. It names the unique non-isolated point whose neighbourhoods are inspected by countable-exception schedules.
\item $I$ is the isolated-point ledger. For every displayed point row apart from $\omega$, it stores the singleton-open read used by topology and Sierpinski consumers.
\item $Q$ is the countable-exception $\StreamNameUp$ schedule. Each finite prefix lists the points excluded from the current neighbourhood of $\omega$ and records that later prefixes may only refine the visible exception ledger.
\item $O$ is the observable-open row. It exposes the truth of membership in an inspected open only through the isolated singleton rows in $I$ or the distinguished-point neighbourhood schedule in $Q$.
\item $T$ is the $\TopologyUp$ provenance row, recording finite union and intersection reads only for the opens generated by $I,Q,O$.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the point carrier, distinguished point, isolated rows, exception schedule, observable-open row, topology provenance, replay, package, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay of the route
$$
X,\omega,I,Q \longmapsto O \longmapsto T .
$$
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\FortissimoSpaceUp$, $\TopologyUp$, $\SierpinskiSpaceUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\SetLikeUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\FortissimoSpaceUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $X,\omega,I,Q,O,T,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for a host set of all open subsets, a global choice of countable exception enumerations, a separation theorem, compactness, metrizability, or ambient real-line structure.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[FortissimoSpace NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:fortissimo-space-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\FortissimoSpaceUp$ carrier
$$
F=(X,\omega,I,Q,O,T,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\FortissimoSpaceUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission, distinguished-point admission, isolated singleton-open admission, countable-exception schedule exactness, observable-open truth through $\SierpinskiSpaceUp$, $\TopologyUp$ open-row provenance, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming over the rows of \autoref{def:fortissimo-space-carrier}. No obligation row asserts a host topology, a completed lattice of opens, a selected global enumeration of all countable exception sets, separation axioms, compactness, metrizability, or real-line structure.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Carrier admission is projection from \autoref{def:fortissimo-space-carrier}. The accepted packet first displays the point carrier $X$, the distinguished point $\omega$, the isolated-point ledger $I$, and the countable-exception schedule $Q$.

The observable-open row $O$ is then forced to read membership in only two ways. Away from $\omega$ it uses the isolated singleton rows in $I$; at $\omega$ it uses the finite prefix of the exception schedule in $Q$.

The topology row $T$ records only the finite open-row provenance generated by those reads. The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same finite route without adding any host open-set coordinate.

Because the carrier inventory contains no completed open lattice, selected global exception enumeration, separation theorem, compactness row, metrizability row, or ambient real-line row, none of those objects can be exported as a local NameCert obligation.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[FortissimoSpace Sierpinski topology handoff]
\label{thm:fortissimo-space-sierpinski-topology-handoff}
Every $\TopologyUp$-facing, $\SierpinskiSpaceUp$-facing, or observable-open consumer of an accepted $\FortissimoSpaceUp$ packet must read the route
$$
X,\omega,I,Q,O,T,H,C,P,N .
$$
The row $O$ exposes only observable-open truth from isolated singleton rows and countable-exception neighbourhood prefixes, while $T$ exposes only the corresponding $\TopologyUp$ open-row provenance. The handoff does not import host open sets, selected exception enumerations, separation axioms, compactness, metrizability, or ambient real-line data.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier. The point source is $X$, the distinguished point is $\omega$, the isolated singleton evidence is $I$, and the only neighbourhood schedule at $\omega$ is the finite $\StreamNameUp$ row $Q$.

The observable-open consumer then reads $O$. This row converts the isolated and distinguished-point routes into $\SierpinskiSpaceUp$ truth values without exposing an ambient predicate on all subsets.

The topology consumer reads $T$ only after that observable route has been replayed. The rows $H,C,P,N$ preserve and name the same carrier coordinates, so no later consumer can replace the certificate by a host topology or selected family of exception sets.
\end{proof}
