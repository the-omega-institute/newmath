Following Bishop and Bridges on constructive bisection methods, the $\IntervalHalvingTreeUp$ packet records the finite binary tree of interval halves inspected before a Real and Completion consumer reads a regular rational name. It sits after the dyadic endpoint arithmetic of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the finite observation windows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal real-name boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. It also neighbors the bisection-tree surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-intervalbisection-namecert}, the located interval bisection route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-located-real-interval-bisection-namecert}, and the nested interval surfaces of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-nestedinterval-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-nested-interval-theorem-namecert}. The packet names a constructive halving tree as finite NameCert data; it does not assert ambient real completeness, an infinite branch selector, quotient stream equality, trichotomy, or countable choice.

\begin{definition}[Interval halving tree carrier]
\label{def:interval-halving-tree-carrier}
An $\IntervalHalvingTreeUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
T=(R,D,S,Q,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $R$ is the finite rooted halving-tree row. Each node records a rational interval window, its depth, and the visible left/right child slots that have been opened by the construction.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ arithmetic ledger for endpoints, midpoints, dyadic diameter bounds, child containment, and tolerance comparisons along every displayed edge of $R$.
\item $S$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ schedule row. It names the finite node, endpoint, midpoint, sign, and tolerance observations a downstream consumer may inspect.
\item $Q$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ handoff row obtained from the displayed chain of scheduled intervals and dyadic diameter bounds.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal row reached only after $R,D,S,Q$ have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for tree nodes, dyadic ledgers, stream schedules, regular-rational handoffs, real seals, replay rows, provenance, and local naming.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a halving-tree request through $R,D,S,Q,E$ and then through the structural rows.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\IntervalHalvingTreeUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\IntervalBisectionTreeUp$, $\NestedIntervalUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\IntervalHalvingTreeUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through these nine displayed rows. It has no coordinate for a completed branch, host interval equality, quotient stream equality, selected real limit, countable choice, trichotomy, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Interval halving tree NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:interval-halving-tree-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\IntervalHalvingTreeUp$ carrier
$$
T=(R,D,S,Q,E,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\IntervalHalvingTreeUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the finite rooted halving tree $R$, dyadic midpoint and containment arithmetic through $D$, finite observation scheduling through $S$, regular rational readback through $Q$, terminal real-name sealing through $E$, componentwise transport through $H$, continuation replay through $C$, package provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.

Every bisection or nested-interval consumer must read the same displayed rows before it can use the tree as a certificate. The packet exports no infinite branch selector, quotient stream equality, host interval equality, countable choice, trichotomy, or ambient real-completeness row.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:interval-halving-tree-carrier}. The projection exposes one finite rooted tree, one dyadic arithmetic ledger, one stream schedule, one regular-rational handoff, one real-seal row, and the structural rows.

The tree row $R$ supplies the opened nodes and child slots. The dyadic ledger $D$ checks that every displayed child is a midpoint half of its parent interval and that every exposed diameter comparison is a dyadic tolerance comparison.

The schedule row $S$ fixes which finite observations may be read. The row $Q$ repacks any displayed branch segment as a regular rational readback only after $R,D,S$ have been inspected, and $E$ seals the same route as a real-facing row.

Finally $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the displayed packet. Since the carrier has no coordinate for an unbounded branch or host equality, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Interval halving tree RegSeqRat handoff]
\label{thm:interval-halving-tree-regseqrat-handoff}
Every Real and Completion consumer of an accepted $\IntervalHalvingTreeUp$ packet factors through the finite route
$$
R,D \longmapsto S \longmapsto Q \longmapsto E,H,C,P,N .
$$
Thus the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback $Q$ is downstream from the displayed halving tree, midpoint arithmetic, and finite observation schedule, and the terminal $\RealUp$ seal $E$ is downstream from $Q$. No consumer can use the packet as a completed real, a selected infinite branch, or an ambient nested-interval theorem without leaving the carrier.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the displayed halving tree $R$ and the dyadic ledger $D$. They provide the only interval nodes, midpoint checks, containment checks, and diameter comparisons available to the packet.

Read the finite schedule $S$. It determines which node observations and tolerance requests are opened for the downstream handoff, so the regular rational row cannot inspect an unlisted branch or hidden endpoint.

The row $Q$ is then the $\RegSeqRatUp$ handoff for the scheduled branch segment. The real-seal row $E$ is reached only after that handoff, and the rows $H,C,P,N$ preserve the same finite route under transport, replay, provenance, and naming.

Consequently Real and Completion consumers receive a displayed bisection-tree certificate whose finite route is compatible with the DyadicRatCore, StreamName, RegSeqRat, Real, IntervalBisectionTree, and NestedInterval sibling surfaces. The packet supplies no ambient completeness or choice principle.
\end{proof}
