It sits after the Hilbert projection boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metricprojection-namecert}, the Hilbert carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-hilbert-namecert}, the convex subset surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-convexset-namecert}, the complete-metric limit service of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-completemetric-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. \begin{definition}[Schauder projection carrier]
\label{def:schauder-projection-carrier}
A $\SchauderProjectionUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
S=(M,H,V,K,F,E,R,T,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $M$ is the parent $\MetricProjectionUp$ boundary row, read only as the existing projection-facing endpoint discipline.
\item $H$ is the $\HilbertUp$ source row containing the inner-product, norm, and induced metric surfaces.
\item $V$ is the $\ConvexSetUp$ source row naming the closed convex or affine target surface to which the projection request is confined.
\item $K$ is the $\CompleteMetricUp$ convergence-service row used by the approximation window.
\item $F$ is the finite-rank approximation ledger: displayed finite subspaces, endpoint witnesses, and approximation tolerances.
\item $E$ is the endpoint-candidate row produced by the displayed approximation ledger.
\item $R$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal for the distance or norm bound read after $M,H,V,K,F,E$ have been displayed.
\item $T$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the projection, Hilbert, convex, complete-metric, finite-rank, endpoint, real-seal, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a projection request through finite-rank approximation, endpoint admission, and real-seal readback.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\SchauderProjectionUp$, $\MetricProjectionUp$, $\HilbertUp$, $\ConvexSetUp$, $\CompleteMetricUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\SchauderProjectionUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted carriers only through $M,H,V,K,F,E,R,T,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for a host projection operator, quotient point equality, selected representatives, countable choice, or ambient real completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Schauder projection NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:schauder-projection-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\SchauderProjectionUp$ carrier
$$
S=(M,H,V,K,F,E,R,T,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\SchauderProjectionUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the parent metric-projection boundary, Hilbert source, convex target row, complete-metric convergence service, finite-rank approximation ledger, endpoint candidate, terminal real seal, componentwise transport, displayed replay, package provenance, and local naming. Every projection-facing consumer must expose those rows before reading $E$ or $R$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:schauder-projection-carrier}. The displayed tuple lists every row available to the local certificate, so carrier admission can only expose $M,H,V,K,F,E,R,T,C,P,N$.

Read the analytic rows in order. The parent $\MetricProjectionUp$ boundary supplies the nonescape discipline for projection endpoints, while $H$ and $V$ supply the Hilbert and convex source data. The complete-metric row $K$ is then consumed only by the finite-rank approximation ledger $F$.

The endpoint candidate $E$ and real seal $R$ are downstream of that finite ledger. The structural rows $T,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same route, and they add no hidden projection operator or selected representative.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Schauder projection endpoint nonescape]
\label{thm:schauder-projection-endpoint-nonescape}
Every endpoint read exported by an accepted $\SchauderProjectionUp$ packet factors through
$$
\MetricProjectionUp,\ \HilbertUp,\ \ConvexSetUp,\ \CompleteMetricUp,\ \RealUp
$$
by way of the displayed rows $M,H,V,K,F,E,R,T,C,P,N$. The exported endpoint is a finite Schauder-approximation candidate only; it cannot be read as a host nearest-point selector, quotient representative, countable-choice selection, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness principle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:schauder-projection-namecert-obligations}. They force every endpoint consumer to expose the parent projection boundary, Hilbert source, convex target, complete-metric service, finite-rank ledger, endpoint row, and real seal before the local name can be used.

Use the parent nonescape boundary from \autoref{thm:metric-projection-closest-point-nonescape}. It permits a projection endpoint only as a displayed BEDC projection-facing row, not as a host projection map or selected representative.

Finally inspect the Schauder carrier. The only approximation data are the finite rows in $F$, and the only numerical boundary is the real seal $R$. Since no coordinate stores quotient point equality, countable choice, or ambient completeness, none of those objects can escape through $E$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Schauder projection finite approximation route]
\label{thm:schauder-projection-finite-approximation-route}
Any consumer that uses a $\SchauderProjectionUp$ packet for a projection-facing approximation must first display the finite-rank ledger $F$ over the Hilbert row $H$, convex row $V$, complete-metric service $K$, and parent metric-projection boundary $M$. Only after that route is replayed may the consumer inspect the endpoint candidate $E$ and the terminal $\RealUp$ seal $R$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply \autoref{thm:schauder-projection-namecert-obligations}. The finite-rank ledger is one of the mandatory certificate rows, and the endpoint and real seal occur only after the source and convergence-service rows have been exposed.

Next apply \autoref{thm:schauder-projection-endpoint-nonescape}. It keeps the endpoint as a finite approximation candidate, so the consumer cannot replace $F$ by a hidden nearest-point operator or a selected sequence of representatives.

The structural rows $T,C,P,N$ then preserve the same displayed route. Thus the only accepted projection-facing use is the finite approximation route through $M,H,V,K,F,E,R$.
\end{proof}
