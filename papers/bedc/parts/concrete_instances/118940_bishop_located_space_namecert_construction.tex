\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\BishopLocatedSpaceUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-bishop-located-space-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges, a Bishop-style located space is a metric object whose topology-facing and set-facing locatedness reads are exposed by finite means before completion or real-name consumers can inspect them. The $\BishopLocatedSpaceUp$ packet records this interface on the L10 topology, metric, completion, and Real route of \autoref{sec:roadmap-l10-topology-metric-completion-real}. It sits over the metric source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}, the topology source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert}, the located-subset sibling \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-locatedset-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The certificate names only the displayed finite locatedness route; it does not add quotient completion, selected nearest points, open-cover compactness, real trichotomy, countable choice, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[Bishop located-space carrier]
\label{def:bishop-located-space-carrier}
A $\BishopLocatedSpaceUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
B=(M,T,L,Q,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $M$ is the $\MetricUp$ source row, including the point classifier, distance witness, and finite point-observation histories from which locatedness requests are opened.
\item $T$ is the $\TopologyUp$ source row, recording the displayed open-neighbourhood interface consumed by topology-facing readers of the same point observations.
\item $L$ is the $\LocatedSetUp$ locatedness row. It ties each admitted set or source family to the finite distance interface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-locatedset-namecert}.
\item $Q$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row, packaging the rational comparison windows for metric distances, located-set distances, and topology-neighbourhood radii.
\item $E$ is the $\RealUp$ seal row exposed only after the metric, topology, located-set, and regular-rational rows have all been displayed.
\item $H$ stores componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $M,T,L,Q,E$ and for the structural certificate rows.
\item $C$ stores displayed $\Cont$ replay from metric point observations to topology neighbourhoods, located-set requests, regular rational readback, and the terminal Real seal.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\BishopLocatedSpaceUp$, $\MetricUp$, $\TopologyUp$, $\LocatedSetUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\BishopLocatedSpaceUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $M,T,L,Q,E,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for quotient completion, selected nearest points, open-cover compactness, real trichotomy, countable choice, host equality of points, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[BishopLocatedSpace NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:bishop-located-space-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\BishopLocatedSpaceUp$ carrier
$$
B=(M,T,L,Q,E,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\BishopLocatedSpaceUp}$ surface is exhausted by five obligation rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item metric-source admission for the displayed $\MetricUp$ point classifier, distance witness, and finite observation histories in $M$;
\item topology compatibility for the $\TopologyUp$ neighbourhood row $T$, requiring each topology-facing read to remain attached to the same metric observations;
\item located-set exactness for $L$, requiring every locatedness request to pass through the $\LocatedSetUp$ distance interface before a set-facing distance is exported;
\item regular-rational and Real readback for $Q$ and $E$, requiring every rational comparison window to be sealed as $\RealUp$ only after $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback is visible;
\item structural replay for $H,C,P,N$, requiring componentwise transport, displayed continuation, package provenance, and local naming to preserve exactly the same rows.
\end{enumerate}
No obligation row may call on quotient completion, selected nearest points, open-cover compactness, trichotomy, countable choice, host point equality, or ambient Real completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Carrier admission is projection from \autoref{def:bishop-located-space-carrier}. The metric row $M$, topology row $T$, located-set row $L$, regular rational row $Q$, Real seal row $E$, and structural rows $H,C,P,N$ are the only rows in the packet.

The first three obligations bind the analytic source. A topology-facing read must use the neighbourhood interface $T$ over the same metric observations in $M$, and a set-facing locatedness read must use $L$ before any distance-to-set data are exported.

The fourth obligation routes numerical data through $Q$ and $E$. Rational comparison windows become public only as regular rational readback, and the Real seal is exposed only after that readback is displayed.

Finally, $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same finite route. Since the excluded completion, selection, compactness, trichotomy, choice, host-equality, and ambient-completeness principles have no coordinate in the carrier, the five rows exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[BishopLocatedSpace metric-topology handoff]
\label{thm:bishop-located-space-metric-topology-handoff}
Every topology-facing or located-set-facing consumer of an accepted $\BishopLocatedSpaceUp$ carrier first reads the $\MetricUp$ row $M$, then the $\TopologyUp$ row $T$ or the $\LocatedSetUp$ row $L$, then the $\RegSeqRatUp$ row $Q$, and only then the $\RealUp$ seal row $E$. Thus the public handoff is the finite route
$$
M,T,L,Q \longmapsto E
$$
together with $H,C,P,N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with an accepted carrier $B=(M,T,L,Q,E,H,C,P,N)$. The carrier definition gives no direct route from a topology or set consumer to $E$; every read begins with the metric observations in $M$.

If the consumer asks for a neighbourhood read, the route uses $T$ over the same point observations. If it asks for a located-set distance, the route uses $L$, whose sibling interface is fixed by \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-locatedset-namecert}.

Both cases then pass through $Q$. The regular rational row is the only row that packages the finite comparison windows used by the metric, topology, and located-set reads.

The Real seal $E$ is therefore downstream of $M,T,L,Q$, and $H,C,P,N$ preserve this order under transport, replay, provenance, and naming. No host equality, quotient completion, or selected endpoint can bypass the displayed handoff.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[BishopLocatedSpace Real readback nonescape]
\label{thm:bishop-located-space-real-readback-nonescape}
The $\RealUp$ readback exported by an accepted $\BishopLocatedSpaceUp$ packet is reachable only through $M,T,L,Q,E,H,C,P,N$. A Real-facing consumer receives the displayed metric source, topology interface, located-set distance route, regular rational readback, terminal Real seal, componentwise transport, displayed continuation, package provenance, and local NameCert row; it receives no quotient completion, selected nearest point, open-cover compactness, real trichotomy, countable choice, host point equality, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The only Real-facing coordinate is $E$. By \autoref{def:bishop-located-space-carrier}, $E$ is paired with $Q$, and $Q$ is paired with the metric row $M$, topology row $T$, and located-set row $L$.

Apply \autoref{thm:bishop-located-space-namecert-obligations}. The five local obligation rows require metric-source admission, topology compatibility, located-set exactness, regular-rational and Real readback, and structural replay before the Real seal is public.

Apply \autoref{thm:bishop-located-space-metric-topology-handoff}. It identifies $M,T,L,Q$ as the displayed handoff route to $E$.

Thus every Real readback remains inside the finite carrier. Since the packet contains no excluded quotient, selection, compactness, trichotomy, choice, host-equality, or ambient-completeness coordinate, none of those principles can escape through the Real seal.
\end{proof}
