\begin{lemma}[Calculus derivative and Riemann-integral dependency route]
\label{lem:calculus-riemann-integral-dependency-route}
Every substantive $\CalculusUp$ consumer of a derivative or integral conclusion reads the derivative branch through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-derivative-namecert}, the Riemann finite-sum branch through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-riemannintegral-namecert}, and the shared endpoint readback through the finite $\RegSeqRatUp$ and $\RealUp$ rows. The route is therefore a displayed BEDC dependency through derivative windows, tagged Riemann sums, regular rational readback, and real-name sealing; it is not a host function space, a host measure-space integral, a selected antiderivative, or quotient equality of functions.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Project the $\CalculusUp$ local-linearization and root carrier rows already named in \autoref{def:calculus-local-linearization-carrier} and \autoref{def:calculus-root-public-package}. Their derivative-facing coordinates are the $\DerivativeUp$ rows, and their integral-facing coordinates are the $\RiemannIntegralUp$ or finite-sum rows.

The shared numerical readback is not supplied by a host analytic object. It passes through the regular rational and real-name rows before a public endpoint value is exposed.

The existing nonescape theorems in the chapter exclude host function spaces, host integration functionals, selected antiderivatives, quotient equality of functions, and ambient completeness. Hence the dependency route for calculus consumers is the finite $\DerivativeUp$--$\RiemannIntegralUp$--$\RegSeqRatUp$--$\RealUp$ route stated above.
\end{proof}
