\begin{lemma}[Calculus lower-envelope Real-facing route]
\label{lem:calculus-lower-envelope-real-facing-route}
Every $\CalculusUp$ derivative, integral, or variation consumer that reads a lower-envelope regularization as a Real-facing value must route first through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-lowersemicontinuous-namecert}, then through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-epigraph-namecert}, then through the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and finally through the terminal seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. Thus the calculus packet sees the lower-envelope value only after the finite lower-semicontinuity, epigraph, regular-rational, and Real rows are displayed, with no route through host suprema, quotient functions, selected minimizers, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Read the lower-envelope request as a calculus-facing value. The lower-semicontinuity row supplies the admissible lower-envelope boundary, and the epigraph row supplies the displayed value/lower-bound comparison used by that boundary.

Next pass to regular rational data. The $\RegSeqRatUp$ route records the finite readback through visible windows, so the calculus consumer has a concrete value surface before it can inspect any Real seal.

Finally read the $\RealUp$ seal. Since the route has already passed through the displayed lower-semicontinuity, epigraph, and regular-rational rows, no derivative or integral consumer can replace it by a host supremum, quotient-function equality, selected minimizer, or ambient completeness principle.
\end{proof}
