\begin{theorem}[Calculus real-completion sibling route]
\label{thm:calculus-real-completion-sibling-route}
Every accepted $\CalculusUp$ derivative, integral, or limit consumer that reaches Real-facing completion data must route through the $\RealUp$ and $\RegSeqRatUp$ completion stack, with the $\StreamNameUp$ naming surface available when the consumer reads a displayed sequence. The route is witnessed by \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}. It is not an appeal to ambient Real completeness, host quotient completion, or selected limits outside the accepted completion stack.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Read the accepted $\CalculusUp$ consumer through its derivative, integral, or limit interface. A Real-facing completion request first has to expose the $\RealUp$ certificate surface rather than a host completeness object.

The completion data then factors through the regular rational naming route supplied by $\RegSeqRatUp$. When the consumer carries a sequential name, the visible stream component is the $\StreamNameUp$ naming surface, so the read remains inside the displayed completion stack.

Thus the horizontal dependency is exactly the sibling route through Real, regular rational sequences, and StreamName names. Since no carrier row in the local route names ambient Real completeness, host quotient completion, or selected limits, those endpoints are not exported by the calculus consumer.
\end{proof}
