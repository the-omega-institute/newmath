\begin{theorem}[Calculus L10 dependency route]
\label{thm:calculus-l10-dependency-route}
Every accepted $\CalculusUp$ consumer reads derivative and integral data only after the displayed $\RealUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\LimitUp$, and $\ContinuousMapUp$ style rows have been exposed. The route is therefore horizontal through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-limit-namecert}, and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-continuousmap-namecert}; it is not a host differentiability oracle, a selected antiderivative, an ambient completeness principle, or a quotient of function germs.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project an accepted $\CalculusUp$ packet through the existing dependency routes in this hub. The Real-facing row supplies the scalar seal, the regular rational and stream-name rows supply finite approximation schedules, the limit row supplies the local convergence boundary, and the continuous-map row supplies the function readback surface.

Derivative and integral reads occur only after those rows are displayed. The packet may replay them through its structural $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and $\NameCert$ rows, but those rows preserve the displayed dependencies rather than adding a host-level analytic oracle.

Thus an L10 consumer sees calculus data as a finite dependency route through the sibling chapters named above. No row exports selected antiderivatives, ambient completeness, quotient germs, or an untyped host computation.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Calculus L10 real-completion route exhaustion]
\label{thm:calculus-l10-real-completion-route-exhaustion}
Every accepted $\CalculusUp$ derivative, integral, or limit read that reaches a
$\RealUp$ scalar seal must first pass through the displayed L10 route
$$
\DyadicRatCoreUp,\ \StreamNameUp,\ \RegSeqRatUp,\ \RealUp,\ \LimitUp,\ \ContinuousMapUp .
$$
The route supplies dyadic tolerance ledgers, finite stream windows, regular
rational readback, terminal real sealing, local limit boundaries, and
continuous-map readback. It supplies no Dedekind-cut construction, quotient
real equality, selected limit, selected antiderivative, or ambient completeness
coordinate.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start from the existing dependency route,
\autoref{thm:calculus-l10-dependency-route}. It already forces every
derivative and integral read to expose the Real-facing, regular rational,
stream-name, limit, and continuous-map rows before the calculus datum is
public.

Refine the Real-facing row through the L10 construction order. The dyadic
tolerance ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}
feeds the finite windows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert};
those windows feed the regular rational readback of
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}; and that readback reaches
the terminal seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}.

Now attach the calculus consumers. The local convergence boundary is supplied
by \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-limit-namecert}, and function readback is
supplied by \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-continuousmap-namecert}. The
structural $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and $\NameCert$ rows replay and
name this same route rather than adding another completion principle.

Thus a $\CalculusUp$ consumer that has read the displayed route has exhausted
the real-completion boundary available to calculus. Dedekind cuts, quotient
real equality, selected limits, selected antiderivatives, and ambient
completeness would require coordinates absent from the packet.
\end{proof}
