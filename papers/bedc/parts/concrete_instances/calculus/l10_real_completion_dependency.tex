\begin{theorem}[Calculus L10 Real and Completion dependency]
\label{thm:calculus-l10-real-completion-dependency}
Every $\CalculusUp$ certificate that exports derivative, integral, or limit-facing clauses to the Real and Completion route must first read the L10 substrate through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-continuousmap-namecert}. The calculus row therefore consumes terminal real seals, regular rational readbacks, finite stream windows, and displayed continuous-map evidence before derivative or integral clauses may be used as public BEDC obligations.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Read the finite observation schedule through the StreamName certificate. This fixes the inspected windows before any regular rational or real-facing calculus row is exported.

Use the RegSeqRat and Real certificates next. They attach regular rational readback and terminal real sealing to the same windows.

Finally read the ContinuousMap certificate for displayed function evidence. With those four sibling certificates present, the derivative, integral, and limit-facing calculus clauses have a visible L10 dependency route rather than a kernel-only carrier island.
\end{proof}
