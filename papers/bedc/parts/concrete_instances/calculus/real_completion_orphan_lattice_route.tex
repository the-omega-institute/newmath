\begin{theorem}[Calculus Real-completion orphan lattice route]
\label{thm:calculus-real-completion-orphan-lattice-route}
Every $\CalculusUp$ regulated-integral or limit-facing consumer that reads an endpoint seal must first pass through the L10 Real-completion source lattice of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}. In particular, the consumer may use the $\RegulatedIntegralUp$ handoff of \autoref{thm:calculus-regulated-integral-forward-link} only after the source stream window, regular rational readback, and terminal Real seal are visible as the displayed completion route. It exports no host completion theorem, selected representative of a Cauchy stream, quotient real equality, detached endpoint seal, or ambient integration functional.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.CalculusUp.CalculusRealCompletionOrphanLatticeRoute}
\begin{proof}
Start with the $\CalculusUp$ root package. Its regulated-integral and limit-facing rows are accepted only as finite BEDC reads through the calculus carrier, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.

Use the regulated-integral forward link. It routes endpoint reads through the displayed $\RegulatedIntegralUp$ ledger rather than through a host integral or detached endpoint object.

Finally compose this handoff with the Real-completion source lattice. The $\StreamNameUp$ window supplies the finite source, $\RegSeqRatUp$ supplies regular rational readback, and $\RealUp$ supplies the terminal seal, so every such endpoint read remains inside the L10 completion lattice.
\end{proof}
