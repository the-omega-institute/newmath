\begin{definition}[Bishop interval Cantor carrier]
\label{def:bishop-interval-cantor-carrier}

$$

$$

\begin{enumerate}

\item $N$ is the nested-inclusion ledger, recording that every inspected later window is contained in every earlier displayed interval window.
\item $D$ is the diameter-shrink row, giving a dyadic tolerance bound for every inspected precision request.
\item $M$ is the located midpoint and refinement ledger, naming the finite midpoint checks and interval-refinement choices already displayed by the packet.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ extraction row obtained from the endpoint and midpoint schedules.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal read only after $R$ has displayed the regular rational name.
\item $H$ stores componentwise $\hsame$ transport for endpoint rows, nested inclusion, shrink rows, located midpoint refinements, extraction, Real sealing, replay, provenance, and local naming.

\begin{theorem}[Bishop interval Cantor nested window]
\label{thm:bishop-interval-cantor-nested-window}
For every accepted $\BishopIntervalCantorUp$ carrier $I=(L,U,N,D,M,R,E,H,C,P,A)$, every nested-window read factors through the dyadic endpoint rows $L,U$, the nested-inclusion ledger $N$, the diameter-shrink row $D$, the located midpoint and refinement ledger $M$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport $H$, displayed $\Cont$ replay $C$, $\Pkg$ provenance $P$, and local naming $A$ before any regular rational extraction can be consumed. The read exports no host interval intersection, selected limit, countable-choice schedule, quotient Real equality, or ambient completeness row.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:bishop-interval-cantor-carrier}. The endpoint rows are $L,U$, the nesting and shrink data are $N,D$, and the located refinement ledger is $M$.

Read the route through $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, and $\IntervalDomainUp$. These rows expose finite endpoint windows and containment evidence, so the consumer receives displayed interval data rather than a host compactness theorem.

The structural rows $H,C,P,A$ keep the read inside the same packet. Since the tuple contains no selected limit, quotient equality, choice schedule, or ambient completeness coordinate, the nested-window read has exactly the displayed finite surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Bishop interval Cantor completion handoff]
\label{thm:bishop-interval-cantor-completion-handoff}
Every completion-facing read of an accepted $\BishopIntervalCantorUp$ carrier reaches $\RealUp$ only through the route
$$
\DyadicRatCoreUp,\quad \StreamNameUp,\quad \RegSeqRatUp,\quad \RealUp
$$
displayed by $L,U,N,D,M,R,E,H,C,P,A$. The handoff may expose dyadic endpoints, finite windows, midpoint refinement, a regular rational extraction row, Real sealing, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming; it cannot export Heine--Borel compactness, an arbitrary interval-intersection principle, selected limits, quotient Real equality, countable choice, or ambient Real completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use \autoref{thm:bishop-interval-cantor-nested-window}. It forces the completion-facing consumer to display endpoint, nesting, shrink, refinement, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows before the extraction row is available.

The extraction row $R$ is the only route to a regular rational name. It is read from the same finite StreamName and dyadic endpoint windows, so the Real-facing row $E$ can only seal that regular rational extraction.

Thus the completion handoff is the finite route through $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, and $\RealUp$. No excluded compactness, selected-limit, quotient, choice, or ambient-completeness endpoint has a coordinate in the accepted carrier.
\end{proof}
