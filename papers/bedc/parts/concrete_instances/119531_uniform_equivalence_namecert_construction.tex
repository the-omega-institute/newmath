Following Bourbaki's uniform-space treatment and Bishop--Bridges constructive metric spaces, the $\UniformEquivalenceUp$ packet records the finite bidirectional modulus data needed when a metric or completion-facing construction is invariant under two displayed uniformly continuous routes. It sits beside the metric carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-space-namecert}, the modulus discipline of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-uniformcontinuity-namecert}, the completion handoff of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metriccompletion-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet gives Real and Completion consumers a route through displayed windows and tolerances rather than a host bijection, quotient equality, selected representative, countable choice, or ambient completeness principle.

\begin{definition}[UniformEquivalence carrier]
\label{def:uniform-equivalence-carrier}
A $\UniformEquivalenceUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
E=(X,Y,F,G,M,N,I,R,S,Q,H,C,P,L)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X$ and $Y$ are the source and target $\MetricSpaceUp$ rows, including only the distance requests and finite tolerance ledgers visible to the certificate.
\item $F$ and $G$ are the forward and reverse $\UniformContinuityUp$ rows, each exposing a displayed source tolerance, target tolerance, and finite modulus response.
\item $M$ and $N$ are the forward and reverse modulus rows, so each direction keeps its own tolerance transformer before any consumer reads the route as an equivalence.
\item $I$ is the inverse-route compatibility row, recording that the two directed reads compose back through displayed metric and modulus rows rather than through host point equality.
\item $R$ is the $\RealUp$ tolerance-seal row, binding every completion-facing tolerance to displayed real-name data.
\item $S$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ finite-window row and $Q$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ rational readback row, so observed distances and tolerances pass through inspected windows before reaching $R$.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $X,Y,F,G,M,N,I,R,S,Q$.
\item $C$ records $\Cont$ replay from a source or target request through the matching modulus, inverse-route compatibility, real seal, finite window, rational readback, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\UniformEquivalenceUp$, $\MetricSpaceUp$, $\UniformContinuityUp$, $\MetricCompletionUp$, $\RealUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $L$ is the local $\NameCert_{\UniformEquivalenceUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier reads only $X,Y,F,G,M,N,I,R,S,Q,H,C,P,L$. It has no coordinate for host bijection data, quotient completion equality, selected representatives, pointwise extensionality, countable choice, ambient completeness, or an uninspected real tolerance oracle.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform equivalence bidirectional modulus]
\label{thm:uniform-equivalence-bidirectional-modulus}
For every accepted $\UniformEquivalenceUp$ carrier $E=(X,Y,F,G,M,N,I,R,S,Q,H,C,P,L)$, any equivalence read must factor through both displayed modulus rows along the finite route
$$
X,Y\longmapsto F,G\longmapsto M,N\longmapsto I\longmapsto R,S,Q\longmapsto H,C,P,L .
$$
In particular, a one-sided uniform-continuity certificate, a host bijection, or a quotient completion equality cannot supply the $\NameCert_{\UniformEquivalenceUp}$ obligations.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier rows. The metric sources are visible only as $X$ and $Y$, and the directed uniform-continuity data are visible only as $F$ and $G$.

The forward read must expose $M$, while the reverse read must expose $N$. If either row is absent, the consumer has only a one-sided modulus route and not a bidirectional equivalence packet.

After both modulus rows have been read, inverse-route compatibility is forced through $I$. The row names compatibility only through displayed metric and modulus data, so a host bijection or quotient completion equality cannot replace the finite route.

The tolerance and observation rows $R,S,Q$ then bind real-name sealing, stream windows, and rational readback. Finally $H,C,P,L$ transport, replay, source, and name the same displayed packet, completing the local certificate surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform equivalence completion consumer]
\label{thm:uniform-equivalence-completion-consumer}
Every $\MetricCompletionUp$ or $\RealUp$ consumer of an accepted $\UniformEquivalenceUp$ packet must read the source and target $\MetricSpaceUp$ rows, both $\UniformContinuityUp$ modulus rows, inverse-route compatibility, the $\RealUp$ tolerance seal, the $\StreamNameUp$ and $\RegSeqRatUp$ finite windows, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and the local $\NameCert_{\UniformEquivalenceUp}$ row before treating a construction as invariant under bidirectional uniform modulus.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start from a completion-facing or real-facing consumer route. Its source and target data are the displayed metric rows $X,Y$, not an ambient completion object.

The route then reads $F,G,M,N$ to obtain both directed modulus responses. The inverse-route row $I$ ties those responses together before any completion or real-name tolerance can be consumed.

The real and finite-window rows $R,S,Q$ provide the only displayed tolerance interface. Thus a completion consumer cannot appeal to selected limits, quotient representatives, or an uninspected real oracle.

The structural rows $H,C,P,L$ finish the handoff by transport, replay, provenance, and local naming. Therefore invariance under the packet is exactly invariance under the displayed bidirectional uniform-modulus data.
\end{proof}
