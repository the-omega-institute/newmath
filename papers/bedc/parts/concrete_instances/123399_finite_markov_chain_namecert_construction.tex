Following finite-state Markov-chain practice, the $\FiniteMarkovChainUp$
packet records a stochastic transition system only through displayed finite
state, transition, matrix, probability, distribution, and real-comparison rows.
It sits after the sibling transition surface
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-markovchain-namecert}, the finite matrix row
of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-matrix-namecert}, the probability source of
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-probspace-namecert}, the distribution readback
of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-distribution-namecert}, and the terminal
real comparison boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The
packet names only a finite transition packet; it does not introduce a
measure-space completion, a selected invariant law, countable choice, or a
host stochastic process object.

\begin{definition}[FiniteMarkovChain carrier]
\label{def:finite-markov-chain-carrier}
A $\FiniteMarkovChainUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
K=(S,T,M,P,D,R,H,C,Q,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $S$ is the finite state row, exposing the listed states and the finite
membership classifier used by transition reads.
\item $T$ is the $\MarkovChainUp$ transition row, recording source state,
target state, and transition-entry requests as finite observations.
\item $M$ is the $\MatrixUp$ row whose entries encode the displayed transition
table over $S$.
\item $P$ is the $\ProbSpaceUp$ row that supplies the probability surface for
row-sum and nonnegative-entry reads.
\item $D$ is the $\DistributionUp$ row used for initial or one-step
distribution readback over the same finite state row.
\item $R$ is the $\RealUp$ comparison row for transition entries, row sums,
and distribution coordinates.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the state, transition,
matrix, probability, distribution, real-comparison, continuation, provenance,
and naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a finite transition request
through matrix entry readback, probability admission, distribution readback,
and real comparison.
\item $Q$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\FiniteMarkovChainUp$,
$\MarkovChainUp$, $\MatrixUp$, $\ProbSpaceUp$, $\DistributionUp$, $\RealUp$,
$\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\FiniteMarkovChainUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through these finite rows. It
has no coordinate for a countable state space, a measure completion, a selected
stationary distribution, a host stochastic process, or ambient $\RealUp$
completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[FiniteMarkovChain NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:finite-markov-chain-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\FiniteMarkovChainUp$ packet
$K=(S,T,M,P,D,R,H,C,Q,N)$, the local
$\NameCert_{\FiniteMarkovChainUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier
admission, finite-state admission, transition-table matrix readback,
probability row-sum and nonnegative-entry comparison, distribution handoff,
componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance,
and local naming. Every transition consumer must read the $\MarkovChainUp$,
$\MatrixUp$, $\ProbSpaceUp$, $\DistributionUp$, and $\RealUp$ rows displayed in
the carrier before it can export a finite-step transition packet.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:finite-markov-chain-carrier}. Its rows
name the finite state list, transition observations, matrix entries,
probability surface, distribution readback, real comparisons, transport,
replay, provenance, and local certificate.

The transition table is finite. A consumer reaches a transition entry only by
reading $T$ and $M$ over the same state row $S$, then checking the corresponding
probability and real-comparison rows $P,R$.

The distribution handoff is also finite. The row $D$ reads initial or one-step
distributions over $S$ after the transition table has been exposed, and $H,C$
transport and replay the same displayed rows without adding a new state space
or a selected law.

Finally $Q,N$ package and name the certificate. Since the carrier contains no
coordinate for countable-state processes, measure completions, selected
stationary distributions, host stochastic processes, or ambient completeness,
the listed obligations exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}
