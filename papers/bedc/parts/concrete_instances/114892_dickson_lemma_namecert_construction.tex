\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\DicksonLemmaUp$}

Following Dickson and standard constructive finite-combinatorics, the $\DicksonLemmaUp$ packet records the finite-product order induction surface used to route bad-sequence obstructions through named finite data. It sits after the unary finite-spine carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-nat-namecert}, the product and list ledgers of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-prod-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-list-namecert}, the ordered comparison rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-poset-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-totalorder-namecert}, and the algebra-facing chain rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-noetherian-ring-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-hilbert-basis-namecert}. The packet is a finite NameCert surface for product-order induction; it is not a classical selected subsequence theorem, not a quotient of infinite streams, and not a hidden Hilbert-basis proof.

\begin{definition}[Dickson lemma carrier]\label{def:dickson-lemma-carrier}
A $\DicksonLemmaUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
\mathsf{Dickson}(N,P,L,O,T,B,H,C,Q,D)
$$
with the following visible rows.
\begin{itemize}
\item $N$ is the $\NatUp$ coordinate row. It supplies unary finite-spine witnesses for every displayed coordinate height.
\item $P$ is the $\ProdUp$ finite-product row. It binds the displayed coordinates as one product-order packet rather than as detached projections.
\item $L$ is the $\ListUp$ window row. It records the finite prefix of the candidate bad sequence inspected by the induction.
\item $O$ is the $\PosetUp$ product-order row, with coordinatewise comparison over the same natural-number classifier.
\item $T$ is the $\TotalOrderUp$ coordinate-comparison row used only inside the natural coordinate reads.
\item $B$ is the bounded-bad-prefix row. It records that every obstruction is witnessed by a finite list window, a coordinate, and a product-order comparison failure inside that window.
\item $H,C,Q,D$ are, respectively, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and the local $\NameCert_{\DicksonLemmaUp}$ naming row.
\end{itemize}
The classifier compares accepted packets by replaying exactly these rows. No coordinate exports a selected infinite subsequence, excluded-middle search over streams, quotient sequence equality, or an ambient algebraic Noetherian theorem.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Dickson lemma NameCert obligations]\label{thm:dickson-lemma-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\DicksonLemmaUp$ carrier $\mathsf{Dickson}(N,P,L,O,T,B,H,C,Q,D)$, the local $\NameCert_{\DicksonLemmaUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the ten displayed rows, natural-coordinate access through $N$, finite-product assembly through $P$, finite-window readback through $L$, product-order comparison through $O$, coordinate total-order comparison through $T$, bounded-bad-prefix induction through $B$, componentwise transport through $H$, continuation replay through $C$, provenance nonescape through $Q$, and local naming through $D$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:dickson-lemma-carrier}. The accepted packet exposes only the rows $N,P,L,O,T,B,H,C,Q,D$, so the local certificate cannot read an outside stream object or an algebra theorem as an extra coordinate.

The finite-order part is read first. The row $N$ supplies the natural-number coordinate surfaces, $P$ binds those surfaces into a displayed product, and $L$ records the finite list window on which the induction is performed.

The comparison part is then forced by $O,T,B$. The product-order row $O$ reads comparisons coordinatewise, $T$ supplies the natural coordinate comparison used by that read, and $B$ records the finite obstruction window consumed by the induction. Transport, continuation replay, provenance, and local naming preserve that same finite inventory.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Dickson lemma finite-product order handoff]\label{thm:dickson-lemma-finite-product-order-handoff}
Every downstream consumer of an accepted $\DicksonLemmaUp$ packet that asks for a finite-product order induction route must read the displayed handoff
$$
\NatUp \longrightarrow \ProdUp \longrightarrow \ListUp \longrightarrow \PosetUp \longrightarrow \TotalOrderUp \longrightarrow \NameCert_{\DicksonLemmaUp}.
$$
Consequently a $\NoetherianRingUp$ or $\HilbertBasisUp$ consumer may cite $\DicksonLemmaUp$ only as a finite product-order induction packet over displayed natural coordinates, finite list windows, and product-order comparisons. It cannot substitute selected infinite subsequence extraction, quotient stream equality, maximal ideal choice, or a complete Hilbert-basis proof for that route.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the natural-coordinate and finite-product rows. The row $N$ supplies the unary natural surfaces, and $P$ records how the displayed coordinates are assembled as one product packet.

Next read the finite window. The row $L$ exposes only a finite list prefix, so the induction sees a bounded bad-prefix surface rather than an infinite stream or a selected subsequence.

Finally apply the order rows and the local certificate. The rows $O,T,B$ turn the finite window into a product-order induction witness, while $H,C,Q,D$ transport, replay, source, and name the same packet. Algebra-facing consumers may reuse this handoff as finite combinatorics, but the packet does not contain Noetherian-ring stabilization, a Hilbert-basis construction, or any choice-based extraction principle.
\end{proof}
