\label{def:separated-uniform-completion-carrier}

$$

\item $F$ is the $\CauchyFilterUp$ row, recording the Cauchy-filter demands accepted over the displayed uniform source.
\item $R$ is the separated-reflection row, identifying only those Cauchy-filter readings that the displayed uniform tests cannot distinguish.

\begin{theorem}[Separated uniform completion NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:separated-uniform-completion-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\SeparatedUniformCompletionUp$ carrier $S=(U,F,R,Q,E,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\SeparatedUniformCompletionUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the displayed packet, uniform-source exposure through $U$, Cauchy-filter exactness through $F$, separated-reflection non-escape through $R$, completion handoff through $Q$, completion-seal stability through $E$, and structural transport, replay, provenance, and local naming through $H,C,P,N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:separated-uniform-completion-carrier}. The only source and Cauchy rows are $U$ and $F$, so every completion-facing demand is tested against displayed uniform entourages and displayed Cauchy-filter data.

The separated-reflection row is then read before the completion handoff. The row $R$ is the only place where indistinguishable Cauchy-filter readings are compared, and the row $Q$ can be consumed only after that comparison has been displayed.

Finally $E,H,C,P,N$ seal, transport, replay, source, and name the same finite route. Since the carrier has no row for quotient representatives, hidden filter equality, selected limits, countable choice, host equality, trichotomy, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness, none of those principles can serve as a NameCert obligation.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Separated uniform completion Cauchy-filter route]
\label{thm:separated-uniform-completion-cauchy-filter-route}

$$
\CauchyFilterUp \longmapsto \UniformSpaceUp \longmapsto \SeparatedUniformCompletionUp \longmapsto \UniformSpaceCompletionUp \longmapsto \RealUp .

\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with a Cauchy-filter read of the separated completion packet. The row $F$ supplies the only Cauchy-filter data, and $U$ supplies the only uniform entourage source available to test it.

The route then passes through separation. The row $R$ records the displayed indistinguishability boundary before any completion handoff can be read, so no consumer can skip directly from a filter row to a completion seal.

The completion-facing rows are downstream from that boundary. The handoff $Q$ reads the $\UniformSpaceCompletionUp$ surface, while $E,H,C,P,N$ keep the Real-facing seal, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming attached to the same finite packet.
\end{proof}
