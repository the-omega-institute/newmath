\begin{definition}[CountableBaseMetric carrier]
\label{def:countable-base-metric-carrier}

$$
B=(M,T,I,R,S,W,Q,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $M$ is the $\MetricUp$ source row, exposing only the displayed point windows and finite distance-comparison ledger.
\item $T$ is the $\TopologyUp$ open-row readback, naming the topology generated from metric balls after the same metric source has been shown.
\item $I$ is the countable basis index row, recording the finite portion of the basis schedule inspected by the current request.
\item $R$ is the radius ledger row. It stores the positive rational or dyadic radius windows used to name balls around displayed centers.
\item $S$ is the stability row, recording that a smaller displayed radius refines the same basis read without changing the center window or escaping the metric ledger.
\item $W$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ window row by which L10 consumers request finite basis schedules.
\item $Q$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row for the radius comparisons attached to $W$ and $R$.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for metric, topology, index, radius, stability, stream, rational readback, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a finite ball request through $M,T,I,R,S,W,Q,H$.

\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\CountableBaseMetricUp}$ row.

\begin{theorem}[CountableBaseMetric radius stability]
\label{thm:countable-base-metric-radius-stability}
For every accepted $\CountableBaseMetricUp$ packet $B=(M,T,I,R,S,W,Q,H,C,P,N)$, any basis read that narrows a displayed radius remains inside the same finite metric-ball route
$$
M,T,I,R \longmapsto S \longmapsto W,Q,H,C,P,N .
$$
The stability row $S$ preserves the metric center window, countable basis index, radius ledger, StreamName schedule, and RegSeqRat readback under componentwise $\hsame$ transport and displayed $\Cont$ replay.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:countable-base-metric-carrier}. The metric source row $M$, topology row $T$, basis index row $I$, and radius ledger $R$ are the only rows visible before a stability request can be read.

A radius narrowing is therefore interpreted by $S$ as a refinement of the displayed ball data, not as a new open set drawn from a host powerset. The center window and basis index remain attached to $M,T,I,R$.

The L10 rows $W$ and $Q$ read the same narrowed radius through finite stream windows and regular rational comparisons. Transport through $H$ and replay through $C$ preserve the named route, while $P,N$ record provenance and local naming.

Since selected dense points, countable choice, quotient stream equality, open-cover compactness, and ambient completeness do not occur among the coordinates of $B$, radius stability is confined to the displayed finite route.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}
