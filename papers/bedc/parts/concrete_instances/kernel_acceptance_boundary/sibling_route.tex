\begin{theorem}[Kernel acceptance boundary sibling route]
\label{thm:kernel-acceptance-boundary-sibling-route}
Every $\KernelAcceptanceBoundaryUp$ consumer routes through the existing boundary sibling \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-kernel-observation-sieve-namecert} or through the kernel-facing siblings already named by the child inputs of this chapter. Concretely, the carrier dependency is
$$
Q=(G,K,A,E,L,F,H,C,P,N),
$$
where $G$ is the generated-candidate row, $K$ is the classifier stamp row, $A$ is the accepted-declaration row, $E$ and $L$ are the environment and replay ledgers, $F$ is the axiom-query refusal row, and $H,C,P,N$ are the transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows. A consumer may not detach the classifier stamp $K$ from the accepted-declaration route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-kernelacceptancewitness-namecert}, the refusal route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-axiomqueryledger-namecert}, or the accepted-and-rejected observation route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-kernel-observation-sieve-namecert}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier through \autoref{def:kernel-acceptance-boundary-carrier}. The classifier dependency is the displayed pair of the candidate row $G$ and classifier stamp row $K$, followed by the accepted declaration row $A$ and the refusal row $F$.

The already-input child files identify the sibling rows that feed those coordinates. The accepted-declaration route passes through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-kernelacceptancewitness-namecert}, the refusal route passes through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-axiomqueryledger-namecert}, and the observation-facing boundary is supplied by \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-kernel-observation-sieve-namecert}.

Finally $E,L,H,C,P,N$ keep the same route visible as environment ledger, replay ledger, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming data. Since no carrier row stores a detached classifier, bare kernel acceptance, or semantic theorem-name adequacy, every consumer must follow one of the displayed sibling routes.
\end{proof}
