\begin{theorem}[KernelAcceptanceBoundary sibling route]
\label{thm:kernelacceptanceboundary-sibling-route}
Every accepted $\KernelAcceptanceBoundaryUp$ carrier exposes its accepted-declaration row only after the $\KernelAcceptanceWitnessUp$ source route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-kernelacceptancewitness-namecert} and the $\AxiomQueryLedgerUp$ forbidden-ancestry refusal route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-axiomqueryledger-namecert} have both been displayed. An L10 selector may cite the boundary only as an audit route, not as Real completeness, selector existence, kernel self-correctness, compiler correctness, or semantic adequacy.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier as
$$
Q=(G,K,A,E,L,F,H,C,P,N).
$$
The row $A$ records the accepted declaration that crossed the kernel boundary, and it is read through the $\KernelAcceptanceWitnessUp$ source route.

The row $F$ records the forbidden-ancestry query that refuses hidden axiom ancestry, and it is read through the $\AxiomQueryLedgerUp$ refusal route.

Only after $A$ and $F$ are displayed may the local naming row $N$ be consumed. The replay and provenance rows preserve that order, so an L10 selector receives an audit boundary and none of the excluded Real, selector, kernel-correctness, compiler-correctness, or semantic-adequacy conclusions.

The downstream $\MachineExportBoundaryUp$ consumer of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-machine-export-boundary-namecert} reads $\KernelAcceptanceBoundaryUp$ only after the accepted declaration and axiom-query refusal rows are both visible, so machine-facing export cannot consume a bare kernel acceptance row.
\end{proof}
