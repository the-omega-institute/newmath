\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\KernelAcceptanceBoundaryUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-kernel-acceptance-boundary-namecert}
\origin{human}

The $\KernelAcceptanceBoundaryUp$ packet records the finite audit boundary inherited from the Lean kernel acceptance tradition: a generated candidate is either accepted as a declaration in an environment or refused before it becomes a BEDC theorem row. The packet concretizes \autoref{ch:visions-kernel-acceptance-as-audit-witness} as displayed NameCert data over generated candidates, kernel classifier stamps, environment ledger rows, accepted declarations, axiom-query refusal, replay, provenance, and local naming. It is a finite audit boundary for formal content, not a proof of the kernel implementation, not a semantic interpretation of a theorem name, and not an additional logical axiom.
It consumes $\KernelAcceptanceWitnessUp$ for accepted-declaration rows via \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-kernelacceptancewitness-namecert} and $\AxiomQueryLedgerUp$ for forbidden-ancestry refusal rows via \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-axiomqueryledger-namecert}. It also reads the accepted-declaration trace discipline of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-kernelacceptancetrace-namecert} and the generator-audit closure surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-generatorauditclosure-namecert}; those siblings supply trace replay and closure-classifier rows while this boundary binds them to candidate acceptance and refusal.

\begin{definition}[Kernel acceptance boundary carrier]
\label{def:kernel-acceptance-boundary-carrier}
A $\KernelAcceptanceBoundaryUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
Q=(G,K,A,E,L,F,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $G$ is the generated candidate row: a proposed declaration name, type, and proof term or inductive signature.
\item $K$ is the kernel classifier stamp recording acceptance or refusal at the boundary.
\item $A$ is the accepted declaration row, present only when the kernel classifier admits the candidate.
\item $E$ is the environment ledger row containing the finite imported declarations and namespace context read by the classifier.
\item $L$ is the ledger row for build replay, source location, and command provenance.
\item $F$ is the axiom-query refusal row: forbidden ancestry, unresolved marker targets, and non-kernel semantic claims are refused as coordinates of the accepted declaration.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for generated candidate, classifier, declaration, environment, ledger, and refusal rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from candidate generation to kernel classification, environment lookup, accepted declaration exposure, axiom query, and refusal boundary.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\KernelAcceptanceBoundaryUp$, generated candidates, accepted declarations, environment ledgers, axiom-query refusal, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\KernelAcceptanceBoundaryUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $G,K,A,E,L,F,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for kernel self-correctness, compiler correctness, hardware trust, semantic adequacy of theorem names, or paper-side closure status.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Kernel acceptance boundary NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:kernel-acceptance-boundary-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\KernelAcceptanceBoundaryUp$ carrier
$$
Q=(G,K,A,E,L,F,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\KernelAcceptanceBoundaryUp}$ surface consists exactly of candidate exposure, kernel classifier stamping, accepted declaration exposure, environment ledger display, replay ledger display, axiom-query refusal, componentwise transport, continuation replay, provenance, and local naming. A BEDC audit consumer may cite the boundary only through those finite rows, and it may not replace them by a claim of kernel self-verification, semantic adequacy, compiler correctness, or hardware correctness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier in \autoref{def:kernel-acceptance-boundary-carrier}. The generated candidate, classifier stamp, accepted declaration, environment ledger, replay ledger, and refusal row are all displayed coordinates of the same finite packet.

The kernel-facing obligation is the boundary row $K$ together with $A$ and $E$. Acceptance of a declaration is visible only as the classifier stamp read in the displayed environment; refusal is likewise visible as a boundary stamp rather than as a semantic explanation of the candidate.

The audit-facing obligation is $F$. It records that forbidden ancestry, unresolved marker targets, and non-kernel semantic claims are not accepted-declaration coordinates, so a downstream BEDC audit cannot turn kernel acceptance into unlimited certification.

Finally $H$, $C$, $P$, and $N$ replay and name the finite route. Since no row stores kernel self-correctness, compiler correctness, hardware trust, or paper-side closure status, the local NameCert obligations stop exactly at the accepted-declaration and axiom-query boundary.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Kernel acceptance boundary carrier admission]
\label{thm:kernel-acceptance-boundary-carrier-admission}
For every finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
Q=(G,K,A,E,L,F,H,C,P,N)
$$
accepted by $\KernelAcceptanceBoundaryUp$, carrier admission is exactly the display of generated-candidate rows, kernel classifier stamps, accepted-declaration exposure, environment-ledger rows, replay ledgers, axiom-query refusal rows, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local $\NameCert$ evidence. No consumer can enter the carrier through a hidden theorem-name semantics row, compiler-correctness row, hardware row, or paper-closure row.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the coordinates of $Q$ from \autoref{def:kernel-acceptance-boundary-carrier}. The generated candidate row $G$, classifier stamp $K$, accepted declaration row $A$, environment ledger $E$, replay ledger $L$, and axiom-query refusal row $F$ are the data admitted into the finite carrier.

The transport and replay coordinates $H$ and $C$ keep admission stable under componentwise $\hsame$ transport and displayed $\Cont$ replay. The provenance and naming coordinates $P$ and $N$ then package the same finite route as $\Pkg$ provenance and local $\NameCert$ evidence.

Since the carrier definition lists no hidden coordinate for kernel self-correctness, compiler correctness, hardware trust, theorem-name semantics, or paper closure, carrier admission cannot read such data. It remains a projection of the displayed packet rows.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Kernel acceptance boundary accepted-declaration exposure]
\label{thm:kernel-acceptance-boundary-accepted-declaration-exposure}
In every accepted $\KernelAcceptanceBoundaryUp$ packet
$$
Q=(G,K,A,E,L,F,H,C,P,N),
$$
the accepted declaration row $A$ is exposed only together with the generated candidate $G$, classifier stamp $K$, environment ledger $E$, replay ledger $L$, axiom-query refusal row $F$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport $H$, $\Cont$ replay $C$, $\Pkg$ provenance $P$, and local $\NameCert$ row $N$. The exposure route is the accepted-declaration source row supplied by \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-kernelacceptancewitness-namecert}, with forbidden-ancestry refusal supplied by \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-axiomqueryledger-namecert}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Read the accepted declaration row $A$ from the carrier packet. It is not isolated: the same packet displays the generated candidate $G$, the classifier stamp $K$, and the environment ledger $E$ that record where the declaration was admitted.

Next read $L$ and $F$. The replay ledger keeps the finite command route visible, while the axiom-query refusal row keeps forbidden ancestry and unresolved target queries outside the accepted-declaration coordinates.

Finally use $H$, $C$, $P$, and $N$ to transport, replay, package, and name the exposure route. The cited sibling chapters provide the accepted-declaration and forbidden-ancestry source rows; the theorem asserts only that the local packet exposes those rows, not that the kernel implementation is semantically certified.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Kernel acceptance boundary replay nonescape]
\label{thm:kernel-acceptance-boundary-replay-nonescape}
Every $\Cont$ replay of a $\KernelAcceptanceBoundaryUp$ packet
$$
Q=(G,K,A,E,L,F,H,C,P,N)
$$
remains confined to generated-candidate rows, classifier stamps, accepted-declaration rows, environment ledgers, replay ledgers, axiom-query refusal rows, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local $\NameCert$ evidence. In particular, replay cannot manufacture a theorem from forbidden ancestry, unresolved marker targets, non-kernel semantic claims, or any unstored kernel-correctness premise.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the displayed replay coordinate $C$. By carrier definition, $C$ replays the path from candidate generation through kernel classification, environment lookup, accepted declaration exposure, axiom query, and refusal boundary.

The transport coordinate $H$ is componentwise over the same rows, so replay after $\hsame$ transport still reads only $G,K,A,E,L,F,H,C,P,N$. The provenance and naming rows $P$ and $N$ record that finite route as package and local certificate data.

The refusal coordinate $F$ blocks the only listed escape route: forbidden ancestry, unresolved marker targets, and non-kernel semantic claims are refusal data rather than accepted-declaration data. Since no unstored kernel-correctness premise appears in the carrier, replay non-escape follows by projection of the packet rows.
\end{proof}
