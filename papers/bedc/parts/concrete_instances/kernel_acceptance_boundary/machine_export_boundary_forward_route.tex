\begin{theorem}[KernelAcceptanceBoundary machine export forward route]
\label{thm:kernel-acceptance-boundary-machine-export-forward-route}
Every $\MachineExportBoundaryUp$ consumer of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-machine-export-boundary-namecert} reads the accepted-declaration row and axiom-query refusal row from $\KernelAcceptanceBoundaryUp$ before any machine-facing export is public. \end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the $\KernelAcceptanceBoundaryUp$ carrier. Its accepted-declaration and axiom-query refusal coordinates are the only rows that can certify that a generated declaration became accepted while forbidden ancestry remained refused.

Now read the downstream $\MachineExportBoundaryUp$ carrier from \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-machine-export-boundary-namecert}. A machine-facing export may expose its target only after audit readback and refusal preservation have been displayed, so it must consume the kernel-side accepted-declaration and refusal rows before public export.

The structural rows preserve the route. Componentwise $\hsame$, displayed $\Cont$, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local $\NameCert$ naming keep the export tied to the same kernel boundary, ruling out a detached target, semantic adequacy claim, compiler-correctness claim, or hardware-trust claim.
\end{proof}
