\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\FiniteUnionBoundUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-finite-union-bound-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Boole's inequality and elementary finite probability, the $\FiniteUnionBoundUp$ packet records finite union-bound data over displayed $\ProbSpaceUp$ event windows, not a sigma-completion or almost-sure quotient. It sits after the probability-space source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-probspace-namecert}, the random-variable observation surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-randomvar-namecert}, and the distribution readback surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-distribution-namecert}; the finite ledger is then available to the Borel--Cantelli route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-borel-cantelli-namecert}.

\begin{definition}[FiniteUnionBound carrier]
\label{def:finite-union-bound-carrier}
A $\FiniteUnionBoundUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
U=(\Omega,E,W,S,B,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\Omega$ is the $\ProbSpaceUp$ source row naming the probability carrier from which every event probability is read.
\item $E$ is the finite event-family row, listing the displayed event names and their membership readbacks.
\item $W$ is the finite union-window row, recording the inspected finite subfamily and the displayed union event produced from it.
\item $S$ is the probability-sum ledger, recording the finite sum of the probabilities of the events listed by $W$.
\item $B$ is the bound-comparison row, recording that the probability of the displayed union event is bounded by the ledger value in $S$.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the probability source, event family, union window, sum ledger, bound comparison, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records the displayed $\Cont$ replay by which downstream consumers read $\Omega,E,W,S,B$ in that order.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\FiniteUnionBoundUp$, $\ProbSpaceUp$, $\RandomVarUp$, $\DistributionUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\FiniteUnionBoundUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier contains no coordinate for a completed sigma-algebra, countable union, quotient almost-sure equality, independence converse, limiting theorem, or host probability space outside the displayed finite event rows.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[FiniteUnionBound NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:finite-union-bound-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\FiniteUnionBoundUp$ carrier $U=(\Omega,E,W,S,B,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\FiniteUnionBoundUp}$ surface consists exactly of five finite obligation rows: carrier exposure for the displayed tuple; event-window exactness linking $E$ and $W$ to the union event read in $\Omega$; probability-sum ledger exposure through $S$; bound-comparison exposure through $B$; and transport, replay, provenance, and local naming through $H,C,P,N$. No finite union-bound obligation is stored outside these rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier. The tuple $U=(\Omega,E,W,S,B,H,C,P,N)$ exposes the probability source, event family, union window, sum ledger, comparison row, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.

Read the event-window row before the probability ledger. The window $W$ selects only entries from $E$ and names the displayed finite union event in the source $\Omega$, so the bound has a fixed finite domain.

The sum and comparison rows then give the certificate surface. The row $S$ lists the finite probability sum, and $B$ compares the probability of the displayed union event with that ledger value. Transport through $H$ and replay through $C$ preserve exactly these rows, while $P,N$ record provenance and naming.

Since no remaining coordinate names a sigma-completion, countable union, quotient almost-sure equality, independence converse, or limiting theorem, the five listed obligations exhaust the local certificate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[FiniteUnionBound window-sum ledger]
\label{thm:finite-union-bound-window-sum-ledger}
Every finite union read of an accepted $\FiniteUnionBoundUp$ packet factors through the displayed route
$$
\Omega,\ E,\ W,\ S,\ B.
$$
The event family and union window determine the inspected finite subfamily before the probability-sum ledger is available, and the bound comparison reads only that ledger. Thus a consumer cannot replace the finite event-window sum by an ambient countable union, external subadditivity oracle, or quotient event equality while preserving the packet.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the finite event-family row $E$ and the window row $W$. They determine the displayed union event and the finite list of event probabilities to be inspected in $\Omega$.

The probability-sum ledger $S$ is then the only row that records the bound value. The comparison row $B$ reads the union-event probability and compares it with that value, so every accepted finite union read has passed through the same window-sum ledger.

Finally $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the route without adding another probability object. Coordinate exhaustion excludes countable unions, external subadditivity, and quotient event equality from the accepted read.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[FiniteUnionBound Borel--Cantelli handoff]
\label{thm:finite-union-bound-borel-cantelli-handoff}
Every $\BorelCantelliUp$-facing consumer that requests a finite union estimate receives the $\FiniteUnionBoundUp$ ledger before any tail-sum reasoning is exposed. The handoff supplies the probability source row $\Omega$, finite event family $E$, union window $W$, probability-sum ledger $S$, bound comparison $B$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local $\NameCert$ row; it supplies no sigma-completion, countable-choice selector, almost-sure quotient equality, independence converse, or infinite limiting theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted $\FiniteUnionBoundUp$ packet and read the route of \autoref{thm:finite-union-bound-window-sum-ledger}. A Borel--Cantelli finite-window request first receives $\Omega,E,W,S,B$ as displayed rows.

Only after this finite ledger is present may the downstream Borel--Cantelli packet ask for a tail-sum route. The handoff therefore separates the Boole-inequality ledger from the later tail-event accounting.

Transport, replay, provenance, and naming are inherited through $H,C,P,N$. Since the handoff has no coordinate for sigma-completion, countable choice, almost-sure quotient equality, independence converse, or an infinite limiting theorem, none of those claims is exported to the Borel--Cantelli consumer.
\end{proof}
