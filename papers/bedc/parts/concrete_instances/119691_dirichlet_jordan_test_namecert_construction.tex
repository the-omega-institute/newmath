Following Jordan and Dirichlet on bounded variation and trigonometric series, the $\DirichletJordanTestUp$ packet records the finite BEDC surface needed to hand a bounded-variation row to a Fourier partial-sum consumer through a Dirichlet-kernel window. It sits between the bounded-variation carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-boundedvariation-namecert}, the Fourier row of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-fourier-namecert}, the kernel window of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dirichlet-kernel-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal real seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names the classical test as finite NameCert data without claiming host pointwise convergence, chosen representatives, countable choice, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[Dirichlet--Jordan test carrier]
\label{def:dirichlet-jordan-test-carrier}
A $\DirichletJordanTestUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
D=(B,K,F,O,R,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $B$ is the bounded-variation row read from $\BoundedVariationUp$, naming the finite partition-variation ledger available to the test.
\item $K$ is the $\DirichletKernelUp$ window row, listing the displayed trigonometric-kernel indices and dyadic tail window under inspection.
\item $F$ is the $\FourierUp$ partial-sum row, exposing only the finite coefficient and summation data consumed with $K$.
\item $O$ is the dyadic oscillation-budget row that bounds the inspected jump and adjacent-window oscillation data for the same bounded-variation ledger.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row for the coefficient, kernel, and oscillation windows.
\item $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal row for the displayed real-valued reads.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the bounded-variation, kernel-window, Fourier partial-sum, oscillation-budget, readback, Real-seal, replay, provenance, and naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a test request through $B,K,F,O,R,E$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\BoundedVariationUp$, $\FourierUp$, $\DirichletKernelUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and sibling $\NameCert$ packets.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\DirichletJordanTestUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted carriers only through $B,K,F,O,R,E,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for a host limit of Fourier partial sums, a selected representative of the underlying real function, countable choice over partitions, or ambient real completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Dirichlet--Jordan test NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:dirichlet-jordan-test-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\DirichletJordanTestUp$ carrier $D=(B,K,F,O,R,E,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\DirichletJordanTestUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission, bounded-variation ledger exposure, Dirichlet-kernel window exposure, Fourier partial-sum exposure, dyadic oscillation-budget exactness, regular rational readback, Real sealing, componentwise transport, displayed replay, provenance, and local naming.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:dirichlet-jordan-test-carrier}. The displayed rows expose the bounded-variation ledger, the Dirichlet-kernel window, the Fourier partial-sum row, the dyadic oscillation budget, the regular rational readback, and the Real seal before any consumer can replay the test.

The row order fixes the obligation surface. A Fourier-facing read first consumes the $\BoundedVariationUp$ row, then the $\DirichletKernelUp$ and $\FourierUp$ rows for the inspected partial sum, and only then reads the oscillation and regular-rational budgets through $O$ and $R$.

The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same finite data. Since no carrier coordinate stores a host convergence theorem, a selected representative, countable choice over partitions, or ambient real completeness, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Dirichlet--Jordan Fourier handoff]
\label{thm:dirichlet-jordan-test-fourier-handoff}
Every Fourier consumer of an accepted $\DirichletJordanTestUp$ carrier reads the bounded-variation ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-boundedvariation-namecert}, the kernel window of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dirichlet-kernel-namecert}, the partial-sum row of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-fourier-namecert}, and the regular readback and Real seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert} through the single finite route
$$
B,K,F,O,R,E,H,C,P,N.
$$
The handoff exports only the displayed finite test surface; it does not assert pointwise convergence of the Fourier series in the host theory.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with an accepted carrier. By \autoref{thm:dirichlet-jordan-test-namecert-obligations}, the Fourier consumer receives the bounded-variation row $B$ before it reaches the kernel and partial-sum rows $K$ and $F$.

The oscillation-budget row $O$ and the regular readback row $R$ keep the inspected jump, adjacent-window, coefficient, and kernel data finite. The Real seal $E$ is therefore read only after those displayed rational windows have been exposed.

Finally $H,C,P,N$ preserve the handoff as one named BEDC packet. No row in that packet selects a Fourier-series limit, quotients real-valued functions, chooses a representative, or imports ambient completeness, so the handoff remains a finite sibling route rather than a host convergence theorem.
\end{proof}
