\begin{theorem}[Cauchy setoid modulus extraction route]
\label{thm:cauchy-equivalence-setoid-cauchy-modulus-extraction-route}
Every $\CauchyEquivalenceSetoidUp$ consumer that needs an explicit representative-tail schedule must route the tail read through $\CauchyModulusBridgeUp$ before it asks $\RegSeqRatUp$ for finite-window readback or $\RealUp$ for terminal seal rows. The extraction route is
$$
\CauchyEquivalenceSetoidUp \longmapsto \CauchyModulusBridgeUp \longmapsto \RegSeqRatUp \longmapsto \RealUp .
$$
Thus the setoid interface remains a finite schedule route for displayed representative tails, not a quotient-only sink and not a source of selected representatives, quotient Real equality, countable choice, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the $\CauchyEquivalenceSetoidUp$ consumer to the representative-tail rows it actually reads. An explicit tail schedule is not a bare setoid verdict: it contains a displayed request for the point at which the two representative reads are compared.

Route that request through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-cauchy-modulus-bridge-namecert}. The $\CauchyModulusBridgeUp$ ledger is the finite surface that mediates the schedule before any regular rational readback can be consumed.

After the bridge row is visible, the consumer may read the $\RegSeqRatUp$ finite windows and then the terminal $\RealUp$ seal rows. Since no step introduces a quotient representative, a selected limit, a choice schedule, or an ambient completeness coordinate, the setoid cannot be used as a quotient-only sink.
\end{proof}
