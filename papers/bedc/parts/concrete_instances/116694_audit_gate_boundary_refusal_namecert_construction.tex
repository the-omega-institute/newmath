The $\AuditGateBoundaryRefusalUp$ packet realizes the audit-gate boundary vision of \autoref{ch:visions-audit-gates-as-transcendental-boundary} as finite NameCert data. It records the refusal surface where a source axiom scan, strict proof-ancestry report, paper-Lean name-resolution check, and origin-boundary ledger reject a proposed internal row instead of converting the failed audit into substrate theorem content. The packet sits beside the existing audit-gate boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-auditgateboundary-namecert}, the borrowed-recursor boundary named in the source vision, and the axiom-refusal discipline enforced by the Lean and paper audits.

\begin{definition}[Audit-gate boundary refusal carrier]
\label{def:audit-gate-boundary-refusal-carrier}
An $\AuditGateBoundaryRefusalUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
G=(S,V,A,R,B,T,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $S$ is the gate-surface row, naming the checked syntactic, proof-ancestry, import, or paper-Lean marker condition.
\item $V$ is the verdict row, recording pass or refusal together with the finite diagnostic token, dependency, import edge, or unresolved name that triggered the verdict.
\item $A$ is the audit-replay row, exposing the deterministic replay route by which the verdict is recomputed from $S$.
\item $R$ is the refusal row, recording that a failed gate supplies no internal proof row, no theorem row, and no carrier coordinate for the rejected content.
\item $B$ is the borrowed-recursor boundary row, separating host checking infrastructure from forbidden host axiom propagation.
\item $T$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $S,V,A,R,B$.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay through the same gate surface, verdict, replay, refusal, and boundary rows.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\AuditGateBoundaryRefusalUp$, $\AuditGateBoundaryUp$, $\AxiomRefusalLedgerUp$, $\BorrowedRecursorBoundaryUp$, $\AuditAxisSeparationUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\AuditGateBoundaryRefusalUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $S,V,A,R,B,T,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate that turns an audit failure into an internal proof, no bridge from failed verdicts to theorem rows, and no route that collapses the theory and verification axes.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Audit-gate boundary refusal NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:audit-gate-boundary-refusal-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\AuditGateBoundaryRefusalUp$ carrier
$$
G=(S,V,A,R,B,T,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\AuditGateBoundaryRefusalUp}$ surface consists exactly of gate-surface exposure through $S$, verdict exposure through $V$, deterministic audit replay through $A$, refusal noninternalization through $R$, borrowed-recursor boundary separation through $B$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport through $T$, displayed $\Cont$ replay through $C$, $\Pkg$ provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:audit-gate-boundary-refusal-carrier}. The gate row $S$ and verdict row $V$ are the only rows that can describe the checked boundary and its result.

Read the replay and refusal rows next. The row $A$ recomputes the verdict from the displayed gate surface, while $R$ records that a failed verdict exports no proof row and no theorem content.

The borrowed-recursor row $B$ keeps host checking infrastructure separate from forbidden proof ancestry. Transport, replay, provenance, and local naming through $T,C,P,N$ preserve the same refusal surface, so a failed audit cannot be reclassified as an internal BEDC proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Audit-gate boundary refusal noninternalization]
\label{thm:audit-gate-boundary-refusal-noninternalization}
Every consumer of an accepted $\AuditGateBoundaryRefusalUp$ packet must read the gate surface, verdict, audit replay, refusal row, borrowed-recursor boundary row, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming before it can report an audit result. If the verdict row records failure, the packet exports only the refusal and diagnostic surface; it exports no internal proof row, no checked theorem row for the rejected content, no hidden axiom, and no theory-axis closure upgrade.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the verdict row $V$. A consumer cannot inspect the verdict without the gate surface $S$ and replay row $A$, because the packet contains no detached result coordinate.

When $V$ records failure, the refusal row $R$ is the only downstream content attached to that verdict. It says that the failed item remains outside the substrate theorem surface.

The borrowed-recursor boundary row $B$ prevents the consumer from using host checking machinery as a replacement proof source. The structural rows $T,C,P,N$ preserve the same displayed route, so failure can be replayed and named but not internalized.
\end{proof}

\falsifiablePrediction{No accepted $\AuditGateBoundaryRefusalUp$ packet can turn an audit failure into an internal proof row; any such packet must either change the verdict row to pass by displayed replay or remain a refusal surface with no theorem-content export.}

\independenceWitness{AxiomRefusalLedger, BorrowedRecursorBoundary, AuditAxisSeparation, AuditGateBoundary: $\AuditGateBoundaryRefusalUp$ is not bijective to any listed sibling because it jointly carries gate-surface data, verdict rows, audit replay, refusal noninternalization, borrowed-recursor boundary separation, transport, replay, provenance, and local NameCert rows.}

\begin{theorem}[Audit-gate boundary refusal follow-up requirements]
\label{thm:audit-gate-boundary-refusal-follow-up-requirements}
The Lean follow-up surface for $\AuditGateBoundaryRefusalUp$ requires a nontrivial instance showing that at least one displayed failed gate is carried as a refusal row, and a field-faithful instance showing that changing any of $S,V,A,R,B,T,C,P,N$ changes the accepted refusal packet. These requirements are targets for future declarations named by the $\mathsf{Nontrivial}$ and $\mathsf{FieldFaithful}$ instance patterns over $BEDC.Derived.AuditGateBoundaryRefusalUp$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The nontrivial requirement reads the verdict and refusal rows together. A carrier whose failed verdict cannot be distinguished from a pass verdict would not expose boundary refusal.

The field-faithful requirement reads each carrier coordinate. If any row can be changed without changing the accepted packet, a consumer could hide the gate surface, verdict, replay route, refusal, boundary row, transport, replay, provenance, or local name.

Thus the two follow-up requirements are exactly the instance obligations needed before this packet can be promoted from paper-side refusal surface to checked Lean structure.
\end{proof}
