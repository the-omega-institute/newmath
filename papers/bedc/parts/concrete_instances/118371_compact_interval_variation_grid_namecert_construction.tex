\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\CompactIntervalVariationGridUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-compact-interval-variation-grid-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges constructive analysis, the $\CompactIntervalVariationGridUp$ packet records the finite grid on which bounded variation, Riemann-sum, and compact-interval oscillation consumers inspect an interval function before any ambient real-completeness principle is available. It sits over the compact interval source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-bishop-compact-interval-namecert}, the closed endpoint boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-closedboundedinterval-namecert}, the dyadic tolerance arithmetic of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names a finite variation grid only; it does not choose an optimal partition, quotient real values, or appeal to open-cover compactness.

\begin{definition}[Compact interval variation grid carrier]
\label{def:compact-interval-variation-grid-carrier}
A $\CompactIntervalVariationGridUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
G=(I,K,M,D,V,W,R,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $I$ is the interval-source row, exposing the closed bounded endpoint data through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-closedboundedinterval-namecert}.
\item $K$ is the $\BishopCompactIntervalUp$ row, recording the compact-interval finite inspection surface attached to $I$.
\item $M$ is the finite mesh-grid row, listing ordered cells and adjacent endpoints over the displayed interval source.
\item $D$ is the dyadic tolerance row, read through $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, for mesh width, local oscillation budgets, and variation comparison budgets.
\item $V$ is the finite variation ledger, recording the displayed adjacent changes and the finite sum row that a variation consumer may inspect.
\item $W$ is the window-read row, carrying the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readbacks used at the mesh endpoints and at any displayed refinement cell.
\item $R$ is the $\RealUp$ seal row for the terminal scalar comparisons exported by the packet.
\item $H$ is the componentwise $\hsame$ transport row for interval source, compact row, mesh, dyadic tolerances, variation ledger, window reads, real seal, replay, provenance, and local naming.
\item $C$ is the displayed $\Cont$ replay from an interval-grid request through $I,K,M,D,V,W,R,H,P,N$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\CompactIntervalVariationGridUp$, $\BishopCompactIntervalUp$, $\ClosedBoundedIntervalUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\CompactIntervalVariationGridUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for open-cover compactness, a selected partition outside the displayed grid, quotient equality of real values, countable choice, or ambient completeness of $\RealUp$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Compact interval variation grid NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:compact-interval-variation-grid-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\CompactIntervalVariationGridUp$ carrier
$$
G=(I,K,M,D,V,W,R,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\CompactIntervalVariationGridUp}$ surface consists exactly of the following finite obligation rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item interval and compact-source admission through $I,K$;
\item finite mesh-grid exposure through $M$, with every endpoint and adjacent cell read over the same displayed interval source;
\item dyadic tolerance and variation-ledger exactness through $D,V$;
\item regular readback and real-seal handoff through $W,R$; and
\item componentwise transport, replay, provenance, and local naming through $H,C,P,N$.
\end{enumerate}
No variation-grid obligation is stored outside these five rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Carrier admission is projection from \autoref{def:compact-interval-variation-grid-carrier}. The interval source and Bishop compact interval row are the first displayed coordinates, so every consumer reads $I,K$ before it can inspect the grid.

The finite mesh obligation is projection to $M$. Because the mesh row is attached to the same interval source, endpoint and adjacent-cell reads cannot be detached from the closed bounded interval boundary.

The numerical ledger is read from $D,V$. The dyadic row records the tolerances and comparison budgets, while the variation row records the finite adjacent changes and their finite sum.

The readback and scalar handoff are read from $W,R$. Regular rational windows supply the finite endpoint observations, and the Real row seals only the terminal scalar comparisons already exposed by those windows.

Finally, $H,C,P,N$ exhaust transport, replay, provenance, and local naming. With no further coordinate, the certificate cannot export open-cover compactness, a selected partition outside the grid, quotient equality of real values, countable choice, or ambient Real completeness.
\end{proof}
