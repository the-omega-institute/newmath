\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\KatetovExtensionUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-katetov-extension-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Katetov and Urysohn on finite metric one-point extensions, the $\KatetovExtensionUp$ packet records the standalone finite extension certificate that L10 metric and completion consumers can read before any host Urysohn space is named. It sits over the metric carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}, the separable-metric support schedule of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-separablemetric-namecert}, the isometric handoff surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-isometric-embedding-namecert}, the completion-facing route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metriccompletion-namecert}, the terminal real-distance seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, and the Urysohn finite-extension consumer of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-urysohn-universal-metric-namecert}. The packet exposes a finite support, Real-valued triangle and reverse-triangle budgets, an isometric handoff, and completion-order obligations without claiming a host Urysohn-space existence theorem or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[Katetov extension carrier]
\label{def:katetov-extension-carrier}
A $\KatetovExtensionUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
K=(M,S,R,F,A,I,L,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $M$ is the $\MetricUp$ source row whose finite support distances are inspected.
\item $S$ is the $\SeparableMetricUp$ or finite support schedule row naming exactly the support points used by the one-point request.
\item $R$ is the $\RealUp$ distance-seal row for the support distances and the proposed distances from the new point to each support point.
\item $F$ is the finite Katetov budget row. It lists, for every displayed support pair, the triangle and reverse-triangle inequalities that the proposed distances must satisfy.
\item $A$ is the admission ledger row recording that the inequalities in $F$ have been checked against the same $M,S,R$ surface.
\item $I$ is the $\IsometricEmbeddingUp$ handoff row. It exports the old support points with their displayed distances unchanged before a consumer reads the new-point distances.
\item $L$ is the $\MetricCompletionUp$ order row. It permits completion-facing consumers to read the extension request only after $M,S,R,F,A,I$ have been exposed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the metric source, support schedule, real-distance seal, Katetov budget, admission ledger, isometric handoff, completion order, replay, provenance, and local name.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay for the same finite one-point extension route.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\KatetovExtensionUp$, $\MetricUp$, $\SeparableMetricUp$, $\IsometricEmbeddingUp$, $\MetricCompletionUp$, $\RealUp$, finite Katetov requests, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\KatetovExtensionUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $M,S,R,F,A,I,L,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for a host Urysohn space, an infinite extension chain, quotient completion equality, a selected completion point, countable choice, Lean verification, a standard bridge, or ambient real completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Katetov extension NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:katetov-extension-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\KatetovExtensionUp$ carrier
$$
K=(M,S,R,F,A,I,L,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\KatetovExtensionUp}$ surface consists exactly of six finite obligation rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item carrier admission for the metric source, support schedule, real-distance seal, finite Katetov budget, admission ledger, isometric handoff, completion order, transport, replay, provenance, and local name;
\item common-source discipline, requiring $S,R,F,A,I,L$ to be read over the same metric source row $M$;
\item finite support visibility, requiring every requested new distance to attach to a support point named by $S$;
\item Real-valued Katetov exposure, requiring $F$ and $A$ to display each triangle and reverse-triangle budget through the same $\RealUp$ seal row $R$;
\item isometric and completion order, requiring $I$ to preserve the old support distances before $L$ may be read by a completion-facing consumer;
\item nonescape, excluding host Urysohn spaces, infinite extension chains, quotient completion equality, selected completion points, countable choice, Lean verification, bridge credentials, and ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{enumerate}
No Katetov extension obligation is stored outside these rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:katetov-extension-carrier}. The tuple $M,S,R,F,A,I,L,H,C,P,N$ is the whole public surface, so carrier admission is exactly admission of those rows.

The common-source and finite-support obligations follow from $M$ and $S$. Every proposed new distance is read through a support name displayed by $S$ and through the same metric source row $M$.

The Katetov inequalities are recorded by $F$ and $A$. The budget row lists the triangle and reverse-triangle comparisons, while the admission ledger records that each comparison is checked through the displayed real-distance seal $R$.

Finally $I,L,H,C,P,N$ preserve only that displayed finite route. The old support distances are exported through the isometric handoff before the completion-order row is available, and no carrier coordinate stores a host Urysohn space, quotient completion equality, selected completion point, infinite extension chain, choice functional, Lean credential, bridge credential, or ambient completeness theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Katetov extension isometric handoff]
\label{thm:katetov-extension-isometric-handoff}
Every completion-facing or Urysohn-facing consumer of an accepted $\KatetovExtensionUp$ carrier factors through
$$
\MetricUp \longmapsto \SeparableMetricUp \longmapsto \RealUp \longmapsto F,A \longmapsto \IsometricEmbeddingUp \longmapsto \MetricCompletionUp .
$$
Thus the packet exports a displayed finite one-point metric-extension certificate: the old support is handed off isometrically, the new distances satisfy the displayed Real-valued Katetov budgets, and completion-facing reads occur only after those rows have been exposed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the metric source row $M$ and the support schedule $S$. These rows expose the only old points and support distances available to the extension request.

Read the real-distance row $R$, then the finite Katetov rows $F$ and $A$. They display the proposed new distances and the triangle and reverse-triangle budgets over the same finite support.

The isometric row $I$ then exports the old support without changing its displayed distances. Only after this handoff may the completion-order row $L$ be read.

Consequently any $\UrysohnUniversalMetricUp$ or $\MetricCompletionUp$ consumer receives a finite one-point extension route, not a host universal space, quotient completion construction, selected limit point, choice schedule, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness principle.
\end{proof}
