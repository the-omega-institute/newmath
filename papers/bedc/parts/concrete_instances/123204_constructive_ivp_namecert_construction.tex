\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\ConstructiveIVPUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-constructive-ivp-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges on constructive analysis and standard initial-value problem schemes, the $\ConstructiveIVPUp$ packet records the finite route by which an initial-value problem is read through explicit mesh data, rational moduli, residual bounds, and a regular-Cauchy seal. It sits over the initial-data surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-initial-value-problem-namecert}, the metric source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-space-namecert}, the explicit finite-step route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-euler-method-finite-step-namecert}, the bisection-to-Cauchy handoff of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-bisection-cauchy-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names only displayed finite means; it does not assert classical Peano existence, arbitrary solution choice, quotient equality of paths, global uniqueness, or ambient completeness outside the shown rows.

\begin{definition}[Constructive IVP carrier]
\label{def:constructive-ivp-carrier}
A $\ConstructiveIVPUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
I=(A,M,E,B,R,S,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $A$ is the $\InitialValueProblemUp$ row containing the displayed initial state, interval window, vector-field source, and local reading boundary.
\item $M$ is the finite mesh row: it names the inspected time nodes, step sizes, endpoint cells, and the explicit unary depth at which the approximation is read.
\item $E$ is the $\EulerMethodFiniteStepUp$ row supplying the finite forward-step ledger over the mesh.
\item $B$ is the $\BisectionCauchyUp$ row supplying the interval-retention and diameter-control route used to turn residual signs and mesh refinements into a regular Cauchy read.
\item $R$ is the residual-bound row: it displays rational upper bounds for one-step error, vector-field variation on each mesh cell, and the remaining tolerance budget.
\item $S$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ seal row that records the resulting regular Cauchy rational name before any $\RealUp$ consumer reads it.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the initial-data, mesh, Euler-step, bisection-Cauchy, residual, regular-Cauchy, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay through initial data, mesh refinement, Euler stepping, bisection retention, residual checking, regular-Cauchy readback, provenance, and local naming.
\item $P$ records $\Pkg$ provenance over $\ConstructiveIVPUp$, $\InitialValueProblemUp$, $\MetricSpaceUp$, $\EulerMethodFiniteStepUp$, $\BisectionCauchyUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\ConstructiveIVPUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $A,M,E,B,R,S,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for a Peano existence theorem, arbitrary choice of a solution curve, global uniqueness, a quotient path equality, or an ambient complete-function-space object.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Constructive IVP NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:constructive-ivp-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\ConstructiveIVPUp$ carrier
$$
I=(A,M,E,B,R,S,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\ConstructiveIVPUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the initial-value row, finite mesh row, Euler finite-step ledger, bisection-Cauchy handoff, residual-bound row, regular-Cauchy seal, componentwise transport, displayed replay, package provenance, and local naming. A consumer may cite the constructive IVP route only after those rows have been displayed in the same finite packet.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:constructive-ivp-carrier}. Its analytic rows are precisely $A,M,E,B,R,S$, followed by the structural rows $H,C,P,N$.

The initial-value row $A$ names the local problem before any approximation is read. The mesh row $M$ fixes the finite inspection window, and the Euler row $E$ records the forward-step ledger on that same window.

The bisection-Cauchy row $B$ and residual row $R$ then supply the interval retention, diameter control, one-step error bounds, and remaining tolerance budget. The regular-Cauchy row $S$ is reached only after those finite rows have been displayed.

Since the carrier has no coordinate for classical Peano existence, arbitrary choice, quotient path equality, global uniqueness, or ambient completeness, none of those claims belongs to the local certificate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Constructive IVP bisection regular-Cauchy route]
\label{thm:constructive-ivp-bisection-regular-cauchy-route}
Every Real-facing read of an accepted $\ConstructiveIVPUp$ packet factors through the displayed route
$$
\InitialValueProblemUp \longmapsto \EulerMethodFiniteStepUp \longmapsto \BisectionCauchyUp \longmapsto \RegSeqRatUp \longmapsto \RealUp .
$$
The route may use the finite mesh, explicit modulus, residual bounds, interval retention, and regular-Cauchy seal, but it exports no classical Peano existence theorem, arbitrary solution selector, quotient equality of curves, global uniqueness theorem, or ambient completeness principle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:constructive-ivp-namecert-obligations}. They force the initial-value row, mesh row, Euler-step row, bisection-Cauchy row, residual row, and regular-Cauchy row to appear in one packet before a Real-facing consumer can read the result.

The Euler row supplies only finite forward-step data on the displayed mesh. It is therefore insufficient by itself to name a completed solution curve.

The bisection-Cauchy and residual rows provide the constructive seal: interval retention, diameter control, and rational residual budgets produce the regular-Cauchy readback row $S$.

Finally the Real-facing consumer reads through $S,H,C,P,N$. These rows transport, replay, source, and name the same finite route; none adds a hidden solution selector or a classical existence theorem.
\end{proof}
