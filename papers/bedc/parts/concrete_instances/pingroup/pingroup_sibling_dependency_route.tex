\begin{theorem}[PinGroup sibling dependency route]
\label{thm:pingroup-sibling-dependency-route}
Every $\PinGroupUp$ carrier reads its $\CliffordUp$ multiplication and reflection rows from \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-clifford-namecert} and its $\SpinGroupUp$ even-parity subgroup route from \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-spingroup-namecert} before exposing the local $\NameCert_{\PinGroupUp}$ row. Thus the Pin surface is the finite Clifford reflection-product ledger together with the Spin even-subgroup route and local Pin naming; it exports no host orthogonal group, quotient Clifford equality, or detached parity carrier.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Project the existing PinGroup packet. The Clifford source row is read first, so the multiplication ledger and odd reflection data are available only through the finite $\CliffordUp$ carrier and its $\Cont$ routes.

Next identify the even part through the $\SpinGroupUp$ sibling route. The Spin rows consume the even Clifford surface, while the Pin rows append the visible odd-reflection parity data and the local naming row.

The componentwise $\hsame$ transports and $\Pkg$ provenance keep these reads inside one sibling dependency route. Since the carrier has no coordinate for a host orthogonal group, quotient equality, or detached parity object, the exposed $\NameCert_{\PinGroupUp}$ remains the local finite certificate over Clifford and Spin data.
\end{proof}
