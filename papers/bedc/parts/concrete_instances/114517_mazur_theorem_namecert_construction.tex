Following Mazur and Bourbaki, the $\MazurTheoremUp$ packet records the finite convex weak-closure certificate by which a norm-closed convex claim is read from displayed weak-topology windows without importing a host dual quotient, a selected separating functional, countable choice, or ambient functional-analysis completeness. It sits over the Banach carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-banach-namecert}, the weak-topology observation surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-weak-topology-namecert}, the locally convex seminorm rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-locally-convex-space-namecert}, the convex carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-convexset-namecert}, and the terminal scalar readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}.

\begin{definition}[Mazur theorem carrier]
\label{def:mazur-theorem-carrier}
A $\MazurTheoremUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
M=(B,L,W,V,K,Q,R,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $B$ is the source $\BanachUp$ row, including the normed vector carrier, addition and scalar rows, norm metric, and visible completeness ledger used by norm-facing consumers.
\item $L$ is the $\LocallyConvexSpaceUp$ observation row, exposing only the finite seminorm and neighbourhood tests needed to read weak windows.
\item $W$ is the $\WeakTopologyUp$ finite-window row over $B$ and $L$, naming the inspected weak neighbourhoods and their admissible observation coordinates.
\item $V$ is the $\ConvexSetUp$ row, carrying the displayed convex subcarrier and the affine-combination operations admitted by the packet.
\item $K$ is the convex-combination ledger. It records the finite barycentric coefficients, the inspected points, and the convexity witnesses used to move from weak-window data back to the convex row.
\item $Q$ is the norm-closure handoff row, stating which norm-radius request is being read after the finite convex-combination ledger has been displayed.
\item $R$ is the $\RealUp$ scalar readback row for coefficients, seminorm values, norm radii, and comparison bounds.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for Banach, locally convex, weak-window, convex, convex-combination, norm-closure, scalar, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay of the route
$$
B,L,W,V \longmapsto K \longmapsto Q \longmapsto R .
$$
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\MazurTheoremUp$, $\BanachUp$, $\WeakTopologyUp$, $\LocallyConvexSpaceUp$, $\ConvexSetUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\MazurTheoremUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $B,L,W,V,K,Q,R,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for the whole dual space, quotient weak convergence, a selected separating functional, countable choice, a host closed convex hull, or ambient Banach-space theorem export.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Mazur theorem NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:mazur-theorem-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\MazurTheoremUp$ carrier $M=(B,L,W,V,K,Q,R,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\MazurTheoremUp}$ surface consists exactly of Banach-source admission, locally convex observation admission, weak-topology finite-window exposure, convex-carrier admission, convex-combination ledger exactness, norm-closure handoff visibility, Real scalar readback, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming. Any consumer that reads a weak closure claim as norm-closure data must pass through those rows before the norm-facing claim is visible.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:mazur-theorem-carrier}. The source rows $B,L,W,V$ display the Banach carrier, locally convex observation surface, weak-window ledger, and convex carrier before any closure read is available.

The finite convex step is the ledger $K$. It exposes the barycentric coefficients, inspected points, and convexity witnesses, with scalar comparisons read only through the $\RealUp$ row $R$.

The norm-facing conclusion is read through $Q$ after the weak-window and convex-combination rows have been displayed. Thus a consumer receives a finite norm-closure handoff from the convex ledger, not a quotient weak limit or selected functional.

Finally $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name exactly the same rows. Since the carrier has no coordinate for the whole dual, countable choice, selected separating functionals, or an ambient closed-convex-hull theorem, none of those objects can be exported by the local certificate.
\end{proof}
