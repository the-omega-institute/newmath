\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\SphericalCompletionUp$}

Following Kaplansky and Bourbaki on spherically complete ultrametric spaces, the $\SphericalCompletionUp$ packet records only the constructive finite nested-ball-chain completion boundary. It sits beside the constructive ultrametric completion route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-constructive-ultrametric-completion-namecert}, the metric completion surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metriccompletion-namecert}, the StreamName windows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the RegSeqRat readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal real seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet does not assert maximal spherical completeness, a Zorn-style extension, selected representatives of a host completion, or quotient equality of Cauchy names.

\begin{definition}[Spherical completion carrier]
\label{def:spherical-completion-carrier}
A $\SphericalCompletionUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
X=(U,B,R,S,Q,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $U$ is the ultrametric source row whose balls and strong-triangle comparisons are read before any completion-facing request.
\item $B$ is the finite nested ball-chain row, listing the displayed ball centers and inclusions visible to the packet.
\item $R$ is the radius ledger, recording the finite descent requests attached to the nested chain.
\item $S$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ window row used to expose finite observations of candidate names.
\item $Q$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row for the rational radius and distance data.
\item $E$ is the $\RealUp$ seal that records the terminal real-distance comparison available to completion consumers.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the ultrametric source, ball chain, radius ledger, stream window, readback, seal, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from each completion-facing request through $U,B,R,S,Q,E,H,P,N$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\SphericalCompletionUp$, $\ConstructiveUltrametricCompletionUp$, $\MetricCompletionUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\SphericalCompletionUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier predicate asserts exactly that these rows form one finite nested-ball-chain completion boundary. It has no coordinate for maximal spherical completeness, Zorn extension, quotient completion classes, selected representatives, host ball intersections, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Spherical completion NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:spherical-completion-namecert-obligations}
Every accepted $\SphericalCompletionUp$ carrier of \autoref{def:spherical-completion-carrier} exposes the following finite obligation rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item carrier admission for the displayed ultrametric source $U$, nested ball chain $B$, radius ledger $R$, StreamName window $S$, RegSeqRat readback $Q$, Real seal $E$, transport $H$, replay $C$, provenance $P$, and local NameCert row $N$;
\item nested-chain monotonicity, requiring every later displayed ball in $B$ to be read as contained in the earlier finite rows of the same chain;
\item radius-descent visibility, requiring each radius comparison used by a completion-facing consumer to pass through $R$ and the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback $Q$;
\item readback and seal handoff, requiring every real-distance conclusion to pass from $S$ through $Q$ into the terminal $\RealUp$ seal $E$;
\item nonescape from Zorn arguments, maximal-choice extensions, host intersections, quotient completion classes, selected representatives, countable choice, and ambient completeness.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier tuple. The ten displayed rows are the whole admitted surface, so carrier admission cannot introduce a separate completion object or a hidden ball-intersection row.

Read nested-chain monotonicity before any radius request. Since the only chain coordinate is $B$, each inclusion or later-ball request is compared against the earlier displayed rows of $B$ and transported componentwise by $H$.

Next use the radius ledger, readback, and seal rows. A completion-facing request reaches the radius data through $R$, exposes the finite StreamName window $S$, reads the rational comparison through $Q$, and only then consumes the terminal Real seal $E$.

Finally inspect the replay, provenance, and local naming rows. The displayed $\Cont$ route $C$, $\Pkg$ provenance $P$, and local $\NameCert$ row $N$ name exactly that finite route and contain no coordinate for Zorn, maximal choice, host quotients, selected representatives, or ambient completeness.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Spherical completion ball-chain nonescape]
\label{thm:spherical-completion-ball-chain-nonescape}
Every completion-facing read of an accepted $\SphericalCompletionUp$ packet factors through the displayed finite ball-chain rows
$$
U,B,R,S,Q,E,H,C,P,N .
$$
In particular, a consumer may not replace the chain row $B$ by a maximal nest, the radius ledger $R$ by a hidden convergence modulus, the readback rows $S,Q,E$ by a quotient completion class, or the local certificate $N$ by a host spherical-completeness theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the carrier admission row of \autoref{thm:spherical-completion-namecert-obligations}. It identifies $U,B,R,S,Q,E,H,C,P,N$ as the complete packet inventory.

Use the nested-chain and radius-visibility rows next. They force every ball-chain read through $B$ and every radius descent through $R$ before the StreamName and RegSeqRat rows are available.

Then apply the readback and seal handoff. The only completion-facing Real read passes through $S,Q,E$, so quotient classes, selected representatives, and host limits have no carrier coordinate from which to enter.

The nonescape row excludes the remaining classical routes. Since $H,C,P,N$ only transport, replay, record provenance, and name the displayed finite route, every completion-facing read factors through the stated rows.
\end{proof}
