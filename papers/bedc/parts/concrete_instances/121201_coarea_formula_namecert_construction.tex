\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\CoareaFormulaUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-coarea-formula-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Federer and Evans--Gariepy, the $\CoareaFormulaUp$ packet records the finite BEDC surface by which a displayed Lipschitz or chartwise regular map may hand slice-count data to an integral consumer. It sits after the measure carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-measure-namecert}, the finite integral route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-integral-namecert}, the chart naming of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-manifold-namecert}, the Real-facing seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, and the compact metric window of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}. The packet is a finite coarea ledger over displayed chart, slice, Jacobian, and integral rows; it does not claim unrestricted Lebesgue theory, choice of slices, quotient measure classes, or ambient real completeness.

\begin{definition}[Coarea formula carrier]
\label{def:coarea-formula-carrier}
A $\CoareaFormulaUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
\mathsf{Coarea}(M,X,F,J,S,I,R,K,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $M$ is the $\MeasureUp$ source row for the displayed domain window.
\item $X$ is the $\ManifoldUp$ chart row, naming only the finite chart windows used by the packet.
\item $F$ is the displayed map row, storing the finite source, target, and Lipschitz or chartwise regularity certificates read by the packet.
\item $J$ is the Jacobian-density ledger, recording the finite determinant or metric-volume rows exposed for the displayed chart windows.
\item $S$ is the slice ledger; each entry names a displayed slice window, its admissibility row, and the finite preimage data attached to that slice.
\item $I$ is the $\IntegralUp$ readout row for the domain-side and slice-side finite integral requests.
\item $R$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ comparison row for the displayed values produced by $I$.
\item $K$ is the $\CompactMetricUp$ localization row that bounds the chart and slice windows used by $X,F,J,S,I,R$.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for measure, chart, map, Jacobian, slice, integral, Real, localization, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a coarea request through the finite measure, chart, map, Jacobian, slice, integral, Real, and compact-metric rows.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\CoareaFormulaUp$, $\MeasureUp$, $\IntegralUp$, $\ManifoldUp$, $\RealUp$, $\CompactMetricUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\CoareaFormulaUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
Accepted packets are compared only through these rows. The classifier has no coordinate for unrestricted Lebesgue theory, a slice-choice function, quotient measure classes, a completed ambient real line, or an all-charts theorem outside the displayed finite windows.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Coarea formula NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:coarea-formula-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\CoareaFormulaUp$ carrier $\mathsf{Coarea}(M,X,F,J,S,I,R,K,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\CoareaFormulaUp}$ surface consists exactly of measure-source exposure through $M$, finite chart exposure through $X$, displayed map exposure through $F$, Jacobian-density exposure through $J$, slice-ledger exposure through $S$, Integral readout through $I$, Real comparison through $R$, compact-metric localization through $K$, componentwise transport through $H$, displayed replay through $C$, provenance nonescape through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:coarea-formula-carrier}. The only analytic rows available before the structural rows are the measure source $M$, the chart row $X$, the displayed map row $F$, the Jacobian ledger $J$, the slice ledger $S$, the integral row $I$, the Real comparison row $R$, and the compact-metric localization row $K$.

The slice side is finite. Each slice row in $S$ is tied to a displayed chart window and a displayed preimage entry, and the Jacobian row $J$ is read only through the same chart windows. Thus a consumer cannot replace the ledger by a choice of slices or by a quotient class of measures.

The integral readout is also finite. The row $I$ sends only the displayed domain-side and slice-side requests to $\IntegralUp$, while $R$ records the terminal $\RealUp$ comparisons reached from those requests and $K$ keeps the chart windows localized inside $\CompactMetricUp$.

Finally $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and locally name the same packet. Since no row stores unrestricted Lebesgue theory, a selected slice family, quotient measure classes, or ambient Real completeness, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Coarea formula slice handoff]
\label{thm:coarea-formula-slice-handoff}
Every measure, integration, or geometry consumer of an accepted $\CoareaFormulaUp$ packet that asks for a slice calculation must first read the finite route
$$
\MeasureUp,\ \ManifoldUp,\ F,\ J,\ S,\ \IntegralUp,\ \RealUp,\ \CompactMetricUp \longmapsto H,C,P,N .
$$
The consumer receives only displayed chart windows, map rows, Jacobian-density rows, slice-ledger rows, integral readouts, Real comparisons, compact-metric localization, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming. It receives no unrestricted Lebesgue theory, no choice of slices, no quotient measure class, and no ambient real-completeness theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply \autoref{thm:coarea-formula-namecert-obligations}. A consumer cannot ask for slice data until the measure source, chart window, displayed map, Jacobian ledger, slice ledger, integral readout, Real comparison, and compact-metric localization rows have all been exposed.

The route is local to the displayed windows. The chart row $X$ and localization row $K$ bound the windows, while $F,J,S$ provide only the map, density, and slice records visible in those windows.

The handoff to integration then passes through $I$ and $R$. These rows compare the displayed integral values through $\IntegralUp$ and $\RealUp$, but they do not add an all-slices selector, a quotient measure class, an unrestricted Lebesgue theory, or ambient Real completeness.

The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ preserve that finite route for downstream readers. Therefore a later analysis or geometry packet may cite $\CoareaFormulaUp$ as a slice handoff only at the finite NameCert surface described above.
\end{proof}
