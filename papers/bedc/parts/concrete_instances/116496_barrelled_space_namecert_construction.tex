Following Bourbaki, Schaefer, and Treves on barrelled locally convex spaces, the $\BarrelledSpaceUp$ packet records the finite BEDC surface where a locally convex topological vector space carries enough barrel tests for bounded-family consumers without importing Hahn--Banach, closed graph, uniform boundedness, or a host barrelled-space predicate. It sits after the local-convexity route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-locally-convex-space-namecert}, the topological-vector carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topvecspace-namecert}, the complete-metrizable sibling route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-frechet-space-namecert}, and the functional-analysis consumer boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-functionalanalysis-namecert}.

\begin{definition}[Barrelled space carrier]
\label{def:barrelled-space-carrier}
A $\BarrelledSpaceUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
B=(L,T,F,A,R,M,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $L$ is the $\LocallyConvexSpaceUp$ source row, carrying the finite seminorm ledger and convex balanced neighbourhood evidence.
\item $T$ is the $\TopVecSpaceUp$ source row, carrying the vector topology and the displayed addition and scalar-continuity ledgers.
\item $F$ is the optional $\FrechetSpaceUp$ comparison row used when a complete metrizable locally convex source is available.
\item $A$ is the bounded-family admission row, recording the finite family of linear probes and bounded observation windows seen by the consumer.
\item $R$ is the barrel row, naming the absolutely convex, absorbing, closed neighbourhood test requested by $A$.
\item $M$ is the barrel-to-neighbourhood readback row, recording that each admitted barrel is consumed as a displayed neighbourhood through the locally convex source rather than as an ambient theorem.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $L,T,F,A,R,M$ and the structural rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from locally convex and topological-vector reads to bounded-family admission, barrel tests, neighbourhood readback, provenance, and local naming.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\BarrelledSpaceUp$, $\LocallyConvexSpaceUp$, $\TopVecSpaceUp$, $\FrechetSpaceUp$, $\FunctionalAnalysisUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\BarrelledSpaceUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $L,T,F,A,R,M,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for Hahn--Banach separation, selected dual elements, closed-graph theorems, uniform boundedness principles, quotient functional equality, or a host barrelled-space structure.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Barrelled space NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:barrelled-space-namecert-obligations}
For the carrier of \autoref{def:barrelled-space-carrier}, the local $\NameCert_{\BarrelledSpaceUp}$ surface consists exactly of locally convex source exposure through $L$, topological-vector source exposure through $T$, optional Frechet comparison through $F$, bounded-family admission through $A$, barrel tests through $R$, barrel-to-neighbourhood readback through $M$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport through $H$, displayed $\Cont$ replay through $C$, package provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$. Every $\FunctionalAnalysisUp$ consumer that reads a barrelled-space conclusion must expose those rows before it treats a bounded family as controlled by neighbourhood data.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier $B=(L,T,F,A,R,M,H,C,P,N)$. The locally convex row $L$ supplies the finite seminorm and convex-neighbourhood data, while $T$ supplies the topological-vector continuity source.

The bounded-family row $A$ records the consumer-facing probe family. The barrel row $R$ names the absolutely convex, absorbing, closed test object, and $M$ is the only route by which that barrel is read back as a neighbourhood through the locally convex source.

Finally replay the structural rows. Transport through $H$, continuation through $C$, provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$ preserve the same finite route. Since no carrier coordinate contains Hahn--Banach separation, selected dual elements, closed-graph data, a uniform boundedness theorem, quotient functional equality, or a host barrelled-space predicate, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Barrelled space locally convex route]
\label{thm:barrelled-space-locally-convex-route}
Every accepted $\BarrelledSpaceUp$ packet reaches its barrelled read only through the route
$$
\LocallyConvexSpaceUp,\ \TopVecSpaceUp,\ \FrechetSpaceUp,\ \FunctionalAnalysisUp
\longmapsto
L,T,F,A,R,M,H,C,P,N .
$$
Thus a downstream functional-analysis or weak-star consumer may cite barrelled-space data only after the locally convex source, topological-vector source, bounded-family ledger, barrel row, readback row, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming have been exposed; the route exports no Hahn--Banach principle, selected dual, closed-graph theorem, uniform boundedness theorem, or ambient barrelled topology.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the locally convex and topological-vector siblings. The chapters \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-locally-convex-space-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topvecspace-namecert} provide the only vector-topology and neighbourhood rows that the barrelled packet may inspect.

When a complete metrizable source is relevant, the comparison row $F$ reads \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-frechet-space-namecert}; it does not replace the local convexity route by a host complete locally convex space. The bounded-family row $A$ and barrel row $R$ then stay tied to those displayed sources.

The readback row $M$ converts the barrel test into neighbourhood data inside the same finite packet. Rows $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the route for the $\FunctionalAnalysisUp$ consumer boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-functionalanalysis-namecert}.

Therefore the barrelled-space conclusion is only the displayed locally convex route. None of the cited rows supplies Hahn--Banach separation, selected dual elements, a closed-graph theorem, a uniform boundedness theorem, or an ambient barrelled topology.
\end{proof}
