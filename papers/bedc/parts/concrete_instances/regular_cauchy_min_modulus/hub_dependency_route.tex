\begin{theorem}[Regular Cauchy minimum-modulus hub dependency route]
\label{thm:regular-cauchy-min-modulus-hub-route}
Every accepted $\RegularCauchyMinModulusUp$ packet consumes the $\CauchyModulusMeetUp$ meet row, the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ comparison ledger, the $\StreamNameUp$ finite windows, the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row, and the $\RealUp$ seal before exporting the minimum-modulus row to any downstream consumer. In carrier notation, the route exposes
$$
S_0,S_1,M,m,W,Q,D,E,H,C,P,N
$$
with $M$ read as the meet surface, $D$ read as the dyadic comparison ledger, $W$ read as the finite window service, $Q$ read as the regular-rational readback, and $E$ read as the Real-facing seal.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:regular-cauchy-min-modulus-carrier}. The minimum-modulus row $m$ is not an isolated output; it is displayed inside the finite packet $S_0,S_1,M,m,W,Q,D,E,H,C,P,N$.

Read the sibling route already present in the chapter. The $\CauchyModulusMeetUp$ row $M$ synchronizes the two source schedules before $m$ can be consumed, and the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ row $D$ supplies the comparison ledger that makes the displayed minimum meaningful.

Now read the Real-and-Completion handoff. The $\StreamNameUp$ windows $W$ and $\RegSeqRatUp$ row $Q$ provide the finite readback surface, while $E$ seals the same route for $\RealUp$ consumers. Transport, replay, provenance, and local naming remain attached through $H,C,P,N$, so the hub dependency route exports only the displayed minimum-modulus packet and no private completion or choice coordinate.
\end{proof}
