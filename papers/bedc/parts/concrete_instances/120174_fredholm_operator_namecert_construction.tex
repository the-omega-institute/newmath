Following Fredholm and Riesz operator theory, the $\FredholmOperatorUp$ packet records the finite BEDC carrier by which a displayed bounded linear operator is read together with finite kernel, cokernel-facing obstruction, and index ledger rows. It sits after the bounded-operator surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-boundedlinearoperator-namecert}, the compact-operator route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactoperator-namecert}, the Banach and Hilbert endpoint carriers of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-banach-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-hilbert-namecert}, and the alternative-facing consumer of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-fredholm-alternative-namecert}. The packet supplies the operator/index dependency read by downstream index arguments such as \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-atiyahsinger-namecert}; it does not import a host quotient cokernel, selected complement, ambient index equality, spectral decomposition, Lean verification, or bridge checking.

\begin{definition}[Fredholm operator carrier]
\label{def:fredholm-operator-carrier}
A $\FredholmOperatorUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
F=(X,Y,B,K,E,C,I,A,H,R,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X$ is the source endpoint row, read through a displayed $\BanachUp$ carrier and, when orthogonal data are present, a compatible $\HilbertUp$ carrier.
\item $Y$ is the target endpoint row, read through the same Banach or Hilbert endpoint discipline.
\item $B$ is the $\BoundedLinearOperatorUp$ row naming the displayed linear action and norm-bound ledger from $X$ to $Y$.
\item $K$ is the kernel row, recording the finite kernel witness, finite-dimensional-kernel ledger, or explicit kernel-refusal boundary for $B$.
\item $E$ is the cokernel-facing obstruction row, recording finite codimension evidence, range-defect data, or a displayed refusal to pass to a host quotient.
\item $C$ is the $\CompactOperatorUp$ compatibility row, available when the Fredholm read is consumed through a compact perturbation or compact remainder route.
\item $I$ is the index ledger row, recording only the displayed difference between the kernel side $K$ and the cokernel-facing side $E$ as BEDC data.
\item $A$ is the $\FredholmAlternativeUp$ handoff row, exposing the same kernel-or-obstruction boundary when an alternative-facing consumer reads the operator.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for endpoint, bounded-action, kernel, cokernel-facing, compactness, index, and handoff rows.
\item $R$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from endpoint admission through bounded action, kernel/cokernel-facing exposure, index readback, and handoff.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\FredholmOperatorUp$, $\BoundedLinearOperatorUp$, $\CompactOperatorUp$, $\BanachUp$, $\HilbertUp$, $\FredholmAlternativeUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\FredholmOperatorUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for a host quotient cokernel, selected complement of the range, selected kernel basis, ambient Fredholm index equality, spectral decomposition, parametrix theorem, Lean proof, or standard bridge.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Fredholm operator NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:fredholm-operator-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\FredholmOperatorUp$ carrier
$$
F=(X,Y,B,K,E,C,I,A,H,R,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\FredholmOperatorUp}$ surface consists exactly of endpoint admission through $X,Y$, bounded-action exposure through $B$, kernel exposure through $K$, cokernel-facing obstruction exposure through $E$, compact-operator compatibility through $C$, index-ledger readback through $I$, alternative-facing handoff through $A$, componentwise transport through $H$, displayed replay through $R$, package provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier tuple. The endpoint rows $X,Y$ are the only Banach or Hilbert sources, and the bounded-action row $B$ is the only operator action available to the packet.

The finite Fredholm data are read next. The kernel side is $K$, the cokernel-facing obstruction side is $E$, and the index ledger $I$ records only the displayed comparison between those two rows. Thus the index read cannot be replaced by a host quotient or an ambient dimension theorem.

The compactness and alternative rows are dependency handoffs. The compact row $C$ lets compact-operator consumers recognize the same bounded action, while $A$ exposes the same kernel-or-obstruction boundary to the $\FredholmAlternativeUp$ surface.

Finally $H,R,P,N$ preserve and name the route. Since the carrier contains no quotient cokernel, selected complement, selected basis, spectral decomposition, Lean target, or bridge coordinate, the listed rows exhaust the local certificate obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Fredholm operator index kernel--cokernel handoff]
\label{thm:fredholm-operator-index-kernel-cokernel-handoff}
Every index-facing consumer of an accepted $\FredholmOperatorUp$ packet must read the bounded operator row $B$ over the endpoint rows $X,Y$, then read the kernel row $K$ and the cokernel-facing obstruction row $E$, before consuming the index ledger $I$ or the $\FredholmAlternativeUp$ handoff row $A$. The exported index surface is the finite route
$$
X,Y,B \longmapsto K,E \longmapsto I,A
$$
together with $H,R,P,N$; it does not export a host quotient cokernel, selected range complement, ambient Fredholm index equality, spectral decomposition, Lean verification, or bridge checking.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:fredholm-operator-namecert-obligations}. They place endpoint admission and bounded-action exposure before the kernel, cokernel-facing, index, and handoff rows.

The rows $K$ and $E$ are paired but not identified with host objects. The kernel side records finite kernel evidence, and the cokernel-facing side records obstruction or finite-codimension evidence without passing to an ambient quotient carrier.

The index ledger $I$ is downstream of that pair. It can be consumed only as the displayed BEDC comparison between $K$ and $E$, and the alternative handoff $A$ carries the same boundary into \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-fredholm-alternative-namecert}.

Componentwise $\hsame$ transport, $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local $\NameCert$ naming preserve this same route. Therefore downstream index packets, including the analytic-index side of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-atiyahsinger-namecert}, receive a finite operator/index dependency rather than an external Fredholm operator notion.
\end{proof}
