\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\OdeComparisonUp$}

Following Walter or Hartman on ODE comparison principles, the $\OdeComparisonUp$ packet records the finite BEDC surface by which two displayed ODE rows, one shared interval window, and one monotonicity or majorant ledger can be read before a downstream comparison consumer reaches a scalar bound. It sits after the ODE source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-ode-namecert}, the Gronwall handoff of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-gronwall-inequality-namecert}, the zero-order comparison route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-sturm-comparison-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names comparison data as displayed rows only; it is not a host solution theorem, a selected flow, a quotient path equality, or an ambient completeness principle.

\begin{definition}[ODE comparison carrier]
\label{def:ode-comparison-carrier}
An $\OdeComparisonUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
O=(D_0,D_1,I,L,M,G,S,R,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
whose rows have the following roles.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $D_0$ and $D_1$ are displayed $\OdeUp$ source rows carrying the two differential data packets that will be compared.
\item $I$ is the shared interval or window row over which both ODE source rows are read.
\item $L$ is the located-order or monotonicity ledger for the comparison request, and $M$ is the comparison majorant row.
\item $G$ is the $\GronwallInequalityUp$ handoff used when the comparison is controlled by an integral or differential majorant.
\item $S$ is the $\SturmComparisonUp$ handoff used when the request is a zero-order or oscillation-window comparison.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row attached to the finite comparison windows, and $E$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal.
\item $H$, $C$, $P$, and $N$ are the componentwise $\hsame$ transport row, displayed $\Cont$ replay row, $\Pkg$ provenance row, and local $\NameCert_{\OdeComparisonUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through the displayed tuple $D_0,D_1,I,L,M,G,S,R,E,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for host solution equality, selected flows, quotient paths, trichotomy, global existence, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[ODE comparison NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:ode-comparison-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\OdeComparisonUp$ carrier $O=(D_0,D_1,I,L,M,G,S,R,E,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\OdeComparisonUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission; two ODE source exposures through $D_0,D_1$; shared interval exposure through $I$; monotonicity or majorant exactness through $L,M$; Gronwall and Sturm handoff exactness through $G,S$; regular rational readback through $R$; real-name sealing through $E$; componentwise transport through $H$; displayed replay through $C$; provenance through $P$; and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier tuple of \autoref{def:ode-comparison-carrier}. Each listed obligation is one coordinate of the finite packet or the local naming row attached to that coordinate.

The two ODE source obligations are exactly the projections $D_0$ and $D_1$, and the common-domain obligation is the projection $I$. The comparison ledger is exhausted by $L$ and $M$, so no additional monotonicity or majorant witness remains outside the displayed rows.

The downstream handoff rows are $G$ for Gronwall-style comparison and $S$ for Sturm-style zero-order comparison. Readback and terminal sealing are then forced through $R$ and $E$.

Finally $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, package, and name the same displayed tuple. Coordinate exhaustion leaves no host solution equality, selected flow, quotient path, trichotomy, global existence, or ambient real-completeness coordinate to export.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[ODE comparison Gronwall and Sturm handoff]
\label{thm:ode-comparison-gronwall-sturm-handoff}
Every downstream local-flow or zero-window comparison consumer of an accepted $\OdeComparisonUp$ packet must first read the displayed $\OdeUp$ source rows $D_0,D_1$, the shared interval row $I$, the $\GronwallInequalityUp$ or $\SturmComparisonUp$ handoff row, the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row $R$, the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance ledger underlying the displayed comparison windows, and the terminal $\RealUp$ seal $E$. The consumer receives only the finite comparison route
$$
D_0,D_1,I,L,M,G,S,R,E,H,C,P,N
$$
and no host solution equality, global existence theorem, selected flow, quotient path, trichotomy split, or ambient real-completeness principle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the carrier projection of \autoref{def:ode-comparison-carrier}. The source rows $D_0,D_1$ and the shared window row $I$ are the only places where the comparison request can read ODE and interval data.

For a local-flow comparison, the consumer reads the majorant ledger $M$ through the Gronwall row $G$. For a zero-window comparison, it reads the order or oscillation ledger $L$ through the Sturm row $S$. In both cases the finite windows then pass through the dyadic tolerance ledger, the regular rational readback row $R$, and the terminal Real seal $E$.

The rows $H,C,P,N$ preserve the same packet by componentwise transport, displayed replay, provenance, and local naming. Since the packet has no coordinate for host solution equality, global existence, selected flows, quotient paths, trichotomy, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness, no downstream comparison consumer can read such data from this handoff.
\end{proof}
