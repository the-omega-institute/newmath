\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\StreamShuffleUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-stream-shuffle-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges on sequences in constructive analysis, the
$\StreamShuffleUp$ packet records the finite recombination route by which two
displayed stream-name windows are read through parity-indexed windows before a
regular rational readback and Real-seal handoff are available. It sits beside
the stream carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the
regular rational route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert},
the even/odd Cauchy shuffle of
\autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regular-cauchy-shuffle-namecert}, and the
finite merge boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-stream-merge-namecert}.
The packet is a finite NameCert object; it is not a host stream, quotient
stream equality, selected representative, countable-choice schedule, or
ambient completeness principle.

\begin{definition}[Stream shuffle carrier]
\label{def:stream-shuffle-carrier}
A $\StreamShuffleUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
S=(A,B,E,O,W,Q,R,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $A$ and $B$ are the two source $\StreamNameUp$ rows.
\item $E$ is the even-index window row reading source $A$.
\item $O$ is the odd-index window row reading source $B$.
\item $W$ is the combined finite window exposed to downstream stream
consumers.
\item $Q$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row assembled from the parity-indexed
windows when the shuffled stream is read as a regular rational sequence.
\item $R$ is the Real-seal handoff row available only after $A,B,E,O,W,Q$ have
been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the source, parity,
window, readback, seal, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay of the finite route
$$
A,B,E,O \longmapsto W \longmapsto Q \longmapsto R .
$$
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\StreamShuffleUp$, $\StreamNameUp$,
$\StreamMergeUp$, $\RegularCauchyShuffleUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$,
$\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\StreamShuffleUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through
$A,B,E,O,W,Q,R,H,C,P,N$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Stream shuffle NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:stream-shuffle-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\StreamShuffleUp$ carrier
$$
S=(A,B,E,O,W,Q,R,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\StreamShuffleUp}$ surface consists exactly of the
following five obligation rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item source admission for the two $\StreamNameUp$ rows $A,B$;
\item parity-window exposure through $E,O,W$;
\item $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback through $Q$;
\item Real-seal handoff through $R$;
\item componentwise transport, replay, provenance, and local naming through
$H,C,P,N$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:stream-shuffle-carrier}. The source rows
$A,B$ are displayed before either parity window is read, so the shuffled
stream cannot be introduced as an external host stream.

Next read the parity-indexed windows. The even row $E$ and odd row $O$ expose
finite windows of the two source streams, and the combined row $W$ is the only
window made public to a downstream $\StreamNameUp$ or $\StreamMergeUp$
consumer.

Now attach the readback and seal rows. The $\RegSeqRatUp$ row $Q$ reads the
same parity-indexed window, and the Real-seal handoff $R$ is downstream of
$Q$. This is the point at which the packet can meet the
$\RegularCauchyShuffleUp$ route, but it still exposes only the displayed
finite rows.

Finally use $H,C,P,N$. Componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$
replay, package provenance, and local naming preserve the same carrier
surface. Since no coordinate names quotient stream equality, a selected
representative, countable choice, host stream equality, or ambient
completeness, the five listed obligation rows exhaust the local certificate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Stream shuffle parity-window nonescape]
\label{thm:stream-shuffle-parity-window-nonescape}
Every accepted $\StreamShuffleUp$ consumer reads the shuffled window only
through
$$
A,B,E,O,W,H,C,P,N .
$$
If the consumer asks for regular rational or Real data, the same route
continues through $Q$ and $R$; no consumer receives a separate host stream,
quotient stream equality, selected representative, or countable-choice
schedule.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the source admission and parity rows of
\autoref{thm:stream-shuffle-namecert-obligations}. They force each finite
window read to enter through $A,B,E,O$ before $W$ is public.

For regular rational and Real-facing reads, use the readback and seal rows
$Q,R$. These rows are downstream of the same parity window and cannot select a
second source schedule.

The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ only transport, replay, source, and name the
displayed packet. Therefore the consumer remains inside the finite shuffle
surface and cannot escape to the excluded host or quotient objects.
\end{proof}
