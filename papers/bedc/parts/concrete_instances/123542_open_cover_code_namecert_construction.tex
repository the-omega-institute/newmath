\chapter{OpenCoverCode NameCert construction}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-open-cover-code-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Bishop and Bridges constructive topology, the $\OpenCoverCodeUp$ packet records open-cover data only when the cover has already been coded by finite observable rows. It sits in the L10 Real and Completion corridor after the topology carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert}, the compact-metric naming surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}, the stream-window discipline of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the Real seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, and the countable-choice boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-countable-choice-boundary-namecert}. The packet is a coded-cover boundary object: it names finite cover fragments without importing ambient powerset covers, open-cover compactness, countable choice, quotient equality, or selected subcovers.

\begin{definition}[OpenCoverCode carrier]
\label{def:open-cover-code-carrier}
An $\OpenCoverCodeUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
O=(T,M,S,Q,R,E,F,B,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $T$ is the $\TopologyUp$ indexed-open row from which every coded open name is read.
\item $M$ is the $\CompactMetricUp$ metric-boundary row, used only as a compactness-facing source tag.
\item $S$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ stream-window row naming the finite list of open-code requests.
\item $Q$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ rational readback row attached to those windows.
\item $R$ is the $\RealUp$ seal row reached after $T,S,Q$ have been displayed.
\item $E$ is the finite enumeration row of open-code names and membership tests.
\item $F$ is the finite refinement-candidate row. It records only displayed finite fragments, not a selected subcover.
\item $B$ is the boundary-refusal row excluding ambient powerset covers, open-cover compactness, countable choice, quotient equality, and selected subcovers.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for topology, compact-metric tag, stream window, rational readback, Real seal, finite code enumeration, refinement candidate, boundary refusal, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from $T,M,S,Q,R,E,F,B,H$ to the local name.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\OpenCoverCodeUp$, $\TopologyUp$, $\CompactMetricUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\CountableChoiceBoundaryUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\OpenCoverCodeUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through these twelve rows. It has no coordinate for an ambient powerset cover, a proof of open-cover compactness, a countable-choice principle, quotient equality on Real presentations, a selected subcover, Lean verification, or bridge checking.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[OpenCoverCode NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:open-cover-code-namecert-obligations}
For the carrier of \autoref{def:open-cover-code-carrier}, the local $\NameCert_{\OpenCoverCodeUp}$ surface consists of carrier admission for
$$
O=(T,M,S,Q,R,E,F,B,H,C,P,N),
$$
coded-open visibility through $T$, compact-metric source visibility through $M$, finite stream-window and rational readback visibility through $S,Q$, Real-seal visibility through $R$, finite enumeration and refinement-candidate visibility through $E,F$, boundary-refusal exactness through $B$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport through $H$, displayed $\Cont$ replay through $C$, $\Pkg$ provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier. The packet exposes exactly the rows $T,M,S,Q,R,E,F,B,H,C,P,N$, so each local naming obligation is read from a displayed finite coordinate.

The cover-facing data are $T,S,Q,E,F$. The topology row names coded opens, the stream and regular-rational rows name the finite observation windows, the enumeration row lists the displayed codes, and the refinement row records only finite candidate fragments.

The compactness-facing rows are boundaries rather than compactness theorems. The row $M$ names the compact-metric source surface, $R$ seals the Real-facing route, and $B$ refuses ambient powerset covers, open-cover compactness, countable choice, quotient equality, and selected subcovers.

Finally $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same finite packet. Since no other coordinate exists, the NameCert obligations are exhausted by the displayed coded-cover rows.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[OpenCoverCode finite refinement boundary]
\label{thm:open-cover-code-finite-refinement-boundary}
Every accepted $\OpenCoverCodeUp$ packet may expose a finite refinement-candidate read only through the finite route
$$
T,M,S,Q,R,E,F,B,H,C,P,N .
$$
The route supports later Real and Completion statements that inspect coded finite cover fragments, but it does not export ambient powerset covers, general open-cover compactness, countable choice, quotient equality of Real presentations, or selected subcovers.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with a refinement-facing consumer of $O$. The carrier first exposes the topology row $T$, the compact-metric source tag $M$, the stream window $S$, the rational readback $Q$, and the Real seal $R$ before any finite refinement candidate is available.

Next read $E$ and $F$. The enumeration row lists the displayed open-code names, while the refinement-candidate row records finite fragments already present in the packet. Neither row chooses from an ambient cover family.

Apply the boundary row $B$. It blocks the passage from finite coded fragments to ambient powerset covers, open-cover compactness, countable choice, quotient equality, or selected subcovers.

The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ preserve the same displayed route for transport, replay, provenance, and local naming. Hence the packet is usable as a finite coded-cover boundary in the Real and Completion corridor without importing a compactness or choice principle.
\end{proof}
