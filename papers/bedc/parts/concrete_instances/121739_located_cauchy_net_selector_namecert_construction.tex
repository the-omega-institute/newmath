Following Moore and Smith, Kelley, and Bishop, the $\LocatedCauchyNetSelectorUp$ packet records the finite selector surface needed when a located Cauchy net is read through a regular-Cauchy window before reaching a $\RealUp$ seal. It sits after the located net packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-located-cauchy-net-namecert}, the Moore--Smith source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-moore-smith-net-namecert}, the regular subnet selector of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regular-cauchy-subnet-selector-namecert}, the stream naming row of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the dyadic tolerance core of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, and the real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet selects only a finite located window and its regular readback route; it does not assert a global choice function on all nets, a quotient of directed indices, or ambient completeness of $\RealUp$.

\begin{definition}[Located Cauchy net selector carrier]
\label{def:located-cauchy-net-selector-carrier}
A $\LocatedCauchyNetSelectorUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
S=(D,W,L,T,R,A,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $D$ is the directed index data: finite index names, comparison rows, and upper-bound witnesses used by the local Moore--Smith window.
\item $W$ is the selected finite window, storing the inspected indices and the located net values admitted by the $\LocatedCauchyNetUp$ source.
\item $L$ is the located radius row, recording the dyadic precision request and the located comparison data that make the window inspectable.
\item $T$ is the tail witness row, showing that the selected window lies past the displayed directed threshold for the requested radius.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row obtained from $W,L,T$.
\item $A$ is the $\RealUp$ admission seal reached only after the directed data, selected window, located radius, tail witness, and regular rational readback have been displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $D,W,L,T,R,A$ and the structural rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a located Cauchy-net request through the same finite window, radius, tail, readback, and real-seal route.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\LocatedCauchyNetSelectorUp$, $\LocatedCauchyNetUp$, $\MooreSmithNetUp$, $\RegularCauchySubnetSelectorUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\LocatedCauchyNetSelectorUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $D,W,L,T,R,A,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for a global selector, countable choice, quotient net equality, host real equality, or an unbounded family of selected tails.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Located Cauchy net selector NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:located-cauchy-net-selector-namecert-obligations}
For an accepted $\LocatedCauchyNetSelectorUp$ carrier $S=(D,W,L,T,R,A,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\LocatedCauchyNetSelectorUp}$ surface consists exactly of directed index admission, selected finite-window admission, located radius readback, tail-witness exposure, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback, $\RealUp$ seal admission, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and the local naming row. Every completion-facing consumer must read $D,W,L,T$ before it reads $R$ or $A$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Carrier admission is projection from \autoref{def:located-cauchy-net-selector-carrier}. The rows $D,W,L,T$ display the directed substrate, finite window, located radius, and tail witness before any numerical readback is available.

The regular readback is downstream of those rows. The $\RegSeqRatUp$ coordinate $R$ is read from the selected window and located tail data, while the $\RealUp$ seal $A$ is reached only after that regular readback has been displayed.

Transport, replay, provenance, and local naming are componentwise. Since the tuple contains no global selector, countable-choice schedule, quotient of net indices, host real equality, or unbounded family of tails, the NameCert surface is exactly the finite selector route listed above.
\end{proof}
