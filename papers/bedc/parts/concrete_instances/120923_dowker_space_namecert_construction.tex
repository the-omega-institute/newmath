\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\DowkerSpaceUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-dowker-space-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Engelking and Kelley, the $\DowkerSpaceUp$ packet records the standard topology boundary where normality is visible but countable paracompactness is obstructed by the interval product test. It sits over the topology carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert}, the normal-space surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-normalspace-namecert}, the paracompact comparison of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-paracompact-namecert}, the locally finite cover boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-locally-finite-cover-namecert}, and the product-topology route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-product-topology-namecert}. The packet names the finite boundary data only; it does not import host powersets, selected open covers, open-cover compactness, or a choice principle.

\begin{definition}[Dowker space carrier]
\label{def:dowker-space-carrier}
A $\DowkerSpaceUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
D=(T,N,C,P,I,H,R,Q,Z)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $T$ is the $\TopologyUp$ carrier row for the admitted point and open-set names.
\item $N$ is the $\NormalSpaceUp$ exposure row recording the displayed normal separation source.
\item $C$ is the countable-paracompactness obstruction row; it records the finite refusal boundary rather than a selected countable cover.
\item $P$ is the $\ParacompactUp$ comparison row, read only through the local obstruction and locally finite cover surfaces.
\item $I$ is the interval and product-normality test row, routing the boundary through the product topology comparison.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for topology, normality, obstruction, comparison, product test, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $R$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a consumer request through the topology row, normal-source row, obstruction row, paracompact comparison, and interval-product test.
\item $Q$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over topology, normal space, paracompact comparison, locally finite cover, interval-product topology, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and local naming rows.
\item $Z$ is the local $\NameCert_{\DowkerSpaceUp}$ row naming this boundary object.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for a host powerset, a selected open cover, open-cover compactness, quotient equality of covers, or a choice-based paracompactness witness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Dowker space NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:dowker-space-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\DowkerSpaceUp$ carrier $D=(T,N,C,P,I,H,R,Q,Z)$, the local NameCert surface consists of carrier admission for the nine displayed rows, normal-source exposure through $N$, countable-paracompact obstruction through $C$, product-normality boundary through $I$, componentwise transport through $H$, replay through $R$, provenance through $Q$, and local naming through $Z$. The paracompact comparison row $P$ is readable only as a comparison against the displayed obstruction and does not supply a selected cover, a host powerset, open-cover compactness, or a global choice principle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:dowker-space-carrier}. The tuple displays exactly the topology carrier, normal source, countable-paracompact obstruction, paracompact comparison, interval-product test, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.

The positive topological read starts with $T$ and $N$. These rows expose the underlying topology and the normal-space source, but they do not create a paracompactness witness.

The boundary read is $C,P,I$. The obstruction row $C$ records the countable-paracompactness refusal, $P$ records only the comparison with paracompact and locally finite cover surfaces, and $I$ records the interval-product normality test that consumes that same boundary.

The structural rows $H,R,Q,Z$ transport, replay, source, and name the finite surface. Since the carrier omits host powersets, selected covers, open-cover compactness, and choice-based cover selectors, no consumer can extract those objects from the NameCert.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Dowker space normal-paracompact boundary]
\label{thm:dowker-space-normal-paracompact-boundary}
Every consumer of an accepted $\DowkerSpaceUp$ packet that uses the normality row must keep $\NormalSpaceUp$, the $\ParacompactUp$ and countably-paracompact comparison row, the $\TopologyUp$ carrier row, and the interval/product topology row visible. The handoff may compare normality, paracompactness, locally finite covers, and product normality along the displayed rows $T,N,C,P,I,H,R,Q,Z$, but it cannot export host powersets, selected covers, selected refinements, open-cover compactness, or a global paracompactness witness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start from the carrier of \autoref{def:dowker-space-carrier}. A normality consumer reads $N$ only after the topology carrier $T$ has been displayed.

The comparison cannot bypass the obstruction row. The row $C$ keeps the countable-paracompactness boundary visible, while $P$ and $I$ route any paracompact or product-normality request through the displayed comparison surface.

The locally finite cover and product-topology dependencies are read through provenance and replay. Rows $R$ and $Q$ point to the same finite route, and $H$ preserves it componentwise under $\hsame$.

Thus the consumer receives the normal-paracompact boundary as a finite NameCert surface. Host powersets, selected covers, selected refinements, open-cover compactness, and global paracompactness witnesses are absent from the carrier and cannot be exported.
\end{proof}
