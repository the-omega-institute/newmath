Following Baire and Bishop--Bridges constructive analysis, the $\BaireOnePointContinuityUp$ packet records the finite certificate route by which a Baire-one real-valued function exposes one inspected continuity point. It consumes the pointwise-limit ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-baireonefunction-namecert}, the dense-open schedule of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-bairecategory-namecert}, the oscillation window of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-oscillation-namecert}, and the epigraph handoff of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-lowersemicontinuous-namecert}. The packet names a local pointwise-continuity witness over displayed Real and LocatedReal rows; it does not assert a host theorem that every Baire-one map has a continuity point, and it does not choose a point outside the named certificate.

\begin{definition}[BaireOnePointContinuity carrier]
\label{def:baire-one-point-continuity-carrier}
A $\BaireOnePointContinuityUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
Q=(B,D,O,L,Z,R,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $B$ is the $\BaireOneFunctionUp$ source row, exposing the pointwise-limit ledger whose value reads are being tested for local continuity.
\item $D$ is the $\BaireCategoryUp$ dense-open schedule row, recording the finite neighborhood refinement requests available around the inspected point.
\item $O$ is the $\RealOscillationUp$ oscillation-window row, recording the displayed tail-variation and clamped-window evidence used to test a local modulus.
\item $L$ is the $\LowerSemicontinuousUp$ epigraph handoff row, recording the lower-bound route through which oscillation thresholds are made visible to a located consumer.
\item $Z$ is the $\LocatedRealUp$ and $\RealUp$ seal row for the inspected source point and its terminal value read.
\item $R$ is the local point-continuity row, recording the finite neighborhood, tolerance window, oscillation bound, and value-seal handoff accepted by the packet.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the Baire-one source, dense-open schedule, oscillation window, lower-semicontinuous handoff, located-real and real seals, local continuity row, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a point-continuity request through $B,D,O,L,Z,R,H,P,N$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\BaireOnePointContinuityUp$, $\BaireOneFunctionUp$, $\BaireCategoryUp$, $\RealOscillationUp$, $\LowerSemicontinuousUp$, $\LocatedRealUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.

\end{enumerate}
The carrier exports no global Baire theorem, no selected point outside $Z$, no quotient equality of real names, no countable choice over dense-open schedules, no open-cover compactness principle, and no ambient $\RealUp$ completeness theorem.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[BaireOnePointContinuity NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:baire-one-point-continuity-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\BaireOnePointContinuityUp$ carrier
$$
Q=(B,D,O,L,Z,R,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\BaireOnePointContinuityUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the Baire-one source row, dense-open schedule, oscillation window, lower-semicontinuous epigraph handoff, located-real and real seals, local continuity row, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows; dense-open exposure requiring $D$ to be displayed before a local neighborhood is used; oscillation-window exposure requiring $O$ to be displayed before a tolerance claim is read; epigraph-handoff exposure requiring $L$ to route every lower-bound comparison through the existing lower-semicontinuous packet; point-seal exposure requiring $Z$ and $R$ to be displayed before the local continuity claim is public; and ledger nonescape excluding choice, compactness, quotient real equality, semantic liminf data, and ambient completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:baire-one-point-continuity-carrier}. The packet displays exactly the source, dense-open, oscillation, epigraph, point-seal, local-continuity, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.

The dense-open and oscillation obligations are read from $D$ and $O$. The dense-open schedule supplies the neighborhood refinement surface, while the oscillation row supplies the finite variation window that can be tested at the inspected point.

The lower-semicontinuous handoff is read from $L$. It keeps lower-bound comparisons on the same finite epigraph route already named by the source packet, so a consumer cannot replace the row by semantic liminf data or a host topology.

Finally the local point seal $Z,R$ is reached through the structural rows $H,C,P,N$. Since none of these rows contains a selector over all dense-open schedules, a quotient equality of real names, open-cover compactness, or ambient Real completeness, the displayed obligations exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\label{thm:baire-one-point-continuity-oscillation-window}

\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}

\begin{theorem}[BaireOnePointContinuity local point handoff]
\label{thm:baire-one-point-continuity-local-point-handoff}

\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
