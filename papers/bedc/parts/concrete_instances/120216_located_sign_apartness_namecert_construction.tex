\begin{definition}[Located sign apartness carrier]
\label{def:located-sign-apartness-carrier}

$$

\begin{enumerate}

\item $A$ is the located-apartness real bridge row that supplies the positive separation guard from the zero row or from a named comparison endpoint.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ threshold ledger carrying the positive dyadic radius, tolerance arithmetic, and sign-side comparison data.
\item $W$ is the finite $\StreamNameUp$ observation window on which the sign read is inspected.
\item $Q$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row for the same finite window.

\begin{theorem}[Located sign apartness NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:located-sign-apartness-namecert-obligations}

\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:located-sign-apartness-carrier}. The accepted packet exposes exactly the rows $R,A,D,W,Q,E,H,C,P,N$, so the local certificate begins as finite packet admission rather than a global order principle on constructive reals.

Read the guard and threshold rows next. The located-apartness bridge row $A$ supplies the positive guard, while the dyadic row $D$ supplies the displayed radius and sign-side comparison ledger. The finite window $W$ and regular rational readback $Q$ are the only observations from which the guarded sign read is formed.

Finally use the structural rows. The seal row $E$ is reached only after the guard, threshold, window, and readback rows have been displayed, and $H,C,P,N$ preserve, replay, source, and name exactly that route. Since no row stores trichotomy, total sign, quotient real equality, or completeness, those principles are not local NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Located sign apartness threshold window]
\label{thm:located-sign-apartness-threshold-window}
Every permitted guarded sign read of an accepted $\LocatedSignApartnessUp$ packet factors through a single displayed threshold-window route
$$
\text{located-apartness guard } A,\quad
\DyadicRatCoreUp \text{ threshold } D,\quad
\StreamNameUp \text{ window } W,\quad
\RegSeqRatUp \text{ readback } Q .
$$
The route authorizes a positive or negative sign read only relative to the displayed guard and threshold. It does not decide zero versus nonzero for arbitrary $\RealUp$ names.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start from the guard row $A$. A sign read is admitted only when the located-apartness bridge has already supplied the positive separation data needed to ask for a side of zero or of the named comparison endpoint.

Next inspect the threshold and window rows. The dyadic ledger $D$ fixes the positive radius and comparison arithmetic, while $W$ fixes the finite stream observations and $Q$ fixes their regular rational readback.

The exported sign is therefore scoped by those four rows. Without $A$ there is no apartness guard, without $D$ there is no positive threshold, and without $W,Q$ there is no finite readback. The packet consequently refuses an unguarded total sign function on $\RealUp$.
\end{proof}
