\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\NagataDimensionUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-nagata-dimension-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Nagata's covering dimension for metric spaces and Assouad's metric dimension tradition, the $\NagataDimensionUp$ packet records a finite scale-cover certificate rather than a host theorem about arbitrary covers. It sits after the metric carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}, the cover-order surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-coveringdimension-namecert}, the Lebesgue covering route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-lebesgue-covering-dimension-namecert}, the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, and the Assouad scale-control neighbour of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-assouad-dimension-namecert}. The chapter names the standard Nagata-dimensional cover interface as BEDC rows; it does not assert a host dimension theorem, selected cover basis, quotient metric equality, countable choice, or ambient compactness.

\begin{definition}[Nagata dimension carrier]
\label{def:nagata-dimension-carrier}
A $\NagataDimensionUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
G=(M,S,C,L,A,R,H,T,P,N)
$$
with the following visible rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $M$ is the $\MetricUp$ source row carrying the displayed distance observations used by every scale-cover request.
\item $S$ is the scale ledger, listing the inspected radii and the finite multiplicity budgets attached to those radii.
\item $C$ is the cover-family row, consisting of finite bounded-diameter families for the displayed scales.
\item $L$ is the $\LebesgueCoveringDimensionUp$ comparison row used when the same finite cover is read as a Lebesgue-style refinement certificate.
\item $A$ is the $\AssouadDimensionUp$ scale-control row recording the neighbouring doubling and bounded-overlap discipline.
\item $R$ is the terminal $\RealUp$ seal row for numeric radius and diameter readback.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for metric, scale, cover, comparison, Real-seal, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $T$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay through the same cover route.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\NagataDimensionUp$, $\MetricUp$, $\CoveringDimensionUp$, $\LebesgueCoveringDimensionUp$, $\AssouadDimensionUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\NagataDimensionUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through the displayed metric source, scale ledger, cover families, comparison rows, Real readback, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows. It has no coordinate for a selected infinite cover basis, quotient metric equality, countable choice, open-cover compactness, or a host dimension theorem.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Nagata dimension NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:nagata-dimension-namecert-obligations}
For an accepted $\NagataDimensionUp$ carrier $G=(M,S,C,L,A,R,H,T,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\NagataDimensionUp}$ surface consists exactly of metric-source admission, scale-ledger admission, bounded-diameter cover-family exposure, finite multiplicity accounting, Lebesgue-covering comparison, Assouad scale-control comparison, Real readback for radii and diameters, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming. These obligations bind the finite Nagata cover certificate without adding a host cover basis, quotient metric equality, countable choice, open-cover compactness, or ambient dimension theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:nagata-dimension-carrier}. Its analytic rows are exactly the metric source $M$, scale ledger $S$, cover family $C$, Lebesgue-covering comparison $L$, Assouad scale-control row $A$, and Real readback row $R$.

The NameCert obligations are the rows needed to expose those data to a consumer. Transport through $H$ and replay through $T$ preserve the same finite route, while $P$ and $N$ source and name the packet. Since the carrier has no coordinate for the refused host principles, the listed rows exhaust the obligation surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Nagata dimension metric-cover route]
\label{thm:nagata-dimension-metric-cover-route}
Every metric-cover consumer of an accepted $\NagataDimensionUp$ packet must read the route
$$
\MetricUp \longmapsto \CoveringDimensionUp \longmapsto \LebesgueCoveringDimensionUp \longmapsto \NagataDimensionUp \longmapsto \AssouadDimensionUp \longmapsto \RealUp
$$
through the displayed rows $M,S,C,L,A,R,H,T,P,N$. The route exposes scale-indexed bounded-diameter cover families and finite multiplicity budgets before any Real-facing radius or diameter seal is consumed, and it exports no selected cover basis, quotient metric equality, countable-choice schedule, open-cover compactness theorem, or ambient completion principle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the metric source $M$ and scale ledger $S$. A cover request can inspect only the distances and radii already recorded in those finite rows.

Next read the cover-family row $C$ together with the Lebesgue-covering and Assouad comparison rows $L,A$. These rows place the Nagata packet between the cover-order discipline of $\CoveringDimensionUp$, the Lebesgue refinement surface, and the scale-control neighbour of $\AssouadDimensionUp$.

Finally use the Real readback and structural rows. The row $R$ supplies only displayed radius and diameter seals, while $H,T,P,N$ preserve, replay, source, and name the same finite route. No omitted host principle can be recovered from those rows.
\end{proof}
