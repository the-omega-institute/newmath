\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\PartialMetricUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-partial-metric-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Matthews on partial metric spaces, the $\PartialMetricUp$ packet records the constructive distance surface in which self-distance is visible data rather than a suppressed diagonal law. It sits between the pre-distance carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-premetric-namecert}, the zero-distance boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-pseudometric-namecert}, the directed-distance surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-quasi-metric-namecert}, the metric reader of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert}, and the terminal distance seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet names only the finite NameCert rows needed to expose self-distance, partial triangle evidence, and completion-facing handoff; it does not identify points, quotient carriers, select limits, or import ambient completeness.

\begin{definition}[Partial metric carrier]
\label{def:partial-metric-carrier}
A $\PartialMetricUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
P=(S,X,E,D,L,T,M,K,H,C,Q,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $S$ is the source carrier row. It records the traditional partial-metric source and the finite observation context in which point and distance requests are admitted.
\item $X$ is the point row. It records admitted point names and their classifier without quotienting them by distance-zero evidence or by equality-like self-distance comparisons.
\item $E$ is the self-distance row. For each admitted point row $x$, it displays the value $p(x,x)$ as a $\RealUp$-sealed distance witness.
\item $D$ is the directed distance row. It records requested values $p(x,y)$ and keeps the reversed request $p(y,x)$ visible as a separate displayed read unless a later row supplies symmetry evidence.
\item $L$ is the small-self-distance comparison row. It displays the comparisons $p(x,x)\leq p(x,y)$ and $p(y,y)\leq p(x,y)$ as finite ledger data, not as host-order laws.
\item $T$ is the partial triangle ledger. It records the comparison of $p(x,z)$ with the finite route through $p(x,y)$, $p(y,z)$, and the displayed self-distance $p(y,y)$.
\item $M$ is the metric-reflection handoff row. It names the boundary through which a later $\MetricUp$ or $\PseudometricUp$ reader may consume the displayed self-distance and triangle data.
\item $K$ is the completion-facing row. It binds every completion-oriented request to $S,X,E,D,L,T,M$ before any completed point, Cauchy limit, or quotient representative can be named.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for source, point, self-distance, directed distance, small-self-distance, partial triangle, metric-reflection, completion, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a consumer request through point admission, self-distance exposure, directed distance readback, small-self-distance comparison, partial triangle evidence, metric-reflection handoff, completion boundary, transport, provenance, and local naming.
\item $Q$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\PartialMetricUp$, $\PremetricUp$, $\PseudometricUp$, $\QuasiMetricUp$, $\MetricUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\PartialMetricUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $S,X,E,D,L,T,M,K,H,C,Q,N$. It has no coordinate for separatedness, metric completeness, open-cover compactness, quotient completion, selected representatives, selected limits, host metric equality, Lean verification, or bridge checking.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Partial metric NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:partial-metric-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\PartialMetricUp$ carrier
$$
P=(S,X,E,D,L,T,M,K,H,C,Q,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\PartialMetricUp}$ surface consists exactly of the following obligations:
\begin{enumerate}
\item carrier admission for the source row, point row, self-distance row, directed distance row, small-self-distance comparison row, partial triangle ledger, metric-reflection handoff, completion-facing row, componentwise transport, displayed replay, provenance, and local naming;
\item self-distance exposure, requiring every consumer that reads a point row to see the corresponding $E$ row before treating diagonal distance as unavailable or zero;
\item partial triangle ledger, requiring each triangle comparison to read $T$ through the displayed self-distance correction rather than through a host metric triangle axiom;
\item metric-reflection boundary, requiring every $\MetricUp$ or $\PseudometricUp$ consumer to pass through $M$ after $E,D,L,T$ have been displayed;
\item classifier transport, requiring accepted packets to move only by the componentwise $\hsame$ row $H$ and the replay row $C$;
\item ledger nonescape, excluding separatedness, metric completeness, open-cover compactness, quotient completion, selected representatives, selected limits, host metric equality, Lean verification, and bridge checking.
\end{enumerate}
No partial-metric NameCert obligation is stored outside these rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:partial-metric-carrier}. The tuple lists exactly the source, point, self-distance, directed distance, small-self-distance comparison, partial triangle, metric-reflection, completion, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.

Self-distance exposure is the row $E$. A consumer cannot erase diagonal distance before reading $E$, and the comparisons in $L$ keep the small-self-distance boundary visible whenever an off-diagonal distance is inspected.

The partial triangle obligation is the row $T$. It routes each comparison through the displayed self-distance correction, so the triangle evidence is a finite ledger read rather than a detached metric axiom.

Metric and pseudometric consumers reach the packet only through $M$, while completion-facing consumers are stopped at $K$ until the displayed source, distance, comparison, and triangle rows have been consumed. The rows $H,C,Q,N$ then transport, replay, source, and name the same finite surface.

The remaining exclusions follow from the carrier inventory. Since separatedness, metric completeness, open-cover compactness, quotient completion, selected representatives, selected limits, host metric equality, Lean verification, and bridge checking do not occur among the coordinates, none of those data can be exported by the local certificate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Partial metric self-distance boundary]
\label{thm:partial-metric-self-distance-boundary}
Let
$$
P=(S,X,E,D,L,T,M,K,H,C,Q,N)
$$
be an accepted $\PartialMetricUp$ carrier. Every $\MetricUp$ or $\PseudometricUp$ consumer may read equality-like separation only through the displayed self-distance boundary
$$
X,E,D,L,T \longmapsto M \longmapsto H,C,Q,N .
$$
The packet itself never exports quotient point equality, selected representatives, host metric equality, chosen limits, ambient completeness, or a completed quotient carrier.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with an accepted carrier and read the point row $X$. The self-distance row $E$ and directed distance row $D$ remain visible before any equality-like separation request can be interpreted.

The comparison row $L$ and triangle row $T$ bind the self-distance data to the off-diagonal distance ledger. Thus a consumer sees the partial-metric boundary as displayed finite evidence, not as a quotient equality or a host metric equality.

Only after those rows are displayed does the metric-reflection handoff $M$ become available. The support rows $H,C,Q,N$ transport, replay, source, and name that same boundary for metric and pseudometric readers.

The completion row $K$ does not add selected representatives, chosen limits, ambient completeness, or a completed quotient carrier. Those objects are absent from the coordinate list, so equality-like separation remains scoped to the displayed self-distance boundary.
\end{proof}
