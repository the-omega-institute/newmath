\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\LocatedCantorSetUp$}

Following Bishop and Bridges on located subsets of constructive metric spaces, the $\LocatedCantorSetUp$ packet records the middle-third Cantor set as a finite located-subset certificate over real-name windows rather than as a host closed subset of the line. It sits after the located-apartness boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-located-real-apartness-namecert}, the dyadic tolerance core of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-dyadicratcore-namecert}, the finite stream-window surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, the terminal real-name seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}, and the compact-metric carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}. The chapter supplies a concrete L10 neighbour for located-real and compact-metric routes; it does not claim open-cover compactness, a selected ternary expansion, quotient real equality, countable choice, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[Located Cantor set carrier]
\label{def:located-cantor-set-carrier}
A $\LocatedCantorSetUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
K=(X,S,R,D,A,I,B,M,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X$ is the $\RealUp$ name whose Cantor-membership distance is being inspected.
\item $S$ is the finite $\StreamNameUp$ window row through which $X$ is read.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row for the displayed rational approximants.
\item $D$ is the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance ledger containing the inspected dyadic scale and the interval-stage endpoints.
\item $A$ is the $\LocatedRealApartnessUp$ apartness row separating $X$ from a displayed removed middle-third interval whenever such a separation is visible.
\item $I$ is the finite interval-stage ledger: it lists the retained closed dyadic-rational cells and the removed gap cells at the inspected stage.
\item $B$ is the bisection row saying that the next stage is obtained by splitting each retained cell and deleting its middle gap through displayed dyadic comparisons.
\item $M$ is the $\CompactMetricUp$ consumer row, recording that finite retained cells are passed to compact-metric consumers only as a total-boundedness and Cauchy-window surface.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the real name, stream window, regular readback, dyadic ledger, apartness row, interval ledger, bisection row, compact-metric row, replay, provenance, and local naming.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a located-subset request through $X,S,R,D,A,I,B,M,H,P,N$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\LocatedCantorSetUp$, $\LocatedRealApartnessUp$, $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\CompactMetricUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\LocatedCantorSetUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier predicate compares accepted packets only through these rows. It has no coordinate for a selected ternary expansion, quotient stream equality, open-cover compactness, countable choice, host subset equality, or ambient real completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Located Cantor set NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:located-cantor-set-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\LocatedCantorSetUp$ carrier
$$
K=(X,S,R,D,A,I,B,M,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\LocatedCantorSetUp}$ surface consists exactly of real-name admission for $X$, finite stream-window admission for $S$, regular-rational readback for $R$, dyadic interval-stage exactness for $D$ and $I$, located-apartness exposure for $A$, bisection-stage replay for $B$, compact-metric handoff for $M$, componentwise $\hsame$ transport through $H$, displayed $\Cont$ replay through $C$, sibling provenance through $P$, and local naming through $N$.

No located Cantor-set obligation is stored outside these rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:located-cantor-set-carrier}. The inspected point is the real-name row $X$, and it is visible only through the finite stream window $S$ and regular-rational readback $R$.

The dyadic ledger $D$ and interval ledger $I$ supply the finite stage being inspected. The located-apartness row $A$ records separation from a removed gap when the window falls outside a retained cell, while the bisection row $B$ records the next retained-cell refinement.

The compact-metric row $M$ is downstream of these interval data. It receives only retained finite cells and Cauchy-window information, so it cannot turn the packet into open-cover compactness or a selected point theorem.

Finally $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same finite route. Since the carrier lists no other coordinate, the stated rows exhaust the local NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Located Cantor set bisection route]
\label{thm:located-cantor-set-bisection-route}
Every accepted $\LocatedCantorSetUp$ bisection read factors through the finite route
$$
\RealUp,\StreamNameUp,\RegSeqRatUp,\DyadicRatCoreUp,\LocatedRealApartnessUp,\CompactMetricUp,\NameCert_{\LocatedCantorSetUp}.
$$
At each displayed stage, the consumer first reads the real-name window, then the regular rational approximants, then the dyadic interval ledger, then the located-apartness row for the removed middle gap, and only then the retained-cell row offered to compact-metric consumers. The route exports no selected ternary expansion, quotient real equality, open-cover compactness, countable choice, selected limit, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with the window-readback part of the carrier. The rows $X,S,R$ expose a finite real-name observation, not a completed point of a host subset.

Read the dyadic ledger $D$ and the interval ledger $I$. These rows display the current retained cells and removed gap cells at the inspected stage.

The located-apartness row $A$ can only certify separation from one displayed removed gap, and the bisection row $B$ can only refine the displayed retained cells. Thus the stage handoff remains a finite interval calculation.

The compact-metric row $M$ consumes the retained-cell data after those rows have been displayed. The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ preserve the same route, so the bisection read cannot export any of the refused ambient or choice-dependent objects.
\end{proof}
