\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\WallmanCompactificationUp$}

Following Wallman on compactification by closed-set lattices, the $\WallmanCompactificationUp$ packet records the finite route by which a displayed normal lattice of closed rows names boundary filters and their compactification-facing topology. It sits beside the one-point compactification of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-one-point-compactification-namecert}, the Freudenthal end boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-freudenthal-compactification-namecert}, the compact carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compact-namecert}, the indexed topology surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert}, and the Real/L10 readback route through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}. The packet names only the finite closed-set lattice and boundary-filter readback; it is not a host powerset of closed sets, a selected ultrafilter, a quotient compactification space, open-cover compactness, countable choice, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[Wallman compactification carrier]
\label{def:wallman-compactification-carrier}
A $\WallmanCompactificationUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
K=(X,L,F,B,J,T,R,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X$ is the source $\TopologyUp$ row, exposing the indexed open and closed-set membership reads available to the packet.
\item $L$ is the finite closed-set lattice row. It records displayed finite meets, finite joins, inclusion comparisons, and the normal-separation ledger used by the Wallman route.
\item $F$ is the boundary-filter row. Each displayed boundary request names a finite consistent family of closed rows from $L$ together with its finite-refinement witness.
\item $B$ is the compact-boundary admission row, read through the compact carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compact-namecert} without importing an ambient compact host.
\item $J$ is the source-inclusion row, sending a displayed source point to the finite closed-neighbourhood filter it determines.
\item $T$ is the topology handoff row for the compactification carrier. It translates finite closed-lattice and boundary-filter requests into indexed topology reads.
\item $R$ is the L10 readback row, routing finite boundary observations through $\RegSeqRatUp$ and $\RealUp$ seals when a real-valued consumer inspects the compactification.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $X,L,F,B,J,T,R,C,P,N$.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay of closed-lattice, boundary-filter, topology, compactness, and readback requests.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\WallmanCompactificationUp$, $\TopologyUp$, $\CompactUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$ rows.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\WallmanCompactificationUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier predicate compares accepted packets only through these rows. It has no coordinate for a full host closed-set powerset, selected ultrafilter, quotient compactification point, open-cover compactness proof, countable-choice selector, or ambient complete real line.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Wallman compactification NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:wallman-compactification-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\WallmanCompactificationUp$ carrier
$$
K=(X,L,F,B,J,T,R,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\WallmanCompactificationUp}$ surface consists exactly of source topology admission, finite closed-lattice admission, boundary-filter consistency, compact-boundary admission, source-inclusion display, topology handoff, L10 readback, componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local naming. Every compactification-facing read must expose $X,L,F,J,T$ before $B$ or $R$ can be consumed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:wallman-compactification-carrier}. The only source data are the topology row $X$ and the finite closed-lattice row $L$, so every boundary request begins with displayed closed-set membership and finite lattice operations.

The boundary-filter row $F$ is then a finite consistency ledger over $L$. It records which closed rows are jointly requested and which finite refinement witnesses keep the request admissible, rather than selecting an ultrafilter or forming a quotient point.

Read the source-inclusion and topology handoff rows next. The row $J$ ties displayed source points to their closed-neighbourhood filters, and $T$ converts lattice and filter requests into indexed topology reads before any compact-boundary row is public.

Finally consume $B,R,H,C,P,N$. The compact-boundary and L10 readback rows are downstream of the same finite route, while transport, replay, provenance, and local naming add no host powerset, selected boundary point, countable-choice selector, open-cover compactness proof, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Wallman compactification L10 boundary]
\label{thm:wallman-compactification-l10-boundary}
Every $\RegSeqRatUp$ or $\RealUp$ consumer reading a $\WallmanCompactificationUp$ packet receives only the finite route
$$
X,L,F,J,T \longmapsto B,R,H,C,P,N .
$$
Thus an L10 readback may inspect finite closed-set lattice requests, boundary-filter consistency, source inclusion, topology handoff, compact-boundary admission, and the real-valued readback row, but it cannot export a second compactification point, host closed-set powerset, quotient filter equality, selected ultrafilter, countable choice, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with a real-valued or regular-rational consumer. By carrier projection, the consumer first sees the topology source $X$, closed-lattice row $L$, boundary-filter row $F$, source-inclusion row $J$, and topology handoff row $T$.

The compact-boundary admission row $B$ and L10 readback row $R$ are downstream of that prefix. They may cite the compact, RegSeqRat, and Real sibling chapters named in the provenance row, but they receive only the displayed finite route rather than an ambient compactification object.

Transport and replay preserve the same route through $H$ and $C$, and $P,N$ only package and name it. Hence the L10 boundary cannot produce quotient filter equality, selected ultrafilters, countable choice, a second compactification point, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.
\end{proof}
