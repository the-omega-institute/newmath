\begin{theorem}[Sorgenfrey plane lower-limit product lattice link]
\label{thm:sorgenfrey-plane-lower-limit-product-lattice-link}
Every accepted $\SorgenfreyPlaneUp$ product-lattice read is the product of two lower-limit line reads before it becomes a plane topology read. In the notation of \autoref{def:sorgenfrey-plane-carrier}, the component rows $L_X,L_Y,O_X,O_Y$ are read through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-sorgenfrey-line-namecert}; the product row $Q$ and rectangle ledger $B$ are read through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-product-topology-namecert}; and any restricted axis, diagonal, or boundary read is handed onward only through the subspace-topology route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-subspace-topology-namecert}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier $P=(L_X,L_Y,Q,O_X,O_Y,B,S,H,C,R,N)$ from \autoref{def:sorgenfrey-plane-carrier}. The two lower-limit source rows $L_X,L_Y$ supply the one-dimensional half-open windows, and $O_X,O_Y$ are the displayed topology rows exported by those sources.

The product topology row $Q$ consumes those two component surfaces before the rectangle ledger $B$ is available. Thus a product-lattice read is not an independent plane topology; it is the paired lower-limit route supplied by the product-topology sibling.

When a consumer asks for an axis, diagonal, boundary, or other restricted read, the packet can only pass the already displayed product topology to the subspace-topology sibling. Transport through $H$, replay through $C$, provenance through $R$, and local naming through $N$ preserve the same dependency order, so no host product topology, selected rectangle basis, or quotient equality of real pairs is exported.
\end{proof}
