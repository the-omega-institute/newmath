\begin{theorem}[Sorgenfrey plane subspace-topology lattice bridge]
\label{thm:sorgenfrey-plane-subspace-topology-lattice-bridge}
Every coordinate-axis, diagonal-trace, or rectangle-boundary read from an accepted $\SorgenfreyPlaneUp$ packet factors first through the two $\SorgenfreyLineUp$ lower-limit rows, then through the $\ProductTopologyUp$ rectangle row, and only then through the $\SubspaceTopologyUp$ restriction row. The bridge exports the displayed lower-limit product lattice as a finite subspace-topology route and does not supply a host plane topology, selected rectangle basis, quotient equality on $\RealUp\times\RealUp$, metrizability theorem, or ambient separation theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:sorgenfrey-plane-carrier}. The two lower-limit rows $L_X,L_Y$ are the only source rows for one-dimensional lower-limit windows, and the product row $Q$ together with the rectangle ledger $B$ is the only route to a plane-level rectangle read.

Apply the lower-limit product dependency route and product topology link. They require the two $\SorgenfreyLineUp$ rows to be displayed before $Q$ and $B$ can be consumed by any product-open, axis, diagonal, or boundary reader.

Now read the subspace-topology sibling through \autoref{thm:sorgenfrey-plane-subspace-topology-forward-link} and \autoref{thm:sorgenfrey-plane-subspace-forward-lattice}. A restricted axis, diagonal trace, or rectangle boundary is therefore a subspace restriction of the already displayed lower-limit product route.

Since the carrier has no coordinate for a host plane, selected basis, quotient equality of real pairs, metric witness, or ambient separation theorem, the lattice bridge contains only the displayed $\SorgenfreyLineUp$, $\ProductTopologyUp$, $\SubspaceTopologyUp$, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\end{proof}
