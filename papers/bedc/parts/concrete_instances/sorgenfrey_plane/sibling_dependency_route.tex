\begin{theorem}[Sorgenfrey plane sibling dependency route]
\label{thm:sorgenfrey-plane-sibling-dependency-route}
Every accepted $\SorgenfreyPlaneUp$ carrier consumes two $\SorgenfreyLineUp$ lower-limit component rows and a $\ProductTopologyUp$ rectangle-generation row before the plane-level lower-limit product surface is available. In the carrier notation of \autoref{def:sorgenfrey-plane-carrier}, the rows $L_X,L_Y,O_X,O_Y$ are read through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-sorgenfrey-line-namecert}, while $Q$ and $B$ are read through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-product-topology-namecert}. The route is a child dependency of the plane packet, not an independent host topology on $\RealUp \times \RealUp$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the rows of \autoref{def:sorgenfrey-plane-carrier}. The two component lower-limit carriers are $L_X$ and $L_Y$, and their displayed topology rows are $O_X$ and $O_Y$.

The product topology row $Q$ pairs those two component reads before the rectangle ledger $B$ can be inspected. Thus a product-open read first displays the one-dimensional lower-limit data and only then enters the product-topology rectangle route.

Transport through $H$, replay through $C$, provenance through $R$, and local naming through $N$ preserve that dependency order. Since the carrier has no coordinate for a host product topology, selected rectangle basis, powerset plane, or quotient equality of real pairs, the dependency remains the stated sibling route.
\end{proof}
