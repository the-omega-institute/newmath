\begin{theorem}[Sorgenfrey plane forward lower-limit dependency]
\label{thm:sorgenfrey-plane-forward-lower-limit-dependency}
Every product-neighbourhood read exported by an accepted $\SorgenfreyPlaneUp$ packet factors through the lower-limit topology rows of the two component lines and the subspace-topology forward link of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-subspace-topology-namecert}. In the carrier notation of \autoref{def:sorgenfrey-plane-carrier}, the component rows $L_X,L_Y,O_X,O_Y$ are read before the product row $Q$ and rectangle ledger $B$, and any restriction-facing consumer then passes through \autoref{thm:sorgenfrey-plane-subspace-topology-forward-link}. The route is the same lower-limit product dependency named by \autoref{thm:sorgenfrey-plane-lower-limit-product-dependency-route} and \autoref{thm:sorgenfrey-plane-product-topology-forward-link}; it exports no host powerset topology, selected rectangle basis, quotient equality on $\RealUp\times\RealUp$, metrizability theorem, or standalone plane topology.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier. The component lower-limit rows $L_X,L_Y$ and their topology exports $O_X,O_Y$ are the only component sources available before a product neighbourhood can be read.

Apply the lower-limit product dependency route and the product-topology forward link. They force the product row $Q$ and rectangle ledger $B$ to consume the displayed component lower-limit windows before any plane-level neighbourhood is public.

Finally use the subspace-topology forward link. It records the restriction-facing read as downstream of the displayed product and component topology rows, so the forward lower-limit dependency cannot be replaced by a host topology, selected basis, quotient pair equality, or metrizability claim.
\end{proof}
