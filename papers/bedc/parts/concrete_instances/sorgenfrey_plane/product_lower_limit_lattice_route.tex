\begin{theorem}[SorgenfreyPlane product-topology forward link]
\label{thm:sorgenfrey-plane-product-topology-forward-link}
Every $\SorgenfreyPlaneUp$ product-open or separation-facing read consumes the $\SorgenfreyLineUp$ component rows through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-sorgenfrey-line-namecert}, the $\ProductTopologyUp$ product row through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-product-topology-namecert}, and the $\SierpinskiSpaceUp$ observable-open row through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-sierpinski-space-namecert}. This route is a concrete sibling edge for the product lower-limit lattice: component half-open windows are paired by the product row and inspected by the observable-open row, with no assertion of metrizability, normality, selected bases, host powerset topology, quotient equality of real pairs, or ambient set-theoretic plane.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:sorgenfrey-plane-carrier}. The plane packet has two lower-limit component rows, the product topology row, the component indexed-open rows, the rectangle ledger, the observable-open row, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming data.

A product-open read first passes through the $\SorgenfreyLineUp$ component lower-limit rows and then through the $\ProductTopologyUp$ row. Only after those rows are visible may the rectangle ledger be read as plane-level product data.

A separation-facing read follows the same route and then exposes the $\SierpinskiSpaceUp$ observable-open row. Since every route remains inside the displayed component, product, observable-open, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows, no plane-level read bypasses the component lower-limit or product-topology dependencies.
\end{proof}
