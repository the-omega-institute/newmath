\begin{theorem}[Sorgenfrey plane lower-limit product-topology dependency]
\label{thm:sorgenfrey-plane-lower-limit-product-topology-dependency}
Every accepted $\SorgenfreyPlaneUp$ packet consumes the lower-limit topology row and the product-topology row before any separation-facing, compactness-facing, or boundary-read consumer inspects the plane. In the notation of \autoref{def:sorgenfrey-plane-carrier}, the rows $L_X,L_Y,O_X,O_Y,Q,B$ pass through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert}, \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-sorgenfrey-line-namecert}, and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-product-topology-namecert} before the structural rows $S,H,C,R,N$ are available. The route exports no host product plane, selected rectangle basis, quotient equality on $\RealUp\times\RealUp$, ambient compactness theorem, or standalone separation theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier of \autoref{def:sorgenfrey-plane-carrier}. The component lower-limit rows $L_X,L_Y$ and topology rows $O_X,O_Y$ are the only one-dimensional topology inputs visible before a plane-level rectangle can be read.

Apply \autoref{thm:sorgenfrey-plane-lower-limit-product-dependency-route}. It routes every product-open or separation-facing read through the two lower-limit line rows and the ambient $\TopologyUp$ surface before the rectangle ledger is public.

Then apply \autoref{prop:sorgenfrey-plane-product-topology-dependency}. The product row $Q$ and rectangle ledger $B$ are consumed before separation, subspace, compactness, or lattice consumers reach the transport, replay, provenance, and local naming rows. Since the displayed carrier contains no host product plane or selected rectangle basis, no later consumer can bypass the lower-limit product-topology dependency.
\end{proof}
