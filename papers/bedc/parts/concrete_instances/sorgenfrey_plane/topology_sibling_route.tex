\begin{theorem}[Sorgenfrey plane topology sibling route]
\label{thm:sorgenfrey-plane-topology-sibling-route}
Every accepted $\SorgenfreyPlaneUp$ carrier that exposes a plane topology read is downstream of the indexed-open carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert}, the lower-limit line rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-sorgenfrey-line-namecert}, and the product-topology classifier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-product-topology-namecert}. In the notation of \autoref{def:sorgenfrey-plane-carrier}, the rows $L_X,L_Y,O_X,O_Y,Q,B$ show that the carrier is not kernel-isolated: it consumes lower-limit topology and product topology classifier rows before any product-open or separation-facing read is public.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:sorgenfrey-plane-carrier}. The component rows $L_X,L_Y$ first expose lower-limit line data, and the rows $O_X,O_Y$ are the topology rows read from those component sources.

Next read the product-topology row $Q$ and the rectangle ledger $B$. Together with \autoref{thm:sorgenfrey-plane-lower-limit-product-dependency-route}, these rows route every plane-level topology observation through paired lower-limit windows and the displayed product-topology classifier.

The structural rows $S,H,C,R,N$ preserve the same dependency order for observable-open readback, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming. Since the carrier has no standalone topology coordinate outside $O_X,O_Y,Q,B$, no accepted plane topology read can bypass the lower-limit and product-topology siblings.
\end{proof}
