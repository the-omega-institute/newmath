\begin{theorem}[Sorgenfrey plane lower-limit product sibling dependency]
\label{thm:sorgenfrey-plane-lower-limit-product-sibling-dependency}
Every accepted $\SorgenfreyPlaneUp$ carrier read through the sibling lattice must expose the lower-limit line topology of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-sorgenfrey-line-namecert}, the ambient topology surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert}, and the product/topology sibling routes of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-product-topology-namecert} before any plane-level open, separation, or nonnormality-facing read is public. The route treats lower-limit intervals, component topology rows, and product rectangles as one displayed dependency chain; it exports no host product space, selected rectangle basis, powerset topology, quotient equality on $\RealUp\times\RealUp$, or ambient separation theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Read the lower-limit line sources first. They provide the one-dimensional topology rows that the plane carrier records as its two component inputs.

Next pass through the product-topology sibling. Its rectangle ledger combines the two component topology rows before the ambient $\TopologyUp$ surface can be read as a plane topology.

Concretely, $\ProductTopologyUp$ supplies the rectangle ledger, while $\TopologyUp$ supplies the indexed-open carrier used by both component lower-limit rows.

The existing Sorgenfrey-plane sibling routes preserve this order for topology, product, and downstream separation consumers. Hence every public plane-level read remains downstream of the lower-limit product dependency chain rather than an independent host topology.
\end{proof}
