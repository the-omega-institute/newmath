\begin{theorem}[Sorgenfrey plane topology sibling lattice]
\label{thm:sorgenfrey-plane-topology-sibling-lattice-repair}
Every accepted $\SorgenfreyPlaneUp$ carrier consumes its topology dependency lattice through the lower-limit component route and the product-topology route before any plane-level open, rectangle, or separation-facing read is exported. In the notation of \autoref{def:sorgenfrey-plane-carrier}, the rows $L_X,L_Y,O_X,O_Y,Q,B$ exhibit the dependency surface: $L_X,L_Y$ name the two lower-limit sources, $O_X,O_Y$ expose their topology rows, $Q$ is the product-topology classifier, and $B$ records the rectangle basis. Thus the carrier depends on the topology sibling of \autoref{thm:sorgenfrey-plane-topology-sibling-route}, the lower-limit product route of \autoref{thm:sorgenfrey-plane-lower-limit-product-dependency-route}, and the product lattice route of \autoref{thm:sorgenfrey-plane-product-topology-forward-link}; it is not a kernel-only packet.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier rows named in \autoref{def:sorgenfrey-plane-carrier}. The lower-limit line entries $L_X,L_Y$ are read before the component topology rows $O_X,O_Y$, so the plane topology cannot be formed without the two displayed line sources.

Next read $Q$ and $B$. The product-topology row $Q$ combines the component topology data, and the rectangle ledger $B$ records the finite product basis used by plane-level reads.

Finally apply the existing sibling routes. Theorems~\ref{thm:sorgenfrey-plane-topology-sibling-route}, \ref{thm:sorgenfrey-plane-lower-limit-product-dependency-route}, and \ref{thm:sorgenfrey-plane-product-topology-forward-link} keep every exported topology or separation read on the displayed lower-limit and product-topology lattice. No additional kernel coordinate can supply a standalone topology for the plane.
\end{proof}
