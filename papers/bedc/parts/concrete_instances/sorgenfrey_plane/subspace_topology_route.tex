\begin{theorem}[Sorgenfrey plane SubspaceTopology route]
\label{thm:sorgenfrey-plane-subspace-topology-route}
Every accepted $\SorgenfreyPlaneUp$ packet reads the lower-limit line rows from \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-sorgenfrey-line-namecert} and then the product or subspace restriction through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-subspace-topology-namecert} before any separation or compactness consumer can inspect a plane-facing output. The route exposes the displayed lower-limit component rows, the product rectangle ledger, the restriction boundary, componentwise transport, replay, provenance, and local naming. It exports no open-cover compactness theorem, no host product topology, no normality claim, no quotient equality of pairs, and no selected subspace point family.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:sorgenfrey-plane-carrier}. The only one-dimensional sources are the two $\SorgenfreyLineUp$ rows, and the only product-facing surface is the displayed product and rectangle ledger already named by the carrier.

Apply \autoref{thm:sorgenfrey-plane-product-topology-link}. It requires the lower-limit line rows and product row to be visible before any later rectangle, observable-open, separation-facing, or compactness-facing read is consumed.

The subspace-topology sibling can then read a restriction only from that visible product surface. The restriction is therefore a downstream route through the displayed lower-limit rows and rectangle ledger, not an independent topology or a host product topology.

Since the accepted carrier has no row for open-cover compactness, host product topology, normality, quotient equality of pairs, or selected subspace point families, no separation or compactness consumer can obtain those claims from this route.
\end{proof}
