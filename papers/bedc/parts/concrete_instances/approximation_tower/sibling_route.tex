The sibling route makes the Real-facing use of $\ApproximationTowerUp$ explicit: the tower seal is never read as a kernel-only artifact, but through the same L10 rows named by the carrier and consumed by the Real route.

\begin{theorem}[ApproximationTower sibling dependency route]
\label{thm:approximation-tower-sibling-route}
Every $\ApproximationTowerUp$ Real-facing consumer first reads the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, and $\RealUp$ sibling route already named by \autoref{def:approximation-tower-carrier} before it reads the tower seal. In particular, the carrier rows $D,W,R,E$ are exposed as dyadic tolerance, stream window, regular rational readback, and terminal real seal data before the structural rows $H,C,P,N$ package the tower for downstream use.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the accepted carrier $A=(L,W,D,R,E,H,C,P,N)$ of \autoref{def:approximation-tower-carrier}. The rows $D,W,R,E$ are named there as the $\DyadicRatCoreUp$ tolerance row, $\StreamNameUp$ window row, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row, and $\RealUp$ seal row.

Apply \autoref{thm:approximation-tower-real-consumer-route}. A Real-facing consumer reaches the terminal seal only after the finite level ledger, the four sibling rows, and the structural rows have been displayed.

Thus the tower seal is downstream from the dyadic tolerance, stream window, regular readback, and Real seal route already carried by $A$. The rows $H,C,P,N$ preserve and name that route; they do not introduce a separate kernel-only consumption path.
\end{proof}
