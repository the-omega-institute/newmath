\chapter{A Concrete Naming Certificate for $\ParacompactUp$}
\label{ch:concrete-instances-paracompact-namecert}
\origin{human}

Following Kelley and Bourbaki on paracompact spaces and locally finite refinements, the $\ParacompactUp$ packet records the finite BEDC surface by which a displayed open-cover request receives a locally finite open refinement ledger. It sits above the open-row discipline of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert}, beside the separation boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-normalspace-namecert}, and below later partition-of-unity and manifold-gluing consumers. It also records the compatibility route through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-urysohn-lemma-namecert} and, when the source is metric or compact-metric, the finite-radius handoff through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert} and the $\FiniteLebesgueNumberUp$ surface. The packet names only a displayed refinement carrier; it does not select subcovers from arbitrary open families or close partition-of-unity data.

\begin{definition}[Paracompact carrier]
\label{def:paracompact-carrier}
A $\ParacompactUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
Q=(T,A,R,L,N,M,H,C,P,Z)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $T$ is the $\TopologyUp$ source row, exposing the ambient open-set operations and the common observation surface on which the cover and refinement rows are read.
\item $A$ is the open-cover request row, a finite request ledger whose cells name the displayed open rows to be refined rather than an arbitrary host-indexed cover.
\item $R$ is the locally finite open-refinement ledger, recording for each displayed source cell a finite neighborhood row meeting only finitely many refinement cells and recording the refinement map back into $A$.
\item $L$ is the coverage ledger, recording that the refinement cells cover the same displayed source region that $A$ covers, with the containment read carried by the topology row $T$.
\item $N$ is the $\NormalSpaceUp$ compatibility row, requiring the refinement ledger to preserve the closed-pair separation route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-normalspace-namecert} whenever the cover request is used with normal-space separation data.
\item $M$ is the metric-or-finite-radius handoff row, read either from a $\MetricUp$ source or from a $\FiniteLebesgueNumberUp$ positive finite-radius ledger when such data are available; the handoff exports only the displayed finite-radius route.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the topology source, cover request, locally finite refinement ledger, coverage ledger, normal-space compatibility row, metric-or-finite-radius handoff, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a paracompact-refinement request through $T,A,R,L,N,M,H,P,Z$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\ParacompactUp$, $\TopologyUp$, $\NormalSpaceUp$, $\UrysohnLemmaUp$, $\MetricUp$, $\FiniteLebesgueNumberUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $Z$ is the local $\NameCert_{\ParacompactUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $T,A,R,L,N,M,H,C,P,Z$. It has no coordinate for selected subcovers, arbitrary open-cover compactness, partition-of-unity weights, host complement selection, Lean verification, or bridge checking.
\end{definition}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.ParacompactUp.ParacompactTasteGate\_single\_carrier\_alignment}

\begin{theorem}[Paracompact NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:paracompact-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\ParacompactUp$ carrier
$$
Q=(T,A,R,L,N,M,H,C,P,Z),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\ParacompactUp}$ surface consists exactly of the following finite obligation rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item carrier admission for the topology source, open-cover request, locally finite refinement ledger, coverage ledger, normal-space compatibility row, metric-or-finite-radius handoff, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming;
\item open-cover exposure, requiring every refinement request to display $A$ over the topology source $T$ before any refinement cell can be consumed;
\item locally finite refinement exactness, requiring $R$ to display both the refinement cells and the finite neighborhood ledger witnessing local finiteness;
\item coverage and containment exactness, requiring $L$ to record that each displayed source cell covered by $A$ is covered by the refinement ledger and that every refinement cell is contained in its assigned cover cell;
\item normal-space compatibility, requiring every closed-pair separation consumer to pass through the $\NormalSpaceUp$ row $N$ and the Urysohn-facing route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-urysohn-lemma-namecert} before it reads the refinement as separation-compatible;
\item metric-or-finite-radius handoff, requiring a metric consumer to read $M$ through the displayed $\MetricUp$ source or through the $\FiniteLebesgueNumberUp$ finite-radius ledger, with no selected subcover or host compactness coordinate;
\item $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\Pkg$ transport, requiring componentwise transport, displayed replay, and provenance to preserve exactly the listed rows and no hidden host-refinement data.
\end{enumerate}
No local paracompact-refinement obligation is stored outside these seven rows.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.ParacompactUp.ParacompactCarrier\_namecert\_obligations}
\begin{proof}
Carrier admission is projection from \autoref{def:paracompact-carrier}. The accepted tuple displays exactly the topology source, cover request, locally finite refinement ledger, coverage ledger, normal-space compatibility row, metric-or-finite-radius handoff, and structural transport, replay, provenance, and naming rows.

Open-cover exposure follows from the order of the carrier. A refinement request first reads $T$ and then $A$ over that same topological source; no refinement cell is available before the cover request has been displayed.

Locally finite refinement exactness is the projection to $R$. The row records the refinement cells, the assignment back into $A$, and the finite neighborhood ledger witnessing that each displayed source cell meets only finitely many refinement cells.

Coverage and containment exactness is the projection to $L$. It records both directions needed by the local request: coverage of the displayed source region and containment of every refinement cell in its assigned cover cell.

Normal-space compatibility is read through $N$ and the cited Urysohn-facing route. The packet can be consumed together with normal-space separation data only after the closed-pair route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-normalspace-namecert} and the separation-function route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-urysohn-lemma-namecert} have been displayed.

Metric-or-finite-radius handoff is read through $M$. When metric or compact-metric data are present, $M$ exposes the $\MetricUp$ or $\FiniteLebesgueNumberUp$ finite-radius route; otherwise it exports no compactness or subcover-selection principle.

Finally, transport and provenance are exhausted by $H,C,P,Z$. These rows move and replay exactly the displayed carrier coordinates, so no local obligation exports selected subcovers, arbitrary open-cover compactness, partition-of-unity weights, host complement selection, Lean verification, or bridge checking.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Paracompact refinement consumer route]
\label{thm:paracompact-refinement-consumer-route}
Every refinement read exported by an accepted $\ParacompactUp$ packet factors through
$$
\TopologyUp \longrightarrow \ParacompactUp \longrightarrow \NormalSpaceUp \longrightarrow \UrysohnLemmaUp
$$
when consumed by a separation-compatible topology route, and through the displayed $\MetricUp$ or $\FiniteLebesgueNumberUp$ handoff when a metric finite-radius route is available. No exported refinement read factors through a selected subcover, arbitrary open-cover compactness, partition-of-unity weights, host complement selection, Lean verification, or bridge checking.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.ParacompactUp.ParacompactCarrier\_refinement\_consumer\_route}
\begin{proof}
Begin with an accepted carrier $Q=(T,A,R,L,N,M,H,C,P,Z)$. Carrier admission exposes the $\TopologyUp$ source and the displayed open-cover request before any refinement ledger can be inspected.

The refinement gate is $R$ and $L$. These rows provide the locally finite refinement cells, their assignment to the cover request, the finite neighborhood ledger, and the coverage and containment reads.

The separation-compatible gate is $N$. A consumer that also needs normal-space separation must read the displayed $\NormalSpaceUp$ compatibility route and the sibling $\UrysohnLemmaUp$ route before treating the refinement as compatible with separation-function data.

The metric gate is $M$. A metric or compact-metric consumer may read the displayed $\MetricUp$ or $\FiniteLebesgueNumberUp$ handoff, but the row carries only finite-radius data already present in the packet.

The replay and local naming rows $H,C,P,Z$ preserve this order. Since the classifier has no further coordinate, no exported refinement read can obtain selected subcovers, arbitrary open-cover compactness, partition-of-unity weights, host complement selection, Lean verification, or bridge checking.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Paracompact locally finite refinement carrier]
\label{def:paracompact-locally-finite-refinement-carrier}
A locally finite refinement carrier for $\ParacompactUp$ is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
Q_{\mathrm{lf}}=(T,G,A,R,F,L,N,U,M,H,C,P,Z)
$$
whose rows refine \autoref{def:paracompact-carrier} as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $T$ is the $\TopologyUp$ source row and $G$ is the observation window on which cover and neighbourhood reads are made.
\item $A$ is the displayed open-cover request over $T$.
\item $R$ is the refinement-family row, listing the refinement cells and their assignment back into cells of $A$.
\item $F$ is the local-finiteness ledger: for each displayed point or source cell in $G$, it records a neighbourhood row and the finite list of refinement cells that meet that neighbourhood.
\item $L$ is the coverage and containment ledger, recording that the refinement covers the same displayed source region and that every refinement cell is contained in its assigned cover cell.
\item $N$ is the $\NormalSpaceUp$ separation-compatibility row and $U$ is the $\UrysohnLemmaUp$ handoff row used when a closed-pair separation consumer reads the refinement.
\item $M$ is the metric finite-radius handoff, read through $\MetricUp$ and, when available, through the $\FiniteLebesgueNumberUp$ positive-radius ledger.
\item $H,C,P,Z$ are the componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, $\Pkg$ provenance, and local $\NameCert_{\ParacompactUp}$ rows.
\end{enumerate}
The carrier has no coordinate for a selected subcover, an arbitrary host-indexed refinement family, partition-of-unity weights, a host complement selector, open-cover compactness, or an ambient refinement theorem.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Paracompact refinement NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:paracompact-refinement-namecert-obligations}
For a locally finite refinement carrier
$$
Q_{\mathrm{lf}}=(T,G,A,R,F,L,N,U,M,H,C,P,Z),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\ParacompactUp}$ surface is exhausted by carrier admission, cover-refinement rows, the locally finite ledger, normal-space separation compatibility, and transport/replay/provenance/local naming rows.
Concretely, every refinement consumer must read:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $T,G,A$ before a cover request is available;
\item $R,L$ before a refinement cell can be used as covering and contained in its assigned cover cell;
\item $F$ before the refinement can be read as locally finite on the displayed observation window;
\item $N,U$ before the refinement can be consumed by a normal-space or Urysohn-facing separation route;
\item $M$ before a metric finite-radius route is exported; and
\item $H,C,P,Z$ before the transported packet is replayed or named.
\end{enumerate}
No NameCert obligation for locally finite paracompact refinement is stored outside these displayed rows.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.ParacompactUp.ParacompactCarrier\_refinement\_namecert\_obligations}
\begin{proof}
Carrier admission is projection from \autoref{def:paracompact-locally-finite-refinement-carrier}. The tuple displays the topology source, observation window, cover request, refinement family, local-finiteness ledger, coverage and containment ledger, normal-space and Urysohn rows, metric handoff, transport, replay, provenance, and local name.

The cover-refinement obligations are read from $A,R,L$. The cover request $A$ is tied to the same topological source $T$, the refinement row $R$ assigns each refinement cell back to the cover request, and the ledger $L$ records coverage and containment.

Local finiteness is read only from $F$. For each displayed point or source cell in $G$, the row gives a neighbourhood together with the finite list of refinement cells meeting it, so a consumer cannot infer local finiteness from the existence of the refinement family alone.

Separation compatibility is read through $N,U$. These rows force a normal-space consumer to pass through the displayed $\NormalSpaceUp$ and $\UrysohnLemmaUp$ surfaces before treating the refinement as separation-compatible.

Finally, $M,H,C,P,Z$ exhaust metric export, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming. Since the carrier has no further coordinate, selected subcovers, host-indexed refinements, partition-of-unity weights, host complement selection, open-cover compactness, and ambient refinement theorems are not part of the local certificate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Paracompact metric-normal route]
\label{thm:paracompact-metric-normal-route}
Every metric $\ParacompactUp$ refinement read exported from a locally finite refinement carrier first factors through the $\MetricUp$ finite-radius source and the $\NormalSpaceUp$ separation row before it reaches the refinement export. In symbols, the visible route is
$$
\MetricUp \longrightarrow \FiniteLebesgueNumberUp \longrightarrow \TopologyUp \longrightarrow \NormalSpaceUp \longrightarrow \ParacompactUp
$$
whenever a finite-radius ledger is present, and the normal-space row remains mandatory for every separation-compatible export.
\end{theorem}
\leanchecked{BEDC.Derived.ParacompactUp.ParacompactCarrier\_metric\_normal\_route}
\begin{proof}
Start with $Q_{\mathrm{lf}}=(T,G,A,R,F,L,N,U,M,H,C,P,Z)$. The metric information available to the packet is exactly the handoff row $M$, so a metric consumer reads the displayed $\MetricUp$ source and any available $\FiniteLebesgueNumberUp$ finite-radius ledger before it can use a refinement export.

The refinement export itself is still topological. The rows $T,A,R,F,L$ expose the open-cover request, the refinement family, the local-finiteness ledger, and the coverage and containment ledger over the displayed topology.

For separation-compatible use, the route must pass through $N$ and then $U$. Thus the normal-space separation row and the Urysohn-facing handoff are read before the refinement is exported to any consumer that treats it as compatible with closed-pair separation data.

The replay and naming rows $H,C,P,Z$ preserve this order componentwise. With no coordinate for selected subcovers, partition-of-unity weights, host complement selection, open-cover compactness, or ambient normality, every metric paracompact read passes through the displayed metric and normal-space rows before refinement export.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Paracompact obligation closure package]
\label{thm:paracompact-obligation-closure-package}
Every accepted $\ParacompactUp$ packet has its obligation-closure surface given exactly by \autoref{def:paracompact-carrier}, \autoref{thm:paracompact-namecert-obligations}, \autoref{thm:paracompact-refinement-consumer-route}, \autoref{def:paracompact-locally-finite-refinement-carrier}, \autoref{thm:paracompact-refinement-namecert-obligations}, and \autoref{thm:paracompact-metric-normal-route}. The package exposes displayed cover requests, locally finite refinement ledgers, coverage and containment rows, normal-space and Urysohn-facing separation routes, metric finite-radius handoffs, componentwise transport, replay, provenance, and local naming. It exports no selected subcovers, arbitrary open-cover compactness, partition-of-unity closure, or host complement selection.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the displayed carrier of \autoref{def:paracompact-carrier}. Its rows expose the topology source, open-cover request, locally finite refinement ledger, coverage ledger, normal-space compatibility row, metric-or-finite-radius handoff, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming.

Apply \autoref{thm:paracompact-namecert-obligations} and \autoref{thm:paracompact-refinement-consumer-route}. These obligations force every refinement consumer to read the cover request, refinement exactness, coverage and containment rows, separation-compatible handoff, and metric finite-radius route in the displayed order.

For the locally finite surface, project \autoref{def:paracompact-locally-finite-refinement-carrier} and apply \autoref{thm:paracompact-refinement-namecert-obligations}. The local-finiteness ledger is a named row, and it is transported only with the same topology, cover, refinement, separation, metric, replay, provenance, and naming rows.

Finally apply \autoref{thm:paracompact-metric-normal-route}. The metric-normal consumer route remains downstream of the displayed metric finite-radius source and the normal-space separation row, so the package has no coordinate from which selected subcovers, arbitrary open-cover compactness, partition-of-unity closure, or host complement selection could be read.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Paracompact scoped refinement consumer route]
\label{thm:paracompact-scoped-refinement-consumer-route}
Every partition-of-unity, manifold-gluing, or normal-space refinement consumer that reads a $\ParacompactUp$ locally finite refinement must first read the same scoped packet
$$
Q_{\mathrm{lf}}=(T,G,A,R,F,L,N,U,M,H,C,P,Z)
$$
from \autoref{def:paracompact-locally-finite-refinement-carrier}. The consumer may use the refinement only after the $\TopologyUp$ cover request $A$, refinement row $R$, local-finiteness ledger $F$, coverage and containment row $L$, $\NormalSpaceUp/\UrysohnLemmaUp$ handoff $N,U$, optional $\MetricUp/\FiniteLebesgueNumberUp$ handoff $M$, and structural rows $H,C,P,Z$ have been displayed in that order. No partition-of-unity weight, manifold chart gluing datum, or normal-space separation consumer can bypass these rows or read an untracked host refinement.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the obligation closure package of \autoref{thm:paracompact-obligation-closure-package}. It already binds the displayed open-cover request, locally finite refinement ledger, coverage and containment row, normal-space route, metric finite-radius handoff, and structural transport rows into one accepted $\ParacompactUp$ packet.

The refinement NameCert obligations of \autoref{thm:paracompact-refinement-namecert-obligations} force every consumer to read $T,G,A,R,F,L,N,U,M,H,C,P,Z$ before treating the locally finite refinement as available. Thus a partition-of-unity consumer must obtain its local finite support route from $F$ and $L$, a manifold-gluing consumer must read the same cover and containment rows before chart gluing, and a normal-space consumer must pass through $N,U$ before using the refinement as separation-compatible.

Finally apply \autoref{thm:paracompact-metric-normal-route}. When a metric finite-radius handoff is present, it is still read through $M$ after the displayed metric route; otherwise the packet exports no metric shortcut. Since the carrier has no coordinate for partition-of-unity weights, chart gluing data, host refinements, selected subcovers, or arbitrary compactness, every named downstream consumer remains scoped to the displayed refinement route.
\end{proof}
