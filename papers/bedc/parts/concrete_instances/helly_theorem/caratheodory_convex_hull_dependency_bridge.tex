\begin{theorem}[Helly Caratheodory convex-hull sibling route]
\label{thm:helly-carath-lattice-sibling-route}
Every finite-intersection consumer that reads an accepted $\HellyTheoremUp$ packet must read the convex-family row through the $\ConvexSetUp$ surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-convexset-namecert}, the Caratheodory convex-hull route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-caratheodory-convex-hull-namecert}, the finite closed-family boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finite-intersection-property-namecert}, and the Helly carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-helly-theorem-namecert} before the finite intersection witness is exported. The route is a dependency-lattice projection; it does not export an isolated convex-family oracle, ambient separation theorem, quotient convex-hull equality, or selected intersection point.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the Helly carrier in \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-helly-theorem-namecert}. Its finite intersection witness is downstream of the convex-family row, finite closed-family indexing, affine or convex support rows, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming.

The convex-family row is first read through the $\ConvexSetUp$ sibling of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-convexset-namecert}. The finite support and convex-hull read then passes through the Caratheodory route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-caratheodory-convex-hull-namecert}, which records the displayed finite-combination dependency rather than an ambient hull operator.

Finally the finite-family ledger is read through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finite-intersection-property-namecert}. These three sibling routes are already dependency rows for the Helly packet, so composing them before the witness export is only a projection through the existing carrier and lattice.

Since no listed row carries an isolated convex-family oracle, separation theorem, quotient hull equality, or selected intersection point, the exported Helly witness remains the displayed finite-intersection route.
\end{proof}
