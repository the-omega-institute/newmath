\begin{theorem}[Helly convex polytope lattice route]
\label{thm:helly-convex-polytope-lattice-route}
Any accepted $\HellyTheoremUp$ finite-intersection packet reads its convex family through $\ConvexSetUp$, its finite cell witnesses through $\PolytopeUp$, and its ambient coordinates through $\AffineSpaceUp$ before it exposes the local intersection ledger. In carrier notation, the public route first displays the convex-family row $F$, the polytope witness row $W$, and the affine ambient row $A$ of \autoref{def:helly-theorem-carrier}; only then may the subfamily ledger $S$ and full-family row $I$ be consumed. The route exports no host convex-set equality, selected intersection point, separation theorem, quotient affine coordinate, or ambient standard-model convexity.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the Helly carrier of \autoref{def:helly-theorem-carrier}. Its ambient row is $A$, its convex-family row is $F$, and its finite witness row is $W$.

The convex-family row is read through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-convexset-namecert}. Thus an accepted packet displays the $\ConvexSetUp$ membership and convexity rows before a subfamily can be inspected.

The finite cell witnesses are read through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-polytope-namecert}, while the ambient coordinates are read through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-affinespace-namecert}. These rows bind the finite cells to the same affine boundary before the ledger rows $S$ and $I$ are exposed.

Finally inspect the remaining coordinates $T,C,P,N$. They transport, replay, source, and name the same finite packet, and they add no host convex equality, selected intersection point, separation theorem, quotient affine coordinate, or ambient standard-model convexity. The stated convex-polytope-affine route therefore exhausts the public Helly read.
\end{proof}
