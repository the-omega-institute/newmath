\begin{theorem}[Helly convex dependency anchor]
\label{thm:helly-theorem-convex-dependency-anchor}
Every accepted $\HellyTheoremUp$ packet consumes finite convex-family rows and finite-intersection rows before it exports the Helly $\NameCert$ surface. The convex-family rows are read through the $\ConvexSetUp$ membership surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-convexset-namecert}; the finite-intersection rows are read through the subfamily ledger of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finite-intersection-property-namecert}. Only after both rows are displayed may the packet expose its full-family intersection witness, transport rows, replay rows, provenance, and local naming.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:helly-theorem-carrier}. Its family coordinate cannot be read as an ambient convexity assertion; each member is inspected through the displayed convex-set rows.

Use the finite-intersection dependency route already recorded for the Helly packet. That route keeps the subfamily ledger visible before the full-family intersection row is exported.

The remaining rows only transport, replay, source, and name the same finite surface. Hence the Helly export is anchored in the convex-family and finite-intersection siblings rather than in a detached kernel-level theorem.
\end{proof}
