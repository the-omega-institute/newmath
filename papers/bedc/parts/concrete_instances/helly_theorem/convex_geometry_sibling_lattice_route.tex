\begin{proposition}[Helly convex-geometry sibling lattice route]
\label{prop:helly-theorem-convex-geometry-sibling-lattice-route}
Every accepted $\HellyTheoremUp$ finite-intersection packet consumes the convex-set membership rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-convexset-namecert}, the polytope finite witness rows of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-polytope-namecert}, and the convex-hull support surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-caratheodory-convex-hull-namecert} through the same finite intersection data before any local $\NameCert_{\HellyTheoremUp}$ row is read. The route is a sibling dependency lattice over displayed finite subfamilies, not a host convexity theorem or a choice-selected intersection point.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:helly-theorem-carrier}. The convex-family row $F$ supplies only $\ConvexSetUp$ membership data, while the witness row $W$ supplies the $\PolytopeUp$ finite face, simplex, or hull witnesses used by inspected subfamilies.

The finite subfamily ledger $S$ is then the shared handoff. A convex-hull consumer reads it through $\CaratheodoryConvexHullUp$ as bounded support data, and the Helly packet reads the same ledger before exposing the full-family row $I$.

Since $T,C,P,N$ only transport, replay, source, and name the displayed rows, they cannot introduce a second convex-hull carrier, an ambient separation argument, or a selected intersection point. Thus the sibling lattice is exactly the finite route through $\ConvexSetUp$, $\PolytopeUp$, the Caratheodory convex-hull surface, and the local Helly ledger.
\end{proof}

Consequently the $\HellyTheoremUp$ hub records a concrete convex-geometry sibling dependency: finite intersection data is read only after the membership, finite witness, and convex-hull support rows have been displayed inside the same $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, $\Pkg$, and $\NameCert$ surface. The route is the same finite sibling lattice, not a host convexity theorem.
