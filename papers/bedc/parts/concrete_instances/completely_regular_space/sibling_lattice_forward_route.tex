\begin{theorem}[Completely regular space sibling lattice forward route]
\label{thm:completely-regular-space-sibling-lattice-forward}
Every accepted $\CompletelyRegularSpaceUp$ read used by a compact-open or Kelleyfication consumer must first expose the sibling rows for $\TopologyUp$, $\UrysohnLemmaUp$, $\CompactHausdorffUp$, and $\SierpinskiSpaceUp$. The forward route is a finite sibling lattice edge: the consumer reaches the completely regular packet through those displayed rows before it may read compact-open function-space data, Kelleyfication reflections, or real-valued separation output.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the local carrier of \autoref{def:completely-regular-space-carrier}. Its point-versus-closed-set separation rows are usable only after the topology source and the real-valued separator route are displayed.

Attach the sibling rows. The $\TopologyUp$ row supplies the open-set carrier, $\UrysohnLemmaUp$ supplies the separation witness route, $\CompactHausdorffUp$ supplies the compact Hausdorff consumer boundary, and $\SierpinskiSpaceUp$ supplies the open-set classifier read used by lattice-facing consumers.

Now read the downstream consumers. A compact-open consumer reaches the packet through the compact-open route of \autoref{thm:completely-regular-space-compact-open-route}; a Kelleyfication consumer reaches it through the Kelleyfication route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-kelleyfication-namecert}. Both routes therefore see the four sibling rows before treating the completely regular packet as a source.

Since the four rows are finite sibling entries, the route is not a bare host topology principle, a selected family of separators, or an unlogged compactness assertion. It is a forward lattice edge from the displayed topological siblings into the compact-open and Kelleyfication readers.
\end{proof}
