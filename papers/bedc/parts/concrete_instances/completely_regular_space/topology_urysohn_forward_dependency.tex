\begin{theorem}[Completely regular topology--Urysohn forward route]
\label{thm:completely-regular-space-topology-urysohn-route}
Every $\CompletelyRegularSpaceUp$ separation read consumes the $\TopologyUp$ carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert} and the $\UrysohnLemmaUp$ separation row of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-urysohn-lemma-namecert} before any compactification, uniformity, or Kelleyfication consumer uses it.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with the topology--Urysohn dependency route recorded in \autoref{thm:completely-regular-space-topology-dependency-route}. It keeps the completely regular separator attached to the topology source, the point-versus-closed-set separation row, the Real-valued continuous-map row, and the Urysohn-facing consumer route.

Now apply the sibling dependency route of \autoref{thm:completely-regular-space-sibling-dependency}. That route places every compactification, uniformity, or real-valued separation consumer after the displayed topology carrier and the Urysohn-style separation route.

The forward dependency is therefore fixed before downstream use: a consumer reaches the completely regular row only after the $\TopologyUp$ carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert} and the $\UrysohnLemmaUp$ separation row of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-urysohn-lemma-namecert} have both been displayed. Compactification, uniformity, and Kelleyfication rows may reuse that separator, but they cannot read it as a bare host function or as a compactness principle.
\end{proof}
