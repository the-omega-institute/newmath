\begin{theorem}[Completely regular space dossier lattice anchor]
\label{thm:completely-regular-space-dossier-lattice-anchor}
Every $\CompletelyRegularSpaceUp$ dossier read that enters through the lattice route must first consume the $\TopologyUp$ carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert}, the point-versus-closed-set regularity row of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regular-topological-space-namecert}, and the Urysohn-style separation sibling of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-urysohn-lemma-namecert}. The exported route is the displayed finite separator lattice $T,R,C,F,H,K,P,N$ of \autoref{def:completely-regular-space-carrier}, not a host family of separators, an ambient normality theorem, a compactness principle, or a selected external function space.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier rows from \autoref{def:completely-regular-space-carrier}. The topology row $T$ fixes the open and closed vocabulary, while $R$ and $C$ bind the regular point-versus-closed-set request to the same source.

Now apply the topology--Urysohn dependency route and the Urysohn sibling route. They place the real-valued separator row $F$ after \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-urysohn-lemma-namecert}, so every later dossier consumer reads a finite separation route rather than a detached function-space object.

The structural rows $H,K,P,N$ then transport, replay, package, and name that same route. Since no carrier coordinate supplies host separator families, normality, compactness, or an external function space, the dossier lattice anchor is exactly the displayed sibling dependency path.
\end{proof}
