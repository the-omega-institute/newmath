\begin{theorem}[Completely regular space uniformity sibling route]
\label{thm:completely-regular-space-uniformity-sibling-route}
Every accepted $\CompletelyRegularSpaceUp$ packet that is read by a uniformity consumer exposes its point-separation and function-separation rows as a consumer of the $\UniformEntourageSpaceUp$ route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-uniform-entourage-space-namecert} and the Hausdorff separation surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-hausdorff-uniform-space-namecert}. The sibling edge records a finite route through the topology source, real-valued separator, entourage-readback boundary, Hausdorff row, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming. The $\NormalSpaceUp$ chapter of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-normalspace-namecert} remains only a separation sibling: this route does not import Urysohn's lemma, Tietze extension, normality, metrization, a host function space, or an unrestricted uniformity oracle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the completely regular carrier of \autoref{def:completely-regular-space-carrier}. Its point-versus-closed-set request and real-valued separator are visible only through the finite rows $T,R,C,F,H,K,P,N$.

Apply the existing topology--uniformity route of \autoref{thm:completely-regular-space-topology-uniformity-route}. It sends compact-open and uniformity-facing reads through the displayed topology source and separator rows before any uniformity consumer can inspect the packet.

The $\UniformEntourageSpaceUp$ and $\HausdorffUniformSpaceUp$ siblings therefore receive a finite dependency edge. Since no carrier coordinate stores normality, a Tietze operator, a Urysohn construction, metrization, a host function space, or an unrestricted uniformity oracle, the $\NormalSpaceUp$ reference remains a separation sibling rather than an imported theorem.
\end{proof}
