\begin{theorem}[Completely regular space sibling dependency]
\label{thm:completely-regular-space-sibling-dependency}
Every $\CompletelyRegularSpaceUp$ consumer reads the $\TopologyUp$ carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert} together with the local Urysohn-style separation route before any compactification, uniformity, or real-valued separation consumer can use the completely regular row.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The local topology--Urysohn dependency route records the displayed topology carrier, the point-versus-closed-set separation data, the continuous real-valued separator row, and the Urysohn consumer route as sibling inputs to the completely regular packet.

When the packet exports its continuous-separation ledger, the $\CompletelyRegularSpaceUp$ reader consumes the topology source through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert} and the metrization-facing sibling \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-urysohn-metrization-namecert}. Thus the ledger is horizontally tied to the topology carrier and the Urysohn metrization boundary before any consumer treats the real-valued separator as metrization data.

Any later compactification or uniformity reader therefore reaches $\CompletelyRegularSpaceUp$ only after those rows are present. The dependency is a finite topological lattice route through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert} and the local Urysohn-style child, not a hidden compactness principle, a choice principle, or a quotient of separation functions.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Completely regular space compact-open route]
\label{thm:completely-regular-space-compact-open-route}
Every $\CompletelyRegularSpaceUp$ compactification or uniformity consumer reads the $\TopologyUp$ carrier, the $\UrysohnMetrizationUp$ separation boundary, and the $\CompactOpenTopologyUp$ or $\CompactOpenExhaustionUp$ compact-open rows before treating real-valued separators as uniform data.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with \autoref{thm:completely-regular-space-sibling-dependency}. It places every completely regular consumer after the topology carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert} and the local Urysohn-style separation route.

The metrization-facing sibling \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-urysohn-metrization-namecert} supplies the boundary that turns real-valued separators into metric-facing data. Compact-open consumers then read that boundary through \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compact-open-topology-namecert} or through the finite exhaustion route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compact-open-exhaustion-namecert}.

Thus a compactification or uniformity reader cannot consume a separator as bare host function data. It must first pass through the topology row, the Urysohn metrization row, and the compact-open rows named by the sibling lattice.
\end{proof}
