\begin{theorem}[Completely regular space topology--Urysohn sibling lattice]
\label{thm:completely-regular-space-topology-urysohn-sibling-lattice}
Every $\CompletelyRegularSpaceUp$ packet reads its real-valued separating-function route through the $\TopologyUp$ carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-topology-namecert} and the Urysohn-style sibling route of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-urysohn-lemma-namecert} before any $\CompactOpenTopologyUp$, $\CompactOpenExhaustionUp$, $\KelleyficationUp$, or compact-Hausdorff consumer may use the exported separator. The lattice edge is the finite row sequence $T,R,C,F,H,K,P,N$ of \autoref{def:completely-regular-space-carrier}, together with \autoref{thm:completely-regular-space-urysohn-sibling-route} and \autoref{thm:completely-regular-space-compact-hausdorff-urysohn-route}; it is not a host powerset argument, an unrestricted function-space construction, a selected complement, normality, metrization, or ambient compactness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier rows of \autoref{def:completely-regular-space-carrier}. The topology row $T$ supplies the displayed open and closed vocabulary, while the request row $C$ and separator row $F$ remain tied to that same source.

Apply the Urysohn sibling route of \autoref{thm:completely-regular-space-urysohn-sibling-route}. It places the separating-function read after the topology carrier and the Urysohn-style separation route, rather than after a detached host family of real-valued functions.

Now use the compact-Hausdorff Urysohn route of \autoref{thm:completely-regular-space-compact-hausdorff-urysohn-route}. Compact-open and Kelleyfication consumers may reuse the exported separator only after the same topology and Urysohn rows have been displayed.

The structural rows $H,K,P,N$ preserve this finite lattice edge for downstream consumers. Since the carrier has no coordinate for host powersets, unrestricted function spaces, selected complements, normality, metrization, or compactness, none is exported by the sibling lattice.
\end{proof}
