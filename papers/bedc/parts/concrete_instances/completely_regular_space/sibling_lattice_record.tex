\begin{theorem}[Completely regular space sibling lattice]
\label{thm:completely-regular-space-sibling-lattice}
Every accepted $\CompletelyRegularSpaceUp$ separator route is visible in the sibling lattice
$$
\TopologyUp,\ \RegularTopologicalSpaceUp,\ \RealUp,\ \ContinuousMapUp,\ \UrysohnLemmaUp .
$$
The topology row supplies open and closed vocabulary, the regular-space row supplies the point-versus-closed-set request route, the Real and continuous-map rows carry the displayed separator, and the Urysohn row is only a consumer of that finite route. The packet does not identify this lattice with normality, metrization, a host function space, or a selected complement.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier $Q=(T,R,C,F,H,K,P,N)$ from \autoref{def:completely-regular-space-carrier}. The rows $T,R,C,F$ already determine the topology source, regular-space request, point-versus-closed-set data, and Real-valued continuous separator.

Use the real-valued separation window and Urysohn consumer route, \autoref{thm:completely-regular-space-real-valued-separation-window} and \autoref{thm:completely-regular-space-urysohn-consumer-route}. They require every Urysohn-facing read to pass through the same topology, regular-space, Real, and continuous-map rows before any separation consumer receives data.

The structural rows $H,K,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the displayed lattice. Since no carrier coordinate supplies normality, metrization, a host function space, or a selected complement, the sibling lattice remains a dependency route rather than a stronger topological theorem.
\end{proof}
