Following Cartan--Eilenberg and Bourbaki, the $\ProjectiveModuleUp$ packet records the finite idempotent-splitting surface by which a displayed module row is read as a direct summand of a finite free module row. It sits after the scalar $\RingUp$ source of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-ring-namecert}, the module carrier of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-module-namecert}, the $\LinearMapUp$ endomorphism surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-linearmap-namecert}, the exactness surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-abeliancat-namecert}, and the injective comparison surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-injective-module-namecert}. \begin{definition}[Projective module idempotent-splitting carrier]
\label{def:projective-module-carrier}
A $\ProjectiveModuleUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
J=(R,M,E,S,T,L,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $R$ is the scalar $\RingUp$ row.
\item $M$ is the displayed $\ModuleUp$ row whose projective surface is being named.
\item $E$ is the idempotent $\LinearMapUp$ endomorphism row over the finite free ambient module, carrying the displayed equation $E\circ E=E$.
\item $S$ is the section row from $M$ into the displayed ambient module.
\item $T$ is the retraction row from the ambient module back to $M$.
\item $L$ is the linear-map compatibility ledger, recording that $S$, $T$, and $E$ are read through the same scalar ring and module action rows.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the ring, module, idempotent, section, retraction, compatibility, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from a projective-module request through $R,M,E,S,T,L,H,P,N$.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\ProjectiveModuleUp$, $\RingUp$, $\ModuleUp$, $\LinearMapUp$, $\ExactSequenceUp$, $\InjectiveModuleUp$, $\SerreSwanFiniteProjectiveUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\ProjectiveModuleUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier compares accepted packets only through $R,M,E,S,T,L,H,C,P,N$. It has no coordinate for a host category of modules, quotient representatives, arbitrary choice of splittings, selected bases, or derived-functor data.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Projective module NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:projective-module-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\ProjectiveModuleUp$ carrier
$$
J=(R,M,E,S,T,L,H,C,P,N),
$$
the local $\NameCert_{\ProjectiveModuleUp}$ surface consists exactly of scalar ring exposure, module-row exposure, idempotent endomorphism exposure, section and retraction exposure, linear-map compatibility, componentwise transport, displayed replay, provenance, and local naming through $R,M,E,S,T,L,H,C,P,N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:projective-module-carrier}. The displayed rows are precisely the ring, module, idempotent, section, retraction, compatibility, transport, replay, provenance, and local name.

The algebraic read is finite. The idempotent row $E$ is a $\LinearMapUp$ row over the displayed scalar and module rows, while $S$ and $T$ are the only rows that expose the splitting route.

The compatibility ledger $L$ keeps all three linear maps over the same $R$ and $M$ data. The rows $H,C,P,N$ then transport, replay, source, and name the same finite packet.

Since no host module category, quotient representative, arbitrary splitting choice, selected basis, or derived-functor coordinate is present, the listed rows exhaust the local NameCert obligations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Projective module idempotent splitting]
\label{thm:projective-module-idempotent-splitting}
Every accepted $\ProjectiveModuleUp$ read factors through the finite idempotent-splitting route
$$
R,M \longmapsto E \longmapsto S,T,L \longmapsto H,C,P,N.
$$
Thus the packet exposes a displayed direct-summand surface for the module row, not a host module-category theorem, not quotient-representative equality, not an arbitrary choice of splittings, and not an Ext or derived-functor claim.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with a projective-module request. The carrier first exposes the scalar ring row $R$ and the module row $M$, so the request is scoped by displayed BEDC data.

The idempotent row $E$ is read next. Only after the idempotent endomorphism is displayed can the section row $S$, retraction row $T$, and compatibility ledger $L$ certify the splitting surface.

Finally, $H,C,P,N$ preserve the same route by componentwise transport, replay, provenance, and local naming. Any route using host categories, quotient representatives, arbitrary choices, or derived functors would require a coordinate absent from the accepted packet.
\end{proof}
