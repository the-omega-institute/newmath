Following Barbalat's lemma and Bishop--Bridges constructive analysis, the $\BarbalatLemmaUp$ packet records a finite uniform-continuity and tail-integral route before a $\RealUp$ seal is read. It sits beside the uniform-continuity surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-uniformcontinuity-namecert}, the regulated-integral surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regulatedintegral-namecert}, the sequence-window surface of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-streamname-namecert}, the regular-rational readback of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-regseqrat-namecert}, and the real seal of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-real-namecert}. The packet does not export a host limit, a quotient stream, or an ambient completeness theorem; it names the finite rows required to pass from uniform continuity and an integral tail budget to the displayed real readback.

\begin{definition}[Barbalat lemma finite carrier]
\label{def:barbalat-lemma-finite-carrier}
A $\BarbalatLemmaUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
B=(M,W,U,R,E,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $M$ is the $\UniformContinuityUp$ modulus row, recording the finite uniform-continuity control used by the packet.
\item $W$ is the $\StreamNameUp$ window row, exposing the finite observation window over which the control is read.
\item $U$ is the $\RegulatedIntegralUp$ tail-budget row, recording the regulated integral budget or integral tail bound used in the handoff.
\item $R$ is the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback row, naming the regular rational route that receives the displayed tail information.
\item $E$ is the $\RealUp$ seal row, read only after the modulus, window, tail budget, and readback rows are displayed.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for $M,W,U,R,E$ and for the continuation, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay from the uniform-continuity route through the tail budget, regular readback, and real seal.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\BarbalatLemmaUp$, $\UniformContinuityUp$, $\RegulatedIntegralUp$, $\StreamNameUp$, $\RegSeqRatUp$, $\RealUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\BarbalatLemmaUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier has no coordinate for a quotient stream, a selected limit, host real equality, countable choice, or ambient completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Barbalat lemma NameCert obligations]
\label{thm:barbalat-lemma-finite-namecert-obligations}
For every accepted $\BarbalatLemmaUp$ carrier $B=(M,W,U,R,E,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\BarbalatLemmaUp}$ surface consists exactly of carrier admission for the displayed $\UniformContinuityUp$ modulus, $\StreamNameUp$ window, $\RegulatedIntegralUp$ tail budget, $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback, and $\RealUp$ seal, together with componentwise $\hsame$ transport, displayed $\Cont$ replay, provenance, and local naming. The interface surface is finite and does not export a host limit, a quotient stream, or an ambient completeness claim.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:barbalat-lemma-finite-carrier}. The rows $M,W,U,R,E,H,C,P,N$ are the only public coordinates, so the finite NameCert surface is already listed by the carrier itself.

Replay the displayed route. Uniform continuity is read through $M$ and $W$, the tail budget is read through $U$, the regular rational route is read through $R$, and the real seal is reached only after those rows have been displayed. The structural rows $H,C,P,N$ preserve that same finite packet.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Barbalat lemma uniform continuity integral handoff]
\label{thm:barbalat-lemma-uniform-continuity-integral-handoff}
Every accepted $\BarbalatLemmaUp$ carrier routes a $\UniformContinuityUp$ modulus through the displayed $\StreamNameUp$ window and the $\RegulatedIntegralUp$ tail budget before the $\RegSeqRatUp$ readback can reach the terminal $\RealUp$ seal. The handoff therefore uses the finite rows $M,W,U,R,E,H,C,P,N$ and no consumer may bypass $U$ or replace the regulated-integral route by a host limit, selected completeness principle, or quotient-stream equality.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start from the carrier definition. The route is explicitly staged as modulus, window, tail budget, readback, and seal, so the integral handoff is not an ambient principle but a displayed finite route.

Now replay the structural rows. The $\hsame$ row preserves the same packet, the $\Cont$ row replays the same finite handoff, and the provenance and naming rows keep the route attached to the same accepted $\BarbalatLemmaUp$ carrier. Since the packet has no additional coordinate, no bypass can replace the regulated-integral row $U$.
\end{proof}
