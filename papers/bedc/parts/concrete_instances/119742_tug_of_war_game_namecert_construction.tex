Following Peres--Schramm--Sheffield--Wilson on tug-of-war games and infinity-harmonic limits, the $\TugOfWarGameUp$ packet records a finite alternating-play game as displayed BEDC data before any metric or PDE-facing consumer may read its value. It sits beside the viscosity-solution boundary of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-viscosity-solution-namecert}, the metric and compact-metric sources of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-metric-namecert} and \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-compactmetric-namecert}, the finite real-cover packet of \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-finite-real-cover-namecert}, and the fixed-point discipline around \autoref{ch:concrete-instances-banach-contraction-namecert}. The packet names finite play, legal moves, strategy/payoff classification, stopping, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming; it does not assert comparison, uniqueness, measurable strategy selection, martingale convergence, or ambient $\RealUp$ completeness.

\begin{definition}[Tug-of-war game carrier]
\label{def:tug-of-war-game-carrier}
A $\TugOfWarGameUp$ carrier is a finite $\BHist$ packet
$$
G=(X,A,B,L,S,V,T,H,C,P,N)
$$
with the following displayed rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $X$ is the $\MetricUp$ or $\CompactMetricUp$ source row whose inspected positions and distance comparisons constrain every move.
\item $A$ and $B$ are the two finite action rows, recording the legal alternatives available to the first and second player at each inspected position.
\item $L$ is the legal-move ledger. It records the metric or finite-cover witness that each displayed action remains inside the admitted neighbour set.
\item $S$ is the strategy/payoff classifier row. It records only finite response tables, payoff comparisons, and player-role tags visible in the packet.
\item $V$ is the value-readback row, exposing the finite dynamic-programming value requested by a viscosity-solution or fixed-point consumer.
\item $T$ is the payoff and stopping ledger, recording terminal positions, stopping flags, terminal payoff rows, and the finite horizon at which a replay stops.
\item $H$ records componentwise $\hsame$ transport for the source, action, legal-move, strategy/payoff, value, stopping, replay, provenance, and local naming rows.
\item $C$ records displayed $\Cont$ replay for legal moves: a consumer request must replay $X,A,B,L,S,V,T,H$ before a value row is exported.
\item $P$ is the $\Pkg$ provenance over $\TugOfWarGameUp$, $\MetricUp$, $\CompactMetricUp$, $\FiniteRealCoverUp$, $\ViscositySolutionUp$, $\BanachContractionUp$, $\DynamicProgrammingUp$, $\BHist$, $\hsame$, $\Cont$, and $\NameCert$.
\item $N$ is the local $\NameCert_{\TugOfWarGameUp}$ row.
\end{enumerate}
The classifier has no coordinate for host strategies, measurable selectors, infinite plays, optional-stopping theorems, quotient value functions, comparison principles, or ambient real completeness.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Tug-of-war strategy and payoff classifier]
\label{def:tug-of-war-game-strategy-payoff-classifier}
Two accepted $\TugOfWarGameUp$ carriers
$$
G=(X,A,B,L,S,V,T,H,C,P,N)
$$
and
$$
G'=(X',A',B',L',S',V',T',H',C',P',N')
$$
are classified together exactly when the stored $\hsame$ rows transport the source positions, both action rows, the legal-move ledger, the strategy/payoff table, the value-readback row, the payoff and stopping ledger, the provenance, and the local name, and when $C$ and $C'$ replay the same legal-move route. The comparison is finite-observation equality of the displayed game packet, not equality of host strategies or of an ambient value function.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Tug-of-war game carrier obligation]
\label{thm:tug-of-war-game-carrier-obligation}
For every accepted $\TugOfWarGameUp$ carrier $G=(X,A,B,L,S,V,T,H,C,P,N)$, the local $\NameCert_{\TugOfWarGameUp}$ surface consists exactly of the following five rows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item finite play carrier admission through $X,A,B,L$;
\item strategy/payoff classifier admission through $S$ and \autoref{def:tug-of-war-game-strategy-payoff-classifier};
\item componentwise $\hsame$ stability through $H$;
\item displayed $\Cont$ replay for legal moves through $C$;
\item payoff and stopping ledger admission through $T$, with value readback through $V$ and provenance/local naming through $P,N$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Project the carrier of \autoref{def:tug-of-war-game-carrier}. The finite play data are precisely the source row, the two action rows, and the legal-move ledger.

The classifier row $S$ and \autoref{def:tug-of-war-game-strategy-payoff-classifier} compare only displayed response tables and payoff comparisons. Componentwise transport $H$ preserves those rows, while $C$ replays the same legal moves before any value row is read.

The remaining game-specific information is the payoff and stopping ledger $T$, together with value readback, provenance, and local naming. Since no host strategy, infinite play, measurable selector, optional-stopping theorem, quotient value function, comparison principle, or ambient completeness row is stored in $G$, the five displayed rows exhaust the local NameCert surface.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Tug-of-war game payoff ledger]
\label{thm:tug-of-war-game-payoff-ledger}
Every payoff or value read exported by an accepted $\TugOfWarGameUp$ packet factors through
$$
X,A,B,L \longmapsto S \longmapsto V \longmapsto T \longmapsto H,C,P,N.
$$
Thus a viscosity-solution, finite-cover, compact-metric, metric, or Banach-contraction neighbour receives only the displayed finite game ledger: legal moves, finite strategy/payoff classification, value readback, stopping data, transport, replay, provenance, and local naming.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Begin with a payoff-facing consumer. Carrier projection exposes $X,A,B,L$ before any player-role, payoff, or value row is available, so legal moves are certified before they are scored.

Next read the finite classifier and value rows. The row $S$ records the response table and payoff comparison visible at the inspected positions, while $V$ records the value read requested by the downstream consumer.

Finally the stopping ledger $T$ seals the terminal positions and horizon, and $H,C,P,N$ transport, replay, source, and name the same route. The exported read therefore remains a finite game-value ledger and cannot become a host strategy selector, infinite-play value theorem, optional-stopping argument, viscosity comparison theorem, fixed-point existence theorem, quotient equality, or ambient real-completeness claim.
\end{proof}
